## 32.1 What Is a Sentence?

A **sentence** is a group of words that expresses a **complete thought**. It begins with a **capital letter** and ends with a **full stop (.)**, a **question mark (?)** or an **exclamation mark (!)**. Grammatically, a sentence must have at least one **subject** and one **finite verb** (the subject may be understood, as in commands).

::: ex
- The sun rises in the east.
- Where do you live?
- Shut the door. (subject *you* understood)
- What a beautiful flower it is!
- ✘ *In the morning.* / ✘ *Because he was ill.* — not sentences (fragments)
:::

### 32.1.1 Subject and Predicate

Every sentence has two parts:

- **Subject** — the person or thing **about which** something is said.
- **Predicate** — what is **said about** the subject (it always contains the **finite verb**).

| Subject | Predicate |
|---|---|
| The little boy | is crying. |
| My elder brother | works in a bank in Chattogram. |
| Honesty | is the best policy. |
| (You) | Shut the door. |
| Where | do you live? (*you* = subject; *do … live where* = predicate) |

The **simple subject** is the main noun/pronoun (*boy*); the **complete subject** includes its modifiers (*The little boy*). The **simple predicate** is the verb (*is crying*); the **complete predicate** includes objects, complements and modifiers.

### 32.1.2 The Elements of a Sentence and Basic Patterns

A clause can contain five kinds of elements: **Subject (S), Verb (V), Object (O — direct Od / indirect Oi), Complement (C — subjective Cs / objective Co)** and **Adverbial (A)**. They combine into seven basic patterns:

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **S + V** | *Birds **fly**.* / *The baby **is sleeping**.* |
| **S + V + O** | *She **wrote** a letter.* |
| **S + V + C** | *He **is** a doctor. She **looks** tired.* |
| **S + V + A** | *He **lives** in Dhaka. The meeting **is** at five.* |
| **S + V + Oi + Od** | *He **gave** me a book.* |
| **S + V + O + C** | *They **elected** him captain. The news **made** her sad.* |
| **S + V + O + A** | *She **put** the book on the table.* |

Understanding these patterns helps with voice change (Chapter 26), word-order errors and sentence building.

## 32.2 Classification of Sentences by Meaning (Function)

According to **meaning or purpose**, sentences are of **five** kinds:

| Kind | Purpose | Ends with | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Assertive (Declarative)** | states a fact or opinion | . | *Dhaka is the capital of Bangladesh.* |
| **Interrogative** | asks a question | ? | *Where do you live?* |
| **Imperative** | gives a command, request, advice or prohibition | . (or !) | *Please sit down.* |
| **Optative** | expresses a wish or prayer | ! (or .) | *May you live long!* |
| **Exclamatory** | expresses strong feeling | ! | *What a lovely day it is!* |

## 32.3 Assertive (Declarative) Sentences

An **assertive sentence** makes a **statement** — it states a fact, an opinion or a belief. It may be **affirmative** (positive) or **negative**.

**Structure:** Subject + Verb + (Object / Complement / Adverbial). Normal word order.

::: ex
- **Affirmative:** *The earth moves round the sun. I like mangoes. He has finished his work.*
- **Negative:** *He does not smoke. I have never seen a tiger. Nobody came. He can hardly walk.*
:::

::: note
A sentence with ***hardly, scarcely, seldom, rarely, never, no, nobody, nothing, neither, none, few, little*** is **negative in meaning** even if it has no *not* (important for tag questions and transformation).
:::

## 32.4 Interrogative Sentences

An **interrogative sentence** asks a **question** and ends with a **question mark**.

| Type | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Yes/No question** | Auxiliary + S + V …? | ***Are** you ready? **Did** he come? **Can** you swim?* |
| **Wh-question** | Wh-word + auxiliary + S + V …? | ***Where do** you live? **Why are** you late? **What did** he say?* |
| **Wh-question (wh-word as subject)** | Wh-word + V …? (no inversion) | ***Who broke** the glass? **What happened**?* |
| **Alternative question** | … or …? | *Do you want tea **or** coffee?* |
| **Tag question** | statement + tag? | *You are a student, **aren't you?*** (Chapter 28) |
| **Negative question** | Auxiliary + n't + S …? (surprise, expected *yes*) | ***Isn't** it a beautiful day? **Didn't** I tell you?* |
| **Statement question** (spoken) | statement form + ? (rising tone) | *You are leaving already?* |

### 32.4.1 Rhetorical Questions

A **rhetorical question** is a question that **does not expect an answer**; it is used for **effect**, and it really makes an **assertion**. A positive rhetorical question means a **negative** statement, and vice versa.

::: ex
- **Who** does not love his country? (= *Everybody loves his country.*)
- **What** is the use of crying? (= *It is no use crying.*)
- **Can** the leopard change its spots? (= *The leopard cannot change its spots.*)
- **Is** this the way to treat a guest? (= *This is not the way to treat a guest.*)
- **Who** can tell what will happen tomorrow? (= *Nobody can tell …*)
:::

### 32.4.2 Indirect (Embedded) Questions

A question **inside a statement** is not an interrogative sentence; it has **statement word order** and **no question mark**: *I wonder **where he lives**. Tell me **what your name is**.* (Chapter 37)

## 32.5 Imperative Sentences

An **imperative sentence** expresses a **command, order, request, advice, suggestion, warning, invitation or prohibition**. The subject *you* is usually **understood**, and the verb is in the **base form**.

| Meaning | Example |
|---|---|
| Command / order | *Shut the door. Stand up. Get out.* |
| Request | ***Please** help me. **Kindly** lend me your pen. Open the window, **will you?*** |
| Advice | *Work hard. Take care of your health. Never tell a lie.* |
| Prohibition | ***Don't** smoke here. **Do not** make a noise. **Never** do it again.* |
| Warning | *Look out! Beware of dogs. Mind your step.* |
| Invitation | *Come and have dinner with us. Have some tea.* |
| Suggestion / proposal (*Let us*) | ***Let us** go for a walk. **Let's** play.* |
| Permission (*Let me / him*) | ***Let me** go. **Let him** do it.* |
| Emphatic | ***Do** sit down. **Do** come again.* |
| With subject (contrast or emphasis) | ***You** stay here; I'll go. **Somebody** answer the phone.* |

::: note
***Would you mind …? / Could you please …? / Will you …?*** are **interrogative** in form but **imperative (request)** in meaning. In exams, classify by **form** unless the question asks for meaning: *"Will you open the door?"* — interrogative sentence (expressing a request).
:::

## 32.6 Optative Sentences

An **optative sentence** expresses a **wish, prayer, blessing or curse**. It usually begins with ***May***, or uses a subjunctive form (*Long live, God bless*), *Would that, If only, Wish*.

::: ex
- **May** you live long!
- **May** Allah bless you!
- **May** you be happy in your new life!
- **Long live** Bangladesh!
- **God save** the Queen!
- **Would that** I were young again!
- **If only** I could see her once more!
- **Wish** you a happy journey!
- **Good luck** to you! / **Happy** birthday!
:::

::: tip
**Exam Tip — *May* in two kinds of sentences:** *May I come in?* — an **interrogative** sentence (asking permission; ends with ?). *May you prosper!* — an **optative** sentence (expressing a wish; ends with !). The word order differs: *May **I** come* (question) vs *May **you** prosper* (wish to someone).
:::

## 32.7 Exclamatory Sentences

An **exclamatory sentence** expresses a **sudden or strong feeling** — joy, sorrow, surprise, anger, wonder, praise — and ends with an **exclamation mark**.

**Common structures:**

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| ***What* + a/an + (adjective) + singular noun + S + V!** | ***What a** beautiful garden it is!* |
| ***What* + (adjective) + plural/uncountable noun + S + V!** | ***What** lovely flowers they are! **What** fine weather it is!* |
| ***How* + adjective/adverb + S + V!** | ***How** beautiful the garden is! **How** fast he runs!* |
| **Interjection + sentence** | ***Alas!** He is dead. **Hurrah!** We have won.* |
| ***If only / Oh that* …!** | ***If only** I were rich!* |
| **Short exclamations** | *What a pity! How sad! Good heavens!* |

::: ex
- **What a** fool he is!
- **How** sweetly she sings!
- **What** a wonderful scene!
- **How** kind of you!
- **Alas!** We have lost the match.
- **Bravo!** Well done!
:::

::: note
In an exclamatory sentence, the **subject comes before the verb** (statement order): *How beautiful **the garden is**!* (✘ How beautiful is the garden! — this is literary/poetic only.)
:::

## 32.8 Identifying the Kind of Sentence: Tricky Cases

::: errs
1. *Who can tell what will happen?* → **Interrogative** in form (rhetorical; assertive in meaning: *Nobody can tell …*).
2. *Will you please help me?* → **Interrogative** in form (request in meaning).
3. *May you be happy!* → **Optative**.
4. *May I come in?* → **Interrogative**.
5. *Let us go out.* → **Imperative** (proposal).
6. *How do you do?* → **Interrogative** in form (a formal greeting).
7. *How happy he is!* → **Exclamatory**.
8. *How happy is he?* → **Interrogative** (asking the degree of happiness).
9. *I wonder where he is.* → **Assertive** (contains an indirect question).
10. *Never tell a lie.* → **Imperative** (advice).
11. *He never tells a lie.* → **Assertive** (negative).
12. *Long live our motherland!* → **Optative**.
13. *What a pity!* → **Exclamatory**.
14. *What is your name?* → **Interrogative**.
15. *Don't you know him?* → **Interrogative** (negative question).
:::

## 32.9 Sentence Types in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
- **Fragments (SAT):** Every sentence needs a subject and a finite verb in an independent clause. Watch out for "sentences" that are really long phrases or dependent clauses: ✘ *The new library, which opened last year and has thousands of books.* (no main verb for *library*) ✔ *The new library, which opened last year, **has** thousands of books.*
- **Rhetorical questions in GRE/SAT Reading** often signal the author's **position** — the expected answer is the author's point.
- **GMAT Sentence Correction** uses only **declarative** sentences; imperatives and questions do not appear in answer choices.
:::
