## 26.1 What Is Voice?

**Voice** is the form of a verb that shows whether the **subject does the action** or **receives the action**.

- **Active voice:** the subject **acts** (is the doer). *Rahim **wrote** a letter.*
- **Passive voice:** the subject **is acted upon** (is the receiver). *A letter **was written** by Rahim.*

::: ex
- **Active:** The cat killed the rat.
- **Passive:** The rat was killed by the cat.
- **Active:** People speak English all over the world.
- **Passive:** English is spoken all over the world.
:::

### 26.1.1 When Is the Passive Used?

1. When the **doer is unknown**: *My bicycle **has been stolen**.*
2. When the **doer is obvious or unimportant**: *The thief **was arrested**.* (by the police — obvious)
3. When we want to **focus on the action or the receiver**: *The Padma Bridge **was opened** in 2022.*
4. In **formal, scientific and official writing**: *The solution **was heated** to 80 °C. Applications **are invited** for the post …*
5. To **avoid blame** or sound impersonal: *Mistakes **were made**.*

## 26.2 Basic Rules for Changing Active into Passive

::: rule
1. The **object** of the active sentence becomes the **subject** of the passive.
2. The **verb** becomes **be (in the correct tense) + past participle (V3)**.
3. The **subject** of the active sentence becomes the **agent**, usually after ***by*** — and a pronoun changes to the **objective case**.
4. **Only transitive verbs** (verbs with an object) can be changed into the passive.
:::

### 26.2.1 Pronoun Changes

| Active (subjective) | Passive (objective, after *by*) | Active (objective) | Passive (subjective) |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | by me | me | I |
| we | by us | us | we |
| you | by you | you | you |
| he | by him | him | he |
| she | by her | her | she |
| they | by them | them | they |
| it | by it | it | it |
| who | by whom | whom | who |

## 26.3 Passive Forms in All Tenses

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Present Indefinite | He **writes** a letter. | A letter **is written** by him. |
| Present Continuous | He **is writing** a letter. | A letter **is being written** by him. |
| Present Perfect | He **has written** a letter. | A letter **has been written** by him. |
| Past Indefinite | He **wrote** a letter. | A letter **was written** by him. |
| Past Continuous | He **was writing** a letter. | A letter **was being written** by him. |
| Past Perfect | He **had written** a letter. | A letter **had been written** by him. |
| Future Indefinite | He **will write** a letter. | A letter **will be written** by him. |
| Future Perfect | He **will have written** a letter. | A letter **will have been written** by him. |
| *Be going to* | He **is going to write** a letter. | A letter **is going to be written** by him. |
| Modals | He **can / may / must / should write** a letter. | A letter **can / may / must / should be written** by him. |
| Modal + perfect | He **must have written** the letter. | The letter **must have been written** by him. |
| *Used to* | He **used to write** letters. | Letters **used to be written** by him. |
| *Have to* | He **has to write** a letter. | A letter **has to be written** by him. |

::: note
The **perfect continuous** tenses (present, past, future) and the **future continuous** are not normally used in the passive (✘ *has been being written*, ✘ *will be being written*). If such a sentence must be made passive, change the tense meaningfully or say that it has no passive form.
:::

::: ex
- They **are building** a new bridge. → A new bridge **is being built** (by them).
- She **has cooked** the rice. → The rice **has been cooked** by her.
- The police **had arrested** the thief. → The thief **had been arrested** by the police.
- We **shall finish** the work tomorrow. → The work **will be finished** by us tomorrow.
- You **must obey** the rules. → The rules **must be obeyed** (by you).
:::

## 26.4 Verbs with Two Objects

When a verb has an **indirect object** (person) and a **direct object** (thing), either can become the subject of the passive. Using the **person** as subject is more common.

::: ex
- **Active:** He gave **me** **a book**.
- **Passive 1:** **I** was given a book by him. (more common)
- **Passive 2:** **A book** was given **to** me by him.

- **Active:** The teacher taught **us** **English**.
- **Passive:** **We** were taught English by the teacher. / **English** was taught **to** us by the teacher.

- **Active:** They offered **her** **a job**.
- **Passive:** **She** was offered a job. / **A job** was offered **to** her.
:::

## 26.5 Verbs with Object + Complement

When the active verb has an object and an **objective complement**, the complement remains after the passive verb:

::: ex
- They **elected** him **chairman**. → He **was elected chairman**.
- We **made** her **captain**. → She **was made captain**.
- They **called** him **a liar**. → He **was called a liar**.
- The news **made** her **sad**. → She **was made sad** by the news.
- They **painted** the house **red**. → The house **was painted red**.
:::

## 26.6 Passive of Interrogative Sentences

### 26.6.1 Yes/No Questions (Beginning with an Auxiliary)

Change to the passive in the normal way, keeping the **auxiliary at the beginning**.

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| **Did** he write the letter? | **Was** the letter written by him? |
| **Does** she sing a song? | **Is** a song sung by her? |
| **Do** you know him? | **Is** he known **to** you? |
| **Have** you finished the work? | **Has** the work been finished by you? |
| **Is** he doing the sum? | **Is** the sum being done by him? |
| **Will** you help me? | **Shall** I be helped by you? |
| **Can** you solve this problem? | **Can** this problem be solved by you? |
| **Did** they not invite you? | **Were** you not invited by them? |

### 26.6.2 *Wh*-Questions

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| **Who** wrote this book? | **By whom** was this book written? / Who was this book written by? |
| **Whom** did you see? | **Who** was seen by you? (*whom* → *who*) |
| **What** did he say? | **What** was said by him? |
| **Which** book do you want? | **Which** book is wanted by you? |
| **Why** did he beat the dog? | **Why** was the dog beaten by him? |
| **When** will you finish the work? | **When** will the work be finished by you? |
| **Where** did you buy this pen? | **Where** was this pen bought by you? |
| **How** did he solve the problem? | **How** was the problem solved by him? |
| **How many** books have you read? | **How many** books have been read by you? |

## 26.7 Passive of Imperative Sentences

**(a) Commands with an object:** ***Let* + object + *be* + V3**.

::: ex
- Shut the door. → **Let** the door **be shut**.
- Open the window. → **Let** the window **be opened**.
- Do it at once. → **Let** it **be done** at once.
- Don't touch the wire. → **Let** the wire **not be touched**. / **Let not** the wire **be touched**.
- Never tell a lie. → **Let** a lie **never be told**.
:::

**(b) Requests, advice, orders — using *You are requested / advised / ordered / asked / forbidden to*.**

::: ex
- Please help me. → **You are requested to help** me. / Let me be helped.
- Please give me a glass of water. → **You are requested to give** me a glass of water.
- Obey your parents. → **You are advised to obey** your parents. / Let your parents be obeyed.
- Get out. → **You are ordered to get** out.
- Don't smoke here. → **You are forbidden to smoke** here. / Smoking is prohibited here.
- Go home. (intransitive) → **You are ordered to go** home.
:::

**(c) Imperatives with *Let*:**

::: ex
- Let him do the work. → Let the work **be done by him**.
- Let us play football. → Let football **be played by us**.
- Let me help you. → Let you **be helped by me**.
:::

## 26.8 Passive of Infinitives and Gerunds

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| I want **to write** a letter. | I want a letter **to be written**. |
| There is much work **to do**. | There is much work **to be done**. |
| He is said **to have written** fifty books. | Fifty books are said **to have been written** by him. |
| I hate people **cheating** me. | I hate **being cheated** (by people). |
| I remember **them taking** me to the zoo. | I remember **being taken** to the zoo (by them). |
| He insisted on **beating** the boy. | He insisted on the boy **being beaten**. |

::: ex
- She likes people to praise her. → She likes **to be praised**.
- I don't like people to laugh at me. → I don't like **to be laughed at**. / I don't like **being laughed at**.
- The house needs painting. = The house needs **to be painted**.
:::

## 26.9 Passive of Prepositional and Phrasal Verbs

When a verb is followed by a preposition (forming one meaning unit), the **preposition stays with the verb** in the passive.

::: ex
- They **laughed at** the beggar. → The beggar **was laughed at** (by them).
- She **looks after** her mother. → Her mother **is looked after** by her.
- The doctor **sent for** the nurse. → The nurse **was sent for** by the doctor.
- A truck **ran over** the dog. → The dog **was run over** by a truck.
- We must **look into** the matter. → The matter must **be looked into**.
- They **put off** the meeting. → The meeting **was put off**.
- You must **take care of** your health. → Your health must **be taken care of**. / **Care** must be taken **of** your health.
- He **made use of** the money. → The money **was made use of** by him. / **Use** was made **of** the money.
- They **paid attention to** his speech. → His speech **was paid attention to**. / **Attention was paid to** his speech.
:::

::: note
**Don't drop the preposition:** ✘ *The beggar was laughed by them.* ✔ *The beggar was laughed **at** by them.* ✘ *The child was taken care by her.* ✔ *The child was taken care **of** by her.*
:::

## 26.10 Passive with Reporting Verbs (*say, think, believe, know, report, expect, consider*)

There are two passive patterns for sentences such as *People say that he is rich*:

| Active | Passive 1 (*It* + passive + *that*) | Passive 2 (Subject + passive + *to*-infinitive) |
|---|---|---|
| People **say** that he **is** rich. | **It is said** that he is rich. | He **is said to be** rich. |
| People **believe** that he **was** a spy. | **It is believed** that he was a spy. | He **is believed to have been** a spy. |
| They **think** that she **is working** abroad. | **It is thought** that she is working abroad. | She **is thought to be working** abroad. |
| They **reported** that the minister **had resigned**. | **It was reported** that the minister had resigned. | The minister **was reported to have resigned**. |
| We **know** that the earth **is** round. | **It is known** that the earth is round. | The earth **is known to be** round. |

::: tip
**Exam Tip — Choose the right infinitive:** If the action in the *that*-clause happens **at the same time** as the reporting verb, use ***to* + V1** (*is said **to be***). If it happened **earlier**, use ***to have* + V3** (*is said **to have been***).
:::

## 26.11 Passive Agents: *By* and Other Prepositions

The agent is usually introduced by ***by***, but after many past participles expressing **feelings, states and results**, a **different preposition** is used:

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| His conduct **pleased** me. | I was pleased **with** his conduct. |
| The result **satisfied** us. | We were satisfied **with** the result. |
| The news **surprised** me. | I was surprised **at** the news. |
| His behaviour **shocked** us. | We were shocked **at / by** his behaviour. |
| Music **interests** her. | She is interested **in** music. |
| Everybody **knows** him. | He is known **to** everybody. (✘ by everybody) |
| Water **filled** the jar. | The jar was filled **with** water. |
| Snow **covered** the hills. | The hills were covered **with** snow. |
| The box **contains** books. | Books are contained **in** the box. |
| Her rudeness **annoyed** me. | I was annoyed **with / at** her rudeness. |
| The work **tired** him. | He was tired **of / from** the work. |
| The dog **frightened** the child. | The child was frightened **of / by** the dog. |
| The result **disappointed** him. | He was disappointed **at / with** the result. |
| Everyone **loves** her. | She is loved **by** everyone. |

## 26.12 When the Agent Is Omitted

The agent (*by* + doer) is **omitted** when it is a general word (*people, someone, they, we, one, you*), unknown, or obvious:

::: ex
- **Someone** has stolen my pen. → My pen **has been stolen**. (✘ by someone)
- **People** speak English everywhere. → English **is spoken** everywhere.
- **They** grow rice in Bangladesh. → Rice **is grown** in Bangladesh.
- **The police** arrested the thief. → The thief **was arrested**. (police obvious; including it is also correct)
:::

## 26.13 Verbs That Cannot Be Made Passive

1. **Intransitive verbs** (no object): *He sleeps. The baby cried. The sun rises.* — no passive.
2. **Certain stative transitive verbs:** *have* (= possess), *resemble, lack, suit, fit, become* (= suit), *hold* (= contain), *equal, mean, cost, weigh*: ✘ *A car is had by him.* ✘ *His father is resembled by him.* ✘ *Good manners are lacked by him.* ✘ *This dress is suited by her.*
3. **Verbs with a reflexive object:** *He hurt himself.* (✘ Himself was hurt by him.)
4. **Verbs with a cognate object** are rarely made passive: *He lived a happy life* → (*A happy life was lived by him* is awkward).

## 26.14 Quasi-Passive Verbs

Some verbs are **active in form but passive in meaning**. They are called **quasi-passive** verbs. Exams sometimes ask to change them into the "passive":

| Quasi-passive (active form) | Passive meaning |
|---|---|
| The rose **smells** sweet. | The rose **is** sweet **when it is smelt**. |
| Honey **tastes** sweet. | Honey **is** sweet **when it is tasted**. |
| The cloth **feels** soft. | The cloth **is** soft **when it is felt**. |
| This book **sells** well. | This book **is sold** well. |
| The house **is building**. (old) | The house **is being built**. |
| The milk **looks** sour. | The milk **is** sour **when it is looked at**. |
| These shirts **wash** easily. | These shirts **are washed** easily. |

## 26.15 Passive of Complex Sentences

In a complex sentence, the **main clause** and/or the **subordinate clause** may be changed:

::: ex
- **Active:** I know that he wrote the letter.
- **Passive:** **It is known to me** that **the letter was written** by him.

- **Active:** We expect that he will win the prize.
- **Passive:** **It is expected** (by us) that **the prize will be won** by him.

- **Active:** He said that he had seen the film.
- **Passive:** **It was said** by him that **the film had been seen** by him.
:::

## 26.16 The Causative Passive and *Get*-Passive

- **Causative (*have / get* + object + V3)** — someone else does the action for us: *I **had** my car **repaired**. She **got** her hair **cut**.* (Active idea: *A mechanic repaired my car.*)
- **Get-passive** — informal, often for unexpected or unpleasant events: *He **got hurt** in the accident. The window **got broken**. They **got married** last year.*

## 26.17 Special Cases

**(a) *Make, see, hear, let, help* + bare infinitive → *to* + infinitive in the passive:**

::: ex
- They **made** him **work** hard. → He **was made to work** hard.
- I **saw** him **enter** the house. → He **was seen to enter** the house.
- We **heard** her **sing**. → She **was heard to sing**.
- They **let** him **go**. → He **was allowed to go**. (*let* has no direct passive)
:::

**(b) *Born*, *drowned*, *killed* in accidents** — passive in meaning:

::: ex
- He **was born** in 1971. (✘ He born in 1971.)
- Many people **were drowned / were killed** in the flood.
:::

**(c) Passive with *by* + reflexive is not used:** ✘ *The letter was written by himself.* ✔ *He wrote the letter himself.*

**(d) Sentences beginning with *There*:** *There is no time to lose* (active infinitive, idiomatic) / *There is much work to be done* (passive infinitive).

## 26.18 Changing Passive into Active

Reverse the process: the **agent** after *by* becomes the subject; the passive subject becomes the object; *be* + V3 becomes the active verb in the same tense. If there is no agent, supply a suitable general subject (*someone, people, they, we*).

::: ex
- The thief was caught by the police. → **The police caught** the thief.
- The letter has been posted. → **Someone has posted** the letter.
- English is spoken all over the world. → **People speak** English all over the world.
- By whom was this book written? → **Who wrote** this book?
- Let the door be closed. → **Close** the door.
- You are requested to sign here. → **Please sign** here.
:::

## 26.19 Common Errors in Voice

::: errs
1. ✘ The letter was wrote by him. ✔ The letter was **written** by him.
2. ✘ The work is being did. ✔ The work is being **done**.
3. ✘ He was born at 1990. ✔ He was born **in** 1990.
4. ✘ The thief was caught by they. ✔ The thief was caught by **them**.
5. ✘ The beggar was laughed by the boys. ✔ The beggar was laughed **at** by the boys.
6. ✘ He is known by everybody. ✔ He is known **to** everybody.
7. ✘ I was surprised by his conduct. (exam) ✔ I was surprised **at** his conduct.
8. ✘ The jar is filled by water. ✔ The jar is filled **with** water.
9. ✘ A pen was bought to me. ✔ A pen was bought **for** me. / I was bought a pen.
10. ✘ He was made work hard. ✔ He was made **to work** hard.
11. ✘ By whom this book was written? ✔ By whom **was this book** written?
12. ✘ Who was this book written? ✔ **By whom** was this book written?
13. ✘ Let the door be shutted. ✔ Let the door be **shut**.
14. ✘ The baby is cried. (intransitive) ✔ The baby **cries / is crying**.
15. ✘ A car is had by him. ✔ He **has** a car. (no passive)
16. ✘ He is said to be written a novel. ✔ He is said **to have written** a novel.
17. ✘ My watch has stolen. ✔ My watch **has been stolen**.
18. ✘ The child was taken care by the nurse. ✔ … was taken care **of** by the nurse.
19. ✘ The meeting will be held on Monday by us. ✔ The meeting **will be held** on Monday. (omit obvious agent)
20. ✘ Rice is growing in Bangladesh. (meaning: is cultivated) ✔ Rice **is grown** in Bangladesh.
:::

## 26.20 Voice in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
- **Prefer the active voice:** GMAT and SAT reward **concise, direct** sentences. When a choice uses the passive without good reason (*The decision was made by the board to …*), the active version (*The board decided to …*) is usually better. The passive is **not wrong**, however; if all options are passive, judge on other grounds.
- **Passive with dangling modifiers:** ✘ *Having reviewed the data, a decision was made.* (Who reviewed?) ✔ *Having reviewed the data, **the committee** made a decision.*
- **Correct passive forms:** ✘ *has been rose*, ✘ *was began* — check past participles.
- **GRE Reading:** passive constructions like *"it has been argued that …"* or *"it is widely believed that …"* often introduce a view the **author will challenge** — a useful signal for main-idea questions.
:::
