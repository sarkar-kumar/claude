## 42.1 One Form, Many Functions

English allows a single word to work as **different parts of speech without any change in form** — a process called **conversion** or **functional shift**. *Water* is a noun (*a glass of water*), a verb (*water the plants*) and an adjective (*a water tank*). The part of speech is decided by the **function in the sentence** (Chapter 01).

This chapter gives a large reference list of such words with model sentences. Exams ask: "*Use the word X as a noun and as a verb*" or "*Identify the part of speech of the underlined word.*"

::: rule
**Identification reminders:** after *a/an/the/my* → noun; after *to* or a modal, or carrying tense → verb; before a noun or after *be/seem* → adjective; modifying a verb/adjective → adverb; followed by a noun/pronoun object showing relation → preposition; joining clauses → conjunction.
:::

## 42.6 Words Used as Many Parts of Speech: Detailed Examples

### 42.6.1 *Fast*

::: ex
- He is a **fast** runner. (adjective)
- He runs **fast**. (adverb)
- Muslims **fast** during Ramadan. (verb)
- He broke his **fast** at sunset. (noun)
- The boat is held **fast** by the rope. (adverb = firmly)
:::

### 42.6.2 *Light*

::: ex
- The **light** is too bright. (noun)
- Please **light** the candle. (verb)
- This box is **light**. (adjective = not heavy) / a **light** blue shirt (adjective = pale)
- He travels **light**. (adverb)
:::

### 42.6.3 *Last*

::: ex
- This is the **last** chance. (adjective)
- He came **last**. (adverb)
- He was the **last** to leave. (noun/pronoun)
- The rain will not **last** long. (verb)
- The shoemaker put the shoe on the **last**. (noun = a foot-shaped block)
:::

### 42.6.4 *Right*

::: ex
- Education is a basic **right**. (noun)
- He gave the **right** answer. (adjective)
- Turn **right**. (adverb)
- The government should **right** this wrong. (verb)
:::

### 42.6.5 *Even*

::: ex
- Two is an **even** number. (adjective)
- **Even** a child can do it. (adverb)
- They **evened** the score. (verb)
- the **even** (noun, poetic = evening)
:::

### 42.6.6 *Better*

::: ex
- This book is **better**. (adjective)
- She sings **better** than her sister. (adverb)
- He **bettered** the world record. (verb)
- Respect your **betters**. (noun = superiors)
- He got the **better** of his rival. (noun = advantage)
:::

### 42.6.7 *Round*

::: ex
- The earth is **round**. (adjective)
- He won the first **round**. (noun)
- The car **rounded** the corner. (verb)
- The earth moves **round** the sun. (preposition)
- Turn **round**. (adverb)
:::

### 42.6.8 *Since*

::: ex
- I have been here **since** morning. (preposition)
- **Since** he is ill, he cannot come. (conjunction — reason)
- It is ten years **since** he died. (conjunction — time)
- I have not seen him **since**. (adverb)
:::

### 42.6.9 *Over*

::: ex
- The bird flew **over** the house. (preposition)
- The game is **over**. (adverb/adjective)
- He bowled six **overs**. (noun — cricket)
- The children are **over**-excited. (prefix)
:::

### 42.6.10 *Present*

::: ex
- He gave me a **present**. (noun = gift)
- Live in the **present**. (noun = now)
- All the students were **present**. (adjective)
- The **present** government (adjective = current)
- The Principal will **present** the prizes. (verb)
:::

## 42.7 Common Errors from Functional Confusion

::: errs
1. ✘ He runs very fastly. ✔ He runs very **fast**. (*fast* is already an adverb)
2. ✘ He works hardly. (= with effort) ✔ He works **hard**.
3. ✘ She sings very good. ✔ She sings very **well**.
4. ✘ He came lately. (= not on time) ✔ He came **late**.
5. ✘ This is a friendly behaviour of him. ✔ This is **friendly** behaviour on his part. (*friendly* is an adjective; use *in a friendly way* as adverb)
6. ✘ The water of the lake is very stilly. ✔ The water of the lake is very **still**.
7. ✘ He did nothing like I told him. ✔ He did nothing **as** I told him. (formal: *like* is a preposition)
8. ✘ Please light on the lamp. ✔ Please **light** the lamp. / Please **switch on** the light.
:::

## 42.8 Functional Shift in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
- **GRE vocabulary:** many words have a **less common secondary meaning** as a different part of speech, and the GRE loves them: *temper* (v. moderate), *qualify* (v. limit a statement), *champion* (v. support), *table* (v. postpone in AmE / present in BrE), *check* (v. restrain), *flag* (v. weaken), *wanting* (adj. lacking), *wax* (v. grow), *wane* (v. decline), *bent* (n. inclination), *list* (v. lean to one side), *ferret* (v. search out), *badger* (v. pester), *dog* (v. follow persistently), *hound* (v. harass), *parrot* (v. repeat mechanically), *steep* (v. soak; *steeped in tradition*).
- **SAT Words in Context** often tests exactly this: *"the senator's **proposal** was **tabled**"* — here *tabled* means postponed (AmE).
- **GMAT:** ✘ *like* as a conjunction before a clause; ✔ *as*.
:::
