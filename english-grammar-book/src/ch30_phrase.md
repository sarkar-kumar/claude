## 30.1 What Is a Phrase?

A **phrase** is a **group of words** that makes sense but **not complete sense**, and that **does not contain a subject and a finite verb** of its own. It works as **a single part of speech** within a sentence.

::: ex
- **in the morning** — no subject, no finite verb
- **a very beautiful garden**
- **to learn English**
- **having finished the work**
- **at the top of the hill**
:::

### 30.1.1 Phrase vs Clause vs Sentence

| Unit | Subject + finite verb? | Complete sense? | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phrase** | No | No | *in the garden* |
| **Clause** | Yes | Not always (dependent clause: no) | *when he came* / *he came* |
| **Sentence** | Yes (at least one main clause) | Yes | *He came in the morning.* |

::: tip
**Exam Tip — The finite-verb test:** If a group of words contains a **subject + finite verb**, it is a **clause**. If it does not (even if it contains an infinitive, gerund or participle), it is a **phrase**. *Having finished the work* — *having finished* is a participle, not a finite verb → **phrase**. *When he had finished the work* — subject *he* + finite *had finished* → **clause**.
:::

## 30.2 Kinds of Phrases (by Function)

Traditional grammar classifies phrases by the **part of speech** they act as. Modern grammar classifies them by their **head word** (noun phrase, verb phrase, etc.). Both systems are useful; exam questions in Bangladesh usually follow the first.

| Kind (traditional) | Works as | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Noun phrase** | a noun | ***To tell lies** is a sin.* |
| **Adjective phrase** | an adjective | *He is a man **of great wealth**.* |
| **Adverb phrase** | an adverb | *He spoke **in a loud voice**.* |
| **Prepositional phrase** | adjective or adverb (by function) | *The book **on the table** is mine. / He sat **on the table**.* |
| **Verb phrase** | a verb | *He **has been working**.* |
| **Phrasal preposition** | a preposition | *He failed **on account of** illness.* |
| **Phrasal conjunction** | a conjunction | *I will wait **as long as** you want.* |
| **Interjectional phrase** | an interjection | ***Good heavens!** What happened?* |

## 30.3 The Noun Phrase

A **noun phrase** does the work of a **noun**: it can be a **subject, object, complement** or **object of a preposition**. Test: replace it with *it / this / something* — if the sentence still works, it is a noun phrase.

| Structure | Example | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Infinitive phrase | ***To err** is human.* | subject |
| | *He wants **to become a doctor**.* | object |
| | *My aim is **to serve the poor**.* | complement |
| Gerund phrase | ***Reading good books** improves the mind.* | subject |
| | *I enjoy **watching cricket**.* | object |
| | *He is fond of **playing chess**.* | object of preposition |
| Determiner + adjective + noun | ***The old man in the corner** is my uncle.* | subject |
| *Wh*-word + infinitive | *I don't know **how to swim**.* | object |
| | *Tell me **what to do**.* | object |

::: ex
- **Climbing the hill** was difficult. (= *It* was difficult)
- I like **to read detective stories**.
- He tried **to solve the problem**.
- Her hobby is **collecting stamps**.
- **The capital of Bangladesh** is Dhaka.
:::

## 30.4 The Adjective Phrase

An **adjective phrase** does the work of an **adjective**: it **qualifies a noun or pronoun**. Usually it comes **after** the noun. Test: replace it with a single adjective — *a man of wisdom* = *a **wise** man*.

| Structure | Example | One-word equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Prepositional phrase | *He is a man **of great courage**.* | courageous |
| | *She wore a dress **of silk**.* | silk |
| | *The house **on the hill** is ours.* | (identifies which house) |
| Participial phrase | *The man **standing at the gate** is my father.* | — |
| | *A letter **written in red ink** came today.* | — |
| Infinitive phrase | *I have **no money to spend**.* (*to spend* qualifies *money*) | — |
| | *He is the right man **to do the job**.* | — |
| Adjective + complement | *He is a man **proud of his wealth**.* | proud |
| Compound adjective | *a **ten-year-old** boy* | — |

::: ex
- A man **of honesty** is respected everywhere. (= an honest man)
- He wore a cap **made of wool**. (= a woollen cap)
- The girl **with long hair** is my sister.
- The road **to the village** is muddy.
- The boy **sitting next to me** is very clever.
- Give me something **to eat**.
- A house **built of bricks** lasts long.
- The guest **from Japan** spoke Bangla.
:::

## 30.5 The Adverb Phrase

An **adverb phrase** does the work of an **adverb**: it modifies a verb, adjective or adverb, telling ***how, when, where, why*** or ***to what extent***. Test: replace with a one-word adverb — *with care* = *carefully*.

| Meaning | Example | One-word equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Manner (how?) | *He did the work **with great care**.* | carefully |
| | *She spoke **in a low voice**.* | softly |
| Time (when?) | *He came **in the evening**.* | — |
| | *I will see you **after the class**.* | later |
| Place (where?) | *The cat is sleeping **under the bed**.* | — |
| | *He lives **at the foot of the hill**.* | — |
| Reason / purpose (why?) | *He went to Dhaka **to get a job**.* | — |
| | *He came **for help**.* | — |
| Degree | *He was angry **to a great extent**.* | very |
| Frequency | *He visits us **every now and then**.* | occasionally |

::: ex
- He fought **with great courage**. (= courageously)
- The sun sets **in the west**.
- She sang **in a sweet voice**. (= sweetly)
- He went abroad **for higher studies**.
- They came **at last**. (= finally)
- **To my surprise**, he passed.
- He returned home **all of a sudden**. (= suddenly)
- She works **day and night**.
:::

::: note
**Prepositional phrase — adjective or adverb?** A prepositional phrase is an **adjective phrase** if it qualifies a **noun** and an **adverb phrase** if it modifies a **verb**. *The man **in the car** is my uncle* (which man? → adjective). *He sat **in the car*** (where did he sit? → adverb). Always ask: **which word does it describe?**
:::

## 30.6 The Verb Phrase

A **verb phrase** is a group of words that works as the **verb** of a sentence: **auxiliary/modal + main verb**.

::: ex
- He **has been working** since morning.
- The bridge **is being built**.
- You **should have told** me.
- She **will be leaving** tomorrow.
- They **might have been sleeping**.
:::

In modern grammar, "verb phrase" also includes **phrasal verbs** (*give up, look after, put off*) — see Chapter 47.

## 30.7 Phrasal Prepositions and Phrasal Conjunctions

**Phrasal (phrase) prepositions** are groups of words that work as a single **preposition**:

*according to, along with, apart from, as for, because of, by dint of, by means of, by virtue of, by way of, due to, for the sake of, in accordance with, in addition to, in case of, in front of, in lieu of, in order to, in place of, in spite of, instead of, in view of, on account of, on behalf of, owing to, with a view to, with reference to, with regard to, in the event of, in comparison with, in favour of, by reason of, for want of, in consequence of*

::: ex
- **In spite of** the rain, the match continued.
- He was absent **on account of** illness.
- I am speaking **on behalf of** my colleagues.
:::

**Phrasal conjunctions** work as a single **conjunction**:

*as if, as though, as soon as, as long as, as well as, so that, in order that, even if, even though, provided that, in case, as far as, now that, so as, no sooner … than, the moment, by the time*

::: ex
- He talks **as if** he were a king.
- I will help you **as long as** I live.
- Leave **as soon as** you can.
:::

## 30.8 Interjectional Phrases

*Good heavens! Oh dear! Dear me! Thank God! For God's sake! Well done! What a pity! Hear, hear! Alas the day! My goodness!*

## 30.9 Modern Classification by Head Word

International grammars (and SAT/GMAT discussions) classify phrases by their **head** (main word):

| Phrase | Head | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun phrase (NP) | noun / pronoun | ***the tall old building** near the park* |
| Verb phrase (VP) | verb | ***has been working** hard* |
| Adjective phrase (AdjP) | adjective | ***very proud of his son*** |
| Adverb phrase (AdvP) | adverb | ***quite slowly*** |
| Prepositional phrase (PP) | preposition | ***in the morning*** |
| Infinitive phrase | infinitive | ***to learn English quickly*** |
| Gerund phrase | gerund | ***swimming in the river*** |
| Participial phrase | participle | ***having finished his work*** |
| Absolute phrase | noun + participle | ***the sun having set*** |
| Appositive phrase | noun | *Dhaka, **the capital of Bangladesh**, …* |

### 30.9.1 Appositive Phrases

An **appositive phrase** is a noun phrase placed **beside** another noun to **rename or explain** it. It is usually set off with commas.

::: ex
- Rabindranath Tagore, **the first Asian Nobel laureate**, wrote *Gitanjali*.
- My friend, **a doctor in Sylhet**, is coming tomorrow.
- Dhaka, **the capital of Bangladesh**, is very crowded.
:::

### 30.9.2 Absolute Phrases

An **absolute phrase** (noun/pronoun + participle) modifies the **whole sentence** (Chapter 14.5.2).

::: ex
- **The weather being fine**, we went out.
- **His work finished**, he went to bed.
- **God willing**, we shall succeed.
:::

## 30.10 How to Identify a Phrase in an Exam

::: rule
**Step 1:** Check that the group has **no subject + finite verb** (otherwise it is a clause).
**Step 2:** Find the **word the phrase is attached to** or the **job it does**:

- Is it the **subject / object / complement / object of a preposition**? → **Noun phrase**.
- Does it **describe a noun** (answer *which? what kind?*)? → **Adjective phrase**.
- Does it tell **how / when / where / why** about a verb? → **Adverb phrase**.
- Is it **auxiliary + main verb**? → **Verb phrase**.
- Does it **replace a preposition / conjunction**? → **Phrasal preposition / conjunction**.

**Step 3:** Try the **one-word substitution test**: *it* (noun), *wise/rich* etc. (adjective), *carefully/there/then* (adverb).
:::

## 30.11 Worked Examples: Identify the Phrase

::: errs
1. **To forgive** is divine. → *Noun phrase* (subject).
2. I want **to go home**. → *Noun phrase* (object of *want*).
3. He is a man **of strong character**. → *Adjective phrase* (qualifies *man*).
4. He did it **in a hurry**. → *Adverb phrase* (manner — hastily).
5. **Taking a walk in the morning** is good for health. → *Noun phrase* (gerund phrase, subject).
6. The girl **in the red dress** is my cousin. → *Adjective phrase* (which girl?).
7. She lives **in the red house**. → *Adverb phrase* (where?).
8. **Having finished his homework**, he went out to play. → *Adjective (participial) phrase* (qualifies *he*).
9. He arrived **at the eleventh hour**. → *Adverb phrase* (time).
10. Don't forget **to post the letter**. → *Noun phrase* (object).
11. I have a lot of work **to do**. → *Adjective phrase* (qualifies *work*).
12. He went to the market **to buy vegetables**. → *Adverb phrase* (purpose).
13. **In spite of** his illness, he came. → *Phrasal preposition*.
14. **As soon as** he saw me, he ran away. → *Phrasal conjunction*.
15. **The sun having risen**, we started. → *Absolute phrase* (adverbial — time).
16. Nobody knows **how to solve it**. → *Noun phrase* (object).
17. A friend **in need** is a friend indeed. → *Adjective phrase* (qualifies *friend*).
18. He was **at a loss** what to do. → *Adjective phrase* (complement — = puzzled).
19. He **has been sleeping** for hours. → *Verb phrase*.
20. My hobby is **gardening on the roof**. → *Noun phrase* (complement).
:::

## 30.12 Phrases in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
- **Sentence fragments (SAT):** A phrase cannot stand alone as a sentence: ✘ *Hoping to win the scholarship.* ✘ *A city known for its rickshaws.* Attach the phrase to a main clause.
- **Modifier placement (GMAT):** Participial, prepositional and appositive phrases must sit next to the noun they describe (Chapter 29).
- **Parallelism (GMAT/SAT):** Phrases in a list must have the same form — all infinitive phrases, all gerund phrases, or all prepositional phrases (Chapter 39).
- **Concision (GMAT):** Prefer a one-word modifier to a wordy phrase when meaning is unchanged: *in a careful manner* → *carefully*; *at this point in time* → *now*.
:::
