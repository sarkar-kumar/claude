## 50.1 What Is One Word Substitution?

**One word substitution** means replacing a **group of words** (a phrase or clause) with **a single word** that carries the same meaning:

::: ex
- *A person who knows many languages* → **polyglot**
- *A government by the people* → **democracy**
- *Killing of one's own brother* → **fratricide**
- *That which cannot be read* → **illegible**
:::

It is a regular MCQ item in BCS, Bank and admission tests and builds exactly the precise vocabulary needed for **GRE Text Completion** and **GMAT concision**. The words are easier to learn in **families** (*-cide, -cracy, -logy, -phobia, -vorous*) — so this chapter groups them by theme. Learn the roots from Chapter 40 alongside.

## 50.12 One Word Substitution in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
- **GRE Text Completion** often describes a person or quality in a phrase and asks for the single precise word: *"a person who habitually fails to keep promises"* → *unreliable / perfidious*; *"speech that is wordy and roundabout"* → *circumlocutory / verbose*; *"a tendency to put off tasks"* → *procrastination*; *"happening by lucky chance"* → *serendipitous*; *"lasting a very short time"* → *ephemeral*; *"careful about spending"* → *frugal / parsimonious*.
- **GMAT concision:** replace wordy phrases with exact single words: *at the present time* → *now*; *a person who has the ability to* → *a person who can*; *that which cannot be avoided* → *inevitable*.
- **SAT Reading:** knowing roots (*-cide, -cracy, -logy, -phobia, -vorous, omni-, poly-, mono-, pseudo-*) lets you decode unfamiliar words quickly.
:::
