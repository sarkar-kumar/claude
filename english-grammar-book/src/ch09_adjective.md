## 9.1 What Is an Adjective?

An **adjective** is a word that **qualifies** a noun or a pronoun — that is, it **adds something to its meaning** by describing it, limiting it, or telling its number or quantity. The word comes from Latin *adjectivum*, "something added".

::: ex
- a **brave** soldier (what kind?)
- **five** apples (how many?)
- **much** water (how much?)
- **this** book (which one?)
- She is **intelligent**. (describes the pronoun *she*)
:::

### 9.1.1 Three Positions of the Adjective

1. **Attributive** — before the noun: *a **red** rose, an **old** man*.
2. **Predicative** — after a linking verb (*be, seem, look, feel, become, grow, appear, remain, smell, taste, sound, turn*): *The rose is **red**. The man looks **old**. The milk turned **sour**.*
3. **Postpositive** — immediately after the noun (special cases, see 9.5.2): *something **new**, the people **concerned**, the President **elect**.*

## 9.2 Kinds of Adjectives

| Kind | What it tells | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Adjective of Quality** (Descriptive) | what kind | good, bad, honest, large, beautiful, wise, Bangladeshi |
| **Proper Adjective** | quality derived from a proper noun | Bangladeshi, English, Shakespearean, Victorian |
| **Adjective of Quantity** | how much (uncountables) | some, much, little, enough, whole, all, no, half, sufficient |
| **Adjective of Number** (Numeral) | how many / in what order | one, two, first, second, many, few, several, all, some |
| **Demonstrative Adjective** | which one | this, that, these, those, such, the same, the other |
| **Distributive Adjective** | each one separately | each, every, either, neither |
| **Interrogative Adjective** | asks a question (+ noun) | what, which, whose |
| **Possessive Adjective** | ownership | my, our, your, his, her, its, their |
| **Emphasising Adjective** | emphasis | own, very (*my **own** house; the **very** man*) |
| **Exclamatory Adjective** | surprise | what (***What** a genius!*) |

Most demonstrative, distributive, interrogative and possessive adjectives are **determiners** (Chapter 04) in modern grammar.

### 9.2.1 Adjectives of Number

- **Definite numeral adjectives** — an exact number:
  - **Cardinal**: *one, two, ten, hundred*;
  - **Ordinal**: *first, second, tenth, hundredth, last*;
  - **Multiplicative**: *single, double, triple, twofold, threefold*.
- **Indefinite numeral adjectives** — no exact number: *many, few, a few, several, all, some, any, no, numerous, sundry, certain*.
- **Distributive numeral adjectives**: *each, every, either, neither*.

### 9.2.2 Same Word, Quantity or Number?

*Some, any, enough, all, no, more, most* can be adjectives of **quantity** (with uncountables) or **number** (with countable plurals):

::: ex
- I want **some** milk. (quantity) / **Some** boys came. (number)
- Have you **any** money? (quantity) / Are there **any** mangoes? (number)
- We have **enough** rice. (quantity) / We have **enough** chairs. (number)
- **All** the milk was spilt. (quantity) / **All** the boys were present. (number)
:::

## 9.4 Adjectives of Quantity and Number: Correct Use

::: ex
- **Much** time / **many** books (Chapter 03)
- **Little** water / **few** boys; **a little** / **a few**; **less** / **fewer**
- **Some** (positive) / **any** (negative, question)
- **Each** (two or more, separately) / **every** (more than two, collectively)
- **Either / neither** (two only)
- ***Whole*** with singular countable (*the whole class*); ***all*** with plurals and uncountables (*all the boys; all the money*)
- ***Enough*** before a noun, after an adjective: *enough money; strong enough*
:::

## 9.5 Position of Adjectives

### 9.5.1 Attributive and Predicative

Most adjectives can be used in both positions: *a **happy** child / The child is **happy**.* But some adjectives are restricted.

**Adjectives used only predicatively** (after a verb) — mostly beginning with *a-*:

*afraid, alike, alive, alone, asleep, awake, aware, ashamed, afloat, ablaze, aghast, akin, averse, content, glad, ill (= sick, BrE), well (= healthy), unable, sorry (= regretful).*

::: ex
- ✘ an asleep child ✔ a **sleeping** child / The child is **asleep**.
- ✘ an alive fish ✔ a **live / living** fish / The fish is **alive**.
- ✘ an afraid boy ✔ a **frightened** boy / The boy is **afraid**.
- ✘ two alike brothers ✔ two **similar** brothers / The two brothers are **alike**.
- ✘ an alone woman ✔ a **lonely / solitary** woman / She lives **alone**.
- ✘ an ill man (BrE) ✔ a **sick** man / The man is **ill**.
:::

**Adjectives used only attributively** (before a noun):

*chief, main, principal, mere, sheer, utter, only, very (= exact), elder, eldest, former, latter, inner, outer, upper, utmost, live (= living), drunken, lone, sole, entire, total, outright, maiden (voyage), woollen, wooden.*

::: ex
- the **chief** guest (✘ The guest is chief.)
- a **mere** child; **sheer** luck; **utter** nonsense
- my **elder** brother (✘ My brother is elder than me → ✔ **older** than me)
- the **main** road; the **former** president
:::

### 9.5.2 Postpositive Adjectives (Adjective after Noun)

1. **After *something, anything, nothing, everything, someone, anybody* etc.:** *something **new**, nothing **important**, anyone **intelligent**, somewhere **quiet***. (✘ new something)
2. **In fixed (often legal or French-influenced) phrases:** *heir **apparent**, Poet **Laureate**, Governor **General**, Attorney **General**, Secretary **General**, Prince **Royal**, court **martial**, notary **public**, body **politic**, sum **total**, time **immemorial**, God **Almighty**, the President **elect**, things **Bangladeshi**, letters **patent**, blood **royal***.
3. **With *concerned, involved, present, responsible, available, possible*** (often with a different meaning): *the people **concerned*** (= affected) vs *a **concerned** mother* (= worried); *the members **present*** (= in attendance) vs *the **present** members* (= current); *the person **responsible*** vs *a **responsible** person*; *the best route **possible**; tickets **available***.
4. **When the adjective has a complement or modifier:** *a man **eager to learn**; a box **full of toys**; a student **proud of his success***.
5. **Measurement expressions:** *ten feet **tall**, five years **old**, two metres **wide**, a mile **long***.
6. **For emphasis or in poetry, two or more adjectives together:** *a man, **tall and handsome**; a lady **young and beautiful**; the sky **blue and bright***.

### 9.5.3 The Order of Several Adjectives

When several adjectives come before a noun, native speakers follow a natural order. A useful memory aid is **OSASCOMP**:

| Order | Category | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| (Determiner) | article, demonstrative, possessive, number | a, the, this, my, two |
| 1. **O**pinion | judgement | beautiful, nice, ugly, delicious, useful |
| 2. **S**ize | big/small | big, small, tall, long, huge |
| 3. **A**ge | old/new | old, new, young, ancient, modern |
| 4. **S**hape | form | round, square, flat, triangular |
| 5. **C**olour | colour | red, black, green, golden |
| 6. **O**rigin | nationality, place | Bangladeshi, Chinese, Italian |
| 7. **M**aterial | what it is made of | silk, wooden, cotton, steel |
| 8. **P**urpose | what it is for (often a gerund/noun) | wedding, sleeping, running, dining |
| (Noun) | | saree, bag, shoes, table |

::: ex
- a **beautiful old red** car (opinion–age–colour)
- a **big round wooden** table (size–shape–material)
- a **lovely small new Bangladeshi silk wedding** saree
- ✘ a red big ball ✔ a **big red** ball
- ✘ a cotton white shirt ✔ a **white cotton** shirt
- ✘ an old nice house ✔ a **nice old** house
- ✘ the three first chapters ✔ **the first three** chapters (ordinal before cardinal)
:::

### 9.5.4 Commas between Adjectives

**Coordinate adjectives** (of the same category, each modifying the noun equally) are separated by a comma or *and*: *a **long, boring** speech; a **kind and generous** man*. Test: if you can reverse them or put *and* between them, use a comma. **Cumulative adjectives** (of different categories, following OSASCOMP) take **no comma**: *a **big red** ball* (✘ a big, red ball).

## 9.6 Adjectives After Linking Verbs (Not Adverbs!)

After **linking (copular) verbs**, we use an **adjective**, because it describes the **subject**, not the action. Linking verbs: *be, seem, appear, look, feel, smell, taste, sound, become, grow, get, turn, go, remain, keep, stay, prove*.

::: ex
- The rose smells **sweet**. (✘ sweetly)
- The food tastes **delicious**. (✘ deliciously)
- She looks **beautiful**. (✘ beautifully)
- I feel **bad** about it. (✘ badly)
- The milk has turned **sour**.
- Keep **quiet**. / Stay **calm**. / He remained **silent**.
- The plan proved **useful**.
:::

::: note
**Same verb, two uses:** Some of these verbs can also be **action verbs**; then they take an **adverb**. *She **looked** angrily at me* (action of looking → adverb) vs *She **looked** angry* (her appearance → adjective). *He **felt** the cloth carefully* (action → adverb) vs *He **felt** sad* (state → adjective). *The dog **smelt** the bone eagerly* vs *The bone **smelt** bad*.
:::

**Adjectives as object complements** — after verbs like *make, keep, find, consider, paint, leave, drive, set*: *The news made her **sad**. Keep the room **clean**. They painted the door **green**. I found the book **interesting**. The noise drove him **mad**.*

## 9.7 Participial Adjectives: *-ing* vs *-ed*

This is one of the most tested adjective points in admission tests and SAT/GMAT.

::: rule
***-ing*** adjectives describe the **thing or person that causes** a feeling (*the cause*). ***-ed*** adjectives describe the **person who has** the feeling (*the effect*).
:::

| Cause (*-ing*) | Feeling (*-ed*) | Example |
|---|---|---|
| boring | bored | *The lecture was **boring**, so the students were **bored**.* |
| interesting | interested | *The book is **interesting**; I am **interested** in it.* |
| exciting | excited | *It was an **exciting** match; the fans were **excited**.* |
| tiring | tired | *The journey was **tiring**; we were **tired**.* |
| confusing | confused | *The question was **confusing**; I was **confused**.* |
| frightening | frightened | *a **frightening** film; a **frightened** child* |
| surprising | surprised | *a **surprising** result; a **surprised** look* |
| disappointing | disappointed | *His result was **disappointing**; his parents were **disappointed**.* |
| amazing | amazed | *an **amazing** performance; an **amazed** audience* |
| satisfying | satisfied | *a **satisfying** meal; a **satisfied** customer* |
| embarrassing | embarrassed | *an **embarrassing** question; an **embarrassed** boy* |
| shocking | shocked | ***shocking** news; **shocked** relatives* |
| annoying | annoyed | *an **annoying** noise; an **annoyed** neighbour* |
| worrying | worried | *a **worrying** situation; **worried** parents* |
| exhausting | exhausted | *an **exhausting** day; an **exhausted** worker* |
| depressing | depressed | ***depressing** weather; a **depressed** patient* |
| fascinating | fascinated | *a **fascinating** story; **fascinated** listeners* |
| pleasing | pleased | *a **pleasing** manner; a **pleased** teacher* |
| terrifying | terrified | *a **terrifying** storm; **terrified** villagers* |
| inspiring | inspired | *an **inspiring** speech; an **inspired** youth* |

::: ex
- ✘ I am very boring in the class. (= I make others bored!) ✔ I am very **bored** in the class.
- ✘ The film was very interested. ✔ The film was very **interesting**.
- ✘ He felt exciting about the trip. ✔ He felt **excited** about the trip.
:::

## 9.8 Compound Adjectives

A **compound adjective** is formed from two or more words joined by a **hyphen** when it comes **before** a noun.

| Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|
| Number + noun (noun stays singular) | a **five-year** plan, a **ten-taka** note, a **two-hour** journey, a **three-storeyed** building, a **ten-year-old** boy (✘ ten-years-old) |
| Adjective + noun + -ed | **blue-eyed**, **left-handed**, **kind-hearted**, **long-legged**, **absent-minded**, **narrow-minded** |
| Noun + present participle | **heart-breaking**, **time-consuming**, **record-breaking**, **peace-loving**, **mouth-watering** |
| Noun/adverb + past participle | **hand-made**, **home-made**, **well-known**, **well-educated**, **sun-dried**, **man-made**, **tongue-tied** |
| Adjective + present participle | **good-looking**, **easy-going**, **long-lasting**, **far-reaching** |
| Noun + adjective | **world-famous**, **sugar-free**, **duty-free**, **ice-cold**, **sky-blue**, **knee-deep** |

::: note
**No hyphen after the noun:** *a **well-known** writer* but *The writer is **well known***. *A **ten-year-old** boy* but *The boy is **ten years old***.
:::

## 9.9 Adjectives Used as Nouns

1. ***The* + adjective = a class of people (plural verb):** *the rich, the poor, the old, the young, the sick, the blind, the deaf, the dumb, the wounded, the unemployed, the homeless, the dead, the brave, the wise, the righteous, the wicked* — ***The poor are** suffering.*
2. ***The* + adjective = abstract quality (singular):** *the good, the true, the beautiful, the sublime, the unknown, the supernatural* — ***The beautiful is** not always good.*
3. **Some adjectives have become full nouns:** *a **native**, a **savage**, a **criminal**, an **intellectual**, the **accused**, the **deceased**, the **elders**, the **ancients**, **valuables**, **sweets**, **greens**, **eatables**, **drinkables**, the **innocents**, the **Blacks/Whites** (careful usage), **liberals**, **conservatives***.
4. **Fixed phrases with adjective as noun:** *in **short**, in **general**, in **particular**, at **best**, at **worst**, for **good** (= permanently), in **public**, in **private**, from **bad to worse**, through **thick and thin**, **right or wrong**.*

## 9.10 Correct Use of Some Adjectives (Frequently Confused)

| Pair | Rule | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **elder / older** | *elder* = for persons of the same family, attributive only, followed by *to* is wrong; *older* = general, used with *than* | *my **elder** brother; He is **older than** I* (✘ elder than) |
| **eldest / oldest** | *eldest* = within a family; *oldest* = general | *my **eldest** son; the **oldest** building in Dhaka* |
| **later / latter** | *later* = time; *latter* = second of two (order) | *I'll come **later**. Of Rahim and Karim, the **latter** is taller.* |
| **latest / last** | *latest* = newest; *last* = final | *the **latest** news; the **last** chapter* |
| **farther / further** | *farther* = physical distance; *further* = additional (and also distance) | *He walked **farther**. Do you need any **further** information?* |
| **nearest / next** | *nearest* = distance; *next* = order | *the **nearest** hospital; the **next** bus* |
| **former / latter** | for **two** items only (for more, use *first / last*) | *Tea and coffee are both drinks; the **former** is cheaper.* |
| **little / small** | *little* often adds emotion; *small* is neutral size | *a **little** girl (affection); a **small** room* |
| **verbal / oral** | *verbal* = in words (spoken or written); *oral* = spoken | *an **oral** test; a **verbal** agreement* |
| **healthy / healthful** | *healthy* = in good health; *healthful* = good for health | *a **healthy** child; **healthful** food* (now *healthy food* is common) |
| **economic / economical** | *economic* = of the economy; *economical* = thrifty | ***economic** growth; an **economical** car* |
| **historic / historical** | *historic* = important in history; *historical* = related to history | *a **historic** speech (7th March); a **historical** novel* |
| **childish / childlike** | *childish* = silly (−); *childlike* = innocent (+) | ***childish** behaviour; **childlike** simplicity* |
| **industrial / industrious** | *industrial* = of industry; *industrious* = hard-working | ***industrial** area; an **industrious** student* |
| **respectable / respectful / respective** | worthy of respect / showing respect / each one's own | *a **respectable** family; **respectful** to elders; went to their **respective** homes* |
| **sensible / sensitive** | wise / easily affected | *a **sensible** decision; **sensitive** skin* |
| **continual / continuous** | repeated with breaks / without a break | ***continual** interruptions; **continuous** rain* |
| **imaginary / imaginative** | unreal / creative | *an **imaginary** story; an **imaginative** writer* |
| **luxurious / luxuriant** | expensive and comfortable / growing thickly | *a **luxurious** hotel; **luxuriant** vegetation* |
| **credible / creditable / credulous** | believable / praiseworthy / too ready to believe | *a **credible** witness; a **creditable** result; a **credulous** villager* |
| **politic / political** | wise, prudent / of politics | *a **politic** decision; a **political** party* |
| **momentary / momentous** | brief / very important | *a **momentary** pause; a **momentous** decision* |

Chapter 52 gives a much larger list of commonly confused words.

### 9.10.1 *Very* or *Much* before Adjectives and Participles

- ***Very*** modifies **positive adjectives**, **present participles** and past participles used as pure adjectives: *very **good**, very **interesting**, very **tired**, very **pleased**, very **worried***.
- ***Much*** modifies **comparatives** and **past participles in a passive (verbal) sense**: *much **better**, much **taller**, much **obliged**, much **admired**, much **talked about***.

::: ex
- ✘ He is very better today. ✔ He is **much better** today.
- ✘ I am much tired. ✔ I am **very tired**.
- ✔ I am **much obliged** to you. / ✔ He is **much respected** by all.
- ✔ **Very much** + comparative for extra emphasis: *very much better* ✔
:::

### 9.10.2 Absolute Adjectives

Some adjectives already express the **highest degree** or an **all-or-nothing** quality and normally cannot be compared or intensified: ***unique, perfect, complete, entire, round, square, empty, full, dead, ideal, infinite, eternal, universal, supreme, extreme, chief, matchless, impossible, final, total, absolute***. ✘ *more unique*, ✘ *most perfect*, ✘ *very unique* → ✔ *unique*, ✔ *almost perfect*, ✔ *nearly complete*. (See Chapter 24.)

## 9.11 Common Errors with Adjectives

::: errs
1. ✘ He is elder than me. ✔ He is **older than** I.
2. ✘ The rose smells sweetly. ✔ The rose smells **sweet**.
3. ✘ I am very boring with this film. ✔ I am very **bored** with this film.
4. ✘ He is a ten-years-old boy. ✔ He is a **ten-year-old** boy.
5. ✘ She is more cleverer than her sister. ✔ She is **cleverer** than her sister.
6. ✘ He is very better now. ✔ He is **much better** now.
7. ✘ I have a red big car. ✔ I have a **big red** car.
8. ✘ This is the most unique design. ✔ This is a **unique** design.
9. ✘ Don't wake the asleep child. ✔ Don't wake the **sleeping** child.
10. ✘ He is an alive example of honesty. ✔ He is a **living** example of honesty.
11. ✘ Give me something to eat good. ✔ Give me something **good** to eat.
12. ✘ The poors are suffering. ✔ **The poor are** suffering.
13. ✘ He is a Swedish. ✔ He is **a Swede** / He is **Swedish**. (*Swedish* is the adjective; *a Swede* is the person)
14. ✘ He is enough intelligent. ✔ He is **intelligent enough**.
15. ✘ The final chapter is the latter. (of five) ✔ The final chapter is the **last**.
16. ✘ What is the last news? ✔ What is the **latest** news?
17. ✘ He walked further than me. (traditional, distance) ✔ He walked **farther** than I.
18. ✘ My cousin is an industrial student. ✔ My cousin is an **industrious** student.
19. ✘ She is respectful lady. ✔ She is a **respectable** lady.
20. ✘ He was childlike in his anger. ✔ He was **childish** in his anger.
21. ✘ It was a historical moment when Bangladesh won independence. ✔ It was a **historic** moment …
22. ✘ I feel badly. ✔ I feel **bad**.
23. ✘ She looks beautifully in that dress. ✔ She looks **beautiful** in that dress.
24. ✘ The more stronger team won. ✔ The **stronger** team won.
25. ✘ Tulips are a Hollandish flower. ✔ Tulips are a **Dutch** flower. (*Holland / the Netherlands → Dutch*)
:::

## 9.12 Adjectives in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
- **Adjective vs adverb (SAT/GMAT):** ✘ *The surprising large crowd …* ✔ *The **surprisingly** large crowd …* (adverb modifies the adjective *large*). ✘ *a remarkable fast runner* ✔ *a **remarkably** fast runner*.
- **Linking verbs:** ✘ *The proposal seems reasonably* ✔ *seems **reasonable***.
- **Participial adjectives:** SAT passages contrast *-ed* and *-ing*: *a **confusing** argument* vs *a **confused** audience*.
- **GRE Text Completion and Sentence Equivalence** test hundreds of adjectives, especially **eponymous** ones (*quixotic, draconian, Machiavellian, Pyrrhic, mercurial, saturnine, jovial, stoic, spartan, Procrustean*) and **tone** adjectives (*laconic, verbose, sardonic, acerbic, effusive, diffident, sanguine, phlegmatic*). Learn them with Chapter 45.
- **Absolute adjectives:** GMAT does not accept *more unique* or *very unique*.
:::
