## 54.6 Sentence Completion (Structural)

In SSC, HSC, admission tests and some Bank tests, "**Complete the following sentences**" gives the first part of a sentence containing a **structural clue** (*as soon as, if, hardly, it is high time, would rather, lest*), and you must complete it **grammatically and meaningfully**. The clue word decides the structure.

| Beginning | Rule | Model completion |
|---|---|---|
| *As soon as the bell rang, …* | past simple in the main clause | *… the students left the classroom.* |
| *No sooner had he reached the station …* | *than* + past simple | *… than the train started.* |
| *Hardly / Scarcely had we sat down …* | *when* + past simple | *… when the lights went out.* |
| *It is high time …* | subject + past simple / *for* + object + *to*-infinitive | *… we took steps to stop corruption.* |
| *I would rather …* | V1 (+ *than* V1) / subject + past | *… die than beg. / … you stayed here.* |
| *Had I known the truth, …* | *would have* + V3 | *… I would have helped you.* |
| *If I were a bird, …* | *would / could* + V1 | *… I would fly in the blue sky.* |
| *If you work hard, …* | *will* + V1 | *… you will succeed.* |
| *Unless you study regularly, …* | *will* + V1 (no *not*) | *… you will not do well in the exam.* |
| *Walk carefully lest …* | *should* + V1 | *… you should fall.* |
| *He talks as if …* | past subjunctive | *… he were a millionaire.* |
| *I wish …* | past / past perfect | *… I were a doctor. / … I had studied harder.* |
| *Though he is poor, …* | no *but* | *… he is honest.* |
| *He is so weak that …* | *cannot / could not* | *… he cannot walk.* |
| *He is too weak …* | *to* + V1 | *… to walk.* |
| *It was such a hot day that …* | result clause | *… we could not go out.* |
| *The more you read, …* | *the* + comparative | *… the more you learn.* |
| *Since he was ill, …* | result in the main clause | *… he could not attend the meeting.* |
| *It is ten years since …* | past simple | *… I last saw him.* |
| *I have been living in Dhaka since …* | point of time | *… 2015.* |
| *Not only is he clever …* | *but (also)* + parallel clause | *… but he is also hard-working.* |
| *Make haste lest …* | *should* | *… you should miss the train.* |
| *By the time the police arrived, …* | past perfect | *… the robbers had fled.* |
| *He worked hard so that …* | *might / could* | *… he might pass.* |
| *Would you mind …* | V-*ing* | *… opening the window?* |
| *I look forward to …* | V-*ing* / noun | *… seeing you soon.* |
| *It is no use …* | V-*ing* | *… crying over spilt milk.* |
| *He is used to …* | V-*ing* | *… working late at night.* |
| *He used to …* | V1 | *… go for a walk every morning.* |
| *Let us …* | V1 | *… go for a picnic.* |
| *If I had wings, …* | *would* + V1 | *… I would fly to the moon.* |
| *Suppose you were the Prime Minister, …* | *what would you do?* | *… what steps would you take against poverty?* |
| *He behaves in such a way …* | *as if / that* | *… as if he were the owner of the house.* |
| *There is no one who …* | (negative meaning) | *… does not love his motherland.* |
| *Who does not …* | rhetorical question | *… want to be happy?* |

## 54.7 Linkers and Transition Words

Linkers make writing **coherent**. Choosing the right one is also tested in SAT Writing ("transitions") and in Bank/BCS gap-filling.

| Function | Linkers |
|---|---|
| Addition | and, also, too, as well as, in addition, moreover, furthermore, besides, what is more, not only … but also |
| Contrast | but, yet, however, nevertheless, nonetheless, still, on the other hand, in contrast, whereas, while, although, though, even though, despite, in spite of, on the contrary |
| Cause / reason | because, since, as, for, because of, due to, owing to, on account of, thanks to |
| Result / effect | so, therefore, thus, hence, consequently, as a result, accordingly, so … that, such … that |
| Purpose | to, in order to, so as to, so that, in order that, lest, for fear that |
| Condition | if, unless, provided that, as long as, in case, otherwise, or else |
| Example | for example, for instance, such as, namely, to illustrate, in particular, especially |
| Emphasis | indeed, in fact, certainly, above all, particularly, of course, clearly |
| Sequence / time | first, second, then, next, after that, finally, meanwhile, subsequently, eventually, at last, before, after, when, while, as soon as, until |
| Comparison | similarly, likewise, in the same way, just as, like, equally |
| Concession | admittedly, granted, of course, it is true that, even so, all the same |
| Clarification | that is, in other words, to put it differently, i.e., namely |
| Summary / conclusion | in short, in brief, in conclusion, to sum up, on the whole, all in all, in a nutshell, to conclude |

::: note
**Punctuation reminder:** conjunctions (*and, but, so, because*) can join clauses with a comma or no punctuation; **conjunctive adverbs** (*however, therefore, moreover, consequently*) need a **semicolon or full stop** before them when they join two independent clauses (Chapters 19 and 44).
:::

## 54.8 British and American English

Bangladeshi exams follow **British English**; the SAT, GRE and GMAT use **American English**. Both are correct, but be **consistent** in one piece of writing. Spelling differences are in Chapter 46.3.

### 54.8.2 Grammar Differences

| Point | British | American |
|---|---|---|
| Collective nouns | *The team **are** playing well.* (plural possible) | *The team **is** playing well.* (singular) |
| Present perfect with *just, already, yet* | *I've **just** eaten. Have you finished **yet**?* | *I **just** ate. Did you finish **yet**?* (past simple common) |
| *have got* | *I've got a car. Have you got a pen?* | *I have a car. Do you have a pen?* |
| *shall* | *Shall we go?* (common) | *Should we go?* (*shall* rare) |
| Past forms | *learnt, burnt, dreamt, spelt* | *learned, burned, dreamed, spelled* |
| *gotten* | *got* | *gotten* (*He has gotten better.*) |
| Prepositions | *at the weekend; in the street; different from/to; Monday to Friday* | *on the weekend; on the street; different from/than; Monday through Friday* |
| Subjunctive | *I suggest that he **should** go / he **go**.* | *I suggest that he **go**.* |
| Dates | *22 October 2026 (22/10/2026)* | *October 22, 2026 (10/22/2026)* |

## 54.10 Formats of Major Tests

Knowing the **format** of a test lets you prepare efficiently and manage time on the day. Formats change from time to time; always check the latest official circular or test website before your exam.

### 54.10.1 Bangladeshi Tests

| Test | English component (typical) | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **BCS Preliminary** | English Language and Literature MCQs (a significant share of the 200 marks) | grammar rules, vocabulary (synonym/antonym, idioms, one-word substitution, spelling), and literature |
| **BCS Written** | English paper (200 marks): Part A — reading comprehension, grammar items, summary; Part B — translation (Bangla → English), letter/application, essay | accuracy, organisation, translation skill, essay writing |
| **Bank recruitment** (Officer, Senior Officer, Bangladesh Bank AD, private banks) | MCQ (grammar, vocabulary, analogy, sentence correction, comprehension) + written (essay, letter, translation, focus writing) | speed, accuracy, formal writing, current affairs in essays |
| **University admission** (DU, JU, RU, CU, BUP, IBA) | MCQ English section; IBA and some faculties include written English | grammar, vocabulary, comprehension, analytical reading |

### 54.10.2 International Tests

| Test | Verbal / English sections (general description) | What is tested |
|---|---|---|
| **SAT** (digital) | Reading and Writing section in two adaptive modules | words in context, text structure and purpose, command of evidence, inferences, Standard English conventions (grammar, punctuation), transitions, rhetorical synthesis |
| **GRE General** | Verbal Reasoning (Text Completion, Sentence Equivalence, Reading Comprehension) and Analytical Writing (Issue essay) | advanced vocabulary in context, close reading, critical reasoning, essay argument |
| **GMAT Focus** | Verbal Reasoning (Reading Comprehension and Critical Reasoning); Sentence Correction has been removed from the current edition, but its grammar remains vital for business writing and other tests | reading, argument analysis (strengthen, weaken, assumption, inference) |
| **IELTS Academic / General** | Listening, Reading, Writing (Task 1 & 2), Speaking | practical English skills, essay and report writing |

::: note
Although the current GMAT edition has dropped Sentence Correction, the GMAT-style grammar points discussed throughout Part A (modifiers, parallelism, idioms, comparisons, agreement) remain essential for **SAT Writing**, Bangladeshi error-detection questions, professional writing and older GMAT preparation materials.
:::

## 54.11 Exam Strategy and Preparation

### 54.11.1 For MCQ Tests (BCS Preliminary, Bank, Admission)

1. **Know the syllabus and past questions.** Many items repeat with small changes; analyse the last 10–15 years of BCS and Bank questions by topic.
2. **Master the high-frequency topics:** subject–verb agreement, right form of verbs, prepositions, articles, correction, voice, narration, idioms, synonyms/antonyms, one-word substitution, spelling, analogy.
3. **Use elimination:** remove clearly wrong options first; for vocabulary, decide whether the word is positive or negative.
4. **Beware of negative marking** (BCS deducts marks for wrong answers): answer only when you can eliminate at least two options.
5. **Time management:** spend about 30–40 seconds per grammar or vocabulary item; don't get stuck.

### 54.11.2 For Written Tests

1. **Plan before writing:** a 3–5 minute outline for an essay saves time and improves structure.
2. **Write clearly and legibly;** use paragraphs and, where appropriate, headings.
3. **Prefer simple, correct sentences** to complicated, error-filled ones.
4. **Use relevant data and examples** (especially about Bangladesh) to support arguments.
5. **Keep 5–10 minutes for proofreading:** check agreement, tense, articles, spelling and punctuation.

### 54.11.3 A Practical Study Plan

| Area | Daily / weekly practice |
|---|---|
| Grammar | one chapter of Part A per day, with its errors list; weekly revision of rules through past questions |
| Vocabulary | 20–30 new words a day from Chapters 45–53 with example sentences; review on days 1, 3, 7 and 30 (spaced repetition) |
| Reading | one editorial or long article daily; summarise it in three sentences; note new words |
| Writing | one paragraph or letter every two days; one full essay per week; have it checked if possible |
| Translation | translate five Bangla newspaper sentences into English daily |
| Mock tests | one timed full-length test every week in the final two months |

::: tip
**The single most effective habit:** read good English every day **and** write something every day. Grammar rules and word lists become permanent only when you **use** them. This book gives the rules, words and examples; regular reading, writing and revision will turn them into lasting skill.
:::
