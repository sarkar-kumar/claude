# Embedded Question {#ch37}

[Part A · Grammar]{.chpart}

## 37.1 What Is an Embedded Question?

An **embedded question** (also called an **indirect question** or **included question**) is a question that is placed **inside another sentence** — inside a statement or inside another question. It works as a **noun clause** (usually the object of a verb).

::: ex
- Direct question: *Where does he live?*
- Embedded: *I don't know **where he lives**.*
- Embedded: *Can you tell me **where he lives**?*
:::

Embedded questions are polite and very common in spoken and written English (*Could you tell me where the station is?*). They are tested in BCS, Bank and admission exams through **error detection** (the classic error is keeping question word order: ✘ *I don't know where does he live*), and they appear in SAT and GMAT as part of noun-clause structure.

## 37.2 Common Introductory Expressions

| Statements | Questions |
|---|---|
| I don't know … | Do you know …? |
| I wonder … | Can / Could you tell me …? |
| I'd like to know … | Would you mind telling me …? |
| I'm not sure … | Have you any idea …? |
| Nobody knows … | Do you remember …? |
| I can't remember … | Is it true …? |
| Please tell me … | Did he say …? |
| The question is … | Who knows …? |
| It is not clear … | Could you explain …? |
| I asked him … / He wanted to know … | |

## 37.3 The Rules

::: rule
1. **Use statement word order** (subject + verb) in the embedded question — **no inversion**.
2. **Remove *do / does / did*** and put the main verb in the correct tense form.
3. For **yes/no questions**, introduce the embedded question with ***if*** or ***whether***.
4. For **wh-questions**, keep the **wh-word** (*what, where, when, why, how, who, which, whose, how much, how many*) as the linker.
5. **Punctuation** follows the **main sentence**: a full stop if the main sentence is a statement, a question mark if the main sentence is a question.
6. If the main verb is in the **past**, the embedded verb usually shifts into the past (sequence of tenses).
:::

### 37.3.1 Wh-Questions

| Direct question | Embedded question |
|---|---|
| Where **does he live**? | I don't know where **he lives**. |
| What **is her name**? | Can you tell me what **her name is**? |
| Why **was she** crying? | I wonder why **she was** crying. |
| When **will the train** arrive? | Do you know when **the train will** arrive? |
| How **did you solve** it? | Please tell me how **you solved** it. |
| How much **does it cost**? | Could you tell me how much **it costs**? |
| Which bus **should I** take? | I'm not sure which bus **I should** take. |
| Whose bag **is this**? | I don't know whose bag **this is**. |
| Where **is the station**? | Excuse me, could you tell me where **the station is**? |
| What time **is it**? | Do you know what time **it is**? |

### 37.3.2 Yes/No Questions → *If / Whether*

| Direct question | Embedded question |
|---|---|
| **Is** he at home? | I wonder **if / whether he is** at home. |
| **Does** she speak English? | Do you know **if she speaks** English? |
| **Did** they win the match? | I don't know **whether they won** the match. |
| **Can** you swim? | He asked me **if I could** swim. |
| **Will** it rain tomorrow? | Nobody knows **whether it will** rain tomorrow. |
| **Have** you finished? | Could you tell me **if you have** finished? |

### 37.3.3 When the Wh-Word Is the Subject

If the wh-word itself is the **subject** of the question, the word order does **not change** (it is already subject + verb):

::: ex
- *Who **broke** the window?* → *I don't know **who broke** the window.*
- *What **happened** last night?* → *Can you tell me **what happened** last night?*
- *Which team **won**?* → *Do you know **which team won**?*
:::

::: note
***Who is …* / *What is …* questions:** Decide whether the wh-word is the subject or the complement. *Who **is that man**?* → *Do you know **who that man is**?* (*that man* is the subject; *who* is the complement). *What **is the matter**?* → *Tell me **what the matter is*** (also commonly *what is the matter*, because *what* can be the subject).
:::

## 37.4 Punctuation

The final punctuation mark belongs to the **main sentence**, not to the embedded question:

::: ex
- I wonder where he is**.** (statement → full stop; ✘ I wonder where he is?)
- Do you know where he is**?** (question → question mark)
- Please tell me what time it is**.** (request in imperative form → full stop)
- Could you tell me what time it is**?** (question form → question mark)
:::

## 37.5 Embedded Questions with Infinitives

A wh-word (or *whether*) + ***to*-infinitive** is a short embedded question used when the subject is the same as the main subject or is general:

::: ex
- I don't know **what to do**. (= what I should do)
- She asked me **how to solve** the problem.
- Tell me **where to go**.
- He couldn't decide **whether to stay or go**. (✘ if to stay)
- The question is **which one to choose**.
:::

## 37.6 Tense in Embedded Questions (Sequence of Tenses)

When the introductory verb is in the **past**, the embedded verb normally moves one step back (Chapter 27):

::: ex
- *Where **is** he?* → He asked me where he **was**.
- *What **are** you doing?* → She wanted to know what I **was** doing.
- *Did you **see** the film?* → He asked if I **had seen** the film.
- *When **will** you come?* → They asked when I **would** come.
:::

## 37.7 Embedded Questions as Subjects and Complements

An embedded question can also be the **subject** or **complement** of a sentence:

::: ex
- **Where he went** is a mystery. (subject)
- **Whether he will come** is uncertain. (subject — *whether*, not *if*)
- The question is **how we can save money**. (complement)
- It is not clear **why she resigned**. (anticipatory *it*; the embedded question is the real subject)
:::

## 37.8 Common Errors with Embedded Questions

::: errs
1. ✘ I don't know where does he live. ✔ I don't know where **he lives**.
2. ✘ Can you tell me what time is it? ✔ Can you tell me what time **it is**?
3. ✘ I wonder why is she crying. ✔ I wonder why **she is** crying.
4. ✘ Do you know where is the post office? ✔ Do you know where **the post office is**?
5. ✘ I asked him that where he lived. ✔ I asked him **where** he lived.
6. ✘ Tell me how much does it cost. ✔ Tell me how much **it costs**.
7. ✘ I don't know did he come. ✔ I don't know **if / whether** he came.
8. ✘ I wonder where he is? ✔ I wonder where he is**.**
9. ✘ He asked me what was I doing. ✔ He asked me what **I was** doing.
10. ✘ Nobody knows when will the exam start. ✔ Nobody knows when **the exam will** start.
11. ✘ If he will come is not certain. ✔ **Whether** he will come is not certain.
12. ✘ I can't decide if to go or not. ✔ I can't decide **whether** to go or not.
13. ✘ Could you tell me where can I find a bank? ✔ … where **I can** find a bank?
14. ✘ I want to know who is that girl. ✔ I want to know **who that girl is**.
15. ✘ Please tell me what did he say. ✔ Please tell me what **he said**.
:::

## 37.9 Embedded Questions in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
- **SAT Writing** tests embedded questions as an **inversion error**: ✘ *Historians debate why did the empire collapse.* ✔ *… why **the empire collapsed**.*
- **GMAT:** embedded questions introduced by ***whether*** (not *if*) are preferred: *The study examines **whether** diet affects memory.* Also watch for the wordy ***the question as to whether*** — GMAT prefers ***whether*** alone.
- **Punctuation (SAT):** a statement containing an embedded question ends with a **full stop**, not a question mark.
:::
