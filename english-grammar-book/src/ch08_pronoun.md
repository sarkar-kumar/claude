## 8.1 What Is a Pronoun?

A **pronoun** is a word used **in place of a noun** (*pro* = for, instead of). It saves us from repeating the same noun again and again.

::: ex
- ✘ Rahim is a good student. Rahim reads attentively. Rahim's teachers love Rahim.
- ✔ Rahim is a good student. **He** reads attentively. **His** teachers love **him**.
:::

The noun that a pronoun refers to is called its **antecedent** (*ante* = before). In the example above, *Rahim* is the antecedent of *he, his* and *him*. A pronoun must agree with its antecedent in **number, gender and person**, and the reference must be **clear** — two points tested heavily in BCS, Bank, admission tests and especially in GMAT Sentence Correction.

## 8.2 Kinds of Pronoun

| Kind | Members | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Personal | I, we, you, he, she, it, they (and their object forms) | **She** loves **them**. |
| Possessive | mine, ours, yours, his, hers, its, theirs | This book is **mine**. |
| Reflexive | myself, ourselves, yourself, yourselves, himself, herself, itself, themselves | He hurt **himself**. |
| Emphatic | (same forms as reflexive, used for emphasis) | I **myself** saw it. |
| Demonstrative | this, that, these, those, such, the same | **This** is my house. |
| Indefinite | one, none, some, any, all, many, few, somebody, anyone, everything, nothing, others | **Somebody** called you. |
| Distributive | each, either, neither | **Each** of them got a prize. |
| Reciprocal | each other, one another | They love **each other**. |
| Relative | who, whom, whose, which, that, what, as, but | This is the man **who** helped me. |
| Interrogative | who, whom, whose, which, what | **Who** is there? |

## 8.3 Personal Pronouns

### 8.3.1 Person, Number and Case

| Person | Number | Subjective (Nominative) | Objective | Possessive determiner | Possessive pronoun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | Singular | I | me | my | mine |
| 1st | Plural | we | us | our | ours |
| 2nd | Sing./Plural | you | you | your | yours |
| 3rd | Sing. masculine | he | him | his | his |
| 3rd | Sing. feminine | she | her | her | hers |
| 3rd | Sing. neuter | it | it | its | (its) |
| 3rd | Plural | they | them | their | theirs |

- The **subjective** form is used as the **subject** of a verb and as the **complement** after *be* (formal).
- The **objective** form is used as the **object** of a verb or of a **preposition**.

### 8.3.2 Case Rules — Most Tested Points

**Rule 1 — After a preposition, use the objective case.**

::: ex
- ✘ Between you and I ✔ Between you and **me**.
- ✘ This is for he and I. ✔ This is for **him and me**.
- ✘ Everyone except she came. ✔ Everyone except **her** came.
- ✘ All but he were present. ✔ All but **him** were present. (*but* = except, a preposition)
- ✘ Let you and I go. ✔ Let you and **me** go. (*let* takes an object)
- ✘ He gave it to my brother and I. ✔ … to my brother and **me**.
:::

::: tip
**Exam Tip — The "remove the other person" test:** In a compound like *my brother and I/me*, remove the other person and listen: *He gave it to ~~my brother and~~ **me*** ✔ (✘ *He gave it to I*). *~~Rahim and~~ **I** went home* ✔ (✘ *Me went home*).
:::

**Rule 2 — After *than* and *as … as*, the case depends on the understood verb.**

::: ex
- He is taller than **I** (am). ✔ (✘ taller than me — informal; in exams choose *I*)
- She is as clever as **he** (is).
- I love you more than **he** (loves you). — meaning: he loves you less than I do.
- I love you more than (I love) **him**. — meaning: I love him less than I love you.
:::

**Rule 3 — After the verb *be*, formal English uses the subjective case.**

::: ex
- It is **I**. (formal) / It's **me**. (informal, everyday)
- It was **he** who broke the glass.
- If I were **he**, I would apologise.
- ✘ It is them who are responsible. ✔ It is **they** who are responsible.
:::

**Rule 4 — With an infinitive *to be*, the pronoun takes the same case as the word before *to be*.**

::: ex
- I took him to be **me**. (*him* is objective, so *me*)
- I was taken to be **he**. (*I* is subjective, so *he*)
:::

**Rule 5 — In apposition, the pronoun takes the case of the noun it explains.**

::: ex
- **We** students are protesting. (✘ Us students) → *We are protesting.*
- They punished **us** boys. (✘ we boys) → *They punished us.*
:::

### 8.3.3 Order of Personal Pronouns (The 231 / 123 Rule)

When pronouns of different persons come together:

- For **good or neutral** things, use the order **2 → 3 → 1** (*you, he, I*) — out of politeness the speaker puts himself last: ***You, he and I** will go together. **You and I** are friends.*
- For **confessing a fault or guilt**, use **1 → 2 → 3** (*I, you, he*) — the speaker accepts blame first: ***I, you and he** are to blame. **I and he** broke the vase.*
- In the **plural**, the order is **1 → 2 → 3**: ***We, you and they** must work together.*

::: ex
- ✘ I and you will go. ✔ **You and I** will go.
- ✘ He, you and I are guilty. ✔ **I, you and he** are guilty.
:::

### 8.3.4 Agreement of Pronoun with Antecedent

::: rule
A pronoun must agree with its antecedent in **number**, **gender** and **person**.
:::

::: ex
1. Two antecedents joined by ***and*** → plural pronoun: *Rahim **and** Karim did **their** duty.*
2. But if the two nouns refer to **one** person/thing, or are preceded by ***each / every***, use singular: *The secretary and treasurer has given **his** report. **Every** man and woman must do **his or her** duty.*
3. Antecedents joined by ***or, nor, either…or, neither…nor*** → the pronoun agrees with the **nearer** one: *Neither the teacher nor the **students** have finished **their** work. Either the boys or **Rina** has lost **her** book.*
4. **Collective noun** as a unit → singular; as individuals → plural: *The **jury** gave **its** verdict. The **jury** were divided in **their** opinions.*
5. ***Each, every, either, neither, everyone, everybody, anyone, someone, no one, nobody*** → singular pronoun: ***Each** of the boys must bring **his** own pen. **Everyone** should love **his** country.*
6. ***One*** → ***one's / oneself*** (British formal): ***One** should do **one's** duty. **One** must not praise **oneself**.* (✘ *One should do his duty* — American usage accepts *his*; in Bangladeshi exams choose ***one's***.)
:::

### 8.3.5 Special Uses of *It*

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| For things, animals, babies (sex unknown) | *The baby is crying for **its** mother.* |
| Impersonal subject (weather, time, distance) | ***It** is raining. **It** is 10 o'clock. **It** is five kilometres to the station.* |
| Introductory (anticipatory) subject for an infinitive / clause | ***It** is easy to learn English. **It** is certain that he will come.* |
| Introductory object | *I find **it** difficult to believe him.* |
| Emphasis (cleft sentence) | ***It** was Rahim who broke the glass. **It** is you that I want.* |
| For a previously mentioned statement | *He is honest; everybody knows **it**.* |
| Identifying a person | *Who is at the door? — **It** is the postman.* |

## 8.4 Possessive Pronouns

***Mine, ours, yours, his, hers, its, theirs*** stand **alone** (they replace *possessive determiner + noun*).

::: ex
- This pen is **mine** (= my pen). / ✘ This is mine pen. ✔ This is **my** pen.
- Your house is bigger than **ours**.
- The fault is **theirs**, not **yours**.
- A friend of **mine** (✘ a friend of me / ✘ a friend of my).
:::

::: rule
**No apostrophe in possessive pronouns:** ✘ *your's, her's, our's, their's, it's* (as possessive). ✔ ***yours, hers, ours, theirs, its***. *It's* always means *it is* or *it has*.
:::

## 8.5 Reflexive and Emphatic Pronouns

The *-self / -selves* forms: ***myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, oneself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves***. (✘ *hisself, theirselves, ourself* — not standard.)

### 8.5.1 Reflexive Use

A **reflexive pronoun** is used as the **object** when the action of the verb **turns back on the subject** (subject and object are the same person).

::: ex
- He hurt **himself**.
- She looked at **herself** in the mirror.
- We enjoyed **ourselves** at the party.
- They prided **themselves** on their success.
:::

Some verbs **must** be followed by a reflexive pronoun (or an object) in standard English:

*absent oneself (from), avail oneself (of), pride oneself (on), enjoy oneself, acquit oneself (well), betake oneself (to), revenge oneself (on), resign oneself (to), apply oneself (to), exert oneself, oversleep (no reflexive), behave oneself, help oneself (to), excuse oneself.*

::: ex
- ✘ He absented from the class. ✔ He absented **himself** from the class.
- ✘ You should avail of this opportunity. ✔ You should avail **yourself** of this opportunity.
- ✘ We enjoyed very much. ✔ We enjoyed **ourselves** very much. / We enjoyed **the party** very much.
- ✘ He prides on his wealth. ✔ He prides **himself** on his wealth.
:::

Conversely, some verbs **do not take** a reflexive pronoun in normal English:

*keep, hide, qualify, rest, stop, turn, bathe, feel, relax, meet, concentrate, wake up, get up, sit down, move, open, shave, dress, wash (when the meaning is clear).*

::: ex
- ✘ He kept himself away. ✔ He **kept away**.
- ✘ Turn yourself to the right. ✔ **Turn** to the right.
- ✘ Please concentrate yourself on your studies. ✔ Please **concentrate** on your studies.
- ✘ I feel myself better. ✔ I **feel** better.
- ✘ Qualify yourself for the post. ✔ **Qualify** for the post.
:::

### 8.5.2 Emphatic Use

The same forms are **emphatic** when they are placed next to a noun or pronoun to **emphasise** it. They can be removed without changing the grammar.

::: ex
- I **myself** saw the accident. / I saw the accident **myself**.
- The Prime Minister **herself** inaugurated the bridge.
- You **yourself** said so.
:::

::: note
**A *-self* pronoun cannot be the subject by itself.** ✘ *Myself and Rahim went there.* ✔ ***Rahim and I** went there.* ✘ *The tickets were bought by Karim and myself.* ✔ *… by Karim and **me**.* ✘ *Myself is Rina.* ✔ ***I am** Rina* / ***My name is** Rina*.
:::

## 8.6 Demonstrative Pronouns

***This, that, these, those*** (and sometimes ***such, the same***) are **demonstrative pronouns** when they stand alone and point to something.

::: ex
- **This** is my bag; **that** is yours.
- **These** are fresh; **those** are stale.
- The climate of Sylhet is better than **that** of Dhaka. (*that* = the climate)
- The roads of Dhaka are wider than **those** of Old Dhaka.
- He is a fool and must be treated as **such**.
- I bought a pen and gave **the same** to my brother. (commercial style; better: *gave **it***)
:::

## 8.7 Indefinite Pronouns

Indefinite pronouns refer to persons or things **in a general way**, not to anyone in particular.

| Always singular | Always plural | Singular or plural (depends on *of*-noun) |
|---|---|---|
| one, each, either, neither, anybody, anyone, anything, everybody, everyone, everything, nobody, no one, nothing, somebody, someone, something, another, much, little | both, few, many, several, others | all, any, none, some, most, more, half, plenty, a lot |

::: ex
- **Everybody** **is** happy. (✘ are)
- **Nobody** **knows** the answer.
- **Something** **is** wrong.
- **Many** **were** invited, but **few** **came**.
- **Both** **are** guilty.
- **All** of the money **is** gone. / **All** of the books **are** gone.
- **Some** of the milk **was** spilt. / **Some** of the students **were** late.
- **None** of the information **is** useful. / **None** of the boys **is** (formal) / **are** (common) here.
:::

::: note
***None*** literally means *not one*, so traditional grammar prefers a singular verb: ***None** of them **is** present.* Modern usage commonly accepts a plural verb with a plural noun. In Bangladeshi exams, if one option uses *is* and the other *are*, the traditional answer is ***is***. With an uncountable noun, *none* is always singular: ***None** of the work **has** been done.*
:::

## 8.8 Distributive and Reciprocal Pronouns

**Distributive pronouns** (*each, either, neither*) refer to persons or things **one at a time**. They take a **singular** verb and pronoun.

::: ex
- **Each** of the players **has** received **his** medal.
- **Either** of the two roads **leads** to the market. (two only)
- **Neither** of my parents **is** at home. (two only)
- ✘ Either of the three boys … ✔ **Any** of the three boys … / ✘ Neither of the five … ✔ **None** of the five …
:::

**Reciprocal pronouns** show a **mutual** relationship: ***each other*** (traditionally for **two**) and ***one another*** (traditionally for **more than two**).

::: ex
- The husband and wife love **each other**.
- The members of the team help **one another**.
- They looked at **each other's** faces. (possessive form)
:::

## 8.9 Relative Pronouns

A **relative pronoun** does two jobs: (1) it **refers back** to a noun (its antecedent), and (2) it **joins** two clauses — so it works as both a pronoun and a conjunction. The clause it introduces is an **adjective (relative) clause**.

::: ex
- This is the boy. He won the prize. → This is the boy **who** won the prize.
- I found the book. I had lost it. → I found the book **which** I had lost.
:::

### 8.9.1 *Who, Whom, Whose, Which, That, What*

| Relative | Used for | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **who** | persons | subject | *The man **who** came here is my uncle.* |
| **whom** | persons | object | *The man **whom** you met is my uncle.* |
| **whose** | persons (and things) | possession | *The boy **whose** father is a doctor is my friend. The house **whose** roof is red …* |
| **which** | things, animals | subject / object | *The book **which** you gave me is useful.* |
| **that** | persons and things | subject / object (defining clauses only) | *The boy **that** won / the book **that** I read* |
| **what** | things | = *the thing(s) which* (no antecedent) | ***What** he said is true.* |

### 8.9.2 *Who* or *Whom*? — The *he/him* Test

Turn the relative clause into a separate sentence. If ***he/she/they*** fits, use ***who***; if ***him/her/them*** fits, use ***whom***.

::: ex
- This is the man ___ I think is honest. → *I think **he** is honest* → ***who*** ✔ (✘ whom — a classic GMAT/GRE trap)
- This is the man ___ we elected. → *We elected **him*** → ***whom*** ✔
- ___ do you want to see? → *You want to see **him*** → ***Whom*** (formal) / *Who* (informal)
- Give it to ___ needs it most. → ***whoever*** (*he* needs it) ✔ (✘ whomever)
- Give it to ___ you like. → ***whomever*** (*you like him*) ✔
:::

### 8.9.3 When Only *That* Can Be Used

Use ***that*** (not *who/which*) after:

1. **Superlatives:** *He is the **best** man **that** I have ever met.*
2. ***all, any, none, nothing, everything, anything, something, much, little, only, the same, the very, the first, the last***: ***All that** glitters is not gold. This is **the same** book **that** I lost. He is **the only** man **that** can help us. **Nothing that** he said was true.*
3. **Interrogative *who / what*:** ***Who that** has common sense will believe it? **What** is there **that** I cannot do?*
4. **Two antecedents, one a person and one a thing/animal:** *The man **and his dog that** passed by were wet.*
5. **Emphatic *It is/was … that*:** *It is the teacher **that** we respect.*

### 8.9.4 When *That* Cannot Be Used

1. **After a preposition:** ✘ *the house **in that** he lives* → ✔ *the house **in which** he lives* / ✔ *the house **that** he lives **in***.
2. **In a non-defining clause (after a comma):** ✘ *My father, that is a doctor, …* → ✔ *My father, **who** is a doctor, …*

### 8.9.5 Defining and Non-Defining Relative Clauses

- A **defining (restrictive)** clause tells us **which one** is meant; it is essential and has **no commas**: *The students **who studied hard** passed.* (only those students)
- A **non-defining (non-restrictive)** clause gives **extra information** about an already identified noun; it is set off by **commas**: *The students, **who studied hard**, passed.* (all the students studied hard and passed)

::: ex
- My brother **who lives in London** is a doctor. (I have more than one brother.)
- My brother, **who lives in London**, is a doctor. (I have only one brother.)
- Dhaka, **which is the capital of Bangladesh**, is very crowded. (non-defining — commas required)
:::

### 8.9.6 Omission of the Relative Pronoun

In a **defining** clause, an **object** relative pronoun can be omitted: *The book (**that/which**) I bought is good. The man (**whom**) you met is my uncle.* A **subject** relative cannot be omitted: ✘ *The man helped me is my uncle.* ✔ *The man **who** helped me …*

### 8.9.7 *As* and *But* as Relative Pronouns

- ***As*** after ***such*** and ***the same***: *Such boys **as** are lazy will fail. This is **the same** pen **as** mine.* (✘ such boys who …)
- ***But*** = *who … not* after a negative: *There is no one **but** loves his country* (= who does not love). *There is no rose **but** has thorns.*

### 8.9.8 Agreement of the Verb after a Relative Pronoun

::: rule
The verb in a relative clause agrees with the **antecedent** of the relative pronoun in **number and person**.
:::

::: ex
- **I**, who **am** your friend, will help you. (✘ who is)
- **You**, who **are** my friend, should help me.
- It is **I** who **am** to blame. / It is **you** who **are** to blame.
- He is one of **the boys who have** passed. (*who* refers to *boys*, plural)
- He is **the only one** of the boys **who has** passed. (*who* refers to *the only one*, singular)
:::

### 8.9.9 Position of the Relative Pronoun

Place the relative pronoun **as near as possible** to its antecedent, or the sentence becomes absurd:

::: ex
- ✘ I have a cow for my brother which gives good milk. ✔ For my brother I have a cow **which** gives good milk.
- ✘ He bought a horse from a dealer that was lame. ✔ He bought from a dealer a horse **that** was lame.
:::

### 8.9.10 Common Mistakes with *What*

***What*** contains its own antecedent (= *that which*), so it cannot follow a noun or *all*:

::: ex
- ✘ All what he said is true. ✔ **All that** he said is true.
- ✘ The thing what I want … ✔ The thing **that/which** I want … / ✔ **What** I want …
- ✘ This is the book what I bought. ✔ … the book **that/which** I bought.
:::

## 8.10 Interrogative Pronouns

***Who, whom, whose, which, what*** are **interrogative pronouns** when they ask questions.

::: ex
- **Who** is knocking? (subject)
- **Whom** did you invite? (object — formal) / **Who** did you invite? (informal)
- **Whose** is this umbrella? (pronoun) / **Whose** umbrella is this? (determiner)
- **Which** of these is yours? (limited choice)
- **What** do you want? (open choice)
- ✘ Who are you speaking to? (informal) ✔ **To whom** are you speaking? (formal)
:::

## 8.11 Common Errors with Pronouns

::: errs
1. ✘ Between you and I, he is a liar. ✔ Between you and **me**, …
2. ✘ He is taller than me. (in exam) ✔ He is taller than **I** (am).
3. ✘ It is me who am responsible. ✔ It is **I** who **am** responsible.
4. ✘ Myself and my wife will attend. ✔ **My wife and I** will attend.
5. ✘ I and you will go. ✔ **You and I** will go.
6. ✘ Each of the girls have done their work. ✔ Each of the girls **has** done **her** work.
7. ✘ Everyone must bring their own lunch. (traditional) ✔ Everyone must bring **his or her** own lunch.
8. ✘ One should respect his parents. ✔ One should respect **one's** parents.
9. ✘ Is this book your's? ✔ Is this book **yours**?
10. ✘ The dog wagged it's tail. ✔ The dog wagged **its** tail.
11. ✘ He availed of the chance. ✔ He availed **himself** of the chance.
12. ✘ We enjoyed during the holidays. ✔ We enjoyed **ourselves** during the holidays.
13. ✘ Please keep yourself quiet. ✔ Please **keep quiet**.
14. ✘ They hurt theirselves. ✔ They hurt **themselves**.
15. ✘ The man whom I think is guilty has fled. ✔ The man **who** I think is guilty has fled.
16. ✘ Whom do you think will win? ✔ **Who** do you think will win?
17. ✘ This is the pen what I bought. ✔ This is the pen **that** I bought.
18. ✘ All what glitters is not gold. ✔ **All that** glitters is not gold.
19. ✘ He is the best man who I know. ✔ He is the best man **that** I know.
20. ✘ My mother, that is a teacher, … ✔ My mother, **who** is a teacher, …
21. ✘ The house in that he lives is old. ✔ The house **in which** he lives is old.
22. ✘ Such boys who are lazy fail. ✔ Such boys **as** are lazy fail.
23. ✘ He is one of the best players who has ever played. ✔ … **who have** ever played.
24. ✘ Neither of the five candidates was selected. ✔ **None** of the five candidates was selected.
25. ✘ The jury gave their verdict. ✔ The jury gave **its** verdict. (acting as one body)
26. ✘ Let he and I go. ✔ Let **him and me** go.
27. ✘ Us Bangladeshis are hospitable. ✔ **We** Bangladeshis are hospitable.
28. ✘ The teacher punished we boys. ✔ The teacher punished **us** boys.
29. ✘ Whom is there? ✔ **Who** is there?
30. ✘ The climate of Cox's Bazar is better than Dhaka. ✔ … better than **that of** Dhaka.
31. ✘ He and myself are friends. ✔ **He and I** are friends.
32. ✘ The students, who's results were good, were praised. ✔ The students, **whose** results were good, were praised.
33. ✘ Rahim told Karim that he would win. (ambiguous) ✔ Rahim told Karim, "**You** will win" / "**I** will win".
34. ✘ Anybody can do it if they try. (traditional exam) ✔ Anybody can do it if **he** tries.
35. ✘ He, you and I are to blame. ✔ **I, you and he** are to blame.
:::

## 8.12 Pronouns in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
Pronoun questions are among the most common in GMAT Sentence Correction and SAT Writing. Check every pronoun for these four problems:

1. **Ambiguous reference** — the pronoun could refer to two nouns: ✘ *When the manager met the clerk, **he** was angry.* (Who was angry?) Replace the pronoun with the noun.
2. **No antecedent / possessive antecedent** — a pronoun should refer to a noun actually present in the sentence, not to a possessive or an implied idea: ✘ *In **Tagore's** poems, **he** celebrates nature* (GMAT strict rule: *he* cannot refer to the possessive *Tagore's*). ✔ *In **his** poems, Tagore celebrates nature.* ✘ *Although she studied medicine, **it** never interested her* (*it* = medicine? studying?). 
3. **Number disagreement** — singular *company, country, team, government, each, every* need *it/its*, not *they/their*: ✘ *The company increased **their** profits.* ✔ ***its** profits.*
4. **Wrong case** — ✘ *between you and I*, ✘ *the person whom I believe is guilty*.

**GMAT *which* rule:** In GMAT, ***which*** (after a comma) must refer to the **noun immediately before it**, never to a whole clause: ✘ *The prices rose sharply, **which** angered consumers.* ✔ *The prices rose sharply, **a rise that** angered consumers* / *… **angering** consumers.*

**SAT/American English:** ***that*** introduces **restrictive** clauses (no commas); ***which*** introduces **non-restrictive** clauses (with commas): *The car **that** I bought is red* / *My car, **which** is red, is fast.*
:::
