# Identification of Clause {#ch31}

[Part A · Grammar]{.chpart}

## 31.1 What Is a Clause?

A **clause** is a group of words that contains a **subject** and a **finite verb** and forms **part of a sentence** (or a whole simple sentence).

::: ex
- **He came** when **I was sleeping**. → two clauses: *He came* + *when I was sleeping*
- **I know** that **he is honest**. → two clauses
- **The man who lives next door** is a doctor. → *The man … is a doctor* + *who lives next door*
:::

## 31.2 Kinds of Clauses

| Kind | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Independent (Main / Principal) clause** | makes complete sense by itself; can stand alone as a sentence | ***I know*** that he is honest. |
| **Dependent (Subordinate) clause** | does not make complete sense by itself; depends on a main clause; begins with a subordinator | I know **that he is honest**. |
| **Coordinate clause** | one of two or more **equal** independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction | ***He is poor**, but **he is honest**.* |

Subordinate clauses are of **three kinds**, according to the work they do:

1. **Noun clause** — does the work of a **noun**.
2. **Adjective (Relative) clause** — does the work of an **adjective**.
3. **Adverb clause** — does the work of an **adverb**.

## 31.3 The Noun Clause

A **noun clause** is a subordinate clause that does the work of a **noun**. It can be a **subject, object, complement, object of a preposition** or **in apposition** to a noun.

**Common introducers:** *that, whether, if, what, who, whom, whose, which, when, where, why, how, whoever, whatever, whichever*

| Function | Example |
|---|---|
| **Subject** of a verb | ***What he said** is true. **That he is honest** is known to all. **Whether he will come** is doubtful.* |
| **Object** of a verb | *I know **that he is honest**. Tell me **where you live**. I wonder **if it will rain**.* |
| **Object of a preposition** | *Pay attention to **what the teacher says**. It depends on **how hard you work**.* |
| **Complement** of a verb | *My belief is **that he will succeed**. This is **what I want**.* |
| **In apposition** to a noun | *The news **that he had passed** pleased us. The fact **that she is ill** worries me.* |
| **Object of an infinitive / participle** | *I want to know **why he left**. Knowing **that he was ill**, I visited him.* |
| **Subject of a sentence with *It*** (anticipatory *it*) | *It is certain **that he will win**. (= That he will win is certain.)* |

::: ex
- **Who stole the money** is still a mystery. (subject)
- I don't know **whose book this is**. (object)
- The question is **whether he will agree**. (complement)
- He came back to **where he had started**. (object of preposition)
- The rumour **that the minister had resigned** was false. (apposition to *rumour*)
- **Whatever you do**, do well. — careful: here it is an **adverb clause** of concession (= *No matter what you do*); but in ***Whatever you do** will be appreciated* it is a **noun clause** (subject).
:::

::: tip
**Exam Tip — Test for a noun clause:** Replace the clause with ***it*** or ***something***. If the sentence still makes sense, it is a noun clause. *I know **that he is honest*** → *I know **it*** ✔ → noun clause.
:::

## 31.4 The Adjective (Relative) Clause

An **adjective clause** qualifies a **noun or pronoun** (its **antecedent**) that comes just before it. It answers ***which one? what kind?***

**Introducers:**

- **Relative pronouns:** *who, whom, whose, which, that, as, but*
- **Relative adverbs:** *where, when, why* (and *wherein, whereby*)
- The relative may be **omitted** when it is the object: *the book (that) I bought*

::: ex
- The man **who came here yesterday** is my uncle. (qualifies *man*)
- I have lost the pen **which you gave me**. (qualifies *pen*)
- This is the boy **whose father is a doctor**.
- The house **where I was born** is now a school. (*where* = in which; qualifies *house*)
- I remember the day **when we first met**. (qualifies *day*)
- Tell me the reason **why you are late**. (qualifies *reason*)
- He is not the man **(that) I thought he was**.
- Such men **as are honest** are respected. (*as* after *such*)
- There is no one **but loves his country**. (*but* = who … not)
:::

### 31.4.1 Defining and Non-Defining Adjective Clauses

- **Defining (restrictive):** tells us **which** person/thing; **no commas**; essential to the meaning. *The students **who passed** were rewarded.*
- **Non-defining (non-restrictive):** gives **extra** information about an already identified noun; **commas** used; *that* cannot be used. *My father, **who is a doctor**, lives in Sylhet.* (Chapter 08.9.5)

::: tip
**Exam Tip — Test for an adjective clause:** Find the **noun just before** the clause. If the clause **describes that noun** (and the introducer can be replaced by *which / who / in which / at which*), it is an adjective clause. *This is the place **where** he lives* → *the place **in which** he lives* ✔ → adjective clause.
:::

## 31.5 The Adverb Clause

An **adverb clause** modifies a **verb, adjective or adverb** in the main clause, telling **when, where, why, how, with what purpose, with what result, under what condition, in spite of what,** or **to what degree**.

| Kind | Introducers | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | when, whenever, while, as, before, after, till, until, since, as soon as, as long as, once, by the time, now that, the moment | *I will wait **till you come**. **When the cat is away**, the mice will play.* |
| **Place** | where, wherever, whence, whither | *Sit **where you like**. **Wherever he goes**, he makes friends.* |
| **Reason / Cause** | because, since, as, now that, seeing that, inasmuch as | *He failed **because he was lazy**. **As it was raining**, we stayed home.* |
| **Purpose** | that, so that, in order that, lest | *He works hard **so that he may succeed**. Walk carefully **lest you should fall**.* |
| **Result / Consequence** | so … that, such … that, that | *He is **so** weak **that he cannot walk**. It was **such** a cold night **that we could not sleep**.* |
| **Condition** | if, unless, provided (that), on condition that, in case, supposing, as long as, whether … or | ***If you work hard**, you will succeed. **Unless you hurry**, you will miss the bus.* |
| **Concession / Contrast** | though, although, even though, even if, while, whereas, however, whatever, no matter how, as (*Rich as he is*) | ***Though he is poor**, he is honest. **Whatever you may say**, I will not go.* |
| **Comparison / Degree** | than, as … as, so … as, the … the | *He is taller **than I am**. She is **as** clever **as her sister is**. **The more you read**, the more you learn.* |
| **Manner** | as, as if, as though, the way | *Do **as I tell you**. He talks **as if he were mad**.* |

::: tip
**Exam Tip — Test for an adverb clause:** Ask a **when / where / why / how / on what condition / in spite of what** question about the main verb. If the clause answers it, it is an adverb clause. An adverb clause can usually **move** to the front or back of the sentence: ***When he came**, I was sleeping* = *I was sleeping **when he came***.
:::

## 31.6 The Same Introducer in Different Clauses

Many introducers (*that, when, where, why, how, if, whether, what, as*) can begin **any** of the three kinds of clause. The **function** decides the kind — this is the most frequently tested point in clause identification.

### 31.6.1 *When*

::: ex
- I know **when he will come**. → **Noun clause** (object of *know* → "I know *it*").
- I remember the day **when he came**. → **Adjective clause** (qualifies *day*).
- I will go **when he comes**. → **Adverb clause** of time (modifies *will go*).
- **When he will come** is not known. → **Noun clause** (subject).
:::

### 31.6.2 *Where*

::: ex
- I don't know **where he lives**. → **Noun clause** (object).
- This is the village **where he lives**. → **Adjective clause** (qualifies *village*).
- Sit **where you like**. → **Adverb clause** of place.
:::

### 31.6.3 *That*

::: ex
- I know **that he is honest**. → **Noun clause** (object).
- This is the book **that I bought**. → **Adjective clause** (qualifies *book*; *that* = which).
- He works hard **so that he may prosper**. → **Adverb clause** of purpose.
- He is so weak **that he cannot walk**. → **Adverb clause** of result.
- The news **that he has passed** is true. → **Noun clause** in apposition to *news* (*that* cannot be replaced by *which* — compare with the adjective clause: *The news **that he brought** is true* → *which he brought* ✔ → adjective clause).
:::

::: note
**Appositive noun clause vs adjective clause after *the fact / the news / the idea / the belief*:** If *that* can be replaced by ***which*** and the clause has a "gap" (a missing object), it is an **adjective clause**: *The news **that he told me** was false* (he told me *the news* → *which*). If the clause is **complete in itself** and explains the content of the noun, it is a **noun clause in apposition**: *The news **that he had died** was false* (he had died — complete; content of the news).
:::

### 31.6.4 *Why* and *How*

::: ex
- I don't know **why he left**. → **Noun clause**.
- Tell me the reason **why he left**. → **Adjective clause** (qualifies *reason*).
- I know **how he did it**. → **Noun clause**.
- Do it **as I told you**. → **Adverb clause** of manner.
:::

### 31.6.5 *If* and *Whether*

::: ex
- I asked him **if he would help me**. → **Noun clause** (object — indirect question).
- **If you help me**, I shall be grateful. → **Adverb clause** of condition.
- **Whether he will come** is uncertain. → **Noun clause** (subject).
- **Whether he comes or not**, I will go. → **Adverb clause** of condition/concession.
:::

### 31.6.6 *What*, *Whatever*, *Whoever*

::: ex
- **What he said** is true. → **Noun clause**.
- **Whatever he says** is true. → **Noun clause** (subject).
- **Whatever he says**, I will not believe him. → **Adverb clause** of concession.
- **Whoever comes** will be welcome. → **Noun clause** (subject).
- **Whoever comes**, don't open the door. → **Adverb clause** of concession.
:::

### 31.6.7 *As*

::: ex
- **As he was ill**, he could not come. → **Adverb clause** of reason.
- He came **as I was leaving**. → **Adverb clause** of time.
- Do **as I say**. → **Adverb clause** of manner.
- He is as tall **as his brother is**. → **Adverb clause** of comparison.
- **Rich as he is**, he is not happy. → **Adverb clause** of concession.
- Such men **as are honest** are respected. → **Adjective clause** (*as* = who).
:::

### 31.6.8 *Since*, *Before*, *After*, *Till*

::: ex
- It is ten years **since he died**. → **Adverb clause** of time.
- **Since you are tired**, you may rest. → **Adverb clause** of reason.
- Finish it **before he comes**. → **Adverb clause** of time.
:::

## 31.7 Analysis of Complex Sentences (Clause Analysis)

**Clause analysis** means breaking a sentence into its clauses and stating the **kind** and **function** of each. This is asked in admission tests and BCS Written.

::: rule
**Steps:**

1. Find all the **finite verbs** — there are as many clauses as finite verbs.
2. Find the **main clause** (the one that makes sense alone and does not begin with a subordinator).
3. Separate each **subordinate clause** with its introducer.
4. State the **kind** (noun / adjective / adverb) and the **function** (subject, object, qualifies X, modifies Y — time, reason …).
:::

**Example 1:** *When I reached the station, I found that the train had left.*

| Clause | Kind | Function |
|---|---|---|
| *I found* | Main clause | — |
| *When I reached the station* | Subordinate adverb clause of time | modifies *found* |
| *that the train had left* | Subordinate noun clause | object of *found* |

**Example 2:** *The man who was injured in the accident says that he will go to court if he does not get compensation.*

| Clause | Kind | Function |
|---|---|---|
| *The man … says* | Main clause | — |
| *who was injured in the accident* | Adjective clause | qualifies *man* |
| *that he will go to court* | Noun clause | object of *says* |
| *if he does not get compensation* | Adverb clause of condition | modifies *will go* |

**Example 3:** *Tell me where you have kept the book that I gave you.*

| Clause | Kind | Function |
|---|---|---|
| *(You) tell me* | Main clause | — |
| *where you have kept the book* | Noun clause | object of *tell* |
| *that I gave you* | Adjective clause | qualifies *book* |

## 31.8 Reducing Clauses to Phrases

Many clauses can be **shortened into phrases** (useful in transformation questions and for concise GMAT style).

| Clause | Reduced phrase |
|---|---|
| *The man **who is standing** at the gate* (adjective clause) | *The man **standing** at the gate* (participial phrase) |
| *The book **which was written** by him* | *The book **written** by him* |
| ***When he saw** the police, he ran away.* (adverb clause) | ***Seeing** the police, he ran away.* / ***On seeing** the police …* |
| ***As he was** ill, he could not come.* | ***Being** ill, he could not come.* |
| ***After he had finished**, he went out.* | ***Having finished**, he went out.* |
| *He works hard **so that he may succeed**.* | *He works hard **to succeed**.* |
| *I know **that he is honest**.* (noun clause) | *I know him **to be honest**.* / *I know **of his honesty**.* |
| *He is so weak **that he cannot walk**.* | *He is too weak **to walk**.* |
| ***If you work hard**, you will succeed.* | ***By working hard**, you will succeed.* |
| ***Though he is rich**, he is unhappy.* | ***In spite of** being rich, he is unhappy.* |

## 31.9 Worked Examples: Identify the Clause

::: errs
1. I believe **that he is innocent**. → *Noun clause* (object of *believe*).
2. The boy **who stood first** got a prize. → *Adjective clause* (qualifies *boy*).
3. **If it rains**, the match will be cancelled. → *Adverb clause* of condition.
4. **Why he did it** is a mystery. → *Noun clause* (subject).
5. This is the house **where Nazrul lived**. → *Adjective clause* (qualifies *house*).
6. He ran **so fast that he won the race**. → *Adverb clause* of result (*that he won the race*).
7. I shall wait **until you return**. → *Adverb clause* of time.
8. He spoke **as if he were angry**. → *Adverb clause* of manner.
9. The fact **that he lied** is clear. → *Noun clause* in apposition to *fact*.
10. **Though he worked hard**, he failed. → *Adverb clause* of concession.
11. Do you know **who wrote *Agnibeena***? → *Noun clause* (object).
12. He did not come **because he was ill**. → *Adverb clause* of reason.
13. You may go **wherever you like**. → *Adverb clause* of place.
14. I have a friend **whose brother is a pilot**. → *Adjective clause*.
15. He is older **than I am**. → *Adverb clause* of comparison.
16. He saved money **so that he might buy a house**. → *Adverb clause* of purpose.
17. My fear is **that he will not come**. → *Noun clause* (complement).
18. Listen to **what the teacher says**. → *Noun clause* (object of preposition *to*).
19. There is no reason **why you should worry**. → *Adjective clause* (qualifies *reason*).
20. **Whatever you may do**, do it well. → *Adverb clause* of concession.
:::

## 31.10 Common Errors with Clauses

::: errs
1. ✘ I know where does he live. ✔ I know where **he lives**. (no inversion in noun clauses)
2. ✘ What he said it is true. ✔ What he said **is** true. (no extra subject)
3. ✘ The man who he came here is my uncle. ✔ The man **who came** here is my uncle.
4. ✘ The book which I bought it is interesting. ✔ The book which I bought is interesting.
5. ✘ This is the place where I was born there. ✔ This is the place where I was born.
6. ✘ Because he was ill. (fragment) ✔ He was absent **because he was ill**.
7. ✘ That he is honest everybody knows it. ✔ **That he is honest** everybody knows. / Everybody knows **that he is honest**.
8. ✘ I asked him that what he wanted. ✔ I asked him **what he wanted**.
9. ✘ The reason why he failed is because he was lazy. ✔ … is **that** he was lazy.
10. ✘ He is the man which helped me. ✔ He is the man **who** helped me.
:::

## 31.11 Clauses in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
- **Fragments and run-ons (SAT):** a dependent clause alone is a **fragment** (✘ *Although the plan was good.*); two independent clauses joined only by a comma are a **comma splice** (✘ *The plan was good, it failed.*). Fix with a conjunction, semicolon or full stop.
- **Relative clause placement (GMAT):** *which / who / that* must follow the noun it modifies directly ("touch rule").
- **Noun clauses after reporting verbs (GMAT):** use ***that***: ✘ *Scientists claim the discovery as significant.* ✔ *Scientists claim **that** the discovery is significant.* Many GMAT answers that drop *that* after *claim, hypothesise, argue* are marked less clear.
- **GRE Reading:** long sentences with multiple embedded clauses are typical; identifying the **main clause** quickly reveals the author's central claim.
:::
