# Identifying the Missing Word {#ch36}

[Part A · Grammar]{.chpart}

## 36.1 What Is a "Missing Word" Question?

In this type of question, a sentence has a **word left out** — sometimes marked with a blank, sometimes not marked at all — and you must find **which word is missing** and **where**. The missing word is usually a **function word** (article, preposition, auxiliary, conjunction, pronoun, *to*, *that*, *than*), but it can also be a **content word** that the context makes clear.

::: ex
- *He is fond ___ reading.* → missing **of**.
- *I have been waiting you for an hour.* → missing **for** (*waiting **for** you*).
- *She is taller her sister.* → missing **than**.
- *Neither he his brother came.* → missing **nor**.
:::

In BCS, Bank and admission tests this appears as **fill in the gaps**, **"supply the missing word"**, and as a hidden form of **error detection** (an omission is an error). In **GRE Text Completion** and **SAT Sentence Completion**, the "missing word" is a **content word** that must be predicted from context clues (36.6).

## 36.2 A Method for Finding the Missing Word

::: rule
1. **Read for meaning.** Where does the sentence "break"? The gap is usually right there.
2. **Check the skeleton:** Is there a **subject**? a **finite verb**? Does a transitive verb have its **object**?
3. **Check pairs:** Does every *not only* have *but also*, every *neither* a *nor*, every *as* an *as*, every comparative a *than*, every *the more* a *the …*?
4. **Check idioms:** Does a word need a fixed **preposition** (*afraid **of**, depend **on***)?
5. **Check determiners:** Does a singular countable noun have *a/an/the/my*?
6. **Check verb groups:** Does a continuous tense have *be*? a perfect tense *have*? a passive *be*? a question/negative *do*?
:::

## 36.3 The Most Frequently Missing Words

### 36.3.1 Missing Article

::: ex
- He is ___ honest man. → **an**
- ___ sun rises in ___ east. → **The … the**
- She is ___ best student in the class. → **the**
- I have ___ headache. → **a**
- It is ___ pity that he failed. → **a**
- He is such ___ fool! → **a**
- ___ Padma is a mighty river. → **The**
- Half ___ hour has passed. → **an**
:::

### 36.3.2 Missing Preposition

::: ex
- He is afraid ___ dogs. → **of**
- She depends ___ her father. → **on**
- I congratulate you ___ your success. → **on**
- He was born ___ 1990. → **in**
- I will meet you ___ Friday. → **on**
- He has been ill ___ Monday. → **since**
- We listened ___ the speech. → **to**
- She is good ___ mathematics. → **at**
- He prefers tea ___ coffee. → **to**
- Look ___ the board. → **at**
- He insisted ___ paying. → **on**
- He came ___ foot. → **on**
:::

### 36.3.3 Missing Auxiliary or Part of the Verb

::: ex
- He ___ reading a book now. → **is**
- They ___ finished their work. → **have**
- ___ you know him? → **Do**
- He ___ not come yesterday. → **did**
- The letter ___ written by Rina. → **was**
- The bridge is ___ built now. → **being**
- He has ___ working since morning. → **been**
- No sooner ___ he arrived than it rained. → **had**
- Never ___ I seen such a sight. → **have**
- Seldom ___ he come late. → **does**
:::

### 36.3.4 Missing *To* (Infinitive) — or Unwanted *To*

::: ex
- He wants ___ go home. → **to**
- I am going ___ see the doctor. → **to**
- He is too weak ___ walk. → **to**
- You ought ___ respect your elders. → **to**
- He used ___ live here. → **to**
- *But:* Let him ___ go. → **nothing** (✘ to); You must ___ go → **nothing**.
:::

### 36.3.5 Missing Conjunction or Correlative Partner

::: ex
- He is not only intelligent ___ also hard-working. → **but**
- Neither Rahim ___ Karim came. → **nor**
- Either you ___ I must go. → **or**
- Both Rina ___ Mina passed. → **and**
- He is as tall ___ his father. → **as**
- She is taller ___ her sister. → **than**
- No sooner had he left ___ she arrived. → **than**
- Hardly had he left ___ she arrived. → **when**
- He is so weak ___ he cannot walk. → **that**
- Walk carefully ___ you should fall. → **lest**
- I will wait ___ you come. → **till / until**
- The more you read, ___ more you learn. → **the**
:::

### 36.3.6 Missing Relative Pronoun or *That*

::: ex
- The man ___ came here is my uncle. → **who** (subject relative — cannot be omitted)
- This is the house ___ I was born. → **where / in which**
- I know ___ he is honest. → **that** (optional after *know*)
- ___ he is honest is known to all. → **That** (required when the clause is a subject)
- He is the best man ___ I have ever met. → **that**
- Such men ___ are honest are respected. → **as**
:::

### 36.3.7 Missing Pronoun, *It* or *There*

::: ex
- He absented ___ from the class. → **himself**
- ___ is raining. → **It**
- ___ is a book on the table. → **There**
- ___ is easy to learn English. → **It**
- I find ___ difficult to believe him. → **it**
- He hurt ___ while playing. → **himself**
:::

### 36.3.8 Missing *Of* after Determiners and Quantifiers

::: ex
- All ___ us were present. → **of** (✘ all us)
- Both ___ them came. → **of**
- One ___ the boys is absent. → **of**
- Each ___ the students has a book. → **of**
- A number ___ students were absent. → **of**
- Most ___ the people agreed. → **of**
:::

### 36.3.9 Missing Object or Complement

::: ex
- We enjoyed ___ very much. → **ourselves** / **the party**
- He gave ___ a book. → **me / her**
- They elected him ___. → **captain / chairman**
- He made her ___. → **happy / sad** (object complement)
:::

### 36.3.10 Missing Words in Fixed Expressions

::: ex
- In spite ___ the rain → **of**
- On account ___ illness → **of**
- With a view ___ getting a job → **to**
- As soon ___ he came → **as**
- By dint ___ hard work → **of**
- At the eleventh ___ → **hour**
- Once in a blue ___ → **moon**
- A blessing in ___ → **disguise**
:::

## 36.4 Missing Words That Change the Meaning

Some omissions do not make the sentence ungrammatical but change its meaning — exam questions ask you to restore the intended meaning:

::: ex
- *He has few friends.* (almost none) vs *He has **a** few friends.* (some) — the missing ***a***.
- *He is the only one of the boys who **has** passed.* vs *He is one of the boys who **have** passed.* — missing ***only*** changes agreement.
- *She is taller than any girl in the class.* → missing ***other*** makes the comparison illogical.
- *I don't like tea or coffee.* vs *I don't like tea **and** coffee.* (together)
:::

## 36.6 Missing Content Words: SAT and GRE Sentence Completion

In **GRE Text Completion**, **GRE Sentence Equivalence** and **SAT** vocabulary-in-context questions, the missing word is a **content word** (noun, verb, adjective, adverb). It cannot be found by grammar alone; it must be **predicted from context**.

### 36.6.1 Strategy

::: rule
1. **Cover the options.** Read the sentence and find the **clue** — the part that tells you what the blank means.
2. **Find the trigger (signal) words** that show the relationship between the clue and the blank:
   - **Same direction (support):** *and, also, moreover, indeed, in fact, because, since, thus, so, as a result, ; (semicolon), : (colon)*.
   - **Opposite direction (contrast):** *but, yet, however, although, though, despite, while, whereas, rather than, instead of, unlike, surprisingly, ironically, paradoxically*.
3. **Predict your own word** for the blank before looking at the options.
4. **Match** your prediction with the options; check that the chosen word fits **grammatically** and **logically**.
5. In **two- and three-blank** GRE questions, start with the **easiest** blank and use it to unlock the others.
6. In **Sentence Equivalence**, choose **two** options that give sentences of the **same meaning** — usually two near-synonyms.
:::

### 36.6.2 Examples

::: ex
- *Although the lecture was supposed to be **lively**, most students found it ________.* — contrast (*although*) → predict "boring" → *tedious, monotonous, soporific*.
- *The scientist's claims were ________; every experiment confirmed them.* — support (semicolon, explanation) → predict "proven" → *corroborated, substantiated, vindicated*.
- *Far from being ________, the new manager was surprisingly generous with praise.* — *far from being* = opposite of *generous with praise* → *stingy, parsimonious, niggardly* (with praise: *grudging*).
- *His ________ remarks offended many, though he insisted he had meant no harm.* — offensive remarks → *caustic, acerbic, tactless, derogatory*.
- *The novel's plot is so ________ that readers often lose track of who is who.* — result (*so … that*) → *convoluted, labyrinthine, intricate*.
- *Rather than ________ the new policy, the committee chose to praise it publicly.* — opposite of *praise* → *criticise, censure, denounce, disparage*.
:::

::: tip
**Exam Tip — Positive or negative?** Even if you don't know the exact word, decide whether the blank needs a **positive (+)** or **negative (−)** word. This alone often eliminates half the options. Learn the vocabulary in Chapter 45 by its **"charge"** as well as its meaning.
:::

## 36.7 Missing Words in GMAT Sentence Correction

::: gre
GMAT frequently tests **omitted words** that cause ambiguity or illogic:

- **Omitted *that*** after verbs like *claim, say, believe, hypothesise*: ✘ *Scientists believe the rising temperatures will continue.* is acceptable; but ✘ *Analysts reported last year sales fell* is ambiguous (*last year* modifies *reported* or *fell*?). ✔ *Analysts reported **that** last year sales fell.*
- **Omitted verb in comparisons** (ellipsis) that causes ambiguity: ✘ *She likes him more than her brother.* → ✔ *… more than her brother **does*** / *… more than she likes her brother*.
- **Omitted *as* in *as … as***: ✘ *as good or better than* → ✔ *as good **as** or better than*.
- **Omitted preposition or article in parallel lists** when they differ: *interested **in** art and **in** music* (repeat only if needed for clarity).
- **Omitted verb form** that leaves an incomplete verb: ✘ *The company has and will continue to expand.* ✔ *The company has **expanded** and will continue to expand.*
:::
