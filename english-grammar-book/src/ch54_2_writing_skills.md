## 54.5 Writing Skills

Written English papers in BCS, Bank and admission tests ask for **paragraphs, essays, letters/applications, reports, précis/summaries, amplifications** and sometimes **dialogues** and **emails**. The same skills are tested in **IELTS Writing**, the **GRE Analytical Writing** section and the **GMAT** (where available). Good writing is **clear, organised, correct and relevant**.

### 54.5.1 The Paragraph

A **paragraph** is a group of related sentences that develop **one main idea**.

::: rule
**Structure of a good paragraph**

1. **Topic sentence** — states the main idea (usually the first sentence).
2. **Supporting sentences** — explain, give reasons, facts, examples (usually 5–8 sentences).
3. **Concluding sentence** — restates the idea or draws a conclusion.

**Qualities:** *unity* (only one main idea), *coherence* (logical order, linking words), *completeness* (enough support), *correctness* (grammar, spelling).
:::

**Model paragraph — "Traffic Jam in Dhaka City":**

*Traffic jam has become one of the most serious problems of Dhaka city. Every day, millions of people waste hours on the roads because the number of vehicles far exceeds the capacity of the streets. Unplanned urbanisation, narrow roads, illegal parking, the lack of an efficient public transport system and the violation of traffic rules by drivers and pedestrians are the main causes. As a result, office-goers and students cannot reach their destinations on time, fuel is wasted, and the air becomes polluted. Patients in ambulances sometimes die before reaching hospital. To solve this problem, the government should expand the metro rail and bus rapid transit networks, remove illegal structures from footpaths, and enforce traffic laws strictly. Citizens too must follow the rules. Only through coordinated efforts can Dhaka become a liveable city again.*

### 54.5.2 The Essay

An **essay** is a longer piece of writing on a topic, organised into several paragraphs. BCS Written English includes an essay (often 30 marks); admission tests and IELTS/GRE also require essays.

::: rule
**Structure**

1. **Introduction** — a hook (striking fact, question, quotation, proverb), background, and a clear **thesis statement** (your main argument).
2. **Body paragraphs** (3–6) — each with a topic sentence, explanation, evidence/examples, and a link to the thesis.
3. **Conclusion** — restate the thesis in new words, summarise key points, and end with a recommendation, prediction or thought-provoking line.
:::

**Types of essays:**

| Type | Purpose | Example topics |
|---|---|---|
| Descriptive | describe a person, place, event | *A Visit to the Sundarbans; The Padma Bridge* |
| Narrative | tell a story or experience | *A Memorable Day in My Life* |
| Expository / Informative | explain a topic | *Climate Change and Bangladesh; The Role of Remittance in Our Economy* |
| Argumentative / Persuasive | take a position and defend it | *Should Private Tuition Be Banned?; Social Media: Boon or Bane?* |
| Analytical (GRE "Issue" and "Argument") | analyse an issue or critique an argument's logic | *"Governments should invest more in the arts than in sport."* |

**Sample outline — "Climate Change and Bangladesh":**

1. **Introduction:** Bangladesh — one of the most climate-vulnerable countries; thesis: climate change threatens lives, economy and food security, but adaptation and global cooperation can reduce the damage.
2. **Causes of climate change:** greenhouse gas emissions, deforestation (global context).
3. **Impact on Bangladesh:** sea-level rise and salinity in the coastal belt; stronger cyclones; floods and river erosion; drought in the north-west; climate migrants moving to cities.
4. **Economic and social effects:** loss of crops and livelihoods; health risks; pressure on Dhaka.
5. **Measures taken:** Bangladesh Climate Change Strategy and Action Plan, cyclone shelters, embankments, coastal afforestation, the Delta Plan 2100, community early warning.
6. **What more is needed:** climate finance from developed countries, renewable energy, research on saline-tolerant crops, awareness.
7. **Conclusion:** Bangladesh cannot stop climate change alone, but by adapting wisely and pressing for global justice it can protect its people.

**Useful essay expressions:**

| Purpose | Expressions |
|---|---|
| Introducing a topic | *In recent years …; It is widely acknowledged that …; There is no denying that …* |
| Giving opinion | *In my view …; I firmly believe that …; It seems to me that …* |
| Adding points | *Moreover, Furthermore, In addition, Besides, What is more* |
| Contrasting | *However, Nevertheless, On the other hand, In contrast, Although* |
| Giving examples | *For example, For instance, such as, To illustrate* |
| Showing cause / effect | *Because of, Due to, As a result, Consequently, Therefore, Hence* |
| Conceding | *Admittedly, It is true that … but; Granted that …* |
| Concluding | *In conclusion, To sum up, All things considered, In a nutshell* |

::: tip
**BCS essay tips:** Write a **clear outline** first (examiners value organisation); use **headings** if the question allows; include **relevant facts and figures** about Bangladesh (latest government data, policies, achievements such as the Padma Bridge, metro rail, the SDGs and LDC graduation); give **balanced views** on controversial topics; and **leave time to proofread**.
:::

### 54.5.3 Formal Letters and Applications

Formal letters (applications, letters to the editor, complaints, requests) are common in Bank and BCS written tests.

**Format of a formal letter (block style, British convention):**

::: ex
- **Date** — *22 October 2026*
- **Recipient's designation and address** — *The Managing Director, XYZ Bank Limited, Motijheel, Dhaka-1000*
- **Subject line** — *Subject: Application for the post of Senior Officer*
- **Salutation** — *Dear Sir / Dear Madam* (if the name is unknown); *Dear Mr Rahman* (if known)
- **Body** — opening paragraph (purpose), middle paragraph(s) (details, qualifications, request), closing paragraph (action requested, thanks)
- **Complimentary close** — *Yours faithfully* (after *Dear Sir/Madam*); *Yours sincerely* (after a name)
- **Signature and name**
- **Enclosures** (if any) — *Enclosures: CV, copies of certificates*
:::

**Model job application:**

*22 October 2026*

*The Managing Director*\
*XYZ Bank Limited*\
*Motijheel, Dhaka-1000*

*Subject: Application for the post of Senior Officer (General)*

*Dear Sir,*

*In response to your advertisement published in The Daily Star on 15 October 2026, I wish to apply for the post of Senior Officer (General) in your bank.*

*I obtained my MBA (Finance) from the University of Dhaka with a CGPA of 3.70, after completing my BBA in Finance and Banking. For the last two years, I have been working as a Junior Executive at ABC Finance Limited, where I deal with credit analysis, customer service and loan monitoring. I am proficient in MS Office and core banking software, and I can communicate fluently in both Bangla and English.*

*I believe that my academic background and practical experience will enable me to contribute effectively to your organisation. I would be grateful if you would give me an opportunity to appear for an interview.*

*Yours faithfully,*\
*(Signature)*\
*Md. Rakibul Hasan*\
*House 12, Road 5, Dhanmondi, Dhaka-1205*\
*Mobile: 01XXXXXXXXX; Email: rakib@example.com*

*Enclosures: Curriculum vitae, copies of certificates and testimonials, two passport-size photographs*

**Model letter to the editor:**

*To*\
*The Editor*\
*The Daily Star*\
*Dhaka*

*Subject: Need for safe drinking water in the coastal areas*

*Sir,*

*Through your widely read daily, I would like to draw the attention of the authorities concerned to the acute crisis of safe drinking water in the coastal districts of Khulna and Satkhira.*

*[Body: describe the problem, its causes (salinity, damaged ponds after cyclones), its effects (diseases, women walking miles for water), and suggest solutions (rainwater harvesting, desalination plants, pond-sand filters).]*

*I hope the authorities will take immediate steps to solve the problem.*

*Yours faithfully,*\
*(Name, address)*

::: note
**Informal letters** (to friends and family) use the writer's address and date at the top right, a friendly salutation (*Dear Rina*), a conversational style and closings such as *Yours affectionately / With love / Your loving brother*. **Never** use *Yours faithfully* in an informal letter.
:::

### 54.5.4 Emails

Professional emails are now tested in IELTS-type tasks, Bank exams and real job settings.

::: ex
- **Subject line:** short and specific — *Request for leave on 25–27 October*
- **Greeting:** *Dear Mr Karim, / Dear Hiring Manager,*
- **Opening:** state the purpose at once — *I am writing to request …*
- **Body:** short paragraphs, polite and clear
- **Closing line:** *I look forward to hearing from you. / Please let me know if you need any further information.*
- **Sign-off:** *Best regards, / Kind regards, / Sincerely,* + name, designation, contact
- **Etiquette:** no slang, no all-capital letters, check attachments, reply within a working day
:::

### 54.5.5 Reports

**Newspaper report** (a common task in BCS/HSC):

1. **Headline** — short, present tense, without articles (*Fire Guts 50 Shops in Old Dhaka*).
2. **By-line and dateline** — *Staff Correspondent, Dhaka, 21 October*.
3. **Lead (first paragraph)** — answers the **5 Ws and 1 H**: *What, Who, When, Where, Why, How*.
4. **Body** — details in order of importance (inverted pyramid), quotations from witnesses/officials.
5. **Conclusion** — present situation, actions taken.

**Formal report** (for an office or committee): *Title; To/From/Date; Introduction (purpose); Findings; Analysis; Recommendations; Conclusion.*

### 54.5.6 Précis and Summary

A **précis** is a **shortened version** of a passage (usually **one-third** of the original length) that keeps all the **main ideas** in the writer's own words.

::: rule
**Rules for précis writing**

1. Read the passage two or three times; find the **central idea**.
2. Note the **main points**; omit examples, illustrations, repetitions, quotations and figures of speech.
3. Write in **your own words**, in **indirect speech** and the **third person**, usually in the **past tense** for narratives.
4. Keep to about **one-third** of the original length (state the word count if required).
5. Give a **suitable title**.
6. Do not add your own opinion.
:::

**Example.** *Original (approx. 120 words):* "Books are the best companions of man. They are always ready to give us company and never leave us in times of trouble. A good book entertains us when we are bored, consoles us when we are sad and guides us when we are confused. Through books we can travel to distant lands, meet great thinkers of the past and learn about different cultures without leaving our room. Friends may betray us, wealth may desert us, but books remain faithful for ever. That is why great men have always loved books and spent their leisure in reading. A person who reads good books never feels lonely."

*Précis (approx. 40 words) — Title: Books, Our Best Friends:* "Books are man's most faithful companions. They entertain, console and guide us, and let us explore distant places, cultures and great minds. Unlike friends or wealth, they never desert us; so great men love reading and readers never feel lonely."

### 54.5.7 Amplification

**Amplification** is the **opposite** of précis: a short saying, proverb or line of poetry is **expanded** into a paragraph or two by explaining its meaning, giving examples and drawing a conclusion.

::: rule
**Steps:** (1) Explain the **literal meaning**; (2) explain the **deeper/figurative meaning**; (3) give **examples** from life, history or nature; (4) **conclude** with the lesson.
:::

**Example — "Industry is the key to success."**

*Industry means hard work and steady effort. Just as a key opens a locked door, hard work opens the door to success. No one achieves anything worthwhile without labour; talent alone is not enough. History is full of examples: Thomas Edison failed thousands of times before he invented a practical electric bulb, and he said that genius is one per cent inspiration and ninety-nine per cent perspiration. In our own country, millions of farmers and garment workers have changed the economy through tireless labour. On the other hand, a lazy person, however talented, wastes his gifts and lags behind. A student who studies regularly does well in examinations, while one who depends on luck is often disappointed. Therefore, if we want to succeed in life, we must be industrious and never shirk hard work.*

### 54.5.8 Dialogue Writing

A **dialogue** is a written conversation between two or more people. Exams ask for dialogues such as *between two friends about the importance of reading newspapers*, *between a doctor and a patient*, *between you and a bank officer about opening an account*.

::: rule
**Tips:** (1) Write the speaker's name or initial before each line followed by a colon. (2) Begin with a natural greeting and end with a natural closing. (3) Keep each turn short and realistic. (4) Make the conversation **develop the topic** with facts and opinions. (5) Use polite forms (*Could you …? Would you mind …?*), contractions and natural expressions.
:::

**Model — a customer and a bank officer:**

*Customer: Good morning. I would like to open a savings account.*\
*Officer: Good morning. Please have a seat. May I know whether you are a salaried person or a student?*\
*Customer: I am a university student.*\
*Officer: Then you may open a student savings account. You will need a copy of your national ID card or birth certificate, your student ID card, two passport-size photographs and a nominee's photograph and ID.*\
*Customer: Is there any minimum balance?*\
*Officer: For student accounts, the initial deposit is only one hundred taka, and there is no maintenance charge.*\
*Customer: That's great. Can I use mobile banking with this account?*\
*Officer: Certainly. Once the account is active, you can register for our app and internet banking.*\
*Customer: Thank you very much for your help.*\
*Officer: You're welcome. Please fill in this form and I will process it right away.*

### 54.5.9 Curriculum Vitae (CV) / Résumé

A CV for a job application in Bangladesh usually contains:

1. **Personal details** — name, address, phone, email (photo is common in Bangladesh, not in the USA/UK).
2. **Career objective** — two or three lines suited to the job.
3. **Education** — degrees in reverse order with institution, year, result.
4. **Work experience** — position, organisation, period, key responsibilities and achievements (reverse order).
5. **Skills** — computer, language, technical and soft skills.
6. **Training, certifications, extracurricular activities.**
7. **References** — two persons (names, designations, contacts) or *"Available on request"*.

Keep it **one or two pages**, consistent in format, free of errors, and tailored to the post.
