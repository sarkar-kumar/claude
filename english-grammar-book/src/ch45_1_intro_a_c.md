## 45.1 What Are Synonyms and Antonyms?

A **synonym** is a word that has the **same or nearly the same meaning** as another word in a particular context. *Big* and *large*, *begin* and *commence*, *brave* and *courageous* are synonyms.

An **antonym** is a word that has the **opposite meaning** of another word. *Big* and *small*, *begin* and *end*, *brave* and *cowardly* are antonyms.

In the Preliminary tests of BCS, in Bank recruitment tests (Officer, Senior Officer, Cash, AD of Bangladesh Bank), in university admission tests and in the verbal sections of the SAT, GRE and GMAT, vocabulary is tested directly (*"Choose the word closest in meaning to …"* or *"The antonym of … is"*) and indirectly through sentence completion, reading comprehension, analogy and one-word substitution. A strong vocabulary therefore pays off across many sections at once.

### 45.1.1 Perfect Synonyms Are Rare

Very few words mean **exactly** the same thing in every context. Most synonyms differ in **degree**, **tone (connotation)**, **formality** or **usage**:

- **Degree:** *dislike → hate → abhor → loathe* (each is stronger than the last). *Happy → glad → elated → ecstatic.*
- **Connotation (positive / negative feeling):** *thrifty* (good: careful with money) vs *stingy / miserly* (bad: unwilling to spend). *Confident* (good) vs *arrogant* (bad). *Curious* (neutral/good) vs *nosy* (bad).
- **Formality:** *buy* (everyday) vs *purchase* (formal); *begin* vs *commence*; *end* vs *terminate*.
- **Usage / collocation:** We say *a large amount* but *a big mistake*; *heavy rain*, not *strong rain*; *strong tea*, not *powerful tea*.

So, in an exam, you are not looking for a "perfect" synonym — you are looking for the option that is **closest** in meaning **in that context**.

### 45.1.2 Kinds of Antonyms

1. **Complementary (absolute) antonyms** — one excludes the other: *alive / dead, true / false, pass / fail, present / absent.*
2. **Gradable antonyms** — two ends of a scale with middle points: *hot / cold* (warm, cool in between), *rich / poor, young / old, large / small.*
3. **Relational (converse) antonyms** — one implies the other: *buy / sell, teacher / student, give / receive, husband / wife, above / below.*
4. **Antonyms made with prefixes** — *happy / unhappy, legal / illegal, possible / impossible, regular / irregular, honest / dishonest, sense / nonsense, understand / misunderstand.*

## 45.2 How to Solve Synonym and Antonym Questions

### 45.2.1 Ten Golden Strategies

1. **Know the part of speech.** A synonym must be the same part of speech as the question word. If the word is a verb (*abate*), the answer must be a verb (*diminish*), not a noun (*diminution*).
2. **Make a sentence.** Put the word into a short sentence of your own, then replace it with each option. The one that keeps the meaning is right. *The storm **abated**. → The storm **subsided**.* ✔
3. **Use roots, prefixes and suffixes.** *Bene-* = good (benevolent, benefactor); *mal-* = bad (malevolent, malice); *-cide* = killing (homicide); *omni-* = all (omnipotent); *loqu/locu* = speak (loquacious, eloquent); *chron* = time (chronic, chronology). (See Chapter 40 for a full list.)
4. **Decide the "charge".** Is the word positive (+) or negative (−)? A positive word cannot have a negative synonym, and its antonym must be negative. *Laudable* (+) → synonym *praiseworthy* (+); antonym *contemptible* (−).
5. **Eliminate wrong options.** Remove options that are unrelated, that are the wrong part of speech, or that are synonyms when the question asks for antonyms (a very common trap: test-makers deliberately place a synonym among the antonym options).
6. **Watch the "related but not equal" trap.** For *frugal*, the option *poor* is related but wrong; *thrifty* is correct. For *verbose*, the option *verbal* is a look-alike trap; *wordy* is correct.
7. **Look-alike (homonym) traps.** *Eminent* (famous) vs *imminent* (about to happen); *elicit* (draw out) vs *illicit* (illegal); *complement* vs *compliment*; *venal* (corrupt) vs *venial* (forgivable). (See Chapter 52.)
8. **Multiple meanings.** *Grave* = serious (adj) or tomb (n); *Fair* = just / light-coloured / an exhibition; *Sound* = healthy / noise / a narrow sea channel. Choose the meaning that matches the options.
9. **Know common "false friends".** Some words look as if they mean one thing but mean another (see 45.2.3).
10. **Read widely and revise often.** Reading an English daily (editorials), magazines and good novels, and revising word lists in small daily doses, is the only long-term solution.

::: tip
**Exam Tip — The "opposite" options trick:** In an MCQ for antonyms, if two options are opposites of each other (e.g. *praise* and *criticise*), the answer is very often one of them. In a synonym question, if two options mean the same thing as each other, usually **neither** is the answer (because only one answer can be right).
:::

### 45.2.2 Contronyms (Words That Are Their Own Opposites)

Some English words have two meanings that are opposite to each other. The GRE and SAT love them.

| Word | Meaning 1 | Meaning 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Sanction | to approve / permit | a penalty / to punish |
| Cleave | to split apart | to stick closely to |
| Oversight | supervision | a mistake of not noticing |
| Dust | to remove dust | to sprinkle powder on |
| Left | departed | remaining |
| Fast | moving quickly | fixed firmly, not moving |
| Bolt | to fasten / lock | to run away suddenly |
| Screen | to show (a film) | to hide / shield |
| Weather | to wear away | to come through safely |
| Trim | to cut off | to add decorations |
| Garnish | to decorate food | to take money (wages) by law |
| Fine | excellent | a penalty |
| Overlook | to supervise / look over | to fail to notice |
| Temper | to soften / moderate | to harden (steel) / anger |

### 45.2.4 Forming Antonyms with Prefixes and Suffixes

| Prefix / Suffix | Examples |
|---|---|
| un- | able–unable, fair–unfair, kind–unkind, known–unknown |
| in- | active–inactive, correct–incorrect, sane–insane, visible–invisible |
| im- (before b, m, p) | balance–imbalance, mature–immature, polite–impolite, possible–impossible |
| il- (before l) | legal–illegal, legible–illegible, literate–illiterate, logical–illogical |
| ir- (before r) | rational–irrational, regular–irregular, relevant–irrelevant, responsible–irresponsible |
| dis- | agree–disagree, honest–dishonest, obey–disobey, order–disorder |
| mis- | fortune–misfortune, understand–misunderstand, use–misuse |
| non- | sense–nonsense, violent–nonviolent, existent–nonexistent |
| anti- / counter- | social–antisocial, clockwise–anticlockwise, act–counteract |
| mal- | function–malfunction, nutrition–malnutrition, treat–maltreat |
| -ful / -less | careful–careless, useful–useless, hopeful–hopeless, meaningful–meaningless |

::: note
**Careful:** Not every *in-* word is negative. *Inflammable* = flammable; *invaluable* = extremely valuable (✘ not "without value"); *inhabit* = live in. And *valuable / invaluable* are near-synonyms, not antonyms — the antonym of *valuable* is *worthless*.
:::
