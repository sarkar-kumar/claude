## 15.1 What Is an Adverb?

An **adverb** is a word that **modifies** (adds to the meaning of) a **verb**, an **adjective**, or **another adverb**. It may also modify a preposition, a conjunction, a phrase, or a whole sentence. The name means "added to a verb" (*ad* + *verb*), because modifying verbs is its most common job.

::: ex
- She sings **beautifully**. (modifies the verb *sings* — how?)
- He is **very** tall. (modifies the adjective *tall*)
- He ran **quite** fast. (modifies the adverb *fast*)
- The bird flew **right** over my head. (modifies the preposition *over*)
- I like him **simply** because he is honest. (modifies the conjunction *because*)
- **Unfortunately**, the train was late. (modifies the whole sentence)
:::

Adverbs answer the questions ***how? when? where? how often? how much / to what extent? why?***

## 15.2 Kinds of Adverbs (by Meaning)

| Kind | Question | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Manner** | How? | quickly, slowly, well, badly, carefully, happily, so, thus, hard, fast |
| **Time** | When? | now, then, today, tomorrow, yesterday, soon, already, yet, still, lately, recently, before, ago, since, early, late |
| **Place** | Where? | here, there, everywhere, nowhere, up, down, in, out, near, far, above, below, away, abroad, inside, outside |
| **Frequency** | How often? | always, usually, generally, often, frequently, sometimes, occasionally, seldom, rarely, hardly ever, never, once, twice, again, daily |
| **Degree / Quantity** | How much? To what extent? | very, too, quite, rather, fairly, pretty, almost, nearly, enough, so, much, little, hardly, scarcely, barely, fully, entirely, extremely, partly |
| **Affirmation & Negation** | — | yes, no, not, certainly, surely, indeed, of course, never, by no means |
| **Reason / Result** | Why? | therefore, hence, consequently, thus, so, accordingly |
| **Interrogative** | (asks a question) | when? where? why? how? how much? how long? |
| **Relative** | (joins clauses, refers to an antecedent) | when, where, why, how (*This is the place **where** I was born.*) |
| **Sentence / Comment** | (comments on the whole sentence) | fortunately, luckily, clearly, obviously, frankly, honestly, surely, perhaps, certainly |

## 15.3 Formation of Adverbs

### 15.3.1 Adding *-ly* to Adjectives

| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| Most adjectives + ***-ly*** | quick → quickly, careful → carefully, bad → badly, sad → sadly, beautiful → beautifully |
| Consonant + *y* → ***-ily*** | happy → happily, easy → easily, busy → busily, angry → angrily, heavy → heavily |
| Final ***-le*** → ***-ly*** | simple → simply, gentle → gently, terrible → terribly, possible → possibly, able → ably |
| Final ***-ic*** → ***-ically*** | basic → basically, tragic → tragically, scientific → scientifically, dramatic → dramatically (exception: public → publicly) |
| Final ***-ue*** drops *e* | true → truly, due → duly |
| Final ***-ll*** adds only *-y* | full → fully, dull → dully |
| Exceptions | whole → wholly, shy → shyly, sly → slyly, dry → drily / dryly, day → daily, gay → gaily |

### 15.3.2 Adverbs with the Same Form as Adjectives

*fast, hard, late, early, high, low, long, near, far, straight, right, wrong, deep, daily, weekly, monthly, yearly, enough, well, ill, little, much, more, most, less, least, still, kindly, only, pretty, direct, free, clean, loud, sharp, tight, wide*

::: ex
- He is a **hard** worker. (adj) / He works **hard**. (adv)
- The train was **late**. (adj) / The train arrived **late**. (adv)
- a **fast** car (adj) / drives **fast** (adv) — ✘ *fastly* does not exist.
- a **straight** line (adj) / Go **straight**. (adv)
:::

### 15.3.3 Pairs with Different Meanings

Some adverbs have two forms, **with and without *-ly***, which mean **different things**. This is a frequently tested area.

| Without *-ly* | With *-ly* |
|---|---|
| **hard** = with effort: *He works **hard**.* | **hardly** = scarcely, almost not: *He **hardly** works.* |
| **late** = not early: *He came **late**.* | **lately** = recently: *I haven't seen him **lately**.* |
| **near** = close by: *Come **near**.* | **nearly** = almost: *I **nearly** fell.* |
| **high** = at a height: *The kite flew **high**.* | **highly** = very, greatly: *He is **highly** educated / **highly** respected.* |
| **short** = suddenly: *He stopped **short**.* | **shortly** = soon: *He will come **shortly**.* |
| **free** = without payment: *Children travel **free**.* | **freely** = without restriction: *Speak **freely**.* |
| **deep** = far down: *They dug **deep**.* | **deeply** = greatly: *I am **deeply** grateful.* |
| **wide** = fully: *The door was **wide** open.* | **widely** = in many places: *He has travelled **widely**.* |
| **pretty** = fairly: *It's **pretty** good.* | **prettily** = in a pretty way: *She was dressed **prettily**.* |
| **most** = superlative of *much*: *What I value **most** is honesty.* | **mostly** = mainly: *The audience were **mostly** students.* |
| **close** = near: *He sat **close** to me.* | **closely** = carefully: *Watch **closely**.* |
| **direct** = without stopping: *The plane flies **direct** to London.* | **directly** = immediately; straight: *He'll come **directly**.* |
| **right** = correctly, straight: *Turn **right**. You guessed **right**.* | **rightly** = justly, with good reason: *He was **rightly** punished.* |
| **wrong** = incorrectly: *You spelt it **wrong**.* | **wrongly** = unjustly, mistakenly: *He was **wrongly** accused.* |
| **just** = exactly, a moment ago: *He has **just** left.* | **justly** = fairly: *He was **justly** rewarded.* |
| **sharp** = exactly (time): *at 9 o'clock **sharp*** | **sharply** = suddenly, severely: *Prices rose **sharply**.* |

### 15.3.4 Other Ways of Forming Adverbs

- **Compound adverbs:** *somewhere, anywhere, nowhere, everywhere, sometimes, meanwhile, nevertheless, however, therefore, moreover, beforehand, afterwards, hereafter, whereby*.
- **With *a-*:** *abroad, ahead, aloud, asleep, away, afoot, aside, apart, alike, anew, ashore*.
- **With *-ward(s)*, *-wise***: *homeward(s), backward(s), forward(s), upward(s), clockwise, otherwise, likewise, lengthwise*.
- **Phrases used as adverbs (adverb phrases):** *now and then, again and again, by and by, at last, at once, in vain, of course, in short, by heart, on foot, all of a sudden, by no means*.

## 15.4 Comparison of Adverbs

| Type | Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|---|
| One-syllable (and *early*) | fast, hard, soon, late, early | faster, harder, sooner, later, earlier | fastest, hardest, soonest, latest, earliest |
| *-ly* adverbs | carefully, quickly, beautifully | more carefully, more quickly | most carefully, most quickly |
| Irregular | well | better | best |
| | badly, ill | worse | worst |
| | much | more | most |
| | little | less | least |
| | far | farther / further | farthest / furthest |

::: ex
- She sings **better** than her sister. (✘ more well)
- He works **harder** than anyone else.
- Drive **more carefully**. (✘ carefullier)
- Of all the students, Rina writes **most neatly**.
:::

## 15.5 Position of Adverbs

The position of an adverb affects both **grammar** and **meaning**. These are the general rules:

### 15.5.1 Adverbs of Manner, Place and Time — After the Verb (or Object)

Usually placed **after the verb**, or **after the object** if there is one. Never put an adverb between a verb and its direct object.

::: ex
- He did the work **carefully**. (✘ He did carefully the work.)
- She speaks English **fluently**. (✘ She speaks fluently English.)
- I met him **yesterday**.
:::

**Order when several come together — M P T (Manner → Place → Time):**

::: ex
- He spoke **eloquently** (M) **at the meeting** (P) **last night** (T).
- She went **to school** (P) **early** (T) **yesterday** (T).
- After verbs of movement, **Place** often comes first: *He went **home** (P) **quickly** (M) **after the class** (T).*
:::

### 15.5.2 Adverbs of Frequency — Before the Main Verb, After *be* and Auxiliaries

*always, usually, often, sometimes, seldom, rarely, never, ever, hardly, scarcely, generally, frequently, just, already, almost, also, only, still*:

::: ex
- He **always** comes on time. (before the main verb)
- He is **always** punctual. (after *be*)
- I have **never** seen a tiger. (after the first auxiliary)
- You can **always** call me. (after the modal)
- Do you **often** go there? (questions: after the subject)
- ✘ He comes always late. ✔ He **always** comes late.
- ✘ I never have been there. ✔ I have **never** been there.
:::

*Sometimes, usually, often* can also begin or end a sentence: ***Sometimes** I walk to work. / I walk to work **sometimes**.*

### 15.5.3 Adverbs of Degree — Before the Word They Modify

::: ex
- He is **very** clever. / She is **too** tired. / It is **quite** good. / It's **almost** finished.
- Exception: ***enough*** comes **after** the adjective or adverb: *He is **old enough**. She sings **well enough**.* (✘ enough old)
:::

### 15.5.4 Position of *Only*, *Even*, *Just*, *Also*, *Almost*

***Only*** should be placed **immediately before** the word it modifies. Moving it changes the meaning:

::: ex
- **Only** I met him yesterday. (No one else met him.)
- I **only** met him yesterday. (I didn't speak to him / do anything else.)
- I met **only** him yesterday. (I met no one else.)
- I met him **only** yesterday. (It was as recent as yesterday.)
- ✘ He only spent ten taka. (if you mean the amount) ✔ He spent **only** ten taka.
:::

The same is true of *even, just, almost, nearly, merely, scarcely, hardly*: *I **nearly** lost all my money* (I did not lose it) vs *I lost **nearly** all my money* (I lost most of it). Always put the word next to what it limits.

### 15.5.5 Sentence Adverbs

Adverbs that comment on the whole statement usually come **first**, followed by a comma: ***Fortunately**, nobody was hurt. **Frankly**, I don't like him. **Obviously**, he was lying.*

## 15.6 Correct Use of Some Important Adverbs

### 15.6.1 *Very* and *Much*

| ***very*** | ***much*** |
|---|---|
| with positive adjectives/adverbs: *very good, very fast* | with comparatives: *much better, much faster* |
| with present participles used as adjectives: *very interesting, very boring* | with past participles in passive sense: *much admired, much obliged, much talked of* |
| with past participles used as pure adjectives: *very tired, very pleased, very worried* | before *too*: *much too heavy* (✘ very too heavy) |
| with superlatives in the pattern *the very best* | before *the* + superlative: *much the best* |

::: ex
- ✘ He is very stronger than me. ✔ He is **much** stronger than I.
- ✘ The film was much interesting. ✔ The film was **very** interesting.
- ✘ This box is very too heavy. ✔ This box is **much / far** too heavy.
- ✔ I am **very much** obliged to you.
:::

### 15.6.2 *Too* and *Very*

***Very*** = to a high degree (no problem implied). ***Too*** = **more than enough / more than desirable**; it implies a **negative result**, often with *to*-infinitive.

::: ex
- It is **very** hot today. (just a statement)
- It is **too** hot to go out. (so we cannot go out)
- ✘ I am too glad to see you. ✔ I am **very** glad to see you. (*too* gives a negative sense — wrong for a pleasant meaning)
- *Too* = *also* at the end: *I like it **too**.*
- ✘ He is too much intelligent. ✔ He is **very** intelligent. (*too much* modifies nouns or verbs: *He talks **too much**.*)
:::

### 15.6.3 *Enough*

- After adjectives/adverbs: *He is **tall enough** to reach the shelf. She didn't run **fast enough**.*
- Before nouns (as an adjective): *We have **enough time / enough money**.*

### 15.6.4 *Already*, *Yet*, *Still*

| Adverb | Used in | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ***already*** | positive statements (and surprise questions) — something happened earlier than expected | *He has **already** left. Have you finished **already**?* |
| ***yet*** | negatives and questions — something expected has not happened | *He hasn't come **yet**. Has he come **yet**?* |
| ***still*** | continuation — something is continuing longer than expected | *He is **still** sleeping. He **still** hasn't paid me.* |

### 15.6.5 *Ago*, *Before*, *Since*

- ***ago*** = back from **now**; used with the **past simple**: *He left two years **ago**.* (✘ has left two years ago; ✘ ago since)
- ***before*** = back from a **past time**, or "at any time in the past": *He told me that he had left two years **before**. I have seen him **before**.*
- ***since*** = from a point of time until now; used with **perfect tenses**: *I have known him **since** 2015.*

::: ex
- ✘ He has come two days ago. ✔ He **came** two days ago.
- ✘ It is two years ago since I saw him. ✔ It is two years **since** I saw him. / I saw him two years **ago**.
:::

### 15.6.6 *Hardly*, *Scarcely*, *Barely*, *Seldom*, *Rarely*

These adverbs are **negative in meaning**, so they must **not** be used with another negative (*not, no, never, nothing*).

::: ex
- ✘ I can't hardly hear you. ✔ I can **hardly** hear you.
- ✘ He hardly never comes. ✔ He **hardly ever** comes.
- ✘ There was scarcely no food. ✔ There was **scarcely any** food.
- *Hardly … when* (✘ than): ***Hardly** had he arrived **when** it began to rain.* (Chapter 16)
:::

### 15.6.7 *So*, *Such*, *So … that*, *Too … to*

::: ex
- He is **so** weak **that** he cannot walk. = He is **too** weak **to** walk.
- She is **such** a kind lady **that** everyone loves her. = She is **so** kind a lady **that** …
- ✘ He is so weak to walk. ✔ He is **too** weak to walk / **so** weak **that** he cannot walk.
- ✘ It was such hot that we stayed in. ✔ It was **so** hot that we stayed in.
:::

### 15.6.8 *Else* and *Otherwise*

***Else*** follows *anybody, somebody, nobody, everybody, what, who, where, little, nothing*: *Ask **somebody else**. What **else** do you want?* — and is followed by ***but*** (✘ than): *It is nothing **else but** pride.* ***Or else*** = otherwise: *Hurry up, **or else** you'll miss the bus.*

### 15.6.9 *Fairly* and *Rather*

***Fairly*** is used with "good/positive" qualities; ***rather*** usually with "bad/negative" ones (or to express surprise): *The test was **fairly** easy. The test was **rather** difficult. He is **fairly** clever but **rather** lazy.*

### 15.6.10 *Ever* and *Never*

***Ever*** = at any time (questions, negatives, comparisons, superlatives); ***never*** = at no time: *Have you **ever** been to Nepal? This is the best film I have **ever** seen. I have **never** told a lie.* ✘ *I have not never seen …*

### 15.6.11 *Also*, *Too*, *As well*, *Either*

In **positive** sentences use *also / too / as well*; in **negative** sentences use ***either***: *He likes tea; I like it **too**.* / *He doesn't like tea; I don't like it **either**.* (✘ I don't like it too)

### 15.6.12 *Yes* and *No* in Answers to Negative Questions

English answers the **fact**, not the question: *"Didn't you go?" — "**Yes**, I did." / "**No**, I didn't."* (Bangla speakers often reverse these.)

## 15.7 Adjective or Adverb?

Use an **adjective** after **linking verbs** (*be, seem, look, feel, smell, taste, sound, become, grow, turn, remain, keep, prove*) because the word describes the subject. Use an **adverb** to describe **how an action is done** (Chapter 09.6).

::: ex
- The soup tastes **good**. (adj) / She cooks **well**. (adv)
- He looks **angry**. (adj) / He looked **angrily** at me. (adv)
- Keep **quiet**. (adj) / Work **quietly**. (adv)
- I feel **bad**. (adj) / He behaved **badly**. (adv)
- *Good* is an adjective; *well* is the adverb (and also an adjective meaning "healthy"): *He plays **well**. I am **well**, thank you.*
:::

## 15.8 Adverbs and Adverbial Conjunctions (Conjunctive Adverbs)

Words like ***however, therefore, moreover, furthermore, nevertheless, consequently, otherwise, besides, hence, thus, accordingly, meanwhile, instead, still*** are **adverbs**, not conjunctions. They cannot join two independent clauses with only a comma; use a **semicolon** or a **full stop**, or add a real conjunction.

::: ex
- ✘ He was ill, therefore he did not come. (comma splice)
- ✔ He was ill; **therefore**, he did not come.
- ✔ He was ill. **Therefore**, he did not come.
- ✔ He was ill, **and therefore** he did not come.
- ✔ He was ill, **so** he did not come. (*so* is a conjunction)
:::

## 15.9 Common Errors with Adverbs

::: errs
1. ✘ He speaks fluently English. ✔ He speaks English **fluently**.
2. ✘ He always is late. ✔ He is **always** late.
3. ✘ She sings very good. ✔ She sings very **well**.
4. ✘ The flower smells sweetly. ✔ The flower smells **sweet**.
5. ✘ He works very hardly. ✔ He works very **hard**.
6. ✘ He came lately to the office. ✔ He came **late** to the office.
7. ✘ He is very stronger than his brother. ✔ He is **much** stronger than his brother.
8. ✘ I am too happy to meet you. ✔ I am **very** happy to meet you.
9. ✘ He is enough strong. ✔ He is **strong enough**.
10. ✘ He hardly never goes out. ✔ He **hardly ever** goes out.
11. ✘ I can't scarcely believe it. ✔ I can **scarcely** believe it.
12. ✘ He has come two days ago. ✔ He **came** two days ago.
13. ✘ He has not finished his work already. ✔ He has not finished his work **yet**.
14. ✘ I don't like coffee too. ✔ I don't like coffee **either**.
15. ✘ He drives very fastly. ✔ He drives very **fast**.
16. ✘ She dresses beautiful. ✔ She dresses **beautifully**.
17. ✘ He did the sum easily and correct. ✔ … easily and **correctly**.
18. ✘ He is so weak to walk. ✔ He is **too** weak to walk.
19. ✘ It is nothing else than pride. ✔ It is nothing else **but** pride.
20. ✘ I only have five taka. (strict exam style) ✔ I have **only** five taka.
21. ✘ The old man walked slow. ✔ The old man walked **slowly**.
22. ✘ He returned back home. ✔ He **returned** home. (redundant *back*)
23. ✘ Seldom he comes here. ✔ **Seldom does** he come here. (Chapter 16)
24. ✘ He is much intelligent. ✔ He is **very** intelligent.
25. ✘ Nowadays, prices are going very high. ✔ Nowadays, prices are **rising** steeply. (better collocation)
:::

## 15.10 Adverbs in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
- **Adjective/adverb confusion (SAT):** ✘ *The new policy was implemented quick.* ✔ *… **quickly**.* ✘ *a surprising low score* ✔ *a **surprisingly** low score*.
- **Comma splice with conjunctive adverbs (SAT):** ✘ *The data were incomplete, however, the study was published.* ✔ *The data were incomplete; **however**, the study was published.*
- **Misplaced *only* (GMAT):** place limiting modifiers next to the word they limit.
- **GRE Text Completion:** signal adverbs show the logic of a sentence — ***however, nevertheless, yet, still*** (contrast); ***moreover, furthermore, indeed*** (support/extension); ***therefore, hence, thus, consequently*** (result); ***ironically, paradoxically*** (unexpected contrast). Identifying these "trigger words" is the single most powerful strategy for text completion.
:::
