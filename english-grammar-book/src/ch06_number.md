## 6.1 What Is Number?

**Number** is the form of a noun (or pronoun or verb) that shows whether we mean **one** or **more than one**.

- **Singular number** — one person or thing: *boy, book, child, mouse, crisis*.
- **Plural number** — more than one: *boys, books, children, mice, crises*.

Only **countable nouns** have both numbers. Uncountable nouns (Chapter 03) are grammatically singular. Pronouns (*I/we, he/they*) and present-tense verbs (*plays/play*) also show number, which is why number connects directly to subject-verb agreement (Chapter 23).

Plural formation is tested in BCS, Bank and admission tests in two ways: (1) "*What is the plural of …?*" — especially foreign words like *crisis, datum, criterion, phenomenon, memorandum*; and (2) error detection with words like *sheeps, informations, two dozens, sons-in-laws*.

## 6.2 Regular Plurals

### 6.2.1 Add *-s*

Most nouns form their plural by adding ***-s***: *book → books, girl → girls, tree → trees, house → houses, pen → pens*.

### 6.2.2 Add *-es* after *s, ss, sh, ch, x, z*

| Ending | Examples |
|---|---|
| -s, -ss | bus → buses, gas → gases, class → classes, glass → glasses, kiss → kisses |
| -sh | brush → brushes, dish → dishes, bush → bushes |
| -ch (sounding "ch") | bench → benches, church → churches, match → matches, watch → watches |
| -x | box → boxes, fox → foxes, tax → taxes |
| -z | quiz → quizzes, buzz → buzzes, waltz → waltzes |

::: note
When *-ch* sounds like **"k"**, add only ***-s***: *stomach → stomachs, monarch → monarchs, epoch → epochs, patriarch → patriarchs, matriarch → matriarchs, eunuch → eunuchs*.
:::

### 6.2.3 Nouns Ending in *-o*

| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| Consonant + *o* → usually ***-es*** | hero → heroes, potato → potatoes, tomato → tomatoes, echo → echoes, cargo → cargoes, mosquito → mosquitoes, buffalo → buffaloes, volcano → volcanoes, torpedo → torpedoes, veto → vetoes, mango → mangoes (also *mangos*), motto → mottoes (also *mottos*), embargo → embargoes |
| Vowel + *o* → ***-s*** | radio → radios, studio → studios, ratio → ratios, zoo → zoos, bamboo → bamboos, kangaroo → kangaroos, video → videos, cuckoo → cuckoos, cameo → cameos, folio → folios, portfolio → portfolios |
| Short forms, musical and foreign words → ***-s*** | photo → photos, kilo → kilos, piano → pianos, solo → solos, soprano → sopranos, canto → cantos, memo → memos, logo → logos, dynamo → dynamos, auto → autos, euro → euros, casino → casinos, ghetto → ghettos, Eskimo → Eskimos, commando → commandos |

::: tip
**Memory line for *-oes*:** "**Heroes** ate **potatoes** and **tomatoes** while **mosquitoes** buzzed and **volcanoes** gave **echoes** to **buffaloes**." Most other *-o* words take *-s*.
:::

### 6.2.4 Nouns Ending in *-y*

| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| Consonant + *y* → change *y* to ***-ies*** | city → cities, lady → ladies, baby → babies, army → armies, story → stories, fly → flies, country → countries, duty → duties, enemy → enemies, family → families |
| Vowel + *y* → add ***-s*** | boy → boys, day → days, key → keys, monkey → monkeys, donkey → donkeys, valley → valleys, chimney → chimneys, toy → toys, journey → journeys, attorney → attorneys, alley → alleys, storey → storeys |
| Proper names → add ***-s*** | the two Marys, the Kennedys, the Murphys |

::: note
Words ending in *-quy* follow the consonant rule because *u* is pronounced like *w*: *soliloquy → soliloquies, colloquy → colloquies*. *Money* has two plurals: *moneys* and *monies* (= sums of money, used in law and accounts).
:::

### 6.2.5 Nouns Ending in *-f* or *-fe*

| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| Change *f/fe* to ***-ves*** | knife → knives, wife → wives, life → lives, leaf → leaves, loaf → loaves, half → halves, calf → calves, self → selves, shelf → shelves, thief → thieves, wolf → wolves, sheaf → sheaves, elf → elves |
| Add only ***-s*** | roof → roofs, chief → chiefs, proof → proofs, belief → beliefs, grief → griefs, gulf → gulfs, cliff → cliffs, reef → reefs, safe → safes, chef → chefs, serf → serfs, brief → briefs, handkerchief → handkerchiefs, strife → strifes, fife → fifes, staff → staffs (groups of employees) |
| Both forms correct | scarf → scarfs / scarves, hoof → hoofs / hooves, wharf → wharfs / wharves, dwarf → dwarfs / dwarves |

## 6.3 Irregular Plurals

### 6.3.1 Vowel Change (Mutation Plurals)

| Singular | Plural | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| man | men | goose | geese |
| woman | women | mouse | mice |
| foot | feet | louse | lice |
| tooth | teeth | dormouse | dormice |

Compounds follow the same pattern: *Englishman → Englishmen, policeman → policemen, fireman → firemen, chairman → chairmen*. But *German → Germans, Roman → Romans, human → humans, talisman → talismans, Mussulman → Mussulmans* (these are not compounds of *man*).

### 6.3.2 Plurals in *-en*

*ox → oxen, child → children, brother → brethren* (members of a religious community or society; otherwise *brothers*).

### 6.3.3 Same Form in Singular and Plural

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| Animals (hunting/fishing tradition) | sheep, deer, swine, fish (usually), salmon, trout, cod, carp, grouse, bison, moose |
| Craft | aircraft, spacecraft, hovercraft, watercraft |
| Words in *-s* | series, species, means, headquarters, crossroads, barracks, gallows, works (factory), innings (cricket, BrE) |
| Nationalities in *-ese* / *-ss* | Chinese, Japanese, Portuguese, Vietnamese, Swiss |
| Numbers/measures after a number | dozen, score, hundred, thousand, million, gross, stone (weight), head (of cattle), pair (informally), yoke (of oxen) |

::: ex
- **One sheep is** grazing; **ten sheep are** grazing. (✘ sheeps)
- **A species** of bird **is** extinct. **Many species are** endangered.
- **This series** of lectures **is** useful. **Two series** of stamps **were** issued.
- *Fish* vs *fishes*: *I caught ten **fish*** (number of individual fish). *The aquarium has many **fishes*** (= many species of fish).
- ***Two dozen** eggs* (✘ two dozens eggs); ***five hundred** taka*; ***fifty head of** cattle*; but ***dozens of** eggs, **hundreds of** people, **millions of** dollars* (no number before them).
:::

## 6.4 Plurals of Compound Nouns

::: rule
In a compound noun, make the **principal (most important) word** plural. If no word is clearly the principal one, add the plural ending at the **end**.
:::

| Type | Singular → Plural |
|---|---|
| Noun + preposition phrase (first word plural) | son-in-law → **sons**-in-law; daughter-in-law → **daughters**-in-law; mother-in-law → **mothers**-in-law; father-in-law → **fathers**-in-law; brother-in-law → **brothers**-in-law; commander-in-chief → **commanders**-in-chief; man-of-war → **men**-of-war; coat of mail → **coats** of mail; editor-in-chief → **editors**-in-chief |
| Noun + adverb/particle (noun plural) | passer-by → **passers**-by; looker-on → **lookers**-on; hanger-on → **hangers**-on; runner-up → **runners**-up |
| Noun + adjective (noun plural) | court-martial → **courts**-martial; attorney-general → **attorneys**-general (also *attorney-generals*); poet laureate → **poets** laureate; notary public → **notaries** public; heir apparent → **heirs** apparent |
| Noun + noun (last word plural) | step-son → step-**sons**; maid-servant → maid-**servants**; bus stop → bus **stops**; schoolboy → school**boys**; toothbrush → tooth**brushes** |
| *Man / Woman* + noun (both plural) | **man**-servant → **men**-servants; **woman** doctor → **women** doctors; **woman** teacher → **women** teachers; **lady** doctor → **lady** doctors (only last) |
| No noun inside / verb-based (add -s at end) | forget-me-not → forget-me-**nots**; grown-up → grown-**ups**; go-between → go-**betweens**; drawback → draw**backs**; breakdown → break**downs**; take-off → take-**offs**; merry-go-round → merry-go-**rounds**; stand-by → stand-**bys** |
| Words in *-ful* (add -s at end) | spoonful → spoon**fuls**; handful → hand**fuls**; mouthful → mouth**fuls**; cupful → cup**fuls**; bucketful → bucket**fuls** (✘ spoonsful is old-fashioned) |

## 6.5 Plurals of Foreign (Classical) Words

Many words from **Latin, Greek, French, Italian** and **Hebrew** keep their original plurals. These are the **most tested** plurals in Bangladesh and in GRE/GMAT reading passages. Some have a regular English plural as well; where both exist, the meanings may differ.

### 6.5.1 Latin *-us* → *-i*

| Singular | Plural | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| alumnus | alumni | radius | radii |
| bacillus | bacilli | stimulus | stimuli |
| cactus | cacti / cactuses | syllabus | syllabi / syllabuses |
| focus | foci / focuses | terminus | termini / terminuses |
| fungus | fungi / funguses | locus | loci |
| nucleus | nuclei | magus | magi |

::: note
**Exceptions to *-us → -i*:** *genus → genera, corpus → corpora, opus → opera, viscus → viscera*. And many *-us* words take the English plural only: *bonus → bonuses, campus → campuses, census → censuses, chorus → choruses, circus → circuses, virus → viruses, genius → geniuses, octopus → octopuses, prospectus → prospectuses, status → statuses, sinus → sinuses, apparatus → apparatus / apparatuses*.
:::

### 6.5.2 Latin *-a* → *-ae*

*alumna → alumnae, formula → formulae / formulas, larva → larvae, vertebra → vertebrae, antenna → antennae (insects) / antennas (radio), nebula → nebulae, minutia → minutiae, persona → personae*. (But *area → areas, idea → ideas, arena → arenas, dilemma → dilemmas*.)

### 6.5.3 Latin *-um* → *-a*

| Singular | Plural | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| datum | data | medium | media / mediums |
| bacterium | bacteria | memorandum | memoranda / memorandums |
| curriculum | curricula / curriculums | millennium | millennia / millenniums |
| erratum | errata | stratum | strata |
| addendum | addenda | symposium | symposia / symposiums |
| agendum | agenda | referendum | referenda / referendums |
| maximum | maxima / maximums | minimum | minima / minimums |
| quantum | quanta | spectrum | spectra |
| ovum | ova | stadium | stadia / stadiums |
| aquarium | aquaria / aquariums | consortium | consortia |

::: note
***Agenda*** is now used as a **singular** noun in English (*The agenda **is** long; two agendas*). ***Data*** is plural in strict formal usage (*These data **are** reliable*) but is widely used as singular (*The data **is** reliable*); in GMAT/GRE contexts, treat *data* as plural. ***Media*** (newspapers, TV) is plural in strict usage: *The media **are** responsible* — singular use is common. ***Bacteria***, ***criteria***, ***phenomena*** are **always plural**: ✘ *a bacteria*, ✘ *this criteria* → ✔ *a bacterium*, ✔ *this criterion*.
:::

### 6.5.4 Greek *-is* → *-es*

| Singular | Plural | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| analysis | analyses | hypothesis | hypotheses |
| axis | axes | oasis | oases |
| basis | bases | parenthesis | parentheses |
| crisis | crises | synopsis | synopses |
| diagnosis | diagnoses | synthesis | syntheses |
| ellipsis | ellipses | thesis | theses |
| emphasis | emphases | neurosis | neuroses |
| prognosis | prognoses | metamorphosis | metamorphoses |

### 6.5.5 Greek *-on* → *-a*

*criterion → criteria, phenomenon → phenomena, automaton → automata / automatons, polyhedron → polyhedra*. (But *electron → electrons, proton → protons, marathon → marathons*.)

### 6.5.6 Latin *-ex / -ix* → *-ices*

*index → indices (mathematics) / indexes (of books), appendix → appendices (of books) / appendixes (anatomy), matrix → matrices, vertex → vertices, vortex → vortices, apex → apices / apexes, codex → codices, helix → helices*.

### 6.5.7 Greek *-ma* → *-mata*

*dogma → dogmata / dogmas, stigma → stigmata (religious) / stigmas, schema → schemata / schemas, stoma → stomata, lemma → lemmata / lemmas*.

### 6.5.8 French, Italian and Hebrew Plurals

| Origin | Singular → Plural |
|---|---|
| French *-eau → -eaux* | bureau → bureaux / bureaus, plateau → plateaux / plateaus, tableau → tableaux, chateau → chateaux, beau → beaux, portmanteau → portmanteaux |
| French others | madame → mesdames, monsieur → messieurs, corps → corps, chassis → chassis, rendezvous → rendezvous |
| Italian *-o → -i* | bandit → banditti / bandits, libretto → libretti, virtuoso → virtuosi, graffito → graffiti, dilettante → dilettanti, paparazzo → paparazzi, tempo → tempi / tempos |
| Hebrew *-im* | cherub → cherubim (angels) / cherubs (babies in art), seraph → seraphim, kibbutz → kibbutzim |

## 6.8 Plurals of Letters, Figures, Abbreviations and Names

::: ex
- **Letters:** *Mind your **p's and q's**. Dot your **i's** and cross your **t's**.* (apostrophe used for clarity with small letters)
- **Figures:** *Write your **7s** clearly. In the **1990s**.* (*7's* and *1990's* are older forms; modern style prefers no apostrophe)
- **Abbreviations:** *two **MPs**, three **MAs**, several **NGOs**, many **CEOs*** (✘ NGO's for a plural in modern style)
- **Proper names:** *There are two **Rahims** in our class. **The Khans** live next door. Three **Marys** and two **Kennedys**.*
- **Titles:** *Mr → **Messrs** (Messrs Rahman & Co.); Mrs → **Mesdames**; Miss Rahman → **the Misses** Rahman* (formal) or *the Miss **Rahmans*** (informal); *Dr → Drs*.
:::

## 6.9 Number of Nouns in Special Constructions

::: ex
1. **Noun after a number used as an adjective stays singular:** *a **ten-taka** note, a **five-year** plan, a **two-mile** walk, a **three-day** holiday, a **six-month** course* (✘ a ten-takas note, ✘ a five-years plan).
2. **"One of the" + plural noun:** *He is **one of the best players*** (✘ best player).
3. **"More than one" + singular noun + singular verb:** ***More than one student was** absent.* (but *More students than one **were** absent*.)
4. **"One and a half" + plural noun:** ***one and a half hours*** (✘ hour) / ***an hour and a half***.
5. **"A pair of" / "a couple of":** *a pair of shoes **is** …* (verb with *pair*); *a couple of days*.
6. **Every / each + singular noun:** *every **student**, each **day***.
7. **Kind of / sort of:** *this kind of **book**; these kinds of **books***.
8. **Plural nouns used as adjectives keep the plural in some fixed expressions:** *a **sports** car, the **arms** race, a **customs** officer, a **savings** account, a **sales** manager, the **accounts** department, a **goods** train, a **clothes** shop, a **students'** union* — but ordinary nouns go singular: *a **shoe** shop, a **book** shop, a **flower** garden*.
:::

## 6.10 Common Errors with Number

::: errs
1. ✘ I saw many sheeps. ✔ I saw many **sheep**.
2. ✘ Two dozens eggs ✔ Two **dozen** eggs.
3. ✘ He has two sons-in-laws. ✔ He has two **sons-in-law**.
4. ✘ The passer-bys stopped. ✔ The **passers-by** stopped.
5. ✘ This is a five-years plan. ✔ This is a **five-year** plan.
6. ✘ The datas are correct. ✔ The **data are** correct. (formal)
7. ✘ This criteria is important. ✔ This **criterion** is important. / These **criteria are** important.
8. ✘ The phenomena is strange. ✔ The **phenomenon** is strange.
9. ✘ There are many crisis. ✔ There are many **crises**.
10. ✘ A bacteria causes this disease. ✔ A **bacterium** causes this disease. / **Bacteria** cause this disease.
11. ✘ The mouses ran away. ✔ The **mice** ran away.
12. ✘ He is one of the best student. ✔ … one of the best **students**.
13. ✘ More than one boys were absent. ✔ More than one **boy was** absent.
14. ✘ The roof of the houses are red. — Check: *The **roofs** of the houses **are** red.* ✔
15. ✘ I bought two spoonsful of sugar. ✔ two **spoonfuls** of sugar.
16. ✘ The chiefs's decision ✔ The **chief's** decision / the **chiefs'** decision.
17. ✘ My brother's-in-law house ✔ My **brother-in-law's** house.
18. ✘ Two womans doctor came. ✔ Two **women doctors** came.
19. ✘ The thiefs were caught. ✔ The **thieves** were caught.
20. ✘ He wrote three thesis. ✔ He wrote three **theses**.
21. ✘ I have read the Hamlet's two analysis. ✔ I have read two **analyses** of *Hamlet*.
22. ✘ All the ladys were present. ✔ All the **ladies** were present.
23. ✘ The monkies are playing. ✔ The **monkeys** are playing.
24. ✘ He bought three radioes. ✔ He bought three **radios**.
25. ✘ The heros were honoured. ✔ The **heroes** were honoured.
:::

## 6.11 Number in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
- **Latin and Greek plurals as subjects:** GMAT and SAT writers deliberately use *criteria, phenomena, data, media, strata, bacteria, alumni, theses* as subjects to test agreement: ✘ *The criteria for admission **has** changed.* ✔ *The criteria … **have** changed.* ✘ *This phenomena **is** rare.* ✔ *This **phenomenon** is rare.*
- **Logical number:** A noun must agree logically with what it refers to: ✘ *The students raised **their hand**.* ✔ *The students raised **their hands**.* ✘ *All the participants received **a certificate*** is acceptable if each received one; GMAT accepts *certificates* as clearer.
- **Pronoun number:** ✘ *Each of the scientists presented **their** findings* (formal tests prefer ***his or her*** or rewriting: *All the scientists presented their findings*).
- **GRE vocabulary:** words like *data, criteria, phenomena, strata, media, memoranda, millennia* appear constantly in reading passages; knowing they are plural prevents misreading the sentence structure.
:::
