# Classification of Sentence (By Structure) {#ch33}

[Part A · Grammar]{.chpart}

## 33.1 Four Kinds of Sentences by Structure

According to **structure** — that is, the **number and kind of clauses** they contain — sentences are of four kinds:

| Kind | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple** | **one** independent clause (one subject–finite verb unit) | *He is poor but honest.* |
| **Complex** | **one** independent clause + **one or more** dependent clauses | *Though he is poor, he is honest.* |
| **Compound** | **two or more** independent clauses joined by coordinating conjunctions (or a semicolon) | *He is poor, but he is honest.* |
| **Compound-Complex (Mixed)** | **two or more** independent clauses + **at least one** dependent clause | *He is poor, but he is honest because his parents taught him well.* |

::: rule
**The counting method:** (1) Count the **finite verbs** — each finite verb (with its subject) makes a clause. (2) Decide whether each clause is **independent** or **dependent** (does it begin with a subordinator such as *when, because, if, that, who, which, though*?). (3) Apply the table above.
:::

## 33.2 The Simple Sentence

A **simple sentence** has **only one finite verb** (one subject–predicate unit). It may have a **compound subject**, a **compound verb**, and any number of **phrases**, but **no subordinate clause**.

::: ex
- The sun rises in the east.
- Rahim **and** Karim are brothers. (compound subject — still one clause)
- He **came, saw and conquered**. (compound predicate with one subject — traditionally treated as simple)
- **Having finished his work**, he went out to play. (participial phrase — not a clause)
- He is **too** weak **to walk**. (infinitive phrase)
- **In spite of** being poor, he is honest. (prepositional phrase)
- **The sun having set**, we went home. (absolute phrase)
- I want **to know the truth**.
- **On seeing the police**, the thief ran away.
:::

::: tip
**Exam Tip — Non-finite verbs don't count.** Infinitives (*to walk*), gerunds (*walking*) and participles (*having finished, written*) are **not finite verbs**, so they do not create clauses. *Seeing the police, he ran away* has one finite verb (*ran*) → **simple**.
:::

## 33.3 The Complex Sentence

A **complex sentence** has **one main (independent) clause** and **one or more subordinate (dependent) clauses**. The subordinate clauses may be noun, adjective or adverb clauses (Chapter 31).

**Subordinators:** *that, if, whether, who, whom, whose, which, what, when, where, why, how, because, since, as, though, although, unless, until, till, before, after, so that, lest, as soon as, as if, than, while, whereas …*

::: ex
- **When the sun sets**, the birds return to their nests. (adverb clause)
- I know **that he is honest**. (noun clause)
- The man **who came here** is my uncle. (adjective clause)
- **If you work hard**, you will succeed.
- **Though he is rich**, he is not happy.
- He is so weak **that he cannot walk**.
- This is the house **where I was born**.
- Tell me **what you want**.
- He said **that he would come if he got time**. (one main + two subordinate clauses)
:::

## 33.4 The Compound Sentence

A **compound sentence** has **two or more independent (coordinate) clauses**. Each could stand alone as a sentence. They are joined by:

- **Coordinating conjunctions:** *and, but, or, nor, for, so, yet* (FANBOYS);
- **Correlative conjunctions:** *either … or, neither … nor, not only … but also, both … and*;
- **Conjunctive adverbs** with a semicolon: *; however, ; therefore, ; otherwise, ; moreover, ; still, ; nevertheless*;
- A **semicolon** alone.

::: ex
- He is poor, **but** he is honest.
- Work hard, **or** you will fail.
- He came, **and** he left at once.
- She is ill, **so** she can't come.
- He **not only** wrote the letter **but also** posted it. (one subject; compound predicate — usually counted as compound in exams when the correlative joins two predicates)
- **Either** you go, **or** I go.
- He was tired; **nevertheless**, he worked till midnight.
- The sun set; the stars appeared.
:::

### 33.4.1 Kinds of Coordinate Connection

| Relationship | Conjunctions | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Cumulative** (addition) | and, both … and, not only … but also, as well as, moreover | *He is clever **and** he is hard-working.* |
| **Adversative** (contrast) | but, yet, still, however, nevertheless, whereas, while | *He is rich, **yet** he is unhappy.* |
| **Alternative** (choice) | or, either … or, neither … nor, otherwise, else | *Hurry up, **or** you will be late.* |
| **Illative** (result/inference) | so, therefore, hence, consequently, for | *It was raining, **so** we stayed home.* |

## 33.5 The Compound-Complex (Mixed) Sentence

A **compound-complex sentence** has **at least two independent clauses** and **at least one dependent clause**.

::: ex
- He is poor, **but** he is honest **because** his parents taught him well.
- **When** the bell rang, the students went out, **and** the teacher locked the door.
- I know **that** he is clever, **but** he is lazy.
- She said **that** she would come, **but** she did not.
:::

## 33.6 Identifying Sentence Structure: Worked Examples

::: errs
1. *I saw a man walking along the road.* → **Simple** (one finite verb *saw*; *walking* is a participle).
2. *I saw a man who was walking along the road.* → **Complex** (*who was walking* — adjective clause).
3. *I saw a man, and he was walking along the road.* → **Compound**.
4. *He confessed his guilt.* → **Simple**.
5. *He confessed that he was guilty.* → **Complex** (noun clause).
6. *He was guilty, and he confessed it.* → **Compound**.
7. *Being tired, he went to bed.* → **Simple**.
8. *As he was tired, he went to bed.* → **Complex**.
9. *He was tired, so he went to bed.* → **Compound**.
10. *He is too old to work.* → **Simple**.
11. *He is so old that he cannot work.* → **Complex**.
12. *He is very old, and so he cannot work.* → **Compound**.
13. *In spite of his poverty, he is happy.* → **Simple**.
14. *Though he is poor, he is happy.* → **Complex**.
15. *He is poor, but he is happy.* → **Compound**.
16. *Tell me where you live and what you do.* → **Complex** (main clause *Tell me* + two noun clauses joined by *and*; still one independent clause).
17. *He came when I was sleeping, but I did not wake up.* → **Compound-complex**.
18. *Rahim, Karim and Jamal went to Sylhet and visited the tea gardens.* → **Simple** (compound subject and compound predicate, one clause).
19. *The sun having set, we went home.* → **Simple** (absolute phrase).
20. *Hardly had he left when it began to rain.* → **Complex** (*when it began to rain* — adverb clause).
:::

## 33.7 Synthesis of Sentences (Combining Simple Sentences)

**Synthesis** means **combining two or more simple sentences into one** — simple, complex or compound — without changing the meaning. It is the reverse of analysis and a common transformation task.

### 33.7.1 Combining into a Simple Sentence

| Method | Separate sentences | Combined (simple) |
|---|---|---|
| Present participle | *He saw the police. He ran away.* | ***Seeing** the police, he ran away.* |
| Perfect participle | *He finished his work. He went home.* | ***Having finished** his work, he went home.* |
| Past participle | *The book is written in simple English. It is popular.* | ***Written** in simple English, the book is popular.* |
| Infinitive | *He went to Dhaka. He wanted to find a job.* | *He went to Dhaka **to find** a job.* |
| *Too … to* | *He is very weak. He cannot walk.* | *He is **too** weak **to walk**.* |
| Preposition + gerund/noun | *He was ill. He could not attend the meeting.* | ***Because of** his illness, he could not attend the meeting.* / ***Owing to** illness, …* |
| Apposition | *Dhaka is the capital of Bangladesh. It is a busy city.* | *Dhaka, **the capital of Bangladesh**, is a busy city.* |
| Absolute phrase | *The sun set. We returned home.* | ***The sun having set**, we returned home.* |
| Adverb / phrase | *He failed. It was unfortunate.* | ***Unfortunately**, he failed.* |
| *In spite of* | *He is rich. He is not happy.* | ***In spite of** his wealth, he is not happy.* |

### 33.7.2 Combining into a Complex Sentence

| Method | Separate sentences | Combined (complex) |
|---|---|---|
| Noun clause | *He is honest. Everybody knows it.* | *Everybody knows **that** he is honest.* |
| Adjective clause | *This is the man. He helped me.* | *This is the man **who** helped me.* |
| Adverb clause (time) | *The bell rang. The students went out.* | ***When** the bell rang, the students went out.* |
| Adverb clause (reason) | *He was ill. He did not come.* | ***As** he was ill, he did not come.* |
| Adverb clause (concession) | *He is poor. He is honest.* | ***Though** he is poor, he is honest.* |
| Adverb clause (condition) | *Work hard. You will succeed.* | ***If** you work hard, you will succeed.* |
| Adverb clause (result) | *He is very weak. He cannot walk.* | *He is **so** weak **that** he cannot walk.* |

### 33.7.3 Combining into a Compound Sentence

| Method | Separate sentences | Combined (compound) |
|---|---|---|
| *and* | *He came. He saw the house.* | *He came **and** saw the house.* |
| *but* | *He is rich. He is unhappy.* | *He is rich, **but** he is unhappy.* |
| *or / otherwise* | *Work hard. You will fail.* | *Work hard, **or** you will fail.* |
| *so / therefore* | *He was ill. He did not come.* | *He was ill, **so** he did not come.* |
| *not only … but also* | *He is a poet. He is a painter.* | *He is **not only** a poet **but also** a painter.* |
| *either … or / neither … nor* | *He is not rich. He is not poor.* | *He is **neither** rich **nor** poor.* |

## 33.8 Sentence Structure in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
- **Run-on sentences and comma splices (SAT):** two independent clauses must be joined by **a comma + coordinating conjunction**, a **semicolon**, a **colon** (when the second explains the first), or separated by a **full stop**. ✘ *The rain stopped, we went out.* ✔ *The rain stopped, **and** we went out. / The rain stopped; we went out.*
- **Subordination for logic (GMAT):** GMAT often prefers a **complex** sentence that shows the logical relation (*Because …, although …*) over a loose compound sentence joined by *and*.
- **Sentence variety:** In GRE/GMAT essays (Analytical Writing), mixing simple, complex and compound sentences shows mastery; strings of short simple sentences look immature.
:::
