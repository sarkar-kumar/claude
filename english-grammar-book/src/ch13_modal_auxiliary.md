## 13.1 What Are Modal Auxiliaries?

**Modal auxiliaries** (or simply **modals**) are helping verbs that express the speaker's **attitude** toward an action — whether it is possible, necessary, permitted, advisable, certain, or desired. The word *modal* comes from *mode* (manner).

The **core modals** are: ***can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must, ought to***. The **semi-modals** are: ***need, dare, used to*** (which can also act as ordinary verbs) and expressions like ***have to, be able to, had better, would rather, be to, be supposed to***.

### 13.1.1 Special Features of Modals

1. **No *-s* in the third person:** *He **can** swim.* (✘ He cans swim)
2. **Followed by the bare infinitive** (base form without *to*) — except *ought to, used to, have to*: *She **must go**.* (✘ must to go, ✘ must goes, ✘ must going)
3. **No infinitive, *-ing* or past participle forms:** ✘ *to can*, ✘ *canning*, ✘ *have could*. Use substitutes: *to be able to, having to*.
4. **Negatives with *not* directly:** *cannot (can't), could not, may not, might not, shall not (shan't), should not, will not (won't), would not, must not, ought not to, need not, dare not*.
5. **Questions by inversion:** ***Can** you help me? **May** I come in? **Should** we wait?*
6. **Two modals cannot come together:** ✘ *He will can come.* ✔ *He will **be able to** come.* ✘ *You must can swim.* ✔ *You must **be able to** swim.*
7. **Modals are used with all three time references**, and their "past" forms (*could, might, should, would*) often express **politeness or tentativeness**, not past time.

## 13.2 *Can*

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| **Ability** (present) | *He **can** speak four languages. A cat **can** see in the dark.* |
| **Permission** (informal) | *You **can** go now. **Can** I use your phone?* |
| **Possibility** (general/theoretical) | *Smoking **can** cause cancer. Even good students **can** make mistakes.* |
| **Request** (informal) | ***Can** you pass the salt?* |
| **Impossibility / negative deduction** (*can't*) | *It **can't** be true. He **can't** be hungry — he has just eaten.* |
| **Offer** | ***Can** I help you?* |

::: note
***Cannot*** is written as **one word** (*can not* is used only for special emphasis). The contraction is ***can't***. The future of *can* is ***will be able to***: *He **will be able to** walk next week.* (✘ will can)
:::

## 13.3 *Could*

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| **Past ability** (general) | *He **could** swim when he was six.* |
| **Polite request** | ***Could** you help me, please? **Could** I see the manager?* |
| **Possibility** (present/future, less certain) | *It **could** rain later. He **could** be at home now.* |
| **Conditional** (unreal) | *If I had money, I **could** buy a car.* |
| **Suggestion** | *You **could** try asking the teacher.* |
| **Past of *can* in reported speech** | *He said that he **could** swim.* |
| ***Could have* + V3** — past possibility or ability not used | *You **could have** won if you had tried. (but you didn't) He **could have** taken the wrong bus.* |

::: tip
***Could* vs *was able to*:** For a **general ability** in the past, both are correct: *He **could / was able to** run fast.* For **one specific achievement** (= *managed to*), use ***was able to*** in positive sentences: ✘ *The fire was big, but everybody could escape.* ✔ *… everybody **was able to** escape / **managed to** escape.* In negatives, *couldn't* is fine: *He **couldn't** escape.*
:::

## 13.4 *May*

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| **Permission** (formal) | ***May** I come in, sir? You **may** go now. Students **may not** use phones in the exam hall* (prohibition). |
| **Possibility** (about 50%) | *He **may** come today. It **may** rain tonight. The news **may** be true.* |
| **Wish / prayer / blessing** | ***May** you live long! **May** Allah bless you! **May** our country prosper!* |
| **Purpose** (with *so that / that / in order that*) | *He works hard **so that** he **may** succeed.* |
| **Concession** | *He **may** be rich, but he is not happy. Try as he **may**, he cannot win.* |
| ***May have* + V3** — possibility in the past | *He **may have** missed the train.* |

## 13.5 *Might*

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| **Past of *may* in reported speech** | *He said that he **might** come.* |
| **Weaker possibility** than *may* | *It **might** rain, but I doubt it.* |
| **Polite suggestion / request** | *You **might** call him first. **Might** I ask a question?* (very formal) |
| **Purpose** (after a past verb) | *He worked hard so that he **might** succeed.* |
| **Unreal condition** | *If you asked him, he **might** help you.* |
| ***Might have* + V3** | *You **might have** been killed! (but you weren't) He **might have** forgotten.* |
| **Mild reproach** | *You **might have** told me earlier!* |

## 13.6 *Shall*

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| **Simple future with *I/we*** (traditional British) | *I **shall** be twenty next year. We **shall** reach Sylhet by noon.* |
| **Offer / suggestion** (questions with *I/we*) | ***Shall** I open the window? **Shall** we go for a walk?* |
| **Command, promise, threat, determination** with *you / he / they* | *You **shall** be punished. He **shall** have a prize. They **shall not** pass!* |
| **Legal / formal obligation** | *The tenant **shall** pay the rent by the 5th of each month. Every citizen **shall** have the right to vote.* |
| **Asking for instructions** | *What **shall** I do now?* |

### 13.6.1 The Traditional *Shall / Will* Rule

Traditional grammar (still used in Bangladeshi exam answers) gives this rule:

| Meaning | 1st person (*I, we*) | 2nd & 3rd persons (*you, he, she, it, they*) |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple future** (no special feeling) | **shall** | **will** |
| **Determination, promise, command, threat, willingness** | **will** | **shall** |

::: ex
- I **shall** go tomorrow. (simple future)
- I **will** go, whatever happens. (determination)
- He **will** come tomorrow. (simple future)
- He **shall** come, whether he likes it or not. (my determination/command)
- You **shall** get your reward. (promise)
:::

::: note
**Modern usage:** In modern English (especially American, and on the SAT/GRE/GMAT), ***will*** is used for **all persons** in the simple future, and ***shall*** is mainly used in **offers/suggestions** (*Shall I …? Shall we …?*) and in **legal documents**. But in BCS-type exam questions on "shall/will", apply the traditional rule above.
:::

## 13.7 *Will*

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| **Future** (all persons in modern English) | *He **will** come tomorrow. It **will** rain.* |
| **Willingness / volition** | *I **will** help you. He **won't** listen to me.* (refusal) |
| **Determination / promise** (1st person) | *I **will** pass this time. I **will** never forget you.* |
| **Request / invitation** | ***Will** you open the door, please? **Will** you have some tea?* |
| **Habit / characteristic behaviour** | *Boys **will** be boys. He **will** sit for hours reading. Oil **will** float on water.* |
| **Assumption / deduction** | *That **will** be the postman. (= I'm sure it's the postman.)* |
| **Instant decision** | *The phone is ringing — I'**ll** get it.* |

## 13.8 *Should*

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| **Duty / obligation (moral)** | *We **should** obey our parents. You **should** respect your teachers.* |
| **Advice / recommendation** | *You **should** see a doctor. You **shouldn't** smoke.* |
| **Probability / expectation** | *He left at 8; he **should** be here by 10. The exam **should** be easy.* |
| **After *lest*** (always *should*, never *not*) | *Walk carefully **lest** you **should** fall.* (✘ lest you should not fall; ✘ lest you will fall) |
| **Rare / doubtful condition** | *If it **should** rain, the match will be cancelled. **Should** you need help, call me.* |
| **Past of *shall* in reported speech** | *I said that I **should** go.* |
| **After *suggest, insist, recommend, propose, demand* (BrE)** | *I suggest that he **should** go.* (also: *that he go*) |
| **After *It is necessary / important / essential / strange / surprising that*** | *It is necessary that he **should** be present. It is strange that she **should** say so.* |
| **Polite form with *I/we*** (formal BrE) | *I **should** like to meet him.* (= I would like) |
| ***Should have* + V3** — past duty not done | *You **should have** come earlier. (but you didn't) I **shouldn't have** said that.* |
| **Emotional reaction ("putative should")** | *Why **should** I help him? Who **should** come in but the headmaster!* |

## 13.9 *Would*

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| **Past of *will* in reported speech** | *He said that he **would** come.* |
| **Polite request** | ***Would** you mind closing the door? **Would** you please help me?* |
| **Conditional (unreal)** | *If I were rich, I **would** help the poor. If he had come, I **would have** met him.* |
| **Past habit** (repeated action) | *When we were children, we **would** swim in the pond every day.* |
| **Past willingness / refusal** | *He **would not** listen to me. The car **wouldn't** start.* |
| **Wish (with *wish*)** | *I wish you **would** stop talking.* |
| ***Would like / would love*** (polite *want*) | *I **would like** a cup of tea. **Would** you **like** to come?* |
| ***Would rather*** (preference) | *I **would rather** walk than take a rickshaw. I **would rather** you **stayed** here.* |
| **Probability / assumption about the past** | *That **would** be his brother, I suppose.* |
| ***Would have* + V3** — unreal past | *I **would have** helped you if you had asked.* |

::: note
**Do not use *would* in the *if*-clause** of a conditional (in standard and exam English): ✘ *If he would come, I would be happy.* ✔ *If he **came**, I would be happy.* ✘ *If I would have known* ✔ *If I **had known***. (*Would* in an *if*-clause is acceptable only when it means "be willing": *If you would wait a moment, I'll call him.*)
:::

## 13.10 *Must*

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| **Necessity / strong obligation (from the speaker)** | *I **must** finish this today. You **must** work hard to succeed.* |
| **Rules and laws** | *Passengers **must** wear seat belts. All candidates **must** bring their admit cards.* |
| **Prohibition** (*must not / mustn't*) | *You **mustn't** smoke here. Students **must not** cheat.* |
| **Strong deduction / certainty** (present) | *He **must** be tired after the long journey. She **must** be at home — the lights are on.* |
| **Strong recommendation** | *You **must** visit Cox's Bazar. You **must** see this film.* |
| ***Must have* + V3** — certain deduction about the past | *The ground is wet; it **must have** rained last night. He **must have** forgotten.* |

### 13.10.1 *Must* vs *Have to*

- ***Must*** — obligation from the **speaker** (internal, personal feeling): *I **must** stop smoking* (I feel it is necessary).
- ***Have to*** — obligation from **outside** (rules, circumstances): *I **have to** wear a uniform at work* (it's the rule).
- *Must* has no past or future form; use ***had to*** and ***will have to***: *I **had to** walk home yesterday. You **will have to** wait.*

### 13.10.2 *Mustn't* vs *Don't Have To / Needn't*

This difference is tested frequently:

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ***must not / mustn't*** | It is **forbidden** — don't do it | *You **mustn't** park here.* (It is not allowed.) |
| ***don't have to / needn't*** | It is **not necessary** — you can if you want | *You **don't have to / needn't** come tomorrow; it's a holiday.* |

## 13.11 *Ought to*

***Ought*** is always followed by ***to***. It expresses **moral duty**, **advice** or **probability**, and is very close to *should* (slightly stronger and more formal).

::: ex
- We **ought to** love our country. (moral duty)
- You **ought to** apologise to her. (advice)
- He **ought to** pass easily; he has worked hard. (probability)
- You **ought not to** waste money. (negative: *ought not to*, ✘ ought to not)
- **Ought** we **to** go? (question — formal)
- You **ought to have** helped him. (past duty not done)
- ✘ You ought respect your elders. ✔ You **ought to** respect your elders.
:::

## 13.12 *Need*

***Need*** can be a **modal** (in negatives and questions, without *to*, no *-s*) or a **main verb** (with *to*, with *-s*, in all sentence types).

::: ex
- **Modal:** *He **needn't** go. **Need** he go? You **need not** worry.*
- **Main verb:** *He **needs to** go. Does he **need to** go? He **doesn't need to** go.*
- ✘ He needs not go. ✔ He **need not** go / He **doesn't need to** go.
- ✘ He need to go. ✔ He **needs to** go. (positive statement → main verb)
- **Noun:** *There is no **need** to hurry.*
:::

::: note
***Needn't have* + V3** vs ***didn't need to***: *I **needn't have** bought the bread* = I bought it, but it was unnecessary (wasted effort). *I **didn't need to** buy bread* = it was not necessary (and probably I didn't buy any).
:::

## 13.13 *Dare*

Like *need*, ***dare*** is a **modal** in negatives and questions and a **main verb** elsewhere.

::: ex
- **Modal:** *He **dare not** oppose his boss. **How dare** you speak to me like that? **Dare** he deny it?*
- **Main verb:** *He **dares to** speak the truth. He **doesn't dare (to)** open the door. Nobody **dared to** speak.*
- ***I dare say*** = I suppose: *I **dare say** you are tired.*
- ***Dare*** + object = challenge: *I **dare** you **to** jump.*
- ✘ He dares not go. ✔ He **dare not** go / He **does not dare to** go.
:::

## 13.14 *Used to*

***Used to*** + base form expresses a **past habit or state** that is **no longer true**.

::: ex
- I **used to** smoke, but I gave it up.
- There **used to** be a big pond here.
- He **didn't use to** like vegetables. (✘ didn't used to — in careful writing)
- **Did** you **use to** play cricket?
:::

::: rule
**Three structures not to confuse:**

1. ***used to* + base form** = past habit: *I **used to get up** early.* (but not now)
2. ***be used to* + -ing / noun** = be accustomed to: *I **am used to getting up** early.* (it is normal for me) / *He **is used to** hard work.*
3. ***get used to* + -ing / noun** = become accustomed to: *You will soon **get used to living** in Dhaka.*

✘ *I am used to get up early.* ✔ *I am used to **getting** up early.*
:::

***Would* vs *used to* for past habits:** *Would* can express repeated past **actions** but not past **states**: *When I was a child, I **would / used to** play in the field* ✔. *I **used to** have a dog* ✔ (state — ✘ *I would have a dog*).

## 13.15 Other Modal Expressions

| Expression | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ***had better* + base** | strong advice (with warning) | *You **had better** go now, or you'll miss the bus. You **had better not** tell him.* (✘ had better to go; ✘ had better not to) |
| ***would rather* + base** | preference | *I **would rather** stay at home.* |
| ***would rather* + subject + past** | preference about another's action | *I **would rather** you **went** now.* |
| ***would sooner*** | = would rather | *I **would sooner** die than surrender.* |
| ***be able to*** | ability (all tenses) | *She **will be able to** come. I **have been able to** save money.* |
| ***have to / have got to*** | external obligation | *I **have (got) to** leave now.* |
| ***be to*** | plan, order, destiny | *The President **is to** visit Rajshahi tomorrow. You **are to** wait here.* |
| ***be supposed to*** | expectation, duty | *You **are supposed to** be here at 9. The film **is supposed to** be good.* |
| ***be bound to*** | certainty | *He **is bound to** pass.* |
| ***be likely to*** | probability | *It **is likely to** rain.* |

## 13.16 Modal + Perfect Infinitive (*have* + V3): Talking about the Past

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **must have** + V3 | certainty about the past (deduction) | *He **must have** left already.* |
| **can't / couldn't have** + V3 | impossibility in the past | *She **can't have** seen me — I was hiding.* |
| **may / might have** + V3 | possibility in the past | *They **may have** lost the way.* |
| **could have** + V3 | past ability not used / past possibility | *You **could have** asked me for help.* |
| **should / ought to have** + V3 | past duty not done (regret, criticism) | *You **should have** studied harder.* |
| **shouldn't have** + V3 | a wrong action was done | *You **shouldn't have** shouted at him.* |
| **needn't have** + V3 | unnecessary action that was done | *You **needn't have** brought an umbrella; it didn't rain.* |
| **would have** + V3 | unreal past result (3rd conditional) | *I **would have** come if you had invited me.* |

## 13.17 Modals in Reported Speech

When the reporting verb is in the past tense, modals change as follows (Chapter 27):

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| can | could |
| may | might |
| shall | should (or *would*, depending on person) |
| will | would |
| must | must / had to |
| could, might, should, would, ought to, used to | no change |

::: ex
- He said, "I **can** swim." → He said that he **could** swim.
- She said, "I **may** go." → She said that she **might** go.
- He said, "I **will** help you." → He said that he **would** help me.
- The teacher said, "You **must** work hard." → The teacher said that we **must / had to** work hard.
:::

## 13.18 Common Errors with Modals

::: errs
1. ✘ He cans swim. ✔ He **can** swim.
2. ✘ She must goes now. ✔ She must **go** now.
3. ✘ You should to respect elders. ✔ You **should respect** elders.
4. ✘ You ought respect your teachers. ✔ You **ought to** respect your teachers.
5. ✘ He will can come tomorrow. ✔ He will **be able to** come tomorrow.
6. ✘ Walk fast lest you will miss the train. ✔ Walk fast **lest you should** miss the train.
7. ✘ Walk carefully lest you should not fall. ✔ Walk carefully **lest you should fall**.
8. ✘ You had better to go now. ✔ You **had better go** now.
9. ✘ I would rather to stay home. ✔ I **would rather stay** home.
10. ✘ He needs not come. ✔ He **need not** come / He **doesn't need to** come.
11. ✘ He dares not speak. ✔ He **dare not** speak.
12. ✘ I am used to get up early. ✔ I am used to **getting** up early.
13. ✘ He used to playing cricket. ✔ He used to **play** cricket.
14. ✘ If I would be rich, I would help you. ✔ If I **were** rich, I would help you.
15. ✘ May you lives long! ✔ May you **live** long!
16. ✘ He must have went home. ✔ He must have **gone** home.
17. ✘ You mustn't come tomorrow; it's a holiday. (= not necessary) ✔ You **needn't / don't have to** come tomorrow.
18. ✘ Everybody could escape from the burning house. ✔ Everybody **was able to / managed to** escape.
19. ✘ Can I come in, sir? (formal exam answer) ✔ **May** I come in, sir?
20. ✘ He should had come. ✔ He should **have come**.
21. ✘ You could have help me. ✔ You could have **helped** me.
22. ✘ I didn't used to like tea. ✔ I didn't **use** to like tea.
23. ✘ Shall you help me? (request) ✔ **Will** you help me?
24. ✘ He said that he will come. (past reporting) ✔ He said that he **would** come.
25. ✘ He might came today. ✔ He might **come** today.
:::

## 13.19 Modals in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
- **Conditional sequence (GMAT):** *If* + present → *will*; *If* + past → *would*; *If* + past perfect → *would have*. ✘ *If the company would invest more, profits would rise.* ✔ *If the company **invested** more, …*. ✘ *If he had known, he will come.* ✔ *… he **would have come***.
- **Subjunctive and *should*:** GMAT prefers the bare subjunctive: *It is essential that he **be** present* (not *should be*) — both are grammatical, but GMAT often marks *should* as wordy.
- ***May / might / could* nuance in GRE Reading:** the author's degree of certainty is shown through modals — *must* (certain), *may/might/could* (possible), *should* (expected). Inference questions often turn on this.
- **Logical certainty words** like *must, cannot, necessarily, possibly* appear in **GMAT Critical Reasoning** answer choices; an answer saying something *must* happen is stronger (and riskier) than one saying it *may* happen.
:::
