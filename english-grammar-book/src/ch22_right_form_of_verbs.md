## 22.1 What Is "Right Form of Verbs"?

In a **Right Form of Verbs** question, a verb is given in its base form in brackets and you must put it into the **correct form** — tense, number, voice, mood, or a non-finite form (infinitive, gerund, participle):

::: ex
- He (go) to school every day. → He **goes** to school every day.
- I (see) him yesterday. → I **saw** him yesterday.
- He insisted on (go) there. → He insisted on **going** there.
- The letter (write) by Rina. → The letter **was written** by Rina.
:::

This item appears in SSC, HSC, university admission tests, BCS Written and Bank written exams, and in MCQ form in BCS Preliminary and Bank tests. It is really a **test of everything** in Chapters 11–14, 21, 23, 25 and 26. The method is simple:

::: rule
**The Five-Question Method**

1. **Is a finite verb needed?** (Does the clause already have a finite verb? Is the word after a preposition, a modal, *to*, *let/make*?) — If not finite → choose infinitive, gerund or participle.
2. **What is the time?** Look for **time words** (*yesterday, now, since, by the time …*) and the **tense of the other verb** (sequence of tenses).
3. **Who/what is the subject, and is it singular or plural?** (agreement)
4. **Is the subject doing the action or receiving it?** (active or passive)
5. **Is there a special structure?** (*if, wish, as if, it's time, lest, suggest that, hardly … when, would rather*, etc.)
:::

The rest of this chapter lists the **rules** with examples, grouped for easy learning.

## 22.2 Rules Based on Subject and Time (Tense)

**Rule 1 — Present Indefinite for habits, routines and general facts:** third-person singular subject → V1 + *s/es*.

::: ex
- He (read) the newspaper every morning. → **reads**
- The earth (move) round the sun. → **moves**
- Rice (grow) in wet land. → **grows**
- Cats (catch) mice. → **catch**
:::

**Rule 2 — Universal truths stay in the present, even after a past reporting verb.**

::: ex
- The teacher said that the sun (rise) in the east. → **rises**
- He knew that honesty (be) the best policy. → **is**
:::

**Rule 3 — *Now, at present, at this moment, Look!, Listen!, still, day by day* → Present Continuous.**

::: ex
- Look! The boy (climb) the tree. → **is climbing**
- Listen! Someone (knock) at the door. → **is knocking**
- Prices (rise) day by day. → **are rising**
:::

**Rule 4 — *Just, already, yet, ever, never, recently, lately, so far, this week, this year* → Present Perfect.**

::: ex
- He just (leave) the room. → **has just left**
- I never (see) a tiger. → **have never seen**
- Have you (finish) your work yet? → **finished**
:::

**Rule 5 — *Since* / *for* + continuing action up to now → Present Perfect Continuous (or Present Perfect for stative verbs).**

::: ex
- I (wait) for you for an hour. → **have been waiting**
- It (rain) since morning. → **has been raining**
- I (know) him since my childhood. → **have known**
:::

**Rule 6 — *Yesterday, last, ago, in + past year, the other day, just now* → Past Indefinite.**

::: ex
- He (come) home yesterday. → **came**
- I (meet) him two days ago. → **met**
- She (finish) the work just now. → **finished** (but *has just finished* ✔)
:::

**Rule 7 — *It is / It has been* + time + *since* + Past Indefinite.**

::: ex
- It is ten years since he (die). → **died**
- It is long since I (see) you last. → **saw**
:::

**Rule 8 — Present Perfect / *It is* in the main clause + *since* + Past Indefinite in the *since*-clause.**

::: ex
- I have known him since he (be) a child. → **was**
- Many years have passed since he (leave) the village. → **left**
:::

**Rule 9 — *While* + Past Continuous; *when* + Past Indefinite (interruption).**

::: ex
- While he (read), the light went out. → **was reading**
- I was sleeping when he (come). → **came**
- When I reached the station, the train (leave). → **had left** (earlier action — Rule 10)
:::

**Rule 10 — Two past actions: earlier → Past Perfect; later → Past Indefinite (with *before, after, when, by the time, till*).**

::: ex
- The patient (die) before the doctor came. → **had died**
- After he (finish) his work, he went out. → **had finished**
- The train had left before we (reach) the station. → **reached**
- By the time the fire brigade arrived, the house (burn) down. → **had burnt**
:::

**Rule 11 — *Hardly / Scarcely … when* and *No sooner … than*: Past Perfect + Past Indefinite.**

::: ex
- No sooner had he (arrive) than it began to rain. → **arrived** (had arrived)
- Hardly had we sat down when the bell (ring). → **rang**
:::

**Rule 12 — Future time words (*tomorrow, next, soon, in future*) → Future Indefinite in the main clause.**

::: ex
- He (come) tomorrow. → **will come**
- We (visit) Sylhet next month. → **shall/will visit**
:::

**Rule 13 — In time and condition clauses (*when, before, after, until, as soon as, if, unless, in case*) about the future, use the present (not *will*).**

::: ex
- I will wait until he (return). → **returns**
- If it (rain), we shall not go out. → **rains**
- Call me as soon as you (reach) Dhaka. → **reach**
:::

**Rule 14 — *By* + future time / *by the time* + present → Future Perfect.**

::: ex
- By next year, he (complete) his studies. → **will have completed**
- By the time you come, I (finish) the work. → **shall/will have finished**
:::

**Rule 15 — Sequence of tenses: past main verb → past form in the noun clause.**

::: ex
- He said that he (be) ill. → **was**
- I thought that he (will) come. → **would**
- She told me that she (see) the film. → **had seen**
:::

## 22.3 Rules Based on Non-Finite Forms

**Rule 16 — After modals (*can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must*) → V1.**

::: ex
- He can (swim). → **swim**
- You must (obey) your parents. → **obey**
- It may (rain) today. → **rain**
:::

**Rule 17 — After *to* (infinitive marker) → V1.**

::: ex
- I want to (go) home. → **go**
- He decided to (leave) the job. → **leave**
:::

**Rule 18 — After *let, make, bid, help, see, hear, watch, feel, notice* + object → bare V1 (for a complete action).**

::: ex
- Let him (go). → **go**
- She made me (laugh). → **laugh**
- I saw him (cross) the road. → **cross**
- Help me (lift) the box. → **lift / to lift**
:::

**Rule 19 — After *had better, would rather, would sooner, cannot but, do nothing but, rather than* → V1.**

::: ex
- You had better (consult) a doctor. → **consult**
- I would rather (die) than (beg). → **die … beg**
- I cannot but (laugh). → **laugh**
- He did nothing but (sleep). → **sleep**
:::

**Rule 20 — After a preposition → V-*ing* (gerund).**

::: ex
- He is fond of (read). → **reading**
- She left without (say) goodbye. → **saying**
- He insisted on (pay) the bill. → **paying**
- I am tired of (wait). → **waiting**
- Instead of (study), he played. → **studying**
:::

**Rule 21 — *To* as a preposition → V-*ing*:** *look forward to, be used to, be accustomed to, with a view to, object to, in addition to, be devoted to, confess to, be addicted to, prefer … to.*

::: ex
- I look forward to (hear) from you. → **hearing**
- He is used to (work) at night. → **working**
- He went to Dhaka with a view to (get) a job. → **getting**
:::

**Rule 22 — After certain verbs → V-*ing*:** *avoid, enjoy, mind, finish, deny, admit, postpone, consider, keep, give up, can't help, practise, risk, miss, suggest, stop (= end).*

::: ex
- Would you mind (open) the window? → **opening**
- He avoided (meet) me. → **meeting**
- I couldn't help (laugh). → **laughing**
- He gave up (smoke). → **smoking**
:::

**Rule 23 — After certain verbs → *to* + V1:** *want, wish, hope, decide, agree, refuse, promise, plan, expect, afford, fail, manage, pretend, learn, seem, tend, offer, threaten.*

::: ex
- He refused (help) me. → **to help**
- She promised (come) early. → **to come**
- We cannot afford (buy) a car. → **to buy**
:::

**Rule 24 — Fixed expressions → V-*ing*:** *It is no use, It is no good, It is worth, There is no point in, be busy, spend time, feel like, have difficulty (in).*

::: ex
- It is no use (cry) over spilt milk. → **crying**
- This book is worth (read). → **reading**
- He is busy (prepare) for the exam. → **preparing**
- I don't feel like (eat). → **eating**
:::

**Rule 25 — Perfect participle (*Having* + V3) for the earlier of two actions with the same subject.**

::: ex
- (Finish) his work, he went to bed. → **Having finished**
- (Take) his breakfast, he went to school. → **Having taken**
:::

**Rule 26 — Present participle (V-*ing*) for a simultaneous action or a reason; past participle (V3) for a passive sense.**

::: ex
- (Walk) along the road, I saw a snake. → **Walking**
- (Be) ill, he could not come. → **Being**
- (Write) in simple language, the book is popular. → **Written**
- The man (stand) at the gate is my uncle. → **standing**
- The goods (order) last week have arrived. → **ordered**
:::

**Rule 27 — Absolute construction: a noun/pronoun + participle (independent subject).**

::: ex
- The sun (set), we went home. → **having set**
- The weather (be) fine, we went out. → **being**
- God (will), we shall meet again. → **willing**
:::

**Rule 28 — Causative *have / get* + object + V3 (someone else does it).**

::: ex
- I had my hair (cut). → **cut**
- She got her watch (repair). → **repaired**
- He had the house (paint). → **painted**
:::

**Rule 29 — *-ing* vs *-ed* adjectives (cause vs feeling).**

::: ex
- The film was very (bore). → **boring**
- I was (bore) by the film. → **bored**
- It was an (excite) match. → **exciting**
:::

**Rule 30 — *Used to* + V1 (past habit); *be/get used to* + V-*ing*.**

::: ex
- He used to (smoke) a lot. → **smoke**
- I am used to (walk) long distances. → **walking**
:::

## 22.4 Rules Based on Voice (Active/Passive)

**Rule 31 — When the subject receives the action, use the passive: *be* + V3.**

::: ex
- The thief (catch) yesterday. → **was caught**
- English (speak) all over the world. → **is spoken**
- The bridge (build) now. → **is being built**
- The work (finish) by tomorrow. → **will have been finished**
- The letter must (post) today. → **be posted**
:::

**Rule 32 — Passive infinitive and gerund.**

::: ex
- This work is to (do) at once. → **be done**
- There is no time to (lose). → **lose / be lost** (*to lose* is idiomatic)
- He hates (insult). → **being insulted**
- The room needs (clean). → **cleaning / to be cleaned**
:::

**Rule 33 — *Born*: always passive in the sense of birth.**

::: ex
- He (bear) in 1971. → **was born**
- She has (bear) three children. → **borne**
:::

## 22.5 Rules Based on Mood and Special Structures

**Rule 34 — *If* conditionals (Chapter 25).**

::: ex
- If he comes, I (go). → **shall/will go** (First)
- If I were a king, I (help) the poor. → **would help** (Second)
- If he had worked hard, he (pass). → **would have passed** (Third)
- If I (be) you, I would not do it. → **were**
- (Have) I known, I would have come. → **Had**
:::

**Rule 35 — *Wish* + past (present wish) / past perfect (past wish).**

::: ex
- I wish I (be) a bird. → **were**
- I wish I (see) the match yesterday. → **had seen**
- I wish you (stop) talking. → **would stop**
:::

**Rule 36 — *As if / as though* + past subjunctive / past perfect.**

::: ex
- He talks as if he (be) a king. → **were**
- He looked as if he (see) a ghost. → **had seen**
:::

**Rule 37 — *It is time / It is high time* + past simple.**

::: ex
- It is time we (start). → **started**
- It is high time you (change) your habits. → **changed**
- (But: *It is time **to start**.*)
:::

**Rule 38 — *Would rather* + subject + past simple.**

::: ex
- I would rather you (come) tomorrow. → **came**
- I would rather he (not tell) anyone. → **didn't tell**
:::

**Rule 39 — Subjunctive after *suggest, propose, recommend, insist, demand, order, request, urge* + *that*; and after *It is essential / necessary / important that* → base form (or *should* + V1).**

::: ex
- I suggest that he (see) a doctor. → **see / should see**
- The court ordered that he (be) released. → **be**
- It is necessary that every student (attend) the class. → **attend**
:::

**Rule 40 — Optative (wish/prayer): *May* + subject + V1; *Long live*; *God bless*.**

::: ex
- May you (live) long! → **live**
- Long (live) Bangladesh! → **live**
- God (bless) you! → **bless**
:::

**Rule 41 — *Lest* + *should* + V1.**

::: ex
- Walk fast lest you (miss) the train. → **should miss**
:::

**Rule 42 — Purpose: *so that / in order that* + *may* (present) / *might* (past).**

::: ex
- He works hard so that he (prosper). → **may prosper**
- He worked hard so that he (succeed). → **might succeed**
:::

**Rule 43 — Imperative: base form; *Let* + object + V1.**

::: ex
- (Shut) the door. → **Shut**
- Let us (go) out. → **go**
- Don't (make) a noise. → **make**
:::

## 22.6 Rules Based on Agreement

**Rule 44 — Each / every / either / neither / everyone / nobody + singular verb.**

::: ex
- Each of the boys (have) a pen. → **has**
- Every man and woman (be) present. → **was**
- Neither of them (be) guilty. → **is**
:::

**Rule 45 — *One of* + plural noun + singular verb; *one of the … who* + plural verb.**

::: ex
- One of the students (be) absent. → **is**
- He is one of the best players who (have) ever played. → **have**
:::

**Rule 46 — *As well as / along with / with / together with / besides*: verb agrees with the first subject.**

::: ex
- The captain, as well as the players, (be) happy. → **is**
- Rahim with his friends (be) coming. → **is**
:::

**Rule 47 — *Either … or / neither … nor / not only … but also / or*: verb agrees with the nearer subject.**

::: ex
- Neither the teacher nor the students (be) present. → **were**
- Either you or he (be) wrong. → **is**
:::

**Rule 48 — Collective nouns (unit → singular; members → plural); special plural nouns (*people, police, cattle*) → plural; uncountables and *-ics* subjects → singular.**

::: ex
- The jury (be) divided in their opinion. → **were**
- The police (have) arrested the thief. → **have**
- The news (be) good. → **is**
- Mathematics (be) my favourite subject. → **is**
:::

**Rule 49 — *A number of* + plural verb; *The number of* + singular verb.**

::: ex
- A number of students (be) absent. → **were / are**
- The number of students (be) increasing. → **is**
:::

**Rule 50 — Relative pronoun: verb agrees with the antecedent.**

::: ex
- I, who (be) your friend, will help you. → **am**
- It is you who (be) to blame. → **are**
:::

## 22.7 Rules for Stative Verbs, Need and Dare

**Rule 51 — Stative verbs (*know, believe, love, like, want, own, belong, seem, understand*) do not take the continuous form.**

::: ex
- I (know) him for years. → **have known** (✘ have been knowing)
- This book (belong) to me. → **belongs**
- I (understand) the problem now. → **understand**
:::

**Rule 52 — *Need / dare* as modals (negative/question) → V1; as main verbs → *to* + V1.**

::: ex
- You need not (worry). → **worry**
- He needs (go) now. → **to go**
- How dare you (speak) like that? → **speak**
:::

## 22.8 Worked Examples with Reasons

The following sentences are typical of BCS, Bank and admission tests. Read the reason for each answer.

::: errs
1. Many years (pass) since I last saw him. → **have passed** — present perfect + *since*.
2. He (not come) yet. → **has not come** — *yet* → present perfect.
3. The sun (set) before we reached home. → **had set** — earlier past action.
4. I (do) it when I get time. → **shall/will do** — main clause future; *get* is in a time clause.
5. He talks as if he (know) everything. → **knew** — unreal comparison.
6. It is high time you (go) to bed. → **went** — *it is high time* + past.
7. I wish I (can) help you. → **could** — present wish.
8. The boy (punish) by the teacher yesterday. → **was punished** — passive, past.
9. He is one of those who (believe) in hard work. → **believe** — *who* refers to *those*.
10. Neither he nor his friends (come) yesterday. → **came** — past simple (agreement doesn't show in V2).
11. If you had come, you (see) the fun. → **would have seen** — third conditional.
12. Would you mind (lend) me your pen? → **lending** — *mind* + gerund.
13. (Be) a rainy day, we stayed indoors. → **It being** — absolute construction needs its own subject.
14. The old man (walk) for an hour when it began to rain. → **had been walking** — past perfect continuous.
15. My father (return) home before I woke up. → **had returned** — earlier action.
16. Let him (do) whatever he likes. → **do** — after *let*.
17. He suggested that I (take) rest. → **take / should take** — subjunctive.
18. Every boy and every girl (be) given a prize. → **was** — *every … and every* → singular.
19. Bread and butter (be) his only food. → **is** — one dish.
20. The cattle (graze) in the field. → **are grazing** — *cattle* is plural.
21. The number of girls in the class (be) twenty. → **is** — *the number of* → singular.
22. Ten miles (be) a long distance. → **is** — distance as one sum.
23. I am looking forward to (see) you. → **seeing** — *to* is a preposition.
24. She is used to (live) alone. → **living** — *be used to* + V-ing.
25. He used to (go) for a walk every morning. → **go** — *used to* + V1.
26. (See) the police, the thief ran away. → **Seeing** — simultaneous action.
27. (Defeat) in the election, he left politics. → **Defeated / Having been defeated** — passive participle.
28. No sooner had the teacher entered the class than the students (stand) up. → **stood**
29. The man who (steal) the money has been arrested. → **stole / had stolen**
30. Walk carefully lest you (fall). → **should fall**
31. Two-thirds of the land (be) flooded. → **was / is** — verb agrees with *land*.
32. Two-thirds of the people (be) poor. → **are** — agrees with *people*.
33. He (be) ill for a week. → **has been** — duration up to now.
34. He (die) of cholera in 1990. → **died**
35. He said that he (will) help me. → **would**
36. The child (cry) since morning. → **has been crying**
37. Water (freeze) at 0 °C. → **freezes** — scientific fact.
38. What (happen) to him yesterday? → **happened**
39. (Had) I wings, I would fly. → **Had** (= If I had)
40. The poor (be) not always unhappy. → **are** — *the + adjective* = plural.
41. He can't help (sing) when he is happy. → **singing**
42. I saw him (go) out. → **go / going**
43. She has her clothes (wash) every week. → **washed**
44. He made me (do) the work. → **do**
45. He was made (do) the work. → **to do** — passive of *make* takes *to*.
46. There is no point in (argue) with him. → **arguing**
47. I prefer reading to (write). → **writing**
48. By this time next year, I (graduate). → **will have graduated**
49. Long (live) our motherland! → **live**
50. It is no use (try) to persuade him. → **trying**
:::

::: tip
**Final Strategy:** Underline every **clue word** before you decide: time words (*since, ago, yet, while, by the time*), structure words (*if, wish, lest, as if, it's time, suggest that, hardly … when*), position words (a preposition, a modal, *to*, *let/make*) and the **true subject**. Then apply the matching rule. With practice, 90% of these questions can be solved in seconds.
:::

## 22.9 Right Form of Verbs in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
International tests do not give "(verb)" in brackets, but **every Sentence Correction question tests the same skill**: choosing between verb forms such as *is/are, has/had, would/will, to do/doing, is done/does*. The most common verb-form errors in GMAT and SAT are:

1. **Agreement across long distances** (*The list of candidates **has**…*).
2. **Wrong tense sequence** (*By 2020 the company **had** grown* vs *has grown*).
3. **Conditional mix-ups** (*If it **were** … would*).
4. **Subjunctive** (*require that he **be***).
5. **Infinitive vs gerund idioms** (*capable **of doing**, ability **to do***).
6. **Participle/dangling modifiers** (*Having finished the report, **the manager** …*).
:::
