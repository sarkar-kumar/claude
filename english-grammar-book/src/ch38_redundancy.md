## 38.1 What Is Redundancy?

**Redundancy** means using **more words than necessary** — repeating an idea that is **already expressed** by another word in the sentence. It is also called **tautology** (saying the same thing twice) or **pleonasm** (using extra words).

::: ex
- ✘ He **returned back** home. → *Return* already means *come back*. ✔ He **returned** home.
- ✘ Please **repeat** it **again**. → *Repeat* = *say again*. ✔ Please **repeat** it.
- ✘ This is a **new innovation**. → An innovation is always new. ✔ This is an **innovation**.
:::

Redundancy is tested in BCS, Bank and admission tests as **error detection** and **"choose the correct sentence"**. In the **SAT** and especially the **GMAT**, it is part of **concision**: the best answer is the one that says everything needed **in the fewest clear words**.

## 38.2 Why Redundancy Happens

1. **The meaning is already in the word** (Latin prefixes such as *re-* = back/again, *pre-* = before, *co-* = together).
2. **Translating literally** from Bangla (*ফিরে আসা* → "return back"; *একসাথে মিলিত হওয়া* → "meet together").
3. **Mixing two constructions** (*the reason … because*, *though … but*).
4. **Habit and emphasis** in speech that is not acceptable in formal writing.

## 38.5 Grammatical Redundancy

### 38.5.1 Double Negatives

::: ex
- ✘ I **don't** have **no** money. ✔ I don't have **any** money. / I have **no** money.
- ✘ He can't **hardly** walk. ✔ He can **hardly** walk.
- ✘ **Unless** you do **not** work hard … ✔ **Unless** you work hard …
- ✘ Walk carefully **lest** you should **not** fall. ✔ … lest you should fall.
- ✘ He **forbade** me **not** to go. ✔ He forbade me to go.
- ✘ Nobody **cannot** do it. ✔ Nobody **can** do it.
:::

### 38.5.2 Double Comparatives and Superlatives

::: ex
- ✘ **more better**, **more stronger**, **most cleverest** ✔ **better**, **stronger**, **cleverest**
- ✘ **more superior**, **more preferable**, **more senior** ✔ **superior**, **preferable**, **senior** (Chapter 10)
:::

### 38.5.3 Double Conjunctions

::: ex
- ✘ **Though** he is poor, **but** he is honest. ✔ Though he is poor, he is honest.
- ✘ **Because** he was ill, **so** he did not come. ✔ Because he was ill, he did not come.
- ✘ **As** he was late, **therefore** he was punished. ✔ As he was late, he was punished.
- ✘ **Since** it rained, **hence** we stayed home. ✔ Since it rained, we stayed home.
- ✘ **Both** Rahim **as well as** Karim ✔ Both Rahim **and** Karim
- ✘ The **reason** (**why**) he failed is **because** … ✔ The reason he failed is **that** …
- ✘ **Although** … **yet** — acceptable in traditional style, but *yet* is unnecessary in modern English.
:::

### 38.5.4 Double Subjects and Objects

::: ex
- ✘ My father **he** is a doctor. ✔ My father is a doctor.
- ✘ The book which I bought **it** is useful. ✔ The book which I bought is useful.
- ✘ This is the house where I live **there**. ✔ This is the house where I live.
- ✘ The man whom I met **him** yesterday … ✔ The man whom I met yesterday …
:::

### 38.5.5 Double Prepositions and Unnecessary Prepositions

::: ex
- ✘ He fell **off of** the roof. ✔ He fell **off** the roof.
- ✘ Where are you going **to**? ✔ Where are you going?
- ✘ Where is he **at**? ✔ Where is he?
- ✘ I will meet you **on** next Friday. ✔ I will meet you next Friday.
- ✘ Inside **of** the house ✔ inside the house
- ✘ He comprises **of** … ✔ He comprises …
:::

### 38.5.6 Redundant Articles and Determiners

::: ex
- ✘ **The** both brothers ✔ **Both** brothers / both **the** brothers
- ✘ **A** my friend ✔ **a friend of mine**
- ✘ The **my** book ✔ my book
:::

## 38.7 When Repetition Is Acceptable

Not every repetition is an error. Repetition is acceptable:

1. **For emphasis or rhetoric:** *We shall fight, we shall never surrender. Government of the people, by the people, for the people.*
2. **In fixed idioms (binomials):** *safe and sound, null and void, each and every (in speeches), first and foremost (in speeches), hale and hearty, ways and means, rules and regulations, terms and conditions, peace and quiet, by and large*.
3. **Where the extra word adds a real distinction:** *She **completely** destroyed the file* (adds certainty) is normally accepted; *a **true** friend* is fine (not every friend is true).

In exams, however, if a sentence offers a **shorter version with the same meaning**, the shorter version is usually the correct answer.

## 38.9 Common Redundancy Errors (Exam Style)

::: errs
1. ✘ He returned back to his village. ✔ He **returned** to his village.
2. ✘ The two friends met together after many years. ✔ The two friends **met** after many years.
3. ✘ Let us cooperate together. ✔ Let us **cooperate**.
4. ✘ The reason why he left is because he was ill. ✔ The reason he left is **that** he was ill.
5. ✘ He is more superior to me. ✔ He is **superior** to me.
6. ✘ Though he tried hard, but he failed. ✔ Though he tried hard, he failed.
7. ✘ She gave me a free gift. ✔ She gave me a **gift**.
8. ✘ Please repeat it again. ✔ Please **repeat** it.
9. ✘ The meeting was postponed until later. ✔ The meeting was **postponed**.
10. ✘ This is a true fact. ✔ This is a **fact**.
11. ✘ He is my cousin brother. ✔ He is my **cousin**.
12. ✘ The final outcome was good. ✔ The **outcome** was good.
13. ✘ We discussed about the problem. ✔ We **discussed** the problem.
14. ✘ The sky is blue in colour. ✔ The sky is **blue**.
15. ✘ He is the most unique person. ✔ He is a **unique** person.
16. ✘ I don't have no time. ✔ I don't have **any** time.
17. ✘ I'll see you at 10 a.m. in the morning. ✔ I'll see you at **10 a.m.**
18. ✘ Both of them were equally as guilty. ✔ Both of them were **equally guilty**.
19. ✘ My brother he lives in London. ✔ My brother lives in London.
20. ✘ He has the ability to speak five languages. ✔ He **can** speak five languages.
:::

## 38.10 Redundancy in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
- **SAT Writing** has a whole question type on concision: among four choices that are all grammatical, the **shortest one that keeps the meaning** is usually correct. Delete words that repeat ideas (*annual … every year; reason … because; and also; the end result*).
- **GMAT Sentence Correction** treats redundancy as a **real error**, not just a style issue: ✘ *The two companies merged together.* ✘ *The reason is because …* ✘ *Prices rose upward.* ✘ *… the duration of the period of time …* But GMAT's **first priority is meaning and grammar**; never choose a shorter answer that changes the meaning or creates a grammatical error.
- **GRE vocabulary:** *tautology, pleonasm, verbose, prolix, redundant, circumlocution, periphrasis, garrulous, laconic, terse, succinct, pithy, concise* — words about wordiness and brevity are common in text completion.
:::
