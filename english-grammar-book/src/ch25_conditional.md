## 25.1 What Is a Conditional Sentence?

A **conditional sentence** says that **one thing depends on another**. It has two parts:

1. the **if-clause** (the **condition**, also called the *protasis*), and
2. the **main clause** (the **result**, also called the *apodosis*).

::: ex
- **If it rains**, *we will stay at home.* (condition → result)
- *We will stay at home* **if it rains**. (the order can change; use a comma only when the *if*-clause comes first)
:::

The **tense** used in each clause shows **how real or unreal** the condition is and **what time** it refers to. This is why conditionals are tested in Right Form of Verbs, error detection, sentence completion and transformation in all our exams, and are a favourite of GMAT Sentence Correction.

## 25.2 The Main Types of Conditionals

| Type | Meaning | *If*-clause | Main clause | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Zero** | general truth, always true | Present simple | Present simple | *If you heat ice, it **melts**.* |
| **First** | real / likely future | Present simple | *will / shall / can / may* + V1 | *If it **rains**, we **will stay** home.* |
| **Second** | unreal present / unlikely future | Past simple (*were* for *be*) | *would / could / might* + V1 | *If I **were** rich, I **would buy** a car.* |
| **Third** | unreal past (impossible now) | Past perfect | *would / could / might* + *have* + V3 | *If he **had worked** hard, he **would have passed**.* |
| **Mixed** | past condition → present result, or vice versa | Past perfect / Past | *would* + V1 / *would have* + V3 | *If I **had taken** the medicine, I **would be** well now.* |

## 25.3 Zero Conditional — General Truths

***If* + present simple, present simple.** The result **always** follows the condition. *If* can be replaced by *when / whenever*.

::: ex
- If you heat water to 100 °C, it **boils**.
- If you mix red and yellow, you **get** orange.
- If people don't eat, they **die**.
- If I **drink** coffee at night, I **can't** sleep. (personal habit)
- If you **press** this button, the machine **stops**.
:::

## 25.4 First Conditional — Real or Likely Future

***If* + present simple, *will / shall / can / may / must* + V1** (or an imperative).

::: ex
- If you **work** hard, you **will pass**.
- If it **rains** tomorrow, the match **will be** cancelled.
- If he **comes**, I **shall tell** him. (traditional *shall* with *I*)
- If you **need** help, **call** me. (imperative result)
- If you **are** tired, you **may** go home.
- If you **want** to succeed, you **must** work hard.
:::

::: rule
**No *will* in the *if*-clause.** The *if*-clause uses the **present** even though it refers to the future. ✘ *If it will rain, we will stay home.* ✔ *If it **rains**, we will stay home.*

**Exceptions:** *will* is possible in the *if*-clause when it means **willingness** or **insistence**: *If you **will** help me, I'll be grateful* (= if you are willing to). *If you **will** keep eating junk food, of course you'll get fat* (= if you insist on).
:::

### 25.4.1 *Unless* = *If … not*

***Unless*** means *if … not* and is used mainly in **first** (and zero) conditionals. It must not be followed by another *not*.

::: ex
- **Unless** you work hard, you will fail. = **If** you do **not** work hard, you will fail.
- **Unless** it rains, the crops will die.
- ✘ Unless you do not hurry, you will miss the bus. ✔ **Unless** you hurry, you will miss the bus.
:::

## 25.5 Second Conditional — Unreal Present or Unlikely Future

***If* + past simple, *would / could / might* + V1.** The past tense here does **not** refer to the past; it shows that the situation is **imaginary** (contrary to present fact) or **unlikely** in the future.

::: ex
- If I **had** wings, I **would fly**. (I don't have wings.)
- If I **were** a king, I **would help** the poor. (I am not a king.)
- If he **knew** her address, he **would write** to her. (He doesn't know it.)
- If I **won** the lottery, I **would travel** round the world. (unlikely)
- If you **studied** harder, you **could get** a scholarship.
:::

::: rule
***Were* for all persons:** In the second conditional, the formal form of *be* is ***were*** for **all** subjects: *If I **were** you … If he **were** here … If it **were** possible …* (*If I was* is common in informal speech, but in exams and GMAT choose ***were***.)
:::

### 25.5.1 *If I were you* — Giving Advice

::: ex
- **If I were you**, I would accept the offer.
- **If I were in your place**, I would not go.
:::

### 25.5.2 *Were to* — Hypothetical Future

***If* + subject + *were to* + V1** makes a future event seem **very unlikely or imaginary**:

::: ex
- If the sun **were to** rise in the west, I would not change my mind.
- If war **were to** break out, millions would suffer.
:::

## 25.6 Third Conditional — Unreal Past

***If* + past perfect, *would / could / might* + *have* + V3.** It describes an **imaginary past** — what would have happened if the past had been different. It often expresses **regret** or **criticism**.

::: ex
- If he **had worked** hard, he **would have passed**. (He did not work hard; he failed.)
- If I **had known** your address, I **would have visited** you.
- If you **had left** earlier, you **could have caught** the train.
- If it **had not rained**, we **might have won** the match.
- If the doctor **had come** in time, the patient **would not have died**.
:::

::: rule
**No *would have* in the *if*-clause.** ✘ *If I would have known, I would have come.* ✔ *If I **had known**, I would have come.* ✘ *If he would have studied …* ✔ *If he **had studied** …*
:::

## 25.7 Mixed Conditionals

Sometimes the condition and the result refer to **different times**.

**(a) Past condition → present result:** *If* + past perfect, *would* + V1.

::: ex
- If I **had taken** the medicine yesterday, I **would be** well now.
- If he **had studied** medicine, he **would be** a doctor today.
- If we **had left** earlier, we **would be** there by now.
:::

**(b) Present (permanent) condition → past result:** *If* + past simple, *would have* + V3.

::: ex
- If I **were** not afraid of flying, I **would have gone** to London last year.
- If he **were** more careful, he **would not have had** that accident.
- If she **spoke** English, she **would have got** the job.
:::

## 25.8 Conditionals without *If*: Inversion

In formal English, ***if*** can be omitted and the auxiliary ***had, were*** or ***should*** placed before the subject (Chapter 16.4).

| With *if* | Inverted (without *if*) |
|---|---|
| If I **had** known … | **Had** I known … |
| If I **were** you … | **Were** I you … |
| If he **were to** come … | **Were** he **to** come … |
| If you **should** need help … | **Should** you need help … |
| If it **had not been** for you … | **Had** it **not** been for you … |
| If it **were not** for his help … | **Were** it **not** for his help … |

::: ex
- **Had** you told me, I would have helped you.
- **Were** I in your position, I would resign.
- **Should** you see him, give him my regards.
- ✘ Hadn't he helped me, I would have failed. ✔ **Had he not** helped me, I would have failed.
:::

## 25.9 Other Words and Expressions That Introduce Conditions

| Expression | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **unless** | if … not | *Unless you hurry, you'll miss the bus.* |
| **provided (that) / providing (that)** | only if | *You can go **provided** you finish your homework.* |
| **on condition that** | only if (formal) | *He agreed **on condition that** his name would not be published.* |
| **as long as / so long as** | only if; while | *You can borrow it **as long as** you return it tomorrow.* |
| **in case** | as a precaution against the possibility that | *Take an umbrella **in case** it rains.* (✘ in case it will rain) |
| **suppose / supposing** | imagine that | ***Suppose** you won the lottery, what would you do?* |
| **assuming (that) / given (that)** | if we accept that | ***Assuming** that the train is on time, we'll arrive at noon.* |
| **in the event that / in the event of** | if (formal) | ***In the event of** fire, use the stairs.* |
| **even if** | whether or not | ***Even if** it rains, I will go.* |
| **only if** | in that one case | *I will go **only if** you come with me.* (inversion if fronted: ***Only if** you come **will I** go.*) |
| **if only** | strong wish / regret | ***If only** I had listened to you!* |
| **but for** + noun | if it were not for / if it had not been for | ***But for** your help, I would have failed.* |
| **without** + noun | if there were not / had not been | ***Without** water, no one could live.* |
| **otherwise / or (else)** | if not | *Work hard; **otherwise** you will fail.* |
| **whether … or not** | in either case | *I'll go **whether** it rains **or not**.* |

### 25.9.1 *But for* and *Without*

***But for*** + **noun** (never a clause) replaces an *if*-clause in the **second** or **third** conditional:

::: ex
- **But for** your help, I **would have failed**. = If you **had not helped** me, I would have failed. = **Had it not been for** your help, I would have failed.
- **But for** the sun, there **would be** no life on earth. = If it **were not for** the sun, …
- ✘ But for you helped me … ✔ But for **your help** …
:::

### 25.9.2 *In case* vs *If*

***If*** = the result happens **only** when the condition happens. ***In case*** = we do something **now, as a precaution**, because the condition **might** happen.

::: ex
- I'll buy food **if** guests come. (I'll buy only when they come.)
- I'll buy food **in case** guests come. (I'll buy now, just in case.)
:::

## 25.10 Implied Conditionals

Sometimes the condition is **not stated** as a clause but is **implied** by a phrase, an imperative, an infinitive or the context.

::: ex
- **Work hard, and** you will succeed. (= If you work hard, you will succeed.)
- **Hurry up, or** you will miss the train. (= If you don't hurry …)
- **With a little more money**, we could buy a house. (= If we had a little more money …)
- **To hear him speak**, you would think he was a king. (= If you heard him speak …)
- I would have helped him, **but** he didn't ask me. (= If he had asked me …)
- **A true friend** would not desert you. (= If he were a true friend …)
:::

## 25.11 *Wish* and *If only*

***Wish*** and ***if only*** use the same "backshifted" tenses as unreal conditionals:

| Wish about | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| the present (unreal) | *wish / if only* + past simple (*were*) | *I wish I **knew** his number. If only I **were** taller!* |
| the past (regret) | *wish / if only* + past perfect | *I wish I **had studied** harder. If only he **had listened**!* |
| the future / annoying habit / someone else's action | *wish / if only* + *would* + V1 | *I wish it **would** stop raining. I wish you **would** stop talking.* |
| ability | *wish* + *could* | *I wish I **could** swim.* |

::: ex
- ✘ I wish I am rich. ✔ I wish I **were** rich.
- ✘ I wish I studied harder last year. ✔ I wish I **had studied** harder last year.
- ✘ I wish I would be a doctor. ✔ I wish I **were / could be** a doctor. (*would* after *wish* is not used for the subject's own state)
:::

## 25.12 Modal Verbs in Conditionals

The main clause can contain modals other than *will/would* to add meaning:

::: ex
- If you finish early, you **can** go home. (permission)
- If it rains, the match **may** be postponed. (possibility)
- If you want to pass, you **must** study. (necessity)
- If I had a car, I **could** drive you home. (ability — unreal)
- If he had run faster, he **might have** won. (possibility — unreal past)
- If you are ill, you **should** see a doctor. (advice)
:::

## 25.13 Transformation of Conditional Sentences

| Original | Transformed |
|---|---|
| *If you do not work hard, you will fail.* | ***Unless** you work hard, you will fail.* / *Work hard, **or** you will fail.* / *Work hard; **otherwise**, you will fail.* |
| *If I were rich, I would help the poor.* | ***Were** I rich, I would help the poor.* / *I am not rich, **so** I cannot help the poor.* |
| *If he had come, I would have met him.* | ***Had** he come, I would have met him.* / *He did not come, **so** I did not meet him.* |
| *If it had not been for your advice, I would have failed.* | ***But for** your advice, I would have failed.* / ***Without** your advice, I would have failed.* |
| *As he was ill, he could not attend.* | *If he **had not been** ill, he **could have** attended.* |
| *Should you need help, call me.* | ***If** you need help, call me.* |

## 25.14 Common Errors in Conditionals

::: errs
1. ✘ If it will rain, we shall stay home. ✔ If it **rains**, we shall stay home.
2. ✘ If I was a bird, I would fly. ✔ If I **were** a bird, I would fly.
3. ✘ If he would come, I would be happy. ✔ If he **came**, I would be happy.
4. ✘ If I would have known, I would have come. ✔ If I **had known**, I would have come.
5. ✘ If he had worked hard, he would pass. ✔ … he **would have passed**.
6. ✘ If you will work hard, you will succeed. ✔ If you **work** hard, you will succeed.
7. ✘ Unless you do not study, you will fail. ✔ **Unless** you study, you will fail.
8. ✘ Had I know it, I would have told you. ✔ Had I **known** it, …
9. ✘ Hadn't he come, we would have lost. ✔ **Had he not** come, we would have lost.
10. ✘ But for he helped me, I would have died. ✔ But for **his help**, I would have died.
11. ✘ If I am you, I would not go. ✔ If I **were** you, I would not go.
12. ✘ If he studied hard last year, he would have passed. ✔ If he **had studied** hard last year, …
13. ✘ Take your umbrella in case it will rain. ✔ … in case it **rains**.
14. ✘ I wish I was a doctor. ✔ I wish I **were** a doctor.
15. ✘ If you heat ice, it will melt. (general truth) ✔ If you heat ice, it **melts**. (zero conditional preferred for scientific facts; *will melt* is acceptable as a prediction)
16. ✘ Were I knew the answer, I would tell you. ✔ **If I knew** the answer, I would tell you.
17. ✘ Should you will need money, ask me. ✔ **Should you need** money, ask me.
18. ✘ If I had been rich, I would buy a car now. ✔ If I **were** rich, I **would buy** a car now. / If I had been rich last year, I would have bought a car.
19. ✘ Work hard, otherwise you fail. ✔ Work hard; otherwise you **will** fail.
20. ✘ If only I have listened to my father! ✔ If only I **had listened** to my father!
:::

## 25.15 Conditionals in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
- **GMAT's conditional rules:**
  - *If* + present → *will* + V1 (✘ *would*).
  - *If* + past → *would* + V1 (✘ *will*).
  - *If* + past perfect → *would have* + V3.
  - **Never** *would* in the *if*-clause: ✘ *If the government would lower taxes, investment would rise.* ✔ *If the government **lowered** taxes, …*
- ***If* vs *whether*:** *If* introduces conditions; *whether* introduces alternatives. ✘ *The analysts are unsure if the merger will succeed.* GMAT prefers ✔ *… unsure **whether** the merger will succeed.*
- **Subjunctive *were*:** GMAT accepts only ***were*** in hypothetical *if*-clauses (*If the company **were** to …*).
- **GRE Critical Reasoning / GMAT CR:** Conditional logic — *if A then B* does **not** mean *if B then A*. *Only if A, B* means *B → A*. *Unless A, B* means *if not A, then B*. Mastering these is essential for "must be true" and "assumption" questions.
:::
