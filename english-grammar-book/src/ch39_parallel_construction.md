[Part A · Grammar]{.chpart}

## 39.1 What Is Parallel Construction?

**Parallel construction** (parallelism) means that **similar ideas** in a sentence are expressed in **similar grammatical forms**. When two or more items are joined by *and, or, but, not only … but also, either … or*, or appear in a list or comparison, they should be of the **same type**: noun with noun, adjective with adjective, infinitive with infinitive, gerund with gerund, clause with clause.

::: ex
- ✘ She likes **swimming**, **to read** and **painting**.
- ✔ She likes **swimming**, **reading** and **painting**. (all gerunds)
- ✔ She likes **to swim**, **to read** and **to paint**. (all infinitives)

- ✘ He is **honest**, **hard-working** and **a man of intelligence**.
- ✔ He is **honest**, **hard-working** and **intelligent**. (all adjectives)
:::

Parallelism makes writing **clear, balanced and elegant**. It is one of the **most heavily tested** topics in **GMAT Sentence Correction** and **SAT Writing**, and it appears in BCS, Bank and admission error detection, especially with correlative conjunctions.

## 39.2 Where Parallelism Is Required

1. **Items in a list or series** joined by *and / or*.
2. **Pairs joined by coordinating conjunctions** (*and, but, or, nor, yet*).
3. **Correlative conjunctions** (*both … and, either … or, neither … nor, not only … but also, whether … or, not … but, rather than*).
4. **Comparisons** (*than, as … as, like, unlike, more than, less than, prefer … to*).
5. **Certain idiomatic structures** (*from … to, between … and, the more … the more*).

## 39.3 Parallelism in Lists and Series

::: ex
- ✘ The job requires **typing**, **filing** and **to answer** the phone.
- ✔ The job requires **typing**, **filing** and **answering** the phone.

- ✘ He came, **he saw** and **conquered**.
- ✔ He **came**, **saw** and **conquered**.

- ✘ The student was **intelligent**, **diligent** and **had a lot of discipline**.
- ✔ The student was **intelligent**, **diligent** and **disciplined**.

- ✘ We should eat fresh fruit, **do exercise** and **getting** enough sleep.
- ✔ We should **eat** fresh fruit, **do** exercise and **get** enough sleep.

- ✘ The report was **long**, **boring**, and **it had many errors**.
- ✔ The report was **long**, **boring** and **full of errors**.
:::

## 39.4 Parallelism with Correlative Conjunctions

::: rule
Each half of a correlative pair must be followed by the **same grammatical structure**, and each half must be placed **immediately before** the element it joins.
:::

| Correlative | ✘ Not parallel | ✔ Parallel |
|---|---|---|
| **both … and** | *She is **both** intelligent **and** a hard worker.* | *She is **both** intelligent **and** hard-working.* |
| **either … or** | *You can **either** pay **in** cash **or** a cheque.* | *You can pay **either in** cash **or by** cheque.* |
| **neither … nor** | *He **neither** wanted money **nor** to be famous.* | *He wanted **neither** money **nor** fame.* |
| **not only … but also** | *He **not only** lost his job **but also** his house.* | *He lost **not only** his job **but also** his house.* |
| **whether … or** | *I can't decide **whether** to stay **or** going.* | *I can't decide **whether** to stay **or** to go.* |
| **not … but** | *The problem is **not** money **but** that we lack time.* | *The problem is **not** money **but** time.* |
| **rather than** | *She decided to walk **rather than** taking a bus.* | *She decided **to walk rather than (to) take** a bus.* |

::: tip
**Exam Tip — The "cover-up" test:** Cover the correlative words and compare what comes directly after each one. *not only **lost his job** / but also **his house*** → verb phrase vs noun → ✘. *lost not only **his job** / but also **his house*** → noun vs noun → ✔.
:::

## 39.5 Parallelism in Comparisons

Things being compared must be **grammatically and logically parallel** (Chapter 24):

::: ex
- ✘ I like **reading** more than **to watch** TV.
- ✔ I like **reading** more than **watching** TV.

- ✘ **To err** is human; **forgiving** is divine.
- ✔ **To err** is human; **to forgive** is divine.

- ✘ **Walking** is better for you than **to drive**.
- ✔ **Walking** is better for you than **driving**.

- ✘ The climate of Sylhet is cooler than **Dhaka**.
- ✔ The climate of Sylhet is cooler than **that of Dhaka**. (climate vs climate)

- ✘ He prefers **to work** at night **to working** in the day.
- ✔ He prefers **working** at night **to working** in the day. / He prefers **to work** at night **rather than** in the day.
:::

## 39.6 Parallelism of Clauses

When clauses are joined, use the **same kind** of clause:

::: ex
- ✘ I know **that he is honest** and **his hard work**.
- ✔ I know **that he is honest** and **that he works hard**.

- ✘ The teacher told us **to read the chapter** and **that we should answer the questions**.
- ✔ The teacher told us **to read the chapter** and **(to) answer the questions**.
- ✔ The teacher told us **that we should read the chapter** and **(that we should) answer the questions**.

- ✔ He said **that he was tired** and **that he wanted** to go home. (repeating *that* makes the two reported clauses clearly parallel)
:::

## 39.7 Parallelism of Verb Forms and Tenses

::: ex
- ✘ She **was cooking** while he **watches** TV. (tense shift)
- ✔ She **was cooking** while he **was watching** TV.

- ✘ He **has finished** the report and **sent** it yesterday.
- ✔ He **finished** the report and **sent** it yesterday.

- ✘ The company **will hire** new staff and **is opening** a new branch.
- ✔ The company **will hire** new staff and **open** a new branch.
:::

## 39.8 Parallelism of Prepositions, Articles and *To*

A word that applies to **all** items in a series may be written **once** (before the first item) or **repeated before every item** — but not repeated for only some of them.

::: ex
- ✘ He is interested **in** music, art and **in** literature.
- ✔ He is interested **in** music, art and literature. / ✔ … **in** music, **in** art and **in** literature.

- ✘ I want **to** sing, dance and **to** play.
- ✔ I want **to** sing, dance and play. / ✔ I want **to** sing, **to** dance and **to** play.

- ✘ **The** teacher, principal and **the** chairman were present. (three persons)
- ✔ **The** teacher, **the** principal and **the** chairman were present.
:::

::: note
**When the preposition differs, it must be repeated:** ✘ *He is interested and good at music.* ✔ *He is interested **in** and good **at** music.* ✘ *She was fond and proud of her son.* ✔ *She was fond **of** and proud **of** her son* / *She was fond of her son and proud of him.*
:::

## 39.9 Parallelism in Idioms: *From … to*, *Between … and*

::: ex
- ✘ The shop is open **from** 9 a.m. **till** 5 p.m. — acceptable; ✔ **from** 9 a.m. **to** 5 p.m.
- ✘ **Between** 2010 **to** 2020 ✔ **Between** 2010 **and** 2020 / **From** 2010 **to** 2020
- ✘ The difference **between** the rich **with** the poor ✔ The difference **between** the rich **and** the poor
:::

## 39.10 Parallelism and Balance in Style

Parallel structure gives **rhythm and force** to famous sayings and speeches:

::: ex
- *Government **of the people, by the people, for the people**.* — Abraham Lincoln
- *I **came**, I **saw**, I **conquered**.* — Julius Caesar
- *Ask **not what your country can do for you**; ask **what you can do for your country**.* — J. F. Kennedy
- *Reading maketh **a full man**; conference **a ready man**; and writing **an exact man**.* — Francis Bacon
- *This time the struggle is **the struggle for our emancipation**; this time the struggle is **the struggle for our independence**.* — Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujibur Rahman, 7 March 1971
- *To be or not to be.* — Shakespeare
:::

## 39.11 Common Errors in Parallelism

::: errs
1. ✘ He likes reading, writing and to sing. ✔ He likes reading, writing and **singing**.
2. ✘ She is beautiful, intelligent and has kindness. ✔ She is beautiful, intelligent and **kind**.
3. ✘ He not only plays football but also cricket. ✔ He plays **not only** football **but also** cricket.
4. ✘ Either you must pay now or later. ✔ You must pay **either** now **or** later.
5. ✘ The course teaches students how to write, read and speaking clearly. ✔ … how to write, read and **speak** clearly.
6. ✘ He wanted neither money nor to be famous. ✔ He wanted **neither** money **nor** fame.
7. ✘ I prefer walking to drive. ✔ I prefer walking to **driving**.
8. ✘ She sings sweetly and with grace. ✔ She sings sweetly and **gracefully**.
9. ✘ He was asked to stand up and that he should introduce himself. ✔ He was asked to stand up and **(to) introduce** himself.
10. ✘ The manager was rude, careless, and showed no interest. ✔ The manager was rude, careless and **uninterested**.
11. ✘ To succeed you need patience, working hard and luck. ✔ … patience, **hard work** and luck.
12. ✘ He is both a good singer and dances well. ✔ He is **both** a good singer **and** a good dancer.
13. ✘ The old woman was poor, sick and had nobody to help. ✔ … poor, sick and **helpless**.
14. ✘ Swimming is more enjoyable than to run. ✔ Swimming is more enjoyable than **running**.
15. ✘ He spoke clearly, loudly and with confidence. ✔ He spoke clearly, loudly and **confidently**.
:::

## 39.12 Parallelism in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
**GMAT** tests parallelism in nearly every set of Sentence Correction questions. Key points:

1. **Identify the parallel markers:** *and, or, but, both … and, either … or, neither … nor, not only … but also, not X but Y, X rather than Y, more X than Y, as X as Y, from X to Y, between X and Y, whether X or Y, like X, unlike X, compare X with Y*.
2. **Match the form** of X and Y: noun ↔ noun, verb ↔ verb (same tense), clause ↔ clause, infinitive ↔ infinitive, *-ing* ↔ *-ing*.
3. **Logical parallelism:** the items must also be **logically comparable** (*the GDP of Bangladesh* ↔ *that of Vietnam*, not *Vietnam*).
4. **Action nouns vs gerunds:** GMAT prefers parallel **action nouns** (*the **construction** of roads and the **expansion** of ports*) over mixed forms (*the construction of roads and **expanding** ports*).
5. **"Shared" words** (*to, that, the, in*) may be written once or repeated, but consistently.

**SAT Writing** tests parallelism mainly in lists and with correlatives. **GRE Analytical Writing** rewards parallel structure as a mark of mature style.
:::
