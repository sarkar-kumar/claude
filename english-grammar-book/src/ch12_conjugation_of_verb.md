## 12.1 What Is Conjugation?

**Conjugation** means the **systematic arrangement of all the forms of a verb** — in all tenses, persons, numbers, moods and voices. When we "conjugate" a verb we list how it changes: *I write, he writes, I wrote, I have written, I am writing, it is written …*

Every English verb (except modals) has **five principal forms**:

| Form | Name | *write* | *play* | *go* | *be* |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | Base form (present / infinitive) | write | play | go | be (am, is, are) |
| V1 + s | Third-person singular present | writes | plays | goes | is |
| V2 | Past tense | wrote | played | went | was / were |
| V3 | Past participle | written | played | gone | been |
| V + ing | Present participle / gerund | writing | playing | going | being |

The past and past participle of **regular verbs** end in *-ed* and are identical (*played – played*). **Irregular verbs** form them in various ways (*write – wrote – written*). Knowing V2 and V3 accurately is essential for tense, voice, conditionals and right-form-of-verb questions.

## 12.2 Spelling Rules

### 12.2.1 Adding *-s / -es* (Third-Person Singular)

| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| Most verbs: add ***-s*** | play → plays, read → reads, write → writes |
| After *s, ss, sh, ch, x, z, o*: add ***-es*** | pass → passes, wash → washes, watch → watches, fix → fixes, buzz → buzzes, go → goes, do → does |
| Consonant + *y*: change *y* to ***-ies*** | try → tries, cry → cries, study → studies, fly → flies |
| Vowel + *y*: add ***-s*** | play → plays, say → says, obey → obeys, enjoy → enjoys |
| Irregular | have → has, be → is |

### 12.2.2 Adding *-ing*

| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| Drop silent final ***-e*** | make → making, write → writing, come → coming, take → taking |
| But keep *-e* in *-ee, -ye, -oe* and a few others | see → seeing, agree → agreeing, flee → fleeing, dye → dyeing (colour), hoe → hoeing, singe → singeing (burn), be → being |
| Change ***-ie*** to ***-y*** | die → dying, lie → lying, tie → tying, vie → vying |
| Double the final consonant after a single short stressed vowel | run → running, sit → sitting, swim → swimming, stop → stopping, begin → beginning, prefer → preferring, refer → referring, occur → occurring, admit → admitting, forget → forgetting |
| Do **not** double when the last syllable is unstressed | visit → visiting, open → opening, enter → entering, happen → happening, offer → offering, listen → listening, benefit → benefiting |
| Final ***-l*** after a short vowel doubles in **British** English (not American) | travel → travelling (AmE *traveling*), cancel → cancelling, quarrel → quarrelling, label → labelling, level → levelling |
| Final ***-c*** becomes ***-ck*** | panic → panicking, picnic → picnicking, mimic → mimicking, traffic → trafficking |
| Never double *w, x, y* | show → showing, fix → fixing, play → playing |

::: note
***Dying / dyeing*** and ***singing / singeing*** are favourite spelling traps: *The patient is **dying*** (*die*) vs *She is **dyeing** her hair* (*dye*); *He is **singing** a song* (*sing*) vs *The iron is **singeing** the cloth* (*singe* = burn slightly).
:::

### 12.2.3 Adding *-ed*

The same doubling rules apply: *stop → stopped, plan → planned, prefer → preferred, occur → occurred, admit → admitted*; but *visit → visited, open → opened, offer → offered*. Final *-e*: add only *-d* (*love → loved, agree → agreed*). Consonant + *y* → *-ied* (*try → tried, study → studied, carry → carried*); vowel + *y* → *-ed* (*play → played, obey → obeyed*; but *pay → paid, lay → laid, say → said*). *-c* → *-cked* (*panic → panicked, picnic → picnicked*). British *-l* doubling: *travelled, cancelled, quarrelled*.

### 12.2.4 Pronunciation of *-ed* and *-s*

| Ending | Sound | When | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| *-ed* | /ɪd/ | after /t/ and /d/ | wanted, needed, decided, visited |
| *-ed* | /t/ | after voiceless sounds /p, k, f, s, ʃ, tʃ/ | stopped, looked, laughed, missed, washed, watched |
| *-ed* | /d/ | after voiced sounds and vowels | played, lived, called, opened, cried |
| *-s / -es* | /ɪz/ | after /s, z, ʃ, tʃ, dʒ/ | passes, buzzes, washes, watches, judges |
| *-s* | /s/ | after voiceless /p, t, k, f/ | stops, writes, looks, laughs |
| *-s* | /z/ | after voiced sounds and vowels | plays, reads, sings, goes |

## 12.3 Conjugation of *Be*, *Have* and *Do*

| | Present | Past | Past participle | -ing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **be** | I **am**; he/she/it **is**; we/you/they **are** | I/he/she/it **was**; we/you/they **were** | **been** | **being** |
| **have** | I/we/you/they **have**; he/she/it **has** | **had** (all persons) | **had** | **having** |
| **do** | I/we/you/they **do**; he/she/it **does** | **did** (all persons) | **done** | **doing** |

## 12.4 Full Conjugation of a Verb in All Tenses

The verb ***write*** conjugated in the **active voice** (*I, he/she/it, we/you/they*):

| Tense | I | He / She / It | We / You / They |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present Indefinite | write | writes | write |
| Present Continuous | am writing | is writing | are writing |
| Present Perfect | have written | has written | have written |
| Present Perfect Continuous | have been writing | has been writing | have been writing |
| Past Indefinite | wrote | wrote | wrote |
| Past Continuous | was writing | was writing | were writing |
| Past Perfect | had written | had written | had written |
| Past Perfect Continuous | had been writing | had been writing | had been writing |
| Future Indefinite | shall/will write | will write | will write (we: shall/will) |
| Future Continuous | shall/will be writing | will be writing | will be writing |
| Future Perfect | shall/will have written | will have written | will have written |
| Future Perfect Continuous | shall/will have been writing | will have been writing | will have been writing |

The same verb in the **passive voice** (*a letter / letters*):

| Tense | Singular subject | Plural subject |
|---|---|---|
| Present Indefinite | is written | are written |
| Present Continuous | is being written | are being written |
| Present Perfect | has been written | have been written |
| Past Indefinite | was written | were written |
| Past Continuous | was being written | were being written |
| Past Perfect | had been written | had been written |
| Future Indefinite | will be written | will be written |
| Future Perfect | will have been written | will have been written |
| With modals | can / may / must / should be written | can / may / must / should be written |

::: note
The perfect continuous tenses and the future continuous have no passive forms in normal use (✘ *has been being written*). See Chapter 26.
:::

### 12.4.1 Conjugation in Other Moods and Non-Finite Forms

| Form | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Imperative | Write. / Don't write. / Let him write. | Let it be written. |
| Present subjunctive | (that) he write | (that) it be written |
| Past subjunctive | if he wrote / if I were writing | if it were written |
| Infinitive (present) | to write | to be written |
| Infinitive (continuous) | to be writing | — |
| Infinitive (perfect) | to have written | to have been written |
| Gerund / present participle | writing | being written |
| Perfect participle | having written | having been written |
| Past participle | — | written |

## 12.5 Irregular Verbs

Irregular verbs fall into four patterns. Learning them in groups is easier than learning one long list.

### 12.5.1 Pattern A–A–A (All Three Forms the Same)

*bet, bid (offer), broadcast, burst, cast, cost, cut, forecast, hit, hurt, let, put, quit, read (pronounced /ri:d/ – /red/ – /red/), rid, set, shed, shut, slit, split, spread, thrust, upset, wet*

::: ex
- He **cut** his finger yesterday. / The price **has cost** us a lot. / The news **spread** quickly. / I **have read** the book.
:::

**Exam Tip — The most commonly misused V2/V3 pairs:** *begin – began – **begun***; *drink – drank – **drunk***; *ring – rang – **rung***; *sing – sang – **sung***; *swim – swam – **swum***; *sink – sank – **sunk***; *shrink – shrank – **shrunk***; *spring – sprang – **sprung***. :::

## 12.8 Common Errors in Verb Forms

::: errs
1. ✘ He has went home. ✔ He has **gone** home.
2. ✘ I have broke the glass. ✔ I have **broken** the glass.
3. ✘ He swimmed across the river. ✔ He **swam** across the river.
4. ✘ The child was drownded. ✔ The child was **drowned**.
5. ✘ The bell has rang. ✔ The bell has **rung**.
6. ✘ She has sang a song. ✔ She has **sung** a song.
7. ✘ The ship was sank. ✔ The ship **sank** / **was sunk**.
8. ✘ He has drank the milk. ✔ He has **drunk** the milk.
9. ✘ I have wrote a letter. ✔ I have **written** a letter.
10. ✘ He had came before I left. ✔ He had **come** before I left.
11. ✘ The sun has shined all day. ✔ The sun has **shone** all day.
12. ✘ He was hung for murder. ✔ He was **hanged** for murder.
13. ✘ She has lain the book on the table. ✔ She has **laid** the book on the table.
14. ✘ The farmer has sowed the seeds. (acceptable) ✔ The farmer has **sown** the seeds. (preferred)
15. ✘ He beated the dog. ✔ He **beat** the dog.
16. ✘ The thief was catched. ✔ The thief was **caught**.
17. ✘ He has forbade me. ✔ He has **forbidden** me.
18. ✘ The water has froze. ✔ The water has **frozen**.
19. ✘ I seeked his help. ✔ I **sought** his help.
20. ✘ He striked the ball. ✔ He **struck** the ball.
21. ✘ The cloth has shrinked. ✔ The cloth has **shrunk**.
22. ✘ She weeped bitterly. ✔ She **wept** bitterly.
23. ✘ He has strove hard. ✔ He has **striven** hard.
24. ✘ The bird has flew away. ✔ The bird has **flown** away.
25. ✘ He layed down to rest. ✔ He **lay** down to rest.
26. ✘ He is dieing. ✔ He is **dying**.
27. ✘ She is writting a letter. ✔ She is **writing** a letter.
28. ✘ They are begining the work. ✔ They are **beginning** the work.
29. ✘ He has spended all his money. ✔ He has **spent** all his money.
30. ✘ He was drunken last night. ✔ He was **drunk** last night. (*drunken* only before a noun)
:::

## 12.9 Verb Forms in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
- **Wrong past participle** is an easy SAT/GMAT error: ✘ *had went, had ran, had began, has swam, has drank, has rang*.
- ***Lie / lay*** is a perennial SAT item: ✘ *The documents were laying on the desk.* ✔ *… were **lying** on the desk.* ✘ *He lay the book down.* ✔ *He **laid** the book down.*
- ***Rise / raise***, ***sit / set*** follow the same intransitive/transitive logic.
- **Consistency of tense** within a sentence (Chapter 21) depends on choosing the correct V2/V3.
- **GRE vocabulary** contains old participles used as adjectives: *wrought, laden, stricken, sodden, cloven, graven, molten, hewn*.
:::
