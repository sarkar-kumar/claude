[Part A · Grammar]{.chpart}

## 24.1 Degrees of Comparison

Adjectives and adverbs change their form to show **how much** of a quality something has compared with something else. These forms are called **degrees of comparison**. There are three:

| Degree | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive** | the simple quality; or equality between two (*as … as*) | *Rahim is **tall**. Rahim is **as tall as** Karim.* |
| **Comparative** | a higher (or lower) degree between **two** persons/things or groups | *Rahim is **taller than** Karim.* |
| **Superlative** | the highest (or lowest) degree among **three or more** | *Rahim is **the tallest** boy in the class.* |

## 24.2 Formation of Comparatives and Superlatives

### 24.2.1 Regular Comparison

| Rule | Positive → Comparative → Superlative |
|---|---|
| One-syllable words: add ***-er / -est*** | tall → taller → tallest; great → greater → greatest; small → smaller → smallest |
| Ending in *-e*: add ***-r / -st*** | wise → wiser → wisest; large → larger → largest; brave → braver → bravest |
| Short vowel + one consonant: **double** the consonant | big → bigger → biggest; hot → hotter → hottest; thin → thinner → thinnest; sad → sadder → saddest; fat → fatter → fattest |
| Consonant + *y*: change *y* to ***i*** | happy → happier → happiest; easy → easier → easiest; heavy → heavier → heaviest; dry → drier → driest |
| Vowel + *y*: keep *y* | gay → gayer → gayest; grey → greyer → greyest |
| Two-syllable words ending in *-y, -ow, -le, -er, -et*: usually ***-er / -est*** | narrow → narrower; simple → simpler; clever → cleverer; quiet → quieter; gentle → gentler; humble → humbler |
| Most other two-syllable words and all longer words: ***more / most*** | careful → more careful → most careful; beautiful → more beautiful → most beautiful; intelligent → more intelligent → most intelligent; famous → more famous → most famous |
| Adverbs in *-ly*: ***more / most*** | quickly → more quickly → most quickly; carefully → more carefully |
| Lower degree: ***less / least*** | important → less important → least important |

::: note
Some two-syllable words take either form: *common – commoner / more common; polite – politer / more polite; pleasant – pleasanter / more pleasant; stupid – stupider / more stupid*. Adjectives ending in *-ed, -ing, -ful, -less, -ous, -ish, -ive* always take *more/most*: *more tired, more boring, more useful, more famous, more active*. (✘ tireder, ✘ usefuller)
:::

### 24.2.2 Irregular Comparison

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| good / well | better | best | |
| bad / ill / badly | worse | worst | |
| much / many | more | most | *much* (uncountable), *many* (countable) |
| little | less / lesser | least | *lesser* = smaller in importance (*the lesser evil*) |
| far | farther / further | farthest / furthest | *farther* = distance; *further* = additional (and distance) |
| old | older / elder | oldest / eldest | *elder/eldest* for family members, attributive only |
| late | later / latter | latest / last | *later/latest* = time; *latter/last* = order |
| near | nearer | nearest / next | *nearest* = distance; *next* = order |
| fore | former | foremost / first | |
| in | inner | innermost / inmost | |
| out | outer / utter | outermost / utmost / uttermost | |
| up | upper | uppermost / upmost | |
| hind | hinder | hindmost | |
| top | — | topmost | |

## 24.3 Rules for the Positive Degree

**Rule 1 — Equality: *as + positive + as*.**

::: ex
- Karim is **as tall as** Rahim.
- She sings **as sweetly as** a nightingale.
- This book is **as interesting as** that one.
:::

**Rule 2 — Inequality in the negative: *not so / not as + positive + as*.** Traditional grammar prefers ***so*** in the negative.

::: ex
- Karim is **not so tall as** Rahim. (also *not as tall as*)
- ✘ Karim is so tall as Rahim. (positive sentence must use *as … as*)
:::

**Rule 3 — Multiples and fractions: *twice / three times / half + as … as*.**

::: ex
- This road is **twice as long as** that one.
- My house is **half as big as** yours.
- ✘ twice longer than (avoid in formal tests)
:::

**Rule 4 — *As many as / as much as* (countable / uncountable).**

::: ex
- He has **as many** books **as** I (have).
- She earns **as much** money **as** her husband.
:::

## 24.4 Rules for the Comparative Degree

**Rule 5 — Comparative + *than*.**

::: ex
- Gold is **heavier than** silver.
- Rina is **more intelligent than** Mina.
- He works **harder than** I (do).
:::

**Rule 6 — Never use a double comparative.**

::: ex
- ✘ He is **more taller** than me. ✔ He is **taller** than I.
- ✘ This is **more better**. ✔ This is **better**.
- ✘ **more worse**, ✘ **more easier**, ✘ **more cleverer**
:::

**Rule 7 — To intensify a comparative, use *much, far, still, even, a lot, a little, rather, considerably, a great deal, by far* — not *very*.**

::: ex
- He is **much better** today. (✘ very better)
- This is **far more expensive**.
- She is tall, but her sister is **even taller**.
:::

**Rule 8 — *Than any other* + singular / *than all other* + plural, when the subject belongs to the same group.**

The subject must be **excluded** from the group it is compared with; otherwise it is compared with itself.

::: ex
- ✘ Rahim is taller than any boy in the class. (Rahim is also a boy in the class!)
- ✔ Rahim is taller than **any other boy** in the class.
- ✔ Rahim is taller than **all other boys** in the class.
- ✔ Dhaka is bigger than **any other city** in Bangladesh.
- ✔ Dhaka is bigger than **any city** in India. (different group → no *other*)
:::

**Rule 9 — Comparison between two: *the* + comparative + *of the two*.**

::: ex
- Rahim is **the taller of the two** brothers. (✘ the tallest of the two)
- Which is **the better of the two** books?
:::

**Rule 10 — Parallel increase: *the* + comparative …, *the* + comparative.**

::: ex
- **The more** you read, **the more** you learn.
- **The higher** you climb, **the colder** it becomes.
- **The sooner, the better.** **The more, the merrier.**
:::

**Rule 11 — Gradual change: comparative + *and* + comparative.**

::: ex
- It is getting **colder and colder**.
- The situation is becoming **more and more** serious. (✘ more serious and more serious)
:::

**Rule 12 — Compare like with like (logical comparison).** Use *that of / those of* or a possessive.

::: ex
- ✘ The climate of Sylhet is cooler than Dhaka. ✔ … cooler than **that of** Dhaka.
- ✘ The streets of Dhaka are busier than Rajshahi. ✔ … busier than **those of** Rajshahi.
- ✘ My pen is better than Rahim. ✔ My pen is better than **Rahim's**.
:::

**Rule 13 — Case after *than* and *as*: subjective if a verb is understood.**

::: ex
- He is taller than **I** (am). (✘ than me — in exams)
- I like you more than **him**. (= more than I like him) / I like you more than **he** (does).
:::

**Rule 14 — Latin comparatives take *to*, not *than*** (Chapter 10): *superior to, inferior to, senior to, junior to, prior to, preferable to; prefer … to*.

**Rule 15 — *Less* with uncountables, *fewer* with plurals** (Chapter 03): *less sugar, fewer mistakes*.

**Rule 16 — Comparatives with *other than, else than, rather than*.**

::: ex
- He has no other friend **than** you.
- I would **rather** walk **than** take a rickshaw.
:::

**Rule 17 — *More of a* + noun** (degree of a quality expressed by a noun): *He is **more of a** poet **than** a scientist.*

## 24.5 Rules for the Superlative Degree

**Rule 18 — *The* + superlative + *of / in* (three or more).**

::: ex
- Rahim is **the tallest** boy **in** the class. (*in* + place/group)
- He is **the best of** all the players. (*of* + plural)
- This is **the most interesting** book I have ever read.
:::

**Rule 19 — *One of the* + superlative + plural noun.**

::: ex
- Dhaka is **one of the most crowded cities** in the world. (✘ city)
- He is **one of the best students** in the class.
:::

**Rule 20 — *A most* = very (not a real superlative).**

::: ex
- She is **a most** beautiful lady. (= a very beautiful lady)
- It was **a most** interesting story.
:::

**Rule 21 — No double superlatives.**

::: ex
- ✘ the **most** cleverest ✔ the **cleverest**
- ✘ the **most** best ✔ the **best**
:::

**Rule 22 — Superlative with ordinals: *the second / third largest*.**

::: ex
- Chattogram is **the second largest** city in Bangladesh.
:::

**Rule 23 — Absolute (non-gradable) adjectives are not compared.** *Unique, perfect, complete, round, square, empty, full, dead, ideal, infinite, eternal, universal, supreme, extreme, chief, matchless, impossible, entire, total, final, absolute.*

::: ex
- ✘ This is the **most unique** painting. ✔ This is a **unique** painting.
- ✘ more perfect, ✘ most perfect → ✔ perfect, nearly perfect
- ✘ The earth is rounder than … ✔ The earth is **round** / more nearly round …
- ✘ It was the most fatal accident of the year. ✔ It was the **worst** accident of the year / a **fatal** accident.
:::

**Rule 24 — Superlatives can be used without a comparison for emphasis**, sometimes with *ever*: *This is **the best** day **ever**. With **the greatest** pleasure.*

## 24.6 Transformation of Degree (Changing One Degree to Another)

This is a regular question in SSC, HSC, admission and BCS Written. The **meaning must stay the same**.

### 24.6.1 Superlative → Comparative → Positive (Single Highest)

| Degree | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Superlative | X is **the + superlative** + noun **in/of** … | *Rahim is **the tallest** boy in the class.* |
| Comparative | X is **comparative than any other** + singular noun … / **than all other** + plural | *Rahim is **taller than any other** boy in the class.* |
| Positive | **No other** + singular noun … is **so / as** + positive **as** X | ***No other** boy in the class is **as tall as** Rahim.* |

::: ex
- Superlative: *Dhaka is the largest city in Bangladesh.*
- Comparative: *Dhaka is larger than any other city in Bangladesh.*
- Positive: *No other city in Bangladesh is as large as Dhaka.*

- Superlative: *Mount Everest is the highest peak in the world.*
- Comparative: *Mount Everest is higher than any other peak in the world.*
- Positive: *No other peak in the world is as high as Mount Everest.*

- Superlative: *Honesty is the best policy.*
- Comparative: *Honesty is better than any other policy.*
- Positive: *No other policy is as good as honesty.*
:::

### 24.6.2 *One of the* + Superlative

| Degree | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Superlative | X is **one of the** + superlative + plural | *Nazrul is **one of the greatest** poets of Bangladesh.* |
| Comparative | X is **comparative than most other** + plural | *Nazrul is **greater than most other** poets of Bangladesh.* |
| Positive | **Very few** + plural … are **so / as** + positive **as** X | ***Very few** poets of Bangladesh are **as great as** Nazrul.* |

::: ex
- Superlative: *Shakespeare is one of the greatest dramatists in the world.*
- Comparative: *Shakespeare is greater than most other dramatists in the world.*
- Positive: *Very few dramatists in the world are as great as Shakespeare.*
:::

### 24.6.3 Comparative ↔ Positive (Two Things)

| Comparative | Positive |
|---|---|
| *Rahim is **taller than** Karim.* | *Karim is **not so / as tall as** Rahim.* |
| *Gold is **more precious than** silver.* | *Silver is **not so precious as** gold.* |
| *I am **older than** you.* | *You are **not so old as** I.* |
| *He is **not taller than** his brother.* | *His brother is **at least as tall as** he.* |

### 24.6.4 Positive *as … as* ↔ Comparative *no / not less … than*

::: ex
- Positive: *Rina is **as beautiful as** Mina.*
- Comparative: *Mina is **not more beautiful than** Rina.* / *Rina is **no less beautiful than** Mina.*

- Positive: *He is **as wise as** Solomon.*
- Comparative: *Solomon is **not wiser than** he.*
:::

### 24.6.5 Superlative with *Not* or Negative Meaning

::: ex
- Superlative: *He is **not the best** student in the class.*
- Comparative: *Some students in the class are **better than** he.*
- Positive: *He is **not so good as** some other students in the class.*

- Superlative: *Iron is the most useful of all metals.*
- Comparative: *Iron is more useful than any other metal.*
- Positive: *No other metal is as useful as iron.*
:::

### 24.6.6 Using *Few / Some* and Groups

::: ex
- Superlative: *Kolkata is one of the oldest cities in India.*
- Comparative: *Kolkata is older than most other cities in India.*
- Positive: *Very few cities in India are as old as Kolkata.*
:::

::: tip
**Exam Tip — Check three things after transformation:** (1) Did you add ***other*** in the comparative and positive forms when the subject belongs to the group? (2) Did you change the noun to **singular** after *any other* and *no other*, and to **plural** after *all other, most other, very few*? (3) Is the **meaning** unchanged?
:::

## 24.7 Some Special Points

### 24.7.1 *Elder / Older*, *Eldest / Oldest*

- *elder / eldest* — used for members of the **same family**, attributively, and **not followed by *than***: *my **elder** brother, my **eldest** son*.
- *older / oldest* — used for persons and things in general, and with *than*: *He is **older than** I. This is the **oldest** building in Dhaka.*

### 24.7.2 *Later / Latter*, *Latest / Last*

- *later / latest* = time: *I will come **later**. What is the **latest** news?*
- *latter / last* = order: *Of tea and coffee, I prefer the **latter**. This is the **last** chapter.*

### 24.7.3 *Farther / Further*, *Nearest / Next*

- *farther* = distance; *further* = additional (and distance): *Dhaka is **farther** than Gazipur. I need **further** information.*
- *nearest* = distance; *next* = order: *the **nearest** hospital; the **next** bus*.

### 24.7.4 *Less / Lesser*

- *less* = smaller amount (comparative of *little*): *less money*.
- *lesser* = smaller in importance (attributive only): *the **lesser** of two evils; a **lesser** poet*.

### 24.7.5 *Comparative* Adjectives Without *Than*

Some comparatives are used without any comparison: *the **upper** class, the **lower** court, **higher** education, the **younger** generation, **inner** meaning, the **former** prime minister, **further** studies*.

## 24.8 Common Errors in Comparison

::: errs
1. ✘ He is more stronger than me. ✔ He is **stronger** than I.
2. ✘ She is the most cleverest girl. ✔ She is the **cleverest** girl.
3. ✘ He is the tallest of the two brothers. ✔ He is the **taller** of the two brothers.
4. ✘ Rahim is taller than any boy in the class. ✔ … than any **other** boy in the class.
5. ✘ No boy in the class is as tall as Rahim. (Rahim is in the class) ✔ **No other** boy in the class …
6. ✘ He is one of the best student. ✔ He is one of the best **students**.
7. ✘ The population of Dhaka is larger than Khulna. ✔ … larger than **that of** Khulna.
8. ✘ He is very taller than his brother. ✔ He is **much** taller than his brother.
9. ✘ He is elder than me. ✔ He is **older** than I.
10. ✘ This is the most unique design. ✔ This is a **unique** design.
11. ✘ He is so tall as his father. ✔ He is **as** tall **as** his father.
12. ✘ He is superior than me. ✔ He is superior **to** me.
13. ✘ The more you earn, more you spend. ✔ **The** more you earn, **the** more you spend.
14. ✘ What is the last news? ✔ What is the **latest** news?
15. ✘ I have less friends than you. ✔ I have **fewer** friends than you.
16. ✘ He is better than all students. ✔ He is better than **all other** students.
17. ✘ It is getting more cold and more cold. ✔ It is getting **colder and colder**.
18. ✘ Iron is more useful than any metal. ✔ … than any **other** metal.
19. ✘ This road is twice longer than that. ✔ This road is **twice as long as** that.
20. ✘ My house is bigger than Karim. ✔ My house is bigger than **Karim's**.
21. ✘ He is more cleverer than wise. ✔ He is **more clever than wise**. (comparing two qualities of the same person → *more* + positive)
22. ✘ She is wiser than beautiful. ✔ She is **more wise than beautiful**.
23. ✘ He is the most perfect gentleman. ✔ He is a **perfect** gentleman.
24. ✘ Chattogram is the second large city. ✔ Chattogram is the **second largest** city.
25. ✘ He is taller than me. (exam style) ✔ He is taller than **I**.
:::

::: note
**Comparing two qualities of the same person (Rule in errors 21–22):** When two qualities of **one** thing are compared, use ***more* + positive** for both, even for short adjectives: *He is **more brave than wise**.* (✘ braver than wise)
:::

## 24.9 Comparison in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
Comparison is one of the **top three error types** in GMAT Sentence Correction and appears regularly in SAT Writing.

1. **Illogical comparison (apples vs oranges):** ✘ *The salary of a doctor is higher than a teacher.* ✔ *… higher than **that of** a teacher* / *than **a teacher's***. ✘ *Like many developing nations, Bangladesh's economy relies on exports.* ✔ *Like **those of** many developing nations, Bangladesh's economy relies on exports* / *Like many developing nations, **Bangladesh** relies on exports.*
2. **Like vs as:** *like* + noun (*Like her mother, she is a doctor*); *as* + clause (*She practises medicine, **as** her mother did*).
3. **Comparison words must be complete:** *as … as*, *more … than*, *less … than*. ✘ *as good or better than* ✔ *as good **as** or better than*.
4. **Superlatives for three or more, comparatives for two:** ✘ *the most efficient of the two plans* ✔ *the **more** efficient of the two plans*.
5. **Quantity comparisons:** *more / fewer* (countable), *more / less* (uncountable); *as many as* (countable), *as much as* (uncountable). ✘ *a greater number of water* ✔ *a greater **amount** of water*; ✘ *less cars* ✔ ***fewer** cars*.
6. **Different from (✔) / different than (avoid in GMAT)**.
7. **"Than" with complete parallel structure:** ✘ *She likes reading more than to write.* ✔ *She likes **reading** more than **writing**.*
:::
