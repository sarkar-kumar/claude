## 7.1 What Is Gender?

In grammar, **gender** is the form of a noun or pronoun that shows whether it refers to a **male**, a **female**, **either sex**, or **neither sex**. English has **natural gender**: the gender of a word follows the real sex of what it names (unlike Arabic, French or Hindi, where even a table or a river has grammatical gender).

English nouns have **four genders**:

| Gender | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Masculine** | male | man, boy, father, king, lion, cock, bull |
| **Feminine** | female | woman, girl, mother, queen, lioness, hen, cow |
| **Common** | either male or female | parent, child, friend, teacher, student, cousin, baby, person, enemy, servant, orphan |
| **Neuter** | neither male nor female (things without life) | book, table, tree, house, river, stone, pen |

In Bangladeshi exams, gender is usually tested as "*What is the feminine (or masculine) of …?*" and in pronoun reference (*his / her / its*).

## 7.2 Ways of Forming the Feminine

There are **three main ways** to show gender:

1. By using an **entirely different word**.
2. By adding a **suffix** (*-ess, -ine, -a, -trix, -ette*, etc.) to the masculine.
3. By adding a **word** before or after the noun (compound gender words).

### 7.2.1 Using a Different Word

| Masculine | Feminine | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|---|
| bachelor | spinster / maid | lord | lady |
| boar | sow | man | woman |
| boy | girl | monk / friar | nun |
| brother | sister | nephew | niece |
| buck (deer, rabbit) | doe | papa | mama |
| bull / ox | cow | ram | ewe |
| bridegroom | bride | sir | madam / dame |
| cock | hen | son | daughter |
| colt | filly | stag | hind |
| dog | bitch | drone | bee |
| drake | duck | uncle | aunt |
| earl | countess | wizard | witch |
| father | mother | king | queen |
| fox | vixen | hart | roe |
| gander | goose | horse / stallion | mare |
| gentleman | lady | husband | wife |
| cob (swan) | pen | steer / bullock | heifer |
| beau | belle | sire | dam |
| grandfather | grandmother | lad | lass |

::: note
**Meaning notes:** *colt/filly* = young male/female horse; *drake/duck* = male/female duck (the word *duck* is also common gender); *gander/goose* = male/female goose; *hart/roe* and *stag/hind* = male/female deer; *buck/doe* = male/female deer, rabbit, hare or goat; *boar/sow* = male/female pig; *ram/ewe* = male/female sheep; *steer* = young castrated bull; *heifer* = young cow that has not had a calf; *sire/dam* = father/mother of an animal; *drone* = male bee; *vixen* = female fox (also a quarrelsome woman).
:::

### 7.2.3 Other Feminine Suffixes

| Suffix | Masculine → Feminine |
|---|---|
| **-ine** | hero → heroine; Joseph → Josephine; Paul → Pauline; Augustus → Augustine (rare); landgrave → landgravine; margrave → margravine |
| **-a** | sultan → sultana; czar / tsar → czarina / tsarina; signor → signora; don → donna; Augustus → Augusta |
| **-trix** (law) | administrator → administratrix; executor → executrix; testator → testatrix; prosecutor → prosecutrix; aviator → aviatrix (old) |
| **-ette** | usher → usherette; suffragist → suffragette |
| **-euse / -ée** (French) | masseur → masseuse; chanteur → chanteuse; fiancé → fiancée; divorcé → divorcée; protégé → protégée |
| **-ina** | czar → czarina; Georgius → Georgina |
| **-en / others** | fox → vixen; bridegroom → bride (the masculine was formed from the feminine) |

::: note
**Masculine formed from feminine:** In a few pairs the *masculine* is derived from the feminine: *bride → bridegroom, widow → widower, goose → gander, duck → drake* (historically). *Widower* is a common exam item: the masculine of *widow* is **widower**.
:::

## 7.3 Common Gender

A noun that can refer to **either a male or a female** is of **common gender**:

*parent, child, baby, infant, friend, enemy, relative, relation, cousin, spouse, orphan, person, people, pupil, student, teacher, doctor, lawyer, servant, guardian, neighbour, citizen, leader, worker, clerk, cook, thief, monarch, sovereign, owner, singer, writer, artist, musician, author (modern use), deer, sheep, bird, cat, dog (in general use), fish, calf, foal, cub.*

::: ex
- My **cousin** is coming tomorrow. (male or female unknown)
- The **teacher** gave **his/her** opinion. → In formal writing: *The teacher gave **his or her** opinion*, or use the plural: *The teachers gave **their** opinions.*
- Every **student** must bring **his or her** own book. / All **students** must bring **their** own books.
:::

## 7.4 Neuter Gender and Personification

Lifeless things are **neuter** and take the pronoun ***it***: *The tree has lost **its** leaves.*

When lifeless things or ideas are **personified** (spoken of as if they were persons) — mainly in poetry and literary prose — they are given a gender:

::: rule
**Rule of personification:** Things that suggest **strength, power, violence, greatness or terror** are treated as **masculine**; things that suggest **beauty, gentleness, grace, fertility or weakness** are treated as **feminine**.
:::

| Masculine (*he*) | Feminine (*she*) |
|---|---|
| the Sun | the Moon |
| Death | the Earth |
| Time | Nature |
| War | Peace |
| Winter / Summer | Spring |
| Anger | Mercy |
| Love (in classical poetry) | Hope, Faith, Charity |
| Fear, Despair | Liberty, Justice, Fortune |
| Thunder, the Ocean (sometimes) | Religion, Virtue, Fame |
| Mountain (sometimes) | a ship, a country, a city, a university, a boat, a car (by owners) |

::: ex
- The **sun** sheds **his** light on all.
- The **moon** has hidden **her** face behind a cloud.
- **Death** lays **his** icy hand on kings.
- **Spring** has spread **her** beauty over the land.
- The **ship** lost all **her** crew. / **Bangladesh** is proud of **her** freedom fighters.
- **Nature** is kind to those who love **her**.
- **Time** and tide wait for no man.
:::

## 7.5 Gender of Animals and Babies

- Animals are normally ***it***: *The cat drank **its** milk.* But when we know the sex or feel close to an animal (pets, horses, cattle), we use ***he/she***: *Our cow has lost **her** calf.*
- Small animals and insects are usually treated as *it*, but by tradition: *the ant — **she**; the spider — **she**; the bee — **she**; the elephant/horse/dog — **he** (when sex unknown, in older usage)*.
- **Baby / child / infant** can be *it* when sex is unknown or unimportant: *The baby cried for **its** mother*; otherwise *he* or *she*.

## 7.6 Modern Gender-Neutral Usage

International English (and SAT/GRE/GMAT writing) increasingly avoids unnecessary gender marking. Many *-ess* words are now old-fashioned, and gender-neutral terms are preferred in formal writing:

| Old / gendered | Modern / neutral |
|---|---|
| chairman / chairwoman | chairperson, chair |
| spokesman | spokesperson |
| policeman / policewoman | police officer |
| fireman | firefighter |
| air hostess / steward(ess) | flight attendant, cabin crew |
| salesman / saleswoman | salesperson, sales representative |
| mankind | humanity, humankind, people |
| man-made | artificial, synthetic |
| poetess / authoress / manageress | poet / author / manager (for both) |
| actress | actor (increasingly used for both) |
| headmaster / headmistress | head teacher |
| businessman | businessperson, executive |
| cameraman | camera operator |
| workman | worker |

::: note
**Singular *they*:** Modern English often uses *they/their* for a person of unknown or unspecified gender: *Someone left **their** umbrella.* This is now accepted by major dictionaries and style guides, but **traditional Bangladeshi exams and GMAT-style formal grammar** still prefer *his or her* after singular words like *everyone, each, anyone, a person*: *Everyone must do **his or her** duty.* (Older texts use *his* alone: *Everyone must do **his** duty.*)
:::

## 7.7 Common Errors with Gender

::: errs
1. ✘ The feminine of *widow* is … ✔ *Widow* is feminine; its masculine is **widower**.
2. ✘ The feminine of *fox* is *foxess*. ✔ **vixen**.
3. ✘ The feminine of *drake* is *drakess*. ✔ **duck**.
4. ✘ The feminine of *bachelor* is *bachelorette*. ✔ **spinster / maid** (*bachelorette* is informal American).
5. ✘ The feminine of *hero* is *heroess*. ✔ **heroine** (don't confuse with *heroin*, the drug).
6. ✘ The feminine of *sultan* is *sultaness*. ✔ **sultana**.
7. ✘ The feminine of *executor* is *executress*. ✔ **executrix**.
8. ✘ The masculine of *nun* is *nunner*. ✔ **monk / friar**.
9. ✘ The feminine of *stag* is *doe*. ✔ **hind** (*doe* is the female of *buck*).
10. ✘ The feminine of *colt* is *mare*. ✔ **filly** (*mare* is the female of *horse/stallion*).
11. ✘ The tree has lost it's leaves. ✔ The tree has lost **its** leaves.
12. ✘ Everybody should love their country. (traditional exam answer) ✔ Everybody should love **his** country / **his or her** country.
13. ✘ The ship sank with all its crew (in literary style). ✔ The ship sank with all **her** crew. (both accepted; literary tradition uses *her*)
14. ✘ My cousin sister came. ✔ My **cousin** came. (*cousin* is common gender)
15. ✘ The feminine of *wizard* is *wizardess*. ✔ **witch**.
:::

## 7.8 Gender in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
- **Pronoun agreement in gender and number** is tested through antecedents: ✘ *The company and its founder celebrated **its** anniversary* (ambiguous). Make the antecedent clear.
- ***His or her*** vs ***their***: In GMAT Sentence Correction, a singular antecedent (*each, every, a person, the applicant*) needs a singular pronoun: ✘ *Each applicant must submit **their** form.* ✔ *Each applicant must submit **his or her** form*, or better: *All applicants must submit **their** forms.*
- **GRE vocabulary** includes gender-linked words such as *matriarch / patriarch, matrilineal / patrilineal, misogynist* (hater of women), *misandrist* (hater of men), *philogynist, uxorious* (excessively fond of one's wife), *avuncular* (like an uncle; kind), *matronly, virile, effeminate, androgynous*.
:::
