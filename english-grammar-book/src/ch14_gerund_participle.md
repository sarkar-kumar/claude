## 14.1 The Non-Finite Verbs (Verbals)

A **non-finite verb** (also called a **verbal**) is a form of a verb that is **not limited by person, number or tense** and cannot by itself be the main verb of a sentence. English has three:

| Verbal | Form | Works as | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Infinitive** | *to* + V1 (or bare V1) | noun, adjective, adverb | *I want **to learn**.* |
| **Gerund** | V1 + *-ing* | noun | ***Learning** is a lifelong process.* |
| **Participle** | V1 + *-ing* (present), V3 (past), *having* + V3 (perfect) | adjective; part of verb phrases | *a **learning** child; a **learned** man; **Having learnt** the rule, he …* |

Because the gerund and the present participle have the same form (*-ing*), and because the choice between gerund and infinitive after a verb is unpredictable, this chapter is among the most important for error detection, sentence completion and GMAT idioms. Since the infinitive is inseparable from the topic, it is treated here too.

## 14.2 The Infinitive

### 14.2.1 Forms of the Infinitive

| Form | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Present (simple) | to write | to be written |
| Continuous | to be writing | — |
| Perfect | to have written | to have been written |
| Perfect continuous | to have been writing | — |
| Negative | not to write | not to be written |

::: ex
- I want **to write** a letter.
- He seems **to be writing** something.
- He is said **to have written** fifty books. (earlier action)
- The letter is **to be written** today.
- I told him **not to go**. (✘ to not go — avoid in exams)
:::

### 14.2.2 Uses of the Infinitive

**(a) As a noun:**

::: ex
- **To err** is human. (subject) → more natural: *It is human **to err**.*
- I want **to go** home. (object)
- His aim is **to become** a doctor. (complement)
- He has no choice but **to resign**. (object of preposition *but*)
- I don't know **what to do / how to solve** this. (question word + infinitive)
:::

**(b) As an adjective (qualifying a noun):**

::: ex
- Give me some water **to drink**.
- He has a family **to support**.
- This is not the time **to play**.
- She was the first **to arrive** / the last **to leave** / the only one **to pass**.
- I have nothing **to say**.
:::

**(c) As an adverb:**

::: ex
- **Purpose:** *He came **to see** me. We eat **to live**.* (also *in order to / so as to*)
- **After adjectives:** *I am glad **to meet** you. The book is easy **to read**. He is sure **to come**.*
- **Result:** *He lived **to see** his grandchildren. He woke up **to find** himself famous.*
- **With *too* and *enough*:** *He is **too** weak **to walk**. She is old **enough to vote**.* (✘ too weak that he cannot walk)
:::

### 14.2.3 The Bare Infinitive (Without *to*)

The infinitive is used **without *to*** after:

1. **Modal auxiliaries:** *can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must* (but *ought to, used to, have to* keep *to*): *You must **go**.*
2. **Need** and **dare** used as modals: *You needn't **worry**. How dare you **say** so?*
3. ***Let, make, bid, have*** (= cause) + object: *Let him **go**. She made me **laugh**. I had him **repair** the fan.* (✘ made me to laugh)
4. **Verbs of perception** — *see, hear, feel, watch, notice, observe, behold* + object (for a **complete** action): *I saw him **cross** the road. I heard her **sing**.*
5. ***Had better, would rather, would sooner, rather than, cannot but, do nothing but, but, except, than***: *You had better **leave**. I would rather **die** than **beg**. I cannot but **laugh**. He did nothing but **cry**.*
6. ***Help*** — with or without *to*: *Help me **(to) carry** this box.*
7. ***Why / why not***: *Why **worry**? Why not **try** again?*

::: note
**In the passive, *to* comes back:** *They made him work.* → *He **was made to** work.* *I saw him enter.* → *He **was seen to** enter.* *Let* has no direct passive: *Let him go* → *He **was allowed to** go*.
:::

### 14.2.4 Verbs Followed by the Infinitive

| Verbs + *to*-infinitive |
|---|
| afford, agree, aim, appear, arrange, ask, attempt, beg, care, choose, claim, consent, dare, decide, decline, demand, deserve, determine, endeavour, expect, fail, guarantee, happen, hesitate, hope, intend, learn, long, manage, mean (= intend), need, neglect, offer, plan, prepare, pretend, proceed, promise, prove, refuse, resolve, seek, seem, strive, swear, tend, threaten, undertake, venture, volunteer, vow, wait, want, wish, yearn |

::: ex
- He **decided to** resign. (✘ decided resigning)
- They **refused to** pay. / She **promised to** help. / I **hope to** see you soon.
- He **pretended to** be ill. / We **can't afford to** buy a car.
:::

**Verb + object + *to*-infinitive:** *advise, allow, ask, beg, cause, challenge, command, compel, enable, encourage, expect, force, forbid, get, hire, instruct, invite, lead, oblige, order, permit, persuade, remind, request, require, teach, tell, tempt, urge, want, warn, would like*.

::: ex
- The teacher **advised us to** read newspapers.
- My father **allowed me to** go.
- The rain **forced us to** stay at home.
- This scholarship **enabled her to** study abroad.
- ✘ He suggested me to go. ✔ He **suggested that I (should) go** / He **suggested going**. (*suggest* does not take object + infinitive)
:::

### 14.2.5 *To* as a Preposition: The Great Trap

In many expressions ***to*** is a **preposition**, not part of an infinitive. A preposition must be followed by a **noun or gerund**, so these expressions take **V + *-ing***.

| Expression (+ *-ing*) | Example |
|---|---|
| look forward to | *I **look forward to hearing** from you.* (✘ to hear) |
| be used to / get used to / be accustomed to | *He **is used to working** at night.* |
| object to / have an objection to | *I **object to being** treated like this.* |
| with a view to | *He went to London **with a view to studying** law.* |
| in addition to | ***In addition to teaching**, she writes novels.* |
| be committed / dedicated / devoted to | *She **is devoted to helping** the poor.* |
| confess to / admit to | *He **confessed to stealing** the money.* |
| be addicted to / be given to | *He **is addicted to smoking**.* |
| be opposed to / be averse to | *I **am opposed to cutting** trees.* |
| prefer … to | *I **prefer walking to cycling**.* |
| take to (= start a habit) | *He **has taken to drinking**.* |
| contribute to / lead to | *Hard work **leads to succeeding**.* (more natural: *leads to success*) |
| the key to / an approach to | *the **key to solving** the problem* |
| when it comes to | ***When it comes to cooking**, she is the best.* |
| resort to | *They **resorted to using** force.* |
| be equal to / be close to | *He is not **equal to doing** the job.* |
| what do you say to | *What do you say **to going** for a walk?* |

::: tip
**Exam Tip — The *to*-test:** If you can put a **noun** after the *to* (e.g. *I look forward to **the holiday***), then *to* is a preposition, and a verb after it must be in the **-ing** form (*I look forward to **seeing** you*). If only a verb fits (*I want to **go***), it is an infinitive.
:::

### 14.2.6 The Split Infinitive

A **split infinitive** puts an adverb between *to* and the verb: *to **boldly** go, to **fully** understand*. Traditional grammar (and Bangladeshi exams) treats it as an error; modern grammar accepts it when it avoids awkwardness. In exams, prefer: *to understand **fully** / **fully** to understand*.

## 14.3 The Gerund

### 14.3.1 What Is a Gerund?

A **gerund** is the ***-ing*** form of a verb used as a **noun**. It is half verb, half noun — hence it is also called a **verbal noun** in some books.

- **As a verb,** it can take an **object** and be modified by an **adverb**: ***Reading** books **regularly** improves vocabulary.*
- **As a noun,** it can be a **subject, object, complement** or **object of a preposition**, and can be preceded by a possessive: ***His** reading is fluent.*

### 14.3.2 Uses of the Gerund

| Function | Example |
|---|---|
| Subject | ***Swimming** is good exercise. **Smoking** is injurious to health.* |
| Object of a verb | *I enjoy **swimming**. He gave up **smoking**.* |
| Complement | *My hobby is **gardening**. Seeing is **believing**.* |
| Object of a preposition | *He is fond of **reading**. She left without **saying** goodbye.* |
| Apposition | *His hobby, **collecting stamps**, is expensive.* |
| In compound nouns | *a **swimming** pool, a **walking** stick, a **dining** table, **drinking** water* |
| After *no* (prohibition) | ***No smoking.** **No parking.*** |

### 14.3.3 Forms of the Gerund

| Form | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Simple | writing | being written |
| Perfect | having written | having been written |

::: ex
- He hates **being disturbed**. (passive)
- He denied **having stolen** the money. (earlier action)
- She was proud of **having been chosen**.
:::

### 14.3.4 Verbs Followed by the Gerund

| Verbs + gerund (V-*ing*) |
|---|
| admit, anticipate, appreciate, avoid, can't help, can't stand, complete, consider, defer, delay, deny, detest, discuss, dislike, endure, enjoy, escape, excuse, fancy, finish, forgive, give up, imagine, involve, keep (on), mention, mind, miss, pardon, postpone, practise, prevent, put off, quit, recall, recollect, recommend, report, resent, resist, risk, stop, suggest, tolerate, understand |

::: ex
- He **avoided meeting** me. (✘ avoided to meet)
- I **enjoy reading** detective novels. (✘ enjoy to read)
- Would you **mind opening** the window? (✘ mind to open)
- She **finished writing** the report.
- He **denied taking** the money.
- I **can't help laughing** at his jokes. (✘ can't help to laugh)
- They **postponed holding** the meeting.
- Have you **considered changing** your job?
- He **suggested going** by train.
- **Keep trying** — you will succeed.
:::

**Expressions followed by the gerund:**

*it's no use, it's no good, it's (not) worth, there's no point (in), have difficulty / trouble (in), be busy, spend / waste (time / money), feel like, can't bear, be worth, be fond of, be good at, be interested in, be tired of, be afraid of, be capable of, be accustomed to, be used to, look forward to, insist on, succeed in, prevent from, refrain from, abstain from, think of, dream of, apologise for, thank for, accuse of, suspect of, approve of, depend on, instead of, in spite of, without, besides, after, before, on.*

::: ex
- **It's no use crying** over spilt milk.
- This book **is worth reading**.
- **There is no point in arguing** with him.
- I **had difficulty (in) finding** the house.
- He **spends** a lot of time **playing** games.
- I **don't feel like eating**.
- He **succeeded in passing** the exam. (✘ succeeded to pass)
- He **insisted on paying** the bill. (✘ insisted to pay)
- She is **capable of doing** it. (✘ capable to do)
- He was accused **of stealing**.
- **On hearing** the news, she fainted.
- **Before leaving**, he locked the door.
:::

### 14.3.5 Possessive before the Gerund

In formal English, a noun or pronoun **before a gerund** takes the **possessive** form, because the gerund is a noun.

::: ex
- I don't like **his** coming late. (formal) / I don't like **him** coming late. (informal)
- Please excuse **my** being late. (✘ excuse me being late — exam answer)
- We were surprised at **Rahim's** winning the prize.
- Do you mind **my** smoking?
:::

### 14.3.6 Gerund vs Verbal Noun

When an *-ing* word is preceded by ***the*** and followed by ***of***, it is a pure **noun** (sometimes called a *verbal noun*), not a gerund, and cannot take a direct object:

::: ex
- **Writing** letters is his job. (gerund — takes object *letters*)
- **The writing of** letters is his job. (verbal noun — *of* needed)
- ✘ The writing letters is his job. ✘ Writing of letters is his job.
:::

## 14.4 Gerund or Infinitive? Verbs That Change Meaning

Some verbs take **both** structures, sometimes with **no change** of meaning and sometimes with a **big change**.

### 14.4.1 Little or No Change in Meaning

*begin, start, continue, intend, attempt, love, like, hate, prefer, can't bear, can't stand*: *It began **to rain** / began **raining**. I like **to swim** / like **swimming**.* (*Like* + *-ing* = enjoy; *like* + *to* = think it is a good idea: *I like **to visit** the dentist twice a year* — I don't enjoy it.)

### 14.4.2 Important Change in Meaning

| Verb | + *to*-infinitive | + gerund (-*ing*) |
|---|---|---|
| **remember** | remember a **duty** before doing it: *Remember **to post** the letter.* | remember a **past** action: *I remember **posting** the letter.* |
| **forget** | forget a duty: *I forgot **to lock** the door.* (didn't lock) | forget a past event: *I'll never forget **meeting** the President.* |
| **stop** | stop **in order to** do something: *He stopped **to smoke**.* (he started smoking) | **end** an action: *He stopped **smoking**.* (gave up) |
| **try** | **make an effort**: *Try **to lift** this box.* | **experiment**: *Try **adding** some salt — it may taste better.* |
| **regret** | regret to **announce/inform** (now): *I regret **to inform** you that you failed.* | regret a **past** action: *I regret **telling** him the secret.* |
| **go on** | change to a new activity: *After the speech, he went on **to sing** a song.* | continue the same activity: *He went on **talking** for an hour.* |
| **mean** | **intend**: *I meant **to call** you.* | **involve**: *This job means **travelling** a lot.* |
| **need** | active: *I need **to clean** the room.* | passive meaning: *The room needs **cleaning*** (= needs to be cleaned). |
| **allow / permit / advise / recommend / forbid** | with an object: *They allow **us to smoke**.* | without an object: *They allow **smoking** here.* |
| **see / hear / watch / feel** + object | bare infinitive = **complete** action: *I saw him **cross** the road.* | *-ing* = action **in progress**: *I saw him **crossing** the road.* |
| **afraid** | afraid **to** do = unwilling because of fear: *She is afraid **to go** out at night.* | afraid **of** doing = fear that something will happen: *She is afraid **of falling**.* |
| **sorry** | sorry **to** = for a present situation: *Sorry **to disturb** you.* | sorry **for** = for a past action: *I'm sorry **for shouting** at you.* |

## 14.5 The Participle

A **participle** is a verb form that works partly as a **verb** and partly as an **adjective** (it *participates* in both).

| Participle | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Present participle** | V + *-ing* (active, continuing) | *a **running** stream; the **rising** sun* |
| **Past participle** | V3 (passive / completed) | *a **broken** chair; **fallen** leaves; a **written** test* |
| **Perfect participle** | *having* + V3 (completed before another action) | ***Having finished** the work, he went out.* |
| **Perfect passive participle** | *having been* + V3 | ***Having been warned**, he was careful.* |

### 14.5.1 Uses of Participles

**(a) As adjectives:** *a **sleeping** baby, a **barking** dog, a **lost** child, **boiled** eggs, the **wounded** soldier, an **interesting** book.*

**(b) In forming tenses and the passive:** *He **is reading**. They **have finished**. The thief **was caught**.*

**(c) In participial phrases** (to shorten adverb or adjective clauses):

::: ex
- **Walking** along the road, I met an old friend. (= *While I was walking …* — time)
- **Being** ill, he could not attend the meeting. (= *As he was ill …* — reason)
- **Turning** to the left, you will find the post office. (= *If you turn …* — condition)
- **Having finished** his work, he went to bed. (= *After he had finished …*)
- **Written** in simple English, the book is easy to read. (= *As it is written …*)
- The man **standing** at the gate is my uncle. (= *who is standing* — adjective clause)
- The goods **ordered** last week have arrived. (= *which were ordered*)
:::

**(d) After verbs of perception and *keep, leave, find, catch, have, get*:**

::: ex
- I saw him **running**. / I heard her **singing**.
- He kept me **waiting** for an hour.
- They left the door **open** / the work **unfinished**.
- I found him **sleeping**. / The police caught him **stealing**.
- I **had** my hair **cut**. / She **got** her watch **repaired**. (causative — someone else does it)
:::

### 14.5.2 The Absolute Participle (Nominative Absolute)

When a participle has **its own subject** (different from the subject of the main verb), the phrase is called an **absolute phrase** or **nominative absolute**. It is grammatically independent of the rest of the sentence.

::: ex
- **The sun having set**, we returned home.
- **The weather being fine**, we went for a picnic.
- **God willing**, we shall meet again.
- **Weather permitting**, the match will be held tomorrow.
- **All things considered**, it was a good result.
- **The work done**, they went home.
- **Time permitting**, I shall visit you.
:::

::: note
**Absolute phrase vs dangling participle:** In an absolute phrase the participle has **its own subject** (*The sun having set*). If the participle has **no subject of its own**, it automatically refers to the **subject of the main clause** — and if that makes no sense, the participle is **dangling** (Chapter 29): ✘ ***Having set**, we returned home.* (Did *we* set?) ✔ ***The sun having set**, we returned home.*
:::

### 14.5.3 Fixed Participle Expressions (Accepted Without a Subject)

Some participle phrases are so common that they are accepted even though they have no subject: *generally speaking, strictly speaking, judging from/by, considering, taking everything into account, provided (that), supposing, granting (that), roughly speaking, talking of*.

::: ex
- **Generally speaking**, Bangladeshis are hospitable.
- **Judging by** his accent, he is from Chattogram.
- **Considering** his age, he did very well.
:::

## 14.6 How to Tell a Gerund from a Participle

Both end in *-ing*. Ask what the *-ing* word is **doing**:

| Test | Gerund | Present participle |
|---|---|---|
| Function | noun (subject, object, after a preposition) | adjective (describes a noun) or part of a verb |
| Meaning of *-ing* + noun | noun is **for** the action | noun **does** the action |
| Example | ***Swimming** is healthy. a **swimming** pool (pool for swimming)* | *a **swimming** fish (fish that swims); He is **swimming**.* |
| Replace with a noun | ✔ ***Exercise** is healthy.* | ✘ — replace with an adjective: *a **dead** fish* |

::: ex
- **Dancing** is her passion. → gerund (subject)
- The **dancing** girl smiled. → participle (girl who dances)
- I like **dancing**. → gerund (object)
- She is **dancing**. → participle (part of the verb *is dancing*)
- a **dancing** hall → gerund (hall for dancing)
- a **sleeping** pill → gerund; a **sleeping** dog → participle
:::

## 14.7 Common Errors with Gerunds, Infinitives and Participles

::: errs
1. ✘ I look forward to hear from you. ✔ I look forward to **hearing** from you.
2. ✘ He is used to work hard. ✔ He is used to **working** hard.
3. ✘ I avoided to meet him. ✔ I avoided **meeting** him.
4. ✘ Would you mind to close the door? ✔ Would you mind **closing** the door?
5. ✘ He enjoys to swim. ✔ He enjoys **swimming**.
6. ✘ She made me to cry. ✔ She made me **cry**.
7. ✘ Let him to go. ✔ Let him **go**.
8. ✘ You had better to leave. ✔ You had better **leave**.
9. ✘ He suggested me to see a doctor. ✔ He suggested **that I see / should see** a doctor.
10. ✘ He insisted to pay the bill. ✔ He insisted **on paying** the bill.
11. ✘ He succeeded to win the race. ✔ He succeeded **in winning** the race.
12. ✘ She is capable to do it. ✔ She is capable **of doing** it.
13. ✘ It is no use to cry. ✔ It is no use **crying**.
14. ✘ This book is worth to read. ✔ This book is worth **reading**.
15. ✘ He went to Dhaka with a view to get a job. ✔ … with a view to **getting** a job.
16. ✘ I can't help to laugh. ✔ I can't help **laughing**.
17. ✘ He is too weak that he cannot walk. ✔ He is **too weak to walk**.
18. ✘ He prevented me to go. ✔ He prevented me **from going**.
19. ✘ Excuse me being late. ✔ Excuse **my** being late.
20. ✘ He stopped to smoke last year. (meaning *gave up*) ✔ He stopped **smoking** last year.
21. ✘ I remember to see him last year. ✔ I remember **seeing** him last year.
22. ✘ Being a rainy day, we stayed at home. ✔ **It being** a rainy day, we stayed at home.
23. ✘ The writing letters is easy. ✔ **Writing** letters is easy. / **The writing of** letters …
24. ✘ He was made work. ✔ He was made **to work**.
25. ✘ I saw him to go out. ✔ I saw him **go / going** out.
26. ✘ The room needs to clean. ✔ The room needs **cleaning / to be cleaned**.
27. ✘ He is busy to prepare for the exam. ✔ He is busy **preparing** for the exam.
28. ✘ I have no objection to sign it. ✔ I have no objection to **signing** it.
29. ✘ They decided going. ✔ They decided **to go**.
30. ✘ I am looking forward to meet you. ✔ I am looking forward to **meeting** you.
:::

## 14.8 Gerunds and Infinitives in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
**GMAT Idioms with infinitives and gerunds** — memorise the correct pattern:

| Correct (✔) | Wrong (✘) |
|---|---|
| ability **to** do | ability **of** doing |
| attempt **to** do | attempt **at** doing (in most contexts) |
| capable **of** doing | capable **to** do |
| consider X Y | consider X **as** Y / consider X **to be** Y (GMAT prefers no *as*) |
| credit X **with** doing | credit X **for** doing / **as** doing |
| decide **to** do | decide **on** doing (in GMAT) |
| forbid X **to** do | forbid X **from** doing |
| prohibit X **from** doing | prohibit X **to** do |
| require X **to** do | require X **doing** |
| encourage / persuade / enable X **to** do | encourage X **in** doing |
| responsible **for** doing | responsible **to** do |
| insist **on** doing / insist **that** X do | insist **to** do |
| be dedicated / devoted / committed **to** doing | dedicated **to** do |
| in order **to** do | in order **for** doing |
| so as **to** do | so as **for** doing |
| a means **of** doing | a means **to** doing |
| the effect **of** X **on** Y | the effect **of** X **to** Y |

- **Parallelism:** gerunds must be parallel with gerunds, infinitives with infinitives: ✘ *She likes **swimming**, **to jog** and **riding**.* ✔ *She likes **swimming**, **jogging** and **riding**.*
- **Action noun vs gerund (GMAT):** prefer *the **development** of* to *the **developing** of*.
- **Dangling participles** — see Chapter 29; one of GMAT's most frequent errors.
:::
