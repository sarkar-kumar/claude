## 28.1 What Is a Tag Question?

A **tag question** (question tag) is a **short question added at the end of a statement**. Speakers use it to **check information** or to **ask for agreement**. It is very common in spoken English.

::: ex
- You are a student, **aren't you?**
- He doesn't smoke, **does he?**
- They went to Sylhet, **didn't they?**
- She can swim, **can't she?**
:::

The meaning depends on **intonation**:

- **Falling intonation** ↘ — the speaker is fairly sure and expects agreement: *It's a lovely day, isn't it?* ↘
- **Rising intonation** ↗ — a real question; the speaker is not sure: *You are coming tomorrow, aren't you?* ↗

## 28.2 The Basic Rules

::: rule
1. **Positive statement → negative tag**; **negative statement → positive tag.**
2. The tag uses the **same auxiliary** (or *be* / modal) as the statement.
3. If the statement has **no auxiliary**, use ***do / does / did*** in the tag, according to tense and subject.
4. The subject of the tag is always a **pronoun** that refers to the subject of the statement.
5. A negative tag is **contracted**: *isn't, aren't, don't, doesn't, didn't, haven't, can't, won't, shouldn't …*
6. A comma comes before the tag, and a **question mark** after it.
:::

| Statement | Tag |
|---|---|
| He **is** a doctor, | **isn't he?** |
| He **isn't** a doctor, | **is he?** |
| They **were** late, | **weren't they?** |
| You **have** finished, | **haven't you?** |
| She **can** sing, | **can't she?** |
| He **will** come, | **won't he?** |
| You **should** go, | **shouldn't you?** |
| He **plays** cricket, | **doesn't he?** (no auxiliary → *does*) |
| They **play** cricket, | **don't they?** |
| She **went** home, | **didn't she?** |
| He **doesn't** play, | **does he?** |
| She **didn't** go, | **did she?** |
| Rina **has been** working, | **hasn't she?** |
| The book **was written** by Tagore, | **wasn't it?** |

## 28.3 Special Rules

### 28.3.1 *I am* → *aren't I?*

The negative tag for *I am* is ***aren't I?*** (✘ amn't I? — not standard; *am I not?* is formal).

::: ex
- I am right, **aren't I?**
- I am not late, **am I?**
:::

### 28.3.2 Imperative Sentences

| Statement | Tag | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Open the door, | **will you? / won't you? / would you? / can you? / could you?** | request / order |
| Shut up, | **will you?** | impatient order |
| Don't make a noise, | **will you?** | negative imperative → *will you* only |
| Have some tea, | **won't you?** | invitation |
| Please help me, | **will you?** | request |

### 28.3.3 *Let's* and *Let me / him*

::: ex
- **Let's** go for a walk, **shall we?** (suggestion including the speaker)
- **Let us** go, **shall we?** (= let's)
- **Let me** help you, **will you?** (asking permission)
- **Let him** go, **will you?** (request to the listener)
- **Let's not** quarrel, **shall we?**
:::

### 28.3.4 *There is / There are*

The tag uses ***there***:

::: ex
- There **is** a book on the table, **isn't there?**
- There **are** many students in the hall, **aren't there?**
- There **was** no one at home, **was there?**
:::

### 28.3.5 *This / That / These / Those* and *Everything / Nothing*

::: ex
- **This** is your pen, **isn't it?**
- **That** was a good film, **wasn't it?**
- **These** are your books, **aren't they?**
- **Those** were happy days, **weren't they?**
- **Everything** is ready, **isn't it?**
- **Nothing** happened, **did it?** (*nothing* is negative → positive tag)
:::

### 28.3.6 *Everybody, Somebody, Nobody, Everyone, Anyone, No one*

The tag uses ***they*** (with a plural verb):

::: ex
- **Everybody** was present, **weren't they?**
- **Somebody** called me, **didn't they?**
- **Nobody** knows the answer, **do they?**
- **No one** came, **did they?**
- **Neither** of them came, **did they?**
:::

### 28.3.7 Negative and Semi-Negative Words

A statement containing ***never, seldom, rarely, hardly, scarcely, barely, few, little, no, none, nobody, nothing, nowhere, neither*** is **negative in meaning**, so it takes a **positive tag**:

::: ex
- He **never** tells a lie, **does he?**
- She **seldom** comes here, **does she?**
- He can **hardly** walk, **can he?**
- **Few** people came, **did they?**
- There is **little** water in the jar, **is there?**
- He has **no** money, **has he?** (BrE) / **does he?** (AmE)
:::

::: note
***A few* and *a little*** are **positive** in meaning, so they take a **negative** tag: *There are **a few** mangoes, **aren't there?** He has **a little** money, **hasn't he / doesn't he?***
:::

### 28.3.8 *Have* as a Main Verb

When ***have*** means **possess**, British English can use *have* in the tag; American English (and modern usage) uses ***do***. When *have* means **take / eat / experience**, use ***do***.

::: ex
- He **has** a car, **hasn't he?** (BrE) / **doesn't he?** (AmE and modern)
- You **have got** a pen, **haven't you?**
- She **has** breakfast at 8, **doesn't she?** (*have* = eat)
- They **had** a good time, **didn't they?** (*have* = experience)
:::

### 28.3.9 Other Special Verbs

| Statement | Tag |
|---|---|
| He **used to** live here, | **didn't he?** (✘ usedn't he — old) |
| You **ought to** go, | **oughtn't you?** / **shouldn't you?** |
| You **had better** go, | **hadn't you?** |
| You **'d better** hurry, | **hadn't you?** |
| He **'d rather** stay, | **wouldn't he?** (*'d* = *would*) |
| She **'d** finished, | **hadn't she?** (*'d* = *had*, followed by V3) |
| You **must** come, | **mustn't you?** (obligation) |
| He **must** be ill, | **isn't he?** (deduction → tag follows the main verb *be*) |
| He **must** have left, | **hasn't he?** (deduction about the past) |
| You **needn't** go, | **need you?** |
| He **dare not** go, | **dare he?** |
| **One** should be careful, | **shouldn't one?** |

### 28.3.10 Complex Sentences

The tag normally agrees with the **main clause**:

::: ex
- He said that he was ill, **didn't he?**
- You know where he lives, **don't you?**
- I know that he is honest, **don't I?**
:::

But when the main clause is ***I think / I believe / I suppose / I am sure / I feel / I guess*** (expressing an opinion), the tag agrees with the **subordinate clause**:

::: ex
- **I think** he is honest, **isn't he?** (✘ don't I?)
- **I believe** she will come, **won't she?**
- **I don't think** he is right, **is he?** (the negative is transferred)
- **I am sure** you know her, **don't you?**
:::

### 28.3.11 Exclamatory and Optative Sentences

::: ex
- **What** a beautiful day, **isn't it?**
- **How** clever the boy is, **isn't he?**
- **May** you live long! (Optative sentences normally take no tag.)
:::

### 28.3.12 Compound Sentences

In compound sentences, the tag normally agrees with the **nearer (last) clause**:

::: ex
- He is poor, but he is honest, **isn't he?**
- I called him, but he didn't answer, **did he?**
:::

## 28.4 Same-Way (Positive–Positive) Tags

A **positive statement with a positive tag** is used to show **interest, surprise, doubt, sarcasm or a conclusion** — not to ask for agreement:

::: ex
- So you are leaving, **are you?** (surprise/interest)
- Oh, you've passed, **have you?** Congratulations!
- You think you're clever, **do you?** (sarcasm)
:::

## 28.5 Answering Tag Questions

Answer according to the **facts**, not the form of the question. (Bangla speakers often reverse *Yes* and *No* here.)

::: ex
- "You aren't a teacher, are you?" — "**No, I'm not.**" (I am not a teacher.) / "**Yes, I am.**" (I am a teacher.)
- "He didn't come, did he?" — "**No, he didn't.**" (He did not come.)
- "She can swim, can't she?" — "**Yes, she can.**"
:::

## 28.6 Common Errors in Tag Questions

::: errs
1. ✘ He is a doctor, is he? ✔ He is a doctor, **isn't he?**
2. ✘ She plays well, isn't she? ✔ She plays well, **doesn't she?**
3. ✘ They went home, don't they? ✔ They went home, **didn't they?**
4. ✘ I am right, am not I? ✔ I am right, **aren't I?**
5. ✘ Let's go, will we? ✔ Let's go, **shall we?**
6. ✘ Don't do it, won't you? ✔ Don't do it, **will you?**
7. ✘ He never smokes, doesn't he? ✔ He never smokes, **does he?**
8. ✘ Nobody came, didn't they? ✔ Nobody came, **did they?**
9. ✘ Everyone was happy, wasn't he? ✔ Everyone was happy, **weren't they?**
10. ✘ There is a pen, isn't it? ✔ There is a pen, **isn't there?**
11. ✘ Rahim is a good boy, isn't Rahim? ✔ … **isn't he?** (pronoun in tag)
12. ✘ Few people know it, don't they? ✔ Few people know it, **do they?**
13. ✘ He used to smoke, usedn't he? ✔ He used to smoke, **didn't he?**
14. ✘ You had better go, didn't you? ✔ You had better go, **hadn't you?**
15. ✘ I think he is honest, don't I? ✔ I think he is honest, **isn't he?**
16. ✘ This is your bag, isn't this? ✔ This is your bag, **isn't it?**
17. ✘ He can hardly walk, can't he? ✔ He can hardly walk, **can he?**
18. ✘ Nothing is impossible, isn't it? ✔ Nothing is impossible, **is it?**
19. ✘ She has finished her work, didn't she? ✔ She has finished her work, **hasn't she?**
20. ✘ Shut the door, do you? ✔ Shut the door, **will you?**
:::

## 28.7 Tag Questions in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
Tag questions belong to **spoken** English and are not tested in GMAT or GRE. They may appear in **SAT Reading** literary passages and dialogues, where a tag (*"You knew all along, didn't you?"*) reveals the speaker's **attitude** (certainty, accusation, sarcasm). In formal writing, avoid tag questions altogether.
:::
