## 54.1 About This Chapter

## 54.2 Translation: Bangla to English

Translation is compulsory in the BCS Written English paper and appears in Bank written tests and some admission tests. A good translation is **accurate** (same meaning), **grammatical** and **natural** (it reads like original English, not like Bangla in English words).

### 54.2.1 Ten Principles of Good Translation

::: rule
1. **Translate the meaning, not the words.** Read the whole Bangla passage first and understand it fully.
2. **Find the subject and the main verb** of each Bangla sentence. Bangla is **S–O–V** (*আমি ভাত খাই* — I rice eat); English is **S–V–O** (*I eat rice*).
3. **Choose the correct tense** (see 54.2.2); Bangla tense markers do not always match English tenses.
4. **Supply articles** (*a, an, the*), which Bangla does not have.
5. **Supply the verb *be*** in nominal sentences: *সে ডাক্তার* → *He **is** a doctor.*
6. **Use the correct preposition** (Bangla postpositions *-এ, -তে, -থেকে, -দিয়ে* map to many English prepositions).
7. **Translate idioms and proverbs with English equivalents** (Chapters 48 and 53), not word by word.
8. **Break very long Bangla sentences** into two or three English sentences if needed.
9. **Use formal register** for formal passages (newspaper, essay) and simple, natural English for conversation.
10. **Revise:** check agreement, tense consistency, articles, spelling and punctuation.
:::

### 54.2.2 Bangla Verb Endings and English Tenses

| Bangla form | English tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| করি, করে, করো (habitual) | Present Indefinite | আমি প্রতিদিন হাঁটি। — *I walk every day.* |
| করছি, করছে (now) | Present Continuous | সে পড়ছে। — *He is reading.* |
| করেছি, করেছে | Present Perfect | আমি কাজটি শেষ করেছি। — *I have finished the work.* |
| করছি … ধরে / থেকে | Present Perfect Continuous | আমি দুই ঘণ্টা ধরে অপেক্ষা করছি। — *I have been waiting for two hours.* |
| করলাম, করল, করেছিলাম (definite past) | Past Indefinite | গতকাল সে এসেছিল। — *He came yesterday.* |
| করছিলাম, করছিল | Past Continuous | আমি যখন পড়ছিলাম তখন বিদ্যুৎ চলে গেল। — *The light went out while I was reading.* |
| করেছিলাম (earlier of two past) | Past Perfect | ডাক্তার আসার আগেই রোগীটি মারা গিয়েছিল। — *The patient had died before the doctor came.* |
| করতাম (past habit) | used to / would / Past Indefinite | ছোটবেলায় আমি নদীতে সাঁতার কাটতাম। — *I used to swim in the river as a child.* |
| করব, করবে | Future Indefinite | আমি কাল যাব। — *I shall / will go tomorrow.* |
| করতে থাকব | Future Continuous | কাল এ সময় আমি ট্রেনে থাকব। — *At this time tomorrow I will be travelling by train.* |
| করে থাকব | Future Perfect | তুমি আসার আগেই আমি কাজ শেষ করে ফেলব। — *I shall have finished the work before you come.* |

### 54.2.3 Common Bangla Structures and Their English Forms

| Bangla structure | English structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| …-র উচিত | *should / ought to* | আমাদের সময়ের মূল্য দেওয়া উচিত। — *We should value time.* |
| …-কে … হবে / …-তে হবে | *have to / must* | আমাকে এখন যেতে হবে। — *I have to go now.* |
| যদি … তবে | *if … then* (conditionals) | যদি বৃষ্টি হয়, আমরা যাব না। — *If it rains, we will not go.* |
| যদিও … তবুও | *although … / though …* | যদিও সে গরিব, তবুও সে সৎ। — *Though he is poor, he is honest.* |
| যত … তত | *the more … the more* | যত পড়বে, তত শিখবে। — *The more you read, the more you will learn.* |
| …-লেই | *as soon as / no sooner … than* | পুলিশ দেখেই চোরটি পালাল। — *As soon as the thief saw the police, he fled.* |
| পাছে … | *lest … should* | পাছে দেরি হয়, তাই দ্রুত হাঁটো। — *Walk fast lest you should be late.* |
| এত … যে | *so … that / too … to* | সে এত দুর্বল যে হাঁটতে পারে না। — *He is so weak that he cannot walk.* |
| …-ও বটে, …-ও বটে | *not only … but also / both … and* | সে কবি ও শিল্পী। — *He is both a poet and an artist.* |
| কাউকে দিয়ে কিছু করানো | causative *have / get* | আমি চুল কাটালাম। — *I had my hair cut.* |
| …-এর মতো মনে হয় | *seem / look as if* | মনে হচ্ছে বৃষ্টি হবে। — *It looks as if it will rain.* |
| কী সুন্দর! | exclamatory *What / How* | কী সুন্দর দৃশ্য! — *What a beautiful scene!* |
| বলা হয় যে … | passive reporting | বলা হয় যে তিনি একজন মহান নেতা ছিলেন। — *It is said that he was a great leader. / He is said to have been a great leader.* |
| … আছে (possession) | *have* | তার একটি গাড়ি আছে। — *He has a car.* |
| … আছে (existence) | *There is / There are* | টেবিলে একটি বই আছে। — *There is a book on the table.* |

### 54.2.4 Model Translations: Sentences

::: ex
- **বাংলাদেশ একটি নদীমাতৃক দেশ।** → *Bangladesh is a riverine country.*
- **শিক্ষাই জাতির মেরুদণ্ড।** → *Education is the backbone of a nation.*
- **সময় ও স্রোত কারও জন্য অপেক্ষা করে না।** → *Time and tide wait for none.*
- **আমি তাকে দশ বছর ধরে চিনি।** → *I have known him for ten years.*
- **বৃষ্টি থামার পর আমরা বাইরে গেলাম।** → *After the rain had stopped, we went out.*
- **তোমার সাথে দেখা হয়ে খুশি হলাম।** → *I am glad to meet you.*
- **সে আসবে কিনা আমি জানি না।** → *I do not know whether he will come.*
- **দুর্নীতি আমাদের জাতীয় উন্নয়নের পথে প্রধান অন্তরায়।** → *Corruption is the main obstacle to our national development.*
- **জনসংখ্যা বৃদ্ধি বাংলাদেশের একটি বড় সমস্যা।** → *Population growth is a major problem in Bangladesh.*
- **মাদকাসক্তি যুবসমাজকে ধ্বংস করে দিচ্ছে।** → *Drug addiction is ruining the young generation.*
- **আমরা ১৯৭১ সালে স্বাধীনতা অর্জন করেছি।** → *We achieved independence in 1971.*
- **প্রতিদিন সকালে হাঁটা স্বাস্থ্যের জন্য উপকারী।** → *Walking every morning is good for health.*
- **সে এমনভাবে কথা বলে যেন সে সব জানে।** → *He talks as if he knew everything.*
- **তুমি যদি পরিশ্রম কর, তবে সফল হবে।** → *If you work hard, you will succeed.*
- **আমার আর কোনো উপায় ছিল না।** → *I had no other choice / way out.*
- **গাছ কেটো না, গাছ লাগাও।** → *Don't cut down trees; plant more trees.*
- **ছাত্রদের নিয়মিত সংবাদপত্র পড়া উচিত।** → *Students should read newspapers regularly.*
- **শীতকালে গ্রামের দৃশ্য মনোরম হয়।** → *The scenery of a village is charming in winter.*
- **সে আমার চেয়ে দুই বছরের বড়।** → *He is two years older than I. / He is senior to me by two years.*
- **ডাক্তার আসার আগেই রোগীটি মারা গিয়েছিল।** → *The patient had died before the doctor came.*
:::

### 54.2.5 Model Translation: A Passage

**Bangla:**

*বাংলাদেশ একটি উন্নয়নশীল দেশ। এ দেশের অধিকাংশ মানুষ গ্রামে বাস করে এবং কৃষির উপর নির্ভরশীল। সাম্প্রতিক বছরগুলোতে দেশটি অর্থনৈতিক ক্ষেত্রে উল্লেখযোগ্য অগ্রগতি সাধন করেছে। তৈরি পোশাক শিল্প ও প্রবাসী আয় এ উন্নয়নে গুরুত্বপূর্ণ ভূমিকা রাখছে। তবে দুর্নীতি, বেকারত্ব ও জলবায়ু পরিবর্তন এখনও বড় চ্যালেঞ্জ হিসেবে রয়ে গেছে।*

**English:**

*Bangladesh is a developing country. Most of its people live in villages and depend on agriculture. In recent years, the country has made remarkable progress in the economic sector. The ready-made garment industry and remittances from expatriates are playing an important role in this development. However, corruption, unemployment and climate change still remain major challenges.*

::: note
Notice the choices: *অধিকাংশ মানুষ* → *Most of its people* (not *maximum people*); *উল্লেখযোগ্য অগ্রগতি সাধন করেছে* → *has made remarkable progress* (present perfect for recent years); *প্রবাসী আয়* → *remittances from expatriates*; *রয়ে গেছে* → *remain*.
:::

### 54.2.6 Common Translation Errors

::: errs
1. ✘ *Maximum people of our country are poor.* ✔ ***Most** people in our country are poor.*
2. ✘ *I am reading in class ten.* ✔ *I **read / study** in class ten.*
3. ✘ *He is my cousin brother.* ✔ *He is my **cousin**.*
4. ✘ *I have passed SSC in 2020.* ✔ *I **passed** the SSC exam in 2020.*
5. ✘ *Open the light.* ✔ ***Switch on** the light.*
6. ✘ *He gave exam.* ✔ *He **took / sat for the** exam.*
7. ✘ *My father is service holder.* ✔ *My father **is in service / is an employee**.*
8. ✘ *He is staying in Dhaka from 2015.* ✔ *He **has been living** in Dhaka **since** 2015.*
9. ✘ *I went to my uncle house.* ✔ *I went to my **uncle's** house.*
10. ✘ *I am having two brothers.* ✔ *I **have** two brothers.*
:::

## 54.3 Translation: English to Bangla (Brief Guide)

For English-to-Bangla translation (sometimes set in Bank and BCS papers):

1. Use **standard (প্রমিত) Bangla**; avoid mixing *সাধু* and *চলিত* forms in one passage.
2. Convert **S–V–O** order into natural **S–O–V** Bangla order.
3. Translate **idioms by meaning**: *a bolt from the blue* → *বিনা মেঘে বজ্রপাত*.
4. Keep technical terms recognisable: *inflation* → *মুদ্রাস্ফীতি*, *GDP* → *মোট দেশজ উৎপাদন (জিডিপি)*.
5. Avoid over-literal passives: *It is said that …* → *বলা হয় যে …* or *শোনা যায় …*.

## 54.4 Reading Comprehension

Reading comprehension (RC) appears in BCS Written, Bank written tests, IBA/DU admission tests, IELTS, SAT, GRE and GMAT. Good RC is a mix of **reading skill**, **vocabulary** and **logical thinking**.

### 54.4.1 Common Question Types

| Type | What it asks | How to approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Main idea / primary purpose** | What is the passage mainly about? Why did the author write it? | Look at the first and last paragraphs and topic sentences; the answer is broad, not a detail |
| **Specific detail** | According to the passage, what / when / who …? | Locate the key word in the passage; the answer is a paraphrase |
| **Inference** | What can be inferred / suggested? | Choose what **must** be true from the text, not what *might* be true |
| **Vocabulary in context** | In line X, the word "…" most nearly means … | Replace the word with each option in the sentence; context decides, not the common meaning |
| **Author's tone / attitude** | The author's attitude is … | Look for evaluative words (*unfortunately, remarkably, flawed*); tone is usually moderate in academic passages |
| **Function / structure** | Why does the author mention X? How is the passage organised? | Ask what role the sentence plays: example, contrast, evidence, concession |
| **Strengthen / weaken / assumption** (GMAT/GRE) | Which statement would strengthen the argument? | Identify the claim, evidence and gap (assumption) |
| **Title** (BCS/Bank) | Suggest a suitable title | Short, covering the whole passage, not a detail |
| **Summary / answer in your own words** (BCS/IELTS) | Summarise / answer briefly | Use your own words; keep within the word limit |

### 54.4.2 Strategies

::: rule
1. **Read actively.** Ask: *What is the topic? What is the author's point? How does each paragraph support it?*
2. **Map the passage.** Write one short note per paragraph (e.g., *P1: problem; P2: old view; P3: new evidence; P4: author's conclusion*).
3. **Watch the signal words:** *however, but, yet, although* (contrast — the author's real view often follows); *therefore, thus, hence* (conclusion); *for example, for instance* (evidence); *moreover, furthermore* (addition); *in fact, indeed* (emphasis).
4. **Distinguish fact from opinion,** and the author's view from views the author reports (*Some critics argue … However, …*).
5. **Go back to the text** for every detail question; never answer from memory or outside knowledge.
6. **Eliminate extreme answers** (*always, never, completely, proves*) unless the passage is equally extreme.
7. **Beware of "true but irrelevant" options** — a statement can be true according to the passage but not answer the question.
8. **Manage time:** in MCQ tests, don't spend too long on one hard question; mark it and return.
:::

### 54.4.3 Improving Reading Ability

- Read **one English newspaper daily** (*The Daily Star, The Business Standard, Dhaka Tribune*), especially **editorials and op-eds**, and international sources (*The Economist, The Guardian, BBC*), which match the style of GRE/GMAT passages.
- Keep a **vocabulary notebook**: write unknown words with the sentence in which you met them, then revise weekly (spaced repetition).
- **Summarise** each article you read in two or three sentences — this trains main-idea skills.
- Practise **timed reading**: GRE/GMAT passages of 250–450 words should be read in 2–3 minutes with understanding.
