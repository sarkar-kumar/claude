## 27.1 Direct and Indirect Speech

**Narration** means reporting what someone said. There are two ways to do it:

- **Direct speech:** the **exact words** of the speaker are given inside **inverted commas** (quotation marks). *He said, "**I am ill.**"*
- **Indirect (reported) speech:** the **substance** of the speaker's words is reported **in our own words**, without inverted commas, with the necessary changes in pronouns, tenses and other words. *He said **that he was ill**.*

Every narration has two parts:

| Part | Example: *He said to me, "I am ill."* |
|---|---|
| **Reporting verb** (and its subject/object) | *He **said** to me* |
| **Reported speech** (the words reported) | *"I am ill."* |

Narration is tested in SSC, HSC, admission tests and BCS Written (change into indirect/direct), and in MCQ form in BCS Preliminary and Bank tests.

## 27.2 General Rules for Changing Direct into Indirect Speech

To change any sentence, three things must be handled:

1. the **reporting verb** (change *said to* into *told, asked, ordered, wished, exclaimed*, etc., according to the kind of sentence);
2. the **linking word** (*that, if/whether, wh-word, to*);
3. the **reported speech** — changes of **person**, **tense** and **words of nearness/time**.

### 27.2.1 Change of Person — the "SON" Rule

| Person in reported speech | Changes according to | Memory |
|---|---|---|
| **1st person** (*I, me, my, we, us, our*) | the **Subject** of the reporting verb | **S** |
| **2nd person** (*you, your*) | the **Object** of the reporting verb | **O** |
| **3rd person** (*he, she, it, they*) | **No** change | **N** |

::: ex
- **He** said to **me**, "**I** will help **you**." → He told me that **he** would help **me**. (*I* → subject *he*; *you* → object *me*)
- **She** said to **him**, "**You** are **my** friend." → She told him that **he** was **her** friend.
- **They** said to **us**, "**We** know **him**." → They told us that **they** knew **him**. (*him* — 3rd person — unchanged)
- **I** said to **her**, "**You** are late." → I told her that **she** was late.
:::

### 27.2.2 Change of Tense

::: rule
If the **reporting verb is in the past tense**, the tense of the reported speech is changed to the corresponding **past** form. If the reporting verb is in the **present or future**, the tense of the reported speech does **not** change.
:::

| Direct speech | Indirect speech |
|---|---|
| Present Indefinite: *"I **write**."* | Past Indefinite: *he **wrote*** |
| Present Continuous: *"I **am writing**."* | Past Continuous: *he **was writing*** |
| Present Perfect: *"I **have written**."* | Past Perfect: *he **had written*** |
| Present Perfect Continuous: *"I **have been writing**."* | Past Perfect Continuous: *he **had been writing*** |
| Past Indefinite: *"I **wrote**."* | Past Perfect: *he **had written*** |
| Past Continuous: *"I **was writing**."* | Past Perfect Continuous: *he **had been writing*** |
| Past Perfect: *"I **had written**."* | No change: *he **had written*** |
| *will / shall* | *would / should* (*shall* → *would* when the new subject is 2nd/3rd person) |
| *can* | *could* |
| *may* | *might* |
| *must* | *must* / *had to* (obligation) |
| *could, would, should, might, ought to, used to* | No change |

::: ex
- He **says**, "I am busy." → He **says** that he **is** busy. (present reporting verb — no tense change)
- He **will say**, "I am busy." → He will say that he **is** busy.
- He **said**, "I am busy." → He said that he **was** busy.
- She said, "I **have finished** my work." → She said that she **had finished** her work.
- He said, "I **saw** a tiger." → He said that he **had seen** a tiger.
- He said, "I **will** go." → He said that he **would** go.
- She said, "I **can** swim." → She said that she **could** swim.
- He said, "You **must** obey the rules." → He said that I **must / had to** obey the rules.
:::

### 27.2.3 Exceptions: When the Tense Does Not Change

1. **Universal truths, scientific facts, proverbs:** *The teacher said, "The earth **moves** round the sun." → The teacher said that the earth **moves** round the sun.*
2. **Habitual facts still true:** *He said, "I **get** up at six every day." → He said that he **gets / got** up at six every day.*
3. **Historical facts:** *The teacher said, "Bangladesh **became** independent in 1971." → The teacher said that Bangladesh **became** independent in 1971.*
4. **Unreal conditions and wishes (*if*-clauses, *wish*, *as if*, *it's time*):** *He said, "If I **were** a bird, I **would** fly." → He said that if he **were** a bird, he **would** fly.*
5. **Past perfect** and modals *could, would, should, might, ought to*: no change.
6. **When the reporting verb is in the present or future** (see above).

### 27.2.4 Change of Words Showing Nearness and Time

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| now | then |
| today | that day |
| tonight | that night |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| yesterday | the previous day / the day before |
| last night | the previous night / the night before |
| next week / month / year | the following week / month / year |
| ago | before |
| this | that |
| these | those |
| here | there |
| hence | thence |
| hither | thither |
| thus | so / in that way |
| come | go (when the place is no longer near) |

::: ex
- He said, "I came here **yesterday**." → He said that he had gone there **the previous day**.
- She said, "I will do it **tomorrow**." → She said that she would do it **the next day**.
- He said, "I bought **this** book two days **ago**." → He said that he had bought **that** book two days **before**.
:::

::: note
These words change only when the time or place of reporting is **different** from the time or place of speaking. If the report is made on the same day and in the same place, *today, here, this* may remain unchanged: *He said this morning that he would come **today**.*
:::

## 27.3 Assertive (Statement) Sentences

**Rules:** *said* → *said* (no object) or *told* (with an object); join with ***that***; remove inverted commas; change person, tense and words.

::: ex
- He said, "I am happy." → He said **that** he was happy.
- He said to me, "I am happy." → He **told** me **that** he was happy.
- Rina said to her mother, "I have passed the exam." → Rina told her mother that she had passed the exam.
- The boy said, "I shall go to Chattogram tomorrow." → The boy said that he **would** go to Chattogram **the next day**.
- He said, "I was reading a book." → He said that he **had been reading** a book.
- My father said to me, "Honesty is the best policy." → My father told me that honesty **is** the best policy. (universal truth)
- He said, "I may go home." → He said that he **might** go home.
:::

::: note
***Said* vs *told*:** *said* is used **without** a personal object or with *to*: *He said (to me) that …*. *Told* must have a **personal object**: *He told **me** that …* (✘ He told that … / ✘ He said me that …).
:::

## 27.4 Interrogative Sentences

**Rules:**

1. Change *said / said to* into ***asked, enquired of, wanted to know, demanded of***.
2. For **yes/no questions** (beginning with an auxiliary: *Is, Are, Do, Does, Did, Have, Can, Will* …), use ***if*** or ***whether***.
3. For **wh-questions** (*who, what, where, when, why, how, which*), the **wh-word itself** is the linker — **no *that***.
4. Change the **question form** into **statement order** (subject before verb) — **remove *do/does/did*** and the question mark.

::: ex
- He said to me, "**Are** you ill?" → He **asked** me **if / whether** I **was** ill.
- She said to him, "**Do** you know me?" → She asked him if he **knew** her.
- I said to her, "**Did** you go to school?" → I asked her whether she **had gone** to school.
- He said to me, "**Can** you help me?" → He asked me if I **could** help him.
- He said to me, "**Where do you live**?" → He asked me **where I lived**. (✘ where did I live)
- The teacher said to the boy, "**What is your name**?" → The teacher asked the boy **what his name was**.
- She said to me, "**Why are you** crying?" → She asked me **why I was** crying.
- He said, "**Who** broke the glass?" → He asked **who had broken** the glass. (*who* is the subject — normal order)
- I said to him, "**How** did you do it?" → I asked him how he **had done** it.
- He said to me, "**Will you** lend me your pen?" → He asked me **if I would** lend him my pen.
:::

::: tip
**Exam Tip — The two most common mistakes:** (1) keeping question word order (✘ *He asked me where did I live*), and (2) using *that* before *if/whether/wh-word* (✘ *He asked me that where I lived*). The indirect question is a **statement** in form.
:::

## 27.5 Imperative Sentences

**Rules:**

1. Change *said / said to* into a verb that shows the **mood** of the speaker: ***told, ordered, commanded*** (command); ***requested, begged, entreated, implored*** (request); ***advised*** (advice); ***forbade*** (prohibition); ***urged, warned, asked, reminded, invited, proposed, suggested***.
2. Use ***to* + V1** for positive commands and ***not to* + V1** for negative commands (or *forbade … to*).
3. Remove *please / kindly* (expressed through *requested*).

::: ex
- He said to me, "**Go** home." → He **told / ordered** me **to go** home.
- The teacher said to the boys, "**Don't make** a noise." → The teacher **told** the boys **not to make** a noise. / The teacher **forbade** the boys **to make** a noise.
- She said to me, "**Please** give me a pen." → She **requested** me **to give** her a pen.
- The doctor said to the patient, "**Take** rest." → The doctor **advised** the patient **to take** rest.
- The captain said to the soldiers, "**Fire**!" → The captain **commanded** the soldiers **to fire**.
- He said to me, "**Be** careful." → He **warned / advised** me **to be** careful.
- The beggar said, "**Have mercy** on me, sir." → The beggar **respectfully implored** the gentleman **to have** mercy on him.
- He said to me, "**Never tell** a lie." → He **advised** me **never to tell** a lie.
:::

### 27.5.1 Imperatives with *Let*

**(a) *Let us* (proposal / suggestion):** *proposed / suggested* + ***that*** + subject + ***should***:

::: ex
- He said to me, "**Let us** go out for a walk." → He **proposed / suggested to** me **that we should** go out for a walk.
- She said, "Let us play." → She proposed that they should play.
:::

**(b) *Let me / let him* (permission or wish):** *requested / asked* + ***to let*** / ***allow***, or *wished that* + *might*:

::: ex
- He said to me, "**Let me** go." → He **requested** me **to let** him go.
- She said, "**Let him** do the work." → She said that he **might** do the work. / She **asked** them **to let** him do the work.
:::

## 27.6 Optative Sentences (Wishes and Prayers)

**Rules:** *said* → ***wished*** or ***prayed*** (with *that*); ***May*** + subject + V1 → subject + ***might*** + V1.

::: ex
- He said to me, "**May** you **be** happy." → He **wished that** I **might be** happy.
- My mother said to me, "**May** Allah **bless** you." → My mother **prayed that** Allah **might bless** me.
- The people said, "**Long live** the President." → The people **prayed that** the President **might live long**.
- She said, "**Would that** I were young again!" → She **wished that** she were young again.
- He said to me, "**Good morning**." → He **wished** me good morning.
- He said to me, "**Good bye**." → He **bade** me goodbye.
- They said to me, "**Congratulations**!" → They **congratulated** me.
- He said, "**Thank you**." → He **thanked** me.
:::

## 27.7 Exclamatory Sentences

**Rules:**

1. Change *said* into ***exclaimed*** + a phrase showing the emotion: ***with joy, with sorrow, with surprise, with wonder, with regret, with disgust, with contempt***; or ***cried out, applauded, praised***.
2. Remove the **interjection** (*Alas, Hurrah, Oh, Bravo, What, How*) and express its meaning through the phrase above.
3. Join with ***that***; change the exclamation into a statement, often replacing ***What / How*** with ***very / great / very great***.

| Interjection | Change *said* into |
|---|---|
| *Hurrah!* | exclaimed **with joy / delight** |
| *Alas!* | exclaimed **with sorrow / grief** |
| *Oh! / What! / Wow!* | exclaimed **with surprise / wonder** |
| *Bravo!* | **applauded / praised** / exclaimed with approval |
| *Fie! / Pooh! / Pshaw!* | exclaimed **with contempt / disgust** |
| *Ah!* | exclaimed **with regret / sorrow** |

::: ex
- He said, "**Hurrah!** We have won the match." → He **exclaimed with joy** that they had won the match.
- She said, "**Alas!** My son is dead." → She **exclaimed with sorrow** that her son was dead.
- He said, "**What a** beautiful scene it is!" → He **exclaimed with wonder** that it was **a very** beautiful scene.
- They said, "**How** clever the boy is!" → They **exclaimed** that the boy was **very** clever.
- The teacher said to me, "**Bravo!** You have done well." → The teacher **applauded** me, saying that I had done well.
- He said, "**Fie!** You are a coward." → He **exclaimed with contempt** that I was a coward.
- He said, "**What a** fool I am!" → He **exclaimed** that he was **a great** fool.
- She said, "**Oh!** I have lost my purse." → She **exclaimed with sorrow** that she had lost her purse.
:::

## 27.8 Mixed and Multiple Sentences

When the direct speech contains **more than one sentence**, each is reported according to its own type, and they are joined with ***and said that, and added that, and asked, as, but, so*** etc.

::: ex
- He said, "**I am tired. Let me rest.**" → He said that he was tired **and** requested to be allowed to rest.
- She said to me, "**You are late. Where have you been?**" → She told me that I was late **and asked** where I had been.
- The teacher said, "**Boys, you have done well. Go home now.**" → The teacher told the boys that they had done well **and asked** them to go home **then**.
- He said, "**Alas! I am undone. What shall I do now?**" → He **exclaimed with sorrow** that he was undone **and asked** what he would do **then**.
- He said to me, "**I have lost my pen. Will you lend me yours?**" → He told me that he had lost his pen **and asked** me if I would lend him mine.
:::

## 27.9 Special Cases

### 27.9.1 Vocatives (Words of Address)

A name or word of address in direct speech becomes the **object** of the reporting verb or is expressed with *addressing … as*:

::: ex
- He said, "**Rahim**, come here." → He **told Rahim** to come there.
- He said, "**Friends**, help me." → He **requested his friends** to help him. / **Addressing them as friends**, he requested them to help him.
- He said, "**Sir**, I need your help." → He **respectfully told** the gentleman/teacher that he needed his help.
:::

### 27.9.2 *Yes* and *No* in Answers

***Yes / No*** are expressed by ***replied in the affirmative / in the negative***, or by repeating the auxiliary:

::: ex
- I said to him, "Will you come?" He said, "**Yes**." → I asked him if he would come. He **replied in the affirmative**. / He replied that he would.
- She said, "**No**, I can't." → She **replied in the negative** / She replied that she could not.
:::

### 27.9.3 *Must*

- *Must* expressing **obligation** → ***had to*** (or *must*): *He said, "I must go." → He said that he **had to** go.*
- *Must* expressing **certainty / deduction / permanent rule** → **must** (no change): *He said, "She must be ill." → He said that she **must** be ill.*
- *Mustn't* (prohibition) → ***mustn't / was not to***.

### 27.9.4 *Shall* in Questions

*"Shall I …?"* (asking for advice/offer) → ***should***: *He said, "Shall I open the door?" → He asked if he **should** open the door.* *"Shall we …?"* (proposal) → ***proposed / suggested that … should***.

## 27.10 Changing Indirect Speech into Direct Speech

Reverse the steps:

1. Use ***said*** or ***said to*** + object, followed by a comma and inverted commas.
2. Restore the **kind of sentence** (question, command, wish, exclamation) with the proper punctuation.
3. Change persons back (the speaker becomes *I*; the listener becomes *you*).
4. Change tenses **one step forward** (past → present, past perfect → past/present perfect, *would* → *will*, *could* → *can*, *might* → *may*).
5. Restore words of nearness (*that day → today, there → here, the next day → tomorrow*).
6. Insert interjections where the indirect form shows emotion.

::: ex
- He told me that he was ill. → He said to me, "**I am** ill."
- She asked me whether I would go there. → She said to me, "**Will you go** there?"
- The teacher ordered the boys not to make a noise. → The teacher said to the boys, "**Don't make** a noise."
- He prayed that I might be happy. → He said to me, "**May you be** happy!"
- She exclaimed with sorrow that she had lost her purse. → She said, "**Alas! I have lost** my purse."
- He proposed that they should go for a walk. → He said, "**Let us go** for a walk."
- He asked me where I had been the previous day. → He said to me, "**Where were you yesterday**?"
:::

## 27.11 Reporting a Conversation

When a whole conversation is reported, vary the reporting verbs (*asked, replied, added, enquired, exclaimed, agreed, admitted, explained, suggested, insisted*) to make the narrative natural.

::: ex
**Direct:**
"Where are you going?" said the policeman to the boy.
"I am going to the market," replied the boy.
"Why are you running?" asked the policeman.
"My mother is ill. I must buy some medicine," said the boy.

**Indirect:**
The policeman **asked** the boy where he was going. The boy **replied** that he was going to the market. The policeman then **enquired** why he was running. The boy **explained** that his mother was ill and that he **had to** buy some medicine.
:::

## 27.12 Common Errors in Narration

::: errs
1. ✘ He said me that he was ill. ✔ He **told** me that he was ill. / He **said to** me that he was ill.
2. ✘ He told that he was busy. ✔ He **said** that he was busy. / He told **me** that he was busy.
3. ✘ He asked me that where I lived. ✔ He asked me **where I lived**.
4. ✘ He asked me where did I live. ✔ He asked me where **I lived**.
5. ✘ She asked me that if I was ill. ✔ She asked me **if** I was ill.
6. ✘ He said that he will come tomorrow. ✔ He said that he **would** come **the next day**.
7. ✘ The teacher said that the sun rose in the east. ✔ … the sun **rises** in the east.
8. ✘ He ordered me that go away. ✔ He ordered me **to go** away.
9. ✘ He told me don't go there. ✔ He told me **not to go** there.
10. ✘ He requested me that please help him. ✔ He requested me **to help** him.
11. ✘ He wished that I may be happy. ✔ He **prayed / wished** that I **might** be happy.
12. ✘ He exclaimed with sorrow that alas he had failed. ✔ He exclaimed with sorrow that he **had failed**.
13. ✘ She said that she has seen the film. ✔ She said that she **had seen** the film.
14. ✘ He asked me what is my name. ✔ He asked me what **my name was**.
15. ✘ He proposed me to go for a walk. ✔ He **proposed that we should** go for a walk.
16. ✘ He said that he came here yesterday. ✔ He said that he **had gone there the previous day**.
17. ✘ He asked whether I will help him. ✔ He asked whether I **would** help him.
18. ✘ He said that "I am busy". ✔ He said, "I am busy." / He said that he was busy.
19. ✘ He asked me, Do I know him. ✔ He asked me **if I knew** him.
20. ✘ He forbade me not to go. ✔ He **forbade** me **to go**. (*forbade* is already negative)
:::

## 27.13 Reported Speech in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
- **Tense agreement after past reporting verbs (GMAT/SAT):** ✘ *The spokesman said that the company **will** expand next year.* — GMAT may prefer ***would*** when the whole context is past; however, if the event is still in the future at the time of writing, *will* can be acceptable. Look at what the other answer choices do and choose the **logically consistent** one.
- **Indirect questions keep statement order:** ✘ *Researchers asked how much did people spend.* ✔ *… how much people **spent**.*
- ***That* + clause after reporting verbs:** GMAT occasionally tests *claim that, report that, announce that* (✘ *claimed to be that*).
- **GRE/SAT Reading — reported views:** Authors often report others' opinions (*Critics argued that …; Some historians contend that …*) before giving their own; distinguishing **reported view** from **author's view** is essential in reading questions.
:::
