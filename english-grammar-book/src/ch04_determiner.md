## 4.1 What Is a Determiner?

A **determiner** is a word placed **before a noun** (and before any adjectives that describe the noun) to **limit or specify** it. It tells us *which* one, *whose*, *how many* or *how much*.

::: ex
- **the** book, **a** pen, **an** apple (articles)
- **this** house, **those** trees (demonstratives)
- **my** father, **their** school (possessives)
- **some** water, **many** people, **every** student (quantifiers)
- **three** boys, **the first** day (numbers)
- **which** road? **whose** bag? (interrogatives)
:::

Traditional grammar (the grammar followed in most Bangladeshi textbooks) calls these words **adjectives** — *demonstrative adjectives, possessive adjectives, adjectives of number, adjectives of quantity, distributive adjectives* and *interrogative adjectives*. Modern grammar (and international tests) places them in a separate class because they behave differently from true adjectives.

### 4.1.1 Determiner vs Adjective

| Feature | Determiner | Adjective |
|---|---|---|
| Position | Comes **first** in the noun phrase | Comes **after** the determiner |
| Comparison | Cannot be compared (✘ *more this*) | Usually has degrees (*tall, taller, tallest*) |
| *Very* | Cannot take *very* (✘ *very my*) | Can take *very* (*very tall*) |
| Predicative use | Cannot follow *be* (✘ *The book is my*) | Can follow *be* (*The book is new*) |
| Number allowed | Usually only **one** central determiner (✘ *the my book*) | Many adjectives can be stacked (*a big old red bus*) |

::: ex
- **My** new red car ✔ (determiner + adjective + adjective + noun)
- ✘ The my car / ✘ My the car / ✘ A my friend → ✔ **my** car / ✔ **a friend of mine**
- ✘ This my book → ✔ **this book of mine**
:::

## 4.2 Kinds of Determiners

| Kind | Members |
|---|---|
| Articles | a, an, the |
| Demonstratives | this, that, these, those |
| Possessives | my, your, his, her, its, our, their, whose, one's; noun + 's (*Rahim's*) |
| Quantifiers | some, any, no, much, many, more, most, little, less, least, few, fewer, fewest, several, enough, all, both, half, a lot of, plenty of |
| Distributives | each, every, either, neither |
| Numbers | cardinals (*one, two, hundred*), ordinals (*first, second, last*) |
| Multiplicatives & fractions | double, twice, three times, half, one-third |
| Interrogatives | what, which, whose |
| Exclamatives | what (a), such (a) |
| Others | another, other, such, quite, rather |

## 4.3 The Order of Determiners

When more than one determiner comes before a noun, they follow a fixed order:

1. **Pre-determiners:** *all, both, half, double, twice, three times, such, what, quite, rather*
2. **Central determiners:** *a, an, the, this, that, these, those, my, your, his, some, any, no, each, every, either, neither, whose, which*
3. **Post-determiners:** cardinal and ordinal numbers, *many, much, few, little, several, other, last, next, more*

::: ex
- **all the** students (pre + central)
- **both my** parents (pre + central) — ✘ my both parents
- **half an** hour (pre + central) — ✘ a half an hour
- **twice the** price (pre + central)
- **such a** beautiful day (pre + central)
- **what a** pity!
- **quite a** few people / **rather a** long time
- **the first two** chapters (central + ordinal + cardinal) — ✘ the two first chapters
- **my last few** days (central + post + post)
- **these three** books / **all these three** books
- **the other** day / **every other** day (= on alternate days)
:::

::: tip
**Exam Tip — "My all friends" is the most common determiner error in Bangladesh.** *All, both* and *half* come **before** *the / my / these*: ✔ ***All my*** *friends*, ✔ ***both the*** *boys*, ✔ ***half the*** *money*. Also correct: ***all of my*** *friends*, ***both of the*** *boys*, ***half of the*** *money*. With pronouns, *of* is required: ***all of us***, ***both of them*** (✘ *all us*), or place *all/both* after the pronoun: *We **all** went. They **both** came.*
:::

## 4.4 Demonstratives: *This, That, These, Those*

| | Near (here / now) | Far (there / then) |
|---|---|---|
| Singular & uncountable | this | that |
| Plural | these | those |

::: ex
- **This** pen is mine; **that** pen over there is yours.
- **These** mangoes are sweeter than **those** (mangoes).
- I remember **those** days. (past time = "far")
- **This** week / **this** morning (present time)
:::

::: rule
***That of / Those of* in comparisons:** To avoid repeating a noun in a comparison, use ***that of*** for a singular/uncountable noun and ***those of*** for a plural noun. ✘ *The climate of Sylhet is cooler than Dhaka.* ✔ *The climate of Sylhet is cooler than **that of** Dhaka.* ✘ *The houses of the rich are bigger than the poor.* ✔ *… bigger than **those of** the poor.*
:::

::: note
***Kind / sort / type*:** *This kind of book* ✔ / *These kinds of books* ✔ / ✘ *These kind of books*. The demonstrative agrees with *kind*, not with the noun after *of*.
:::

## 4.5 Possessive Determiners

***My, your, his, her, its, our, their, whose*** (and a noun + *'s*) show ownership. They agree with the **possessor**, not the thing possessed: *Rahim and **his** sister; Rina and **her** brother; the tree and **its** leaves; the students and **their** books*.

::: ex
- ✘ The company announced it's profits. ✔ The company announced **its** profits. (*it's* = *it is / it has*)
- ✘ The students left there bags. ✔ … **their** bags. (*there* = in that place)
- ✘ Who's book is this? ✔ **Whose** book is this? (*who's* = *who is / who has*)
- **Each** of the girls brought **her** own lunch. (agrees with *each*, singular)
:::

**Possessive vs *the* with parts of the body.** After a verb + object, English uses *the* (not a possessive) for the body part in expressions of hitting, touching, holding etc.: *He hit me **on the** head. She caught him **by the** arm. He looked me **in the** eye.* But: *I washed **my** face. He broke **his** leg.*

**Own** strengthens a possessive: *my **own** house* (✘ an own house), *a house of **my own***, *on **my own*** (= alone).

## 4.6 *Some, Any, No, None*

| Determiner | Typical use | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **some** | positive sentences; offers and requests | *I have **some** money. Would you like **some** tea? Can I have **some** water?* |
| **any** | negatives and questions; *if*-clauses; = "it doesn't matter which" | *I don't have **any** money. Do you have **any** questions? If you need **any** help, call me. **Any** child can do this.* |
| **no** | = not any (before a noun) | *I have **no** money* (= I don't have any money). |
| **none** | pronoun = not one / not any (stands alone or with *of*) | ***None** of the students passed. I wanted some tea, but there was **none**.* |

::: ex
- ✘ I didn't buy no books. ✔ I didn't buy **any** books. / I bought **no** books. (avoid double negatives)
- ✘ I have none money. ✔ I have **no** money. (*none* is a pronoun; *no* is the determiner)
- *Hardly any, scarcely any, barely any*: ***Hardly any** students came.* (✘ hardly no)
- *Some* in questions expecting "yes": *Did you buy **some** fruit?* (I think you did.)
:::

## 4.7 Distributives: *Each, Every, Either, Neither*

Distributives take a **singular noun** and a **singular verb**.

### 4.7.1 *Each* vs *Every*

- ***Each*** is used for **two or more** things taken **one by one** (individually).
- ***Every*** is used for **three or more** things taken **as a group** (all of them, without exception).

::: ex
- She had a ring on **each** hand. (two hands → *each*; ✘ every hand)
- **Every** student in the class passed. (all, as a group)
- **Each** student was given a separate question. (individually)
- **Each of** the boys has a bicycle. (✘ Every of the boys → ✔ **Every one of** the boys)
- *Every* + number: *The buses leave **every** ten minutes. I visit him **every** two weeks / **every other** week.*
- *Each and every* is emphatic and singular: ***Each and every** citizen **has** a duty.*
:::

::: note
**Everyone vs every one:** *Everyone* (one word) = everybody: ***Everyone** was happy.* *Every one* (two words) = each single one, usually with *of*: ***Every one of** the eggs was broken.* Similarly, *any one* (= any single one) vs *anyone* (= anybody).
:::

### 4.7.2 *Either* vs *Neither*

***Either*** (= one or the other of **two**) and ***neither*** (= not one and not the other of **two**) are used only for **two**. For more than two, use ***any*** and ***none / no***.

::: ex
- You can take **either** road; both lead to the station.
- **Neither** answer **is** correct. (✘ are)
- **Either of** the two brothers **is** responsible.
- ✘ Either of these five roads leads to Dhaka. ✔ **Any** of these five roads leads to Dhaka.
- ✘ Neither of the ten students passed. ✔ **None** of the ten students passed.
- *Either* can mean "both" with *side, end, bank, hand*: *There are trees on **either side** of the road* (= on both sides).
:::

## 4.8 *All, Both, Whole, Half*

::: ex
- **All** + plural countable: ***All** boys like games.* / ***All the** boys of this class are present.*
- **All** + uncountable: ***All the** money was spent. **All** milk contains fat.*
- **Both** = the two together: ***Both** (the) brothers are doctors.* (✘ the both brothers)
- **Both … and** joins two items: ***Both** Rahim **and** Karim came.* (✘ Both Rahim as well as Karim)
- **The whole** + singular countable noun: ***the whole** city, **the whole** day, **the whole** world* = **all** the city / day.
- **Whole** is not used with uncountable nouns or plurals: ✘ *the whole money* → ✔ ***all the** money*; ✘ *the whole students* → ✔ ***all the** students*. (*whole* + plural = "complete, not partial": *Whole villages were destroyed* ✔.)
- **Half:** ***half an** hour* (✘ a half an hour; ✔ *a half-hour* in AmE); ***half the** money*; ***one and a half** hours* (plural noun) / ***an hour and a half***.
:::

## 4.9 *Another, Other, Others, The Other*

| Word | Used with | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **another** (= an + other) | **singular** countable noun; "one more" or "a different one" | *Give me **another** cup of tea. Let's meet **another** day.* (✘ another books) |
| **another** + number + plural | "an additional quantity" | *I need **another** five days / **another** 500 taka.* ✔ |
| **other** | plural or uncountable nouns | ***Other** students were absent. **Other** information is given below.* |
| **the other** | the remaining one(s) of a known set | *One of my brothers is a doctor; **the other** is an engineer.* / ***The other** students were sent home.* |
| **others** (pronoun) | stands alone, = other people/things | *Some people like tea; **others** like coffee.* (✘ others people) |
| **the others** | the remaining ones (pronoun) | *Rahim stayed; **the others** left.* |
| **each other** | two persons (traditional rule) | *Rahim and Karim love **each other**.* |
| **one another** | more than two (traditional rule) | *The students helped **one another**.* |

::: note
**Each other vs one another:** Traditional grammar (followed in BCS and admission tests) says *each other* for **two** and *one another* for **more than two**. Modern usage treats them as interchangeable, but in a Bangladeshi exam, follow the traditional rule.
:::

## 4.10 *Much, Many, Few, Little, More, Most, Enough, Several*

The countable/uncountable choice is covered in Chapter 03. Some additional points:

::: ex
- ***Most** people* ✔ (= people in general) / ***Most of the** people in this village* ✔ / ✘ *Most of people*.
- ***The most*** is the superlative of *much/many*: *Who has **the most** money?*
- ***Several*** = more than two but not many; countable plural only: ***several** times* (✘ several money).
- ***Enough*** comes **before** a noun and **after** an adjective/adverb: ***enough** money; old **enough**; fast **enough***. (✘ enough old)
- ***More*** + noun / ***more of*** + determiner: ***more** rice; **more of the** rice*. ***Many more** books / **much more** money* (✘ much more books).
- ***A lot of / lots of / plenty of*** work with both kinds: ***a lot of** time / **a lot of** friends.*
:::

## 4.11 Numbers as Determiners

::: ex
- **Cardinals:** *one, two, a hundred, two thousand* — *three boys, a hundred taka*.
- **Ordinals:** *first, second, third, last, next* — usually with *the*: ***the first** day, **the second** chapter*. Ordinals come before cardinals: ***the first three** chapters* (✘ the three first).
- **Hundred, thousand, million, dozen, score** have **no -s** after a number: ***two hundred** people, **three dozen** eggs, **five million** taka* (✘ two hundreds people). With *of* and no number they take *-s*: ***hundreds of** people, **dozens of** eggs, **millions of** dollars*.
- **Number + noun as an adjective** is singular and hyphenated: *a **five-year** plan, a **ten-taka** note, a **two-hour** journey*.
- ***One of the*** + plural noun: ***one of the** best **players*** (✘ one of the best player).
:::

## 4.12 Multiplicatives and Fractions

::: ex
- ***Twice / three times / double + the / my / his*** + noun: *He earns **twice my** salary. This box is **three times the** size of that one. Prices have risen to **double the** old level.*
- **Comparison with *as … as*:** *This road is **twice as long as** that one.* (✘ twice longer than — avoid in formal tests)
- **Fractions:** ***one-third of** the students, **three-quarters of** the land, **half of** the work*. The verb agrees with the noun after *of*: *Two-thirds of the land **is** cultivated; two-thirds of the farmers **are** poor.*
:::

## 4.13 Interrogative and Exclamative Determiners

::: ex
- ***What*** = from an open choice: ***What** book are you reading?*
- ***Which*** = from a limited choice: ***Which** book do you want — the red one or the blue one?*
- ***Whose*** = possession: ***Whose** car is this?*
- ***What a*** + singular countable: ***What a** beautiful day!* / ***What*** + uncountable or plural: ***What** fine weather! **What** lovely flowers!* (✘ What a fine weather!)
- ***Such a*** + adj + singular countable: *He is **such a** nice man.* / ***Such*** + uncountable/plural: ***such** lovely music, **such** good friends*.
- ***Quite a / rather a / a rather***: ***quite a** good film; **rather a** long time / **a rather** long time*.
:::

## 4.14 Common Errors with Determiners

::: errs
1. ✘ My all friends came. ✔ **All my** friends came.
2. ✘ The both brothers are doctors. ✔ **Both the** brothers / **Both** brothers are doctors.
3. ✘ Every students passed. ✔ **Every student** passed.
4. ✘ Each of the boys have a pen. ✔ Each of the boys **has** a pen.
5. ✘ Either of the three roads … ✔ **Any** of the three roads …
6. ✘ He gave me another books. ✔ He gave me **another book** / **other books**.
7. ✘ Some people like tea; other like coffee. ✔ … **others** like coffee.
8. ✘ These kind of mangoes are sweet. ✔ **This kind** of mango **is** sweet. / **These kinds** of mangoes are sweet.
9. ✘ The population of Dhaka is larger than Chattogram. ✔ … larger than **that of** Chattogram.
10. ✘ He spent the whole money. ✔ He spent **all the** money.
11. ✘ I waited for a half an hour. ✔ I waited for **half an hour**.
12. ✘ Two hundreds people were present. ✔ Two **hundred** people were present.
13. ✘ The two first chapters are easy. ✔ The **first two** chapters are easy.
14. ✘ She is one of the best singer. ✔ She is one of the best **singers**.
15. ✘ He is enough strong. ✔ He is **strong enough**.
16. ✘ Most of people are honest. ✔ **Most people** are honest.
17. ✘ I have not got no money. ✔ I have **no** money. / I have not got **any** money.
18. ✘ What a fine weather! ✔ **What fine weather!**
19. ✘ The dog wagged it's tail. ✔ The dog wagged **its** tail.
20. ✘ This is the my book. ✔ This is **my** book.
21. ✘ A my friend told me. ✔ **A friend of mine** told me.
22. ✘ Every one of the boys were present. ✔ Every one of the boys **was** present.
23. ✘ He hit me on my head. ✔ He hit me **on the** head.
24. ✘ Both Rahim as well as Karim came. ✔ **Both** Rahim **and** Karim came.
25. ✘ Neither of the two answers are correct. ✔ Neither of the two answers **is** correct.
:::

## 4.15 Determiners in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
- **Ambiguous *this / that / it*:** GMAT marks a sentence wrong if a demonstrative or pronoun could refer to two different nouns. ✘ *The manager told the clerk that **this** was a problem* (what was?). Make the reference clear: *… that **the delay** was a problem*.
- ***That of / those of*** is a standard GMAT fix for **illogical comparisons**: ✘ *The GDP of Bangladesh grew faster than Pakistan.* ✔ *… faster than **that of** Pakistan.*
- ***Each / every*** + singular verb and singular pronoun: ✔ *Each of the companies **has** submitted **its** report.*
- ***Number / amount, fewer / less*** (Chapter 03) and ***between / among*** (Chapter 17) are tested together with determiners.
- SAT Writing often tests **possessive determiners vs contractions**: *its/it's, their/they're/there, whose/who's, your/you're*.
:::
