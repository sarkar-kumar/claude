[Part A · Grammar]{.chpart}

## 20.1 What Is an Interjection?

An **interjection** is a word or phrase that expresses a **sudden feeling or emotion** — joy, sorrow, surprise, pain, approval, disgust, fear, and so on. The word comes from Latin *interjectio*, "something thrown in between", because an interjection is *thrown into* speech without any grammatical connection with the rest of the sentence.

::: ex
- **Alas!** Our leader is no more.
- **Hurrah!** We have won the match.
- **Oh!** What a beautiful sight!
- **Ouch!** That hurts.
- **Bravo!** You have done well.
:::

Because it has no grammatical relation to the other words, an interjection does not change the structure of a sentence. Remove it, and the sentence is still complete: *(Alas!) Our leader is no more.*

## 20.2 Features of Interjections

1. They are usually **followed by an exclamation mark (!)** when the feeling is strong: *Wow!* With a mild feeling, a **comma** is used: *Oh, I see.* / *Well, let me think.*
2. They usually come at the **beginning** of a sentence, but can also come in the middle or at the end: *I won the prize, **hurrah**!*
3. They are common in **speech** and informal writing, rare in formal academic writing.
4. In **narration** (Chapter 27), interjections are removed and their feeling is expressed through the reporting verb and an adverb or phrase: *He said, "Alas! I am ruined."* → *He **exclaimed with sorrow** that he was ruined.*

## 20.3 Interjections Classified by Feeling

| Feeling | Interjections | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Joy / Delight** | Hurrah! Hurray! Huzza! Yippee! Wow! Hooray! Aha! | ***Hurrah!** We have got a holiday.* |
| **Sorrow / Grief** | Alas! Ah! Oh! Woe! Alack! Ah me! | ***Alas!** He has failed again.* |
| **Surprise / Wonder** | Oh! Ah! What! Wow! Indeed! Really! Good heavens! My God! Goodness! Gosh! Whoa! | ***What!** You have passed!* |
| **Approval / Praise** | Bravo! Well done! Excellent! Splendid! Good! Hear, hear! | ***Bravo!** You played well.* |
| **Disapproval / Contempt** | Fie! Pooh! Pshaw! Tut! Tut-tut! Shame! Ugh! Boo! | ***Fie!** You have told a lie.* |
| **Disgust** | Ugh! Yuck! Phew! Bah! | ***Ugh!** What a bad smell.* |
| **Pain** | Ouch! Ow! Oh! Ah! | ***Ouch!** I hurt my finger.* |
| **Calling / Attention** | Hello! Hey! Hi! Ho! Lo! Look! | ***Hello!** Is anybody there?* |
| **Silence** | Hush! Sh! Shh! | ***Hush!** The baby is sleeping.* |
| **Relief** | Phew! Whew! Thank God! At last! | ***Phew!** That was close.* |
| **Hesitation** | Er! Um! Hmm! Well! | ***Well**, I'm not sure.* |
| **Warning** | Look out! Watch out! Careful! Beware! | ***Look out!** A car is coming.* |
| **Farewell / Greeting** | Goodbye! Bye! Welcome! Hello! | ***Welcome!** Please come in.* |
| **Prayer / Wish** | God bless you! Amen! Heaven forbid! | ***God bless** you!* |

## 20.4 Phrases and Other Words Used as Interjections

**Interjection phrases** (groups of words used as interjections):

*Good heavens! Good gracious! Oh dear! Dear me! My goodness! Thank God! For God's sake! What a pity! Well done! Hear, hear! Ah me! Alas the day! Woe is me! Thank goodness! Oh my God! Bless me!*

**Other parts of speech used as interjections** when spoken with emotion:

| Word | Usual class | As interjection |
|---|---|---|
| What | pronoun / determiner | ***What!** You failed again?* |
| Well | adverb / adjective | ***Well**, what do you want?* |
| Indeed | adverb | ***Indeed!** I never knew that.* |
| Silence | noun | ***Silence!** The judge is speaking.* |
| Shame | noun | ***Shame!** How could you do that?* |
| Nonsense | noun | ***Nonsense!** That's not true.* |
| Help | verb | ***Help!** I'm drowning.* |
| Fire | noun | ***Fire!** Run!* |
| Look | verb | ***Look!** A rainbow.* |
| Heavens | noun | ***Heavens!** It's late.* |

## 20.5 Some Interjections in Detail

- ***Alas*** expresses grief, pity or regret — the most commonly tested interjection: ***Alas!** The poor boy died.* In narration: *He said, "Alas! I am undone." → He **exclaimed sorrowfully / with sorrow** that he was undone.*
- ***Hurrah / Hurray / Hooray*** expresses joy and triumph: *The boys said, "Hurrah! We have won." → The boys **exclaimed with joy** that they had won.*
- ***Bravo*** (Italian origin) praises a fine performance: *The audience said, "Bravo!" → The audience **applauded** him / **praised** him.*
- ***Fie*** (old-fashioned) shows contempt or disgust for a shameful act: ***Fie** on you! You are a coward.* → *He **exclaimed with contempt** that I was a coward.*
- ***Pshaw / Pooh*** shows contempt, impatience or disbelief.
- ***Hush*** demands silence.
- ***Lo*** (archaic) = look, see: ***Lo!** The king comes.* (*Lo and behold* = surprisingly)
- ***Hear, hear!*** expresses agreement with a speaker (in Parliament and meetings).
- ***Eureka!*** (Greek: "I have found it!") expresses triumph at a discovery — said by Archimedes.
- ***Amen*** = "so be it", said at the end of a prayer.

## 20.6 Punctuation with Interjections

::: ex
- Strong feeling — exclamation mark: ***Wow!** That's amazing.* / ***Oh!** I forgot my keys.*
- Mild feeling — comma: ***Oh**, is that you? **Well**, I'll think about it. **Yes**, I agree.*
- Inside a sentence — commas around it: *It was, **alas**, too late.*
- *O* (capital, no punctuation directly after it) is used in **direct address**, especially in poetry and prayers: ***O** God, help us! **O** Captain! my Captain!* *Oh* is the ordinary interjection: ***Oh**, how beautiful!*
:::

::: note
***O* vs *Oh*:** ***O*** is always capitalised, is used before a **name in direct address** (vocative), and is not followed directly by a comma or exclamation mark: *O Lord!* is the whole phrase. ***Oh*** expresses surprise, pain or emotion and is followed by a comma or an exclamation mark: *Oh! / Oh, no.*
:::

## 20.7 Interjections and Exclamatory Sentences

An interjection is a **word**; an **exclamatory sentence** is a **sentence** that expresses strong feeling (Chapter 32). They often occur together:

::: ex
- **Alas!** (interjection) / **How sad the news is!** (exclamatory sentence)
- **Hurrah!** **What a victory!**
- Exclamatory sentences often begin with ***What*** or ***How*** without any interjection: ***What** a lovely garden! **How** fast he runs!*
:::

## 20.8 Common Errors with Interjections

::: errs
1. ✘ Alas! We have won the match. ✔ **Hurrah!** We have won the match. (*alas* = sorrow)
2. ✘ Hurrah! His father died yesterday. ✔ **Alas!** His father died yesterday.
3. ✘ Bravo! You have failed again. ✔ **Fie!** / **Shame!** You have failed again.
4. ✘ Hush! What a beautiful scene! ✔ **Oh!** / **Wow!** What a beautiful scene!
5. ✘ Oh God, help me. (vocative, formal/poetic) ✔ **O** God, help me.
6. ✘ He said that alas he was ruined. (indirect) ✔ He **exclaimed with sorrow** that he was ruined.
:::

## 20.9 Interjections in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
Interjections are not tested directly in international exams, since the SAT, GRE and GMAT use formal academic English. However:

- In **SAT Reading**, literary passages (fiction, speeches) use interjections and exclamations to show **tone** — joy, despair, irony. Questions on "the tone of the passage" or "the narrator's attitude" may depend on them.
- In **formal writing tasks** (GRE Analytical Writing, GMAT essay — if taken), avoid interjections entirely.
- **GRE vocabulary** includes words describing exclamations: *ejaculation* (an old word for a sudden cry), *exclamation*, *outburst*, *paean* (a song of praise), *lament / elegy* (expression of grief), *execration* (a curse), *expletive* (an oath or swear word; also a "filler" word like *there* in *There is …*).
:::
