## 11.1 What Is a Verb?

A **verb** is a word that tells us what a person or thing **does**, **is**, or **has** — it shows **action**, **state of being** or **possession**. The word comes from Latin *verbum*, "word", because the verb is *the* word of a sentence: without it there is no sentence.

::: ex
- The baby **sleeps**. (action)
- Rahim **is** a student. (state of being)
- She **has** two brothers. (possession)
- The flowers **seem** fresh. (state)
- The bridge **was built** in 2022. (action, passive)
:::

A sentence is a **subject** + a **predicate**, and the **verb** is the heart of the predicate. One test for a verb: a verb changes form to show **tense** (*go – went*) and **agreement** (*go – goes*), and it can follow *to* (*to go*) or a modal (*can go*).

## 11.2 Classification of Verbs: An Overview

Verbs can be classified in several ways. Each classification answers a different question:

| Basis | Kinds |
|---|---|
| Does the verb need an object? | **Transitive** / **Intransitive** |
| Is it a main verb or a helping verb? | **Principal (Main)** / **Auxiliary** (primary: *be, have, do*; modal: *can, may, will …*) |
| Does it show tense and agree with the subject? | **Finite** / **Non-finite** (infinitive, gerund, participle) |
| How is the past formed? | **Regular** (*-ed*) / **Irregular** |
| Does it link the subject to a description? | **Linking (Copular)** / **Action** |
| Is it a verb + particle unit? | **Phrasal / Prepositional verbs** (Chapter 47) |
| Does the subject act or receive? | **Active** / **Passive** voice (Chapter 26) |
| Does the verb express a fact, command or wish? | **Mood**: Indicative / Imperative / Subjunctive |
| Does the verb express a continuing action? | **Dynamic** (*run, eat*) / **Stative** (*know, love, own*) |

## 11.3 Transitive and Intransitive Verbs

### 11.3.1 Transitive Verbs

A **transitive verb** is one whose action **passes over** (*trans* = across) from the subject to an **object**. Without an object, its meaning is incomplete. Ask "*what?*" or "*whom?*" after the verb — the answer is the object.

::: ex
- The boy **kicked** the ball. (kicked what? → the ball)
- She **loves** her mother. (loves whom? → her mother)
- I **bought** a pen.
- He **told** a lie.
:::

### 11.3.2 Intransitive Verbs

An **intransitive verb** does not need an object; the action stays with the subject.

::: ex
- The baby **cried**.
- The sun **rises** in the east.
- Birds **fly**.
- He **sleeps** soundly. (*soundly* is an adverb, not an object)
- She **went** to school. (*to school* is a prepositional phrase, not an object)
:::

### 11.3.3 Verbs Used Both Ways

Many verbs can be transitive in one sentence and intransitive in another.

| Intransitive | Transitive |
|---|---|
| The bell **rang**. | He **rang** the bell. |
| The ship **sank**. | The enemy **sank** the ship. |
| The horse **walked**. | He **walked** the horse. (= made it walk) |
| The baby **is sleeping**. | — |
| Birds **fly**. | The boys **fly** kites. |
| He **runs** fast. | He **runs** a business. |
| The door **opened**. | She **opened** the door. |
| The glass **broke**. | Who **broke** the glass? |
| He **stopped**. | He **stopped** the car. |
| The fire **burns**. | He **burnt** the papers. |

::: note
**Causative use:** Some intransitive verbs become transitive with the meaning "*cause to do*": *The horse **ran**.* → *He **ran** the horse.* / *The kite **flew**.* → *He **flew** the kite.* / *The child **sat** on the chair.* → *She **sat** the child on the chair.*
:::

### 11.3.4 Verbs with Two Objects

Some transitive verbs take **two objects**: an **indirect object** (usually a person — *to/for whom*) and a **direct object** (usually a thing — *what*). Such verbs include *give, send, show, tell, teach, buy, bring, lend, offer, pay, promise, write, ask, sell, hand, owe, make, cook, read*.

::: ex
- He **gave** **me** **a book**. → *me* = indirect object; *a book* = direct object
- He gave a book **to me**. (the indirect object becomes a prepositional phrase)
- She **bought** **her son** **a shirt**. → She bought a shirt **for** her son.
- The teacher **asked** **him** **a question**.
:::

::: tip
**Verbs that do NOT take an indirect object before the direct object:** *explain, describe, suggest, say, announce, introduce, propose, report, mention, deliver*. ✘ *Explain me the rule.* ✔ *Explain the rule **to me**.* ✘ *Describe me the scene.* ✔ *Describe the scene **to me**.* ✘ *He said me that …* ✔ *He **told** me that …* / ✔ *He **said to** me that …* / ✘ *Suggest me a book* ✔ *Suggest a book **to me***.
:::

### 11.3.5 Cognate Object

An intransitive verb sometimes takes an object **similar in meaning** to itself. This is called a **cognate object**.

::: ex
- He **lived** a happy **life**.
- She **sang** a sweet **song**.
- They **fought** a hard **fight / battle**.
- He **died** a heroic **death**.
- I **dreamt** a strange **dream**.
- He **ran** a **race**. / She **slept** a sound **sleep**. / He **laughed** a hearty **laugh**.
:::

### 11.3.6 Reflexive Object

Some verbs normally take a reflexive pronoun as object (Chapter 08): *absent oneself, avail oneself, pride oneself, enjoy oneself, acquit oneself, revenge oneself*.

## 11.4 Verbs of Incomplete Predication and Complements

Some verbs do not make complete sense alone and need a **complement** (a word or phrase that *completes* the predicate). They are called **verbs of incomplete predication**.

**(a) Intransitive verbs with a subjective complement (linking verbs):**

::: ex
- He **is** *a doctor*. / She **became** *famous*. / They **seem** *happy*.
- The milk **turned** *sour*. / He **looks** *tired*. / The plan **proved** *useless*.
:::

The complement describes the **subject**, so it is called a **subjective complement**. It is a noun or an adjective (Chapter 09.6), not an adverb.

**(b) Transitive verbs with an objective complement:**

::: ex
- They **made** him *captain*. (*him* = object; *captain* = objective complement)
- We **elected** her *chairman*.
- The news **made** her *sad*.
- I **found** the book *interesting*.
- They **called** him *a liar*.
- She **painted** the room *blue*.
:::

### 11.4.1 Linking (Copular) Verbs

| Group | Verbs |
|---|---|
| Being | be, seem, appear, look |
| Sensing | feel, smell, taste, sound, look |
| Becoming | become, get, grow, turn, go, come, fall, run (*run dry*), wear (*wear thin*) |
| Remaining | remain, stay, keep, continue, rest |
| Proving | prove, turn out |

## 11.5 Principal and Auxiliary Verbs

A **principal (main) verb** carries the main meaning. An **auxiliary (helping) verb** helps the main verb to form **tense, voice, mood, questions and negatives**.

::: ex
- He **is writing** a letter. (*is* = auxiliary; *writing* = main verb)
- They **have finished** the work.
- **Do** you **know** him?
- You **must obey** the rules.
- The letter **was written** by him.
:::

### 11.5.1 Primary Auxiliaries: *Be, Have, Do*

These three can be **both main verbs and auxiliaries**.

| Verb | As auxiliary | As main verb |
|---|---|---|
| **be** (*am, is, are, was, were, been, being*) | Continuous tenses: *He **is** reading.* Passive: *The work **was** done.* | *He **is** a teacher. They **were** at home.* |
| **have** (*has, have, had*) | Perfect tenses: *She **has** gone.* | *I **have** a car. We **had** lunch.* |
| **do** (*does, do, did*) | Questions/negatives in simple tenses: ***Do** you smoke? He **did** not come.* Emphasis: *I **do** love you.* | *He **does** his homework. I **did** the work.* |

::: note
**Uses of *be* + infinitive:** *be to* expresses plan, arrangement, order or destiny: *The PM **is to** visit Japan next week. You **are to** report at nine. He **was** never **to** see his home again.* **Uses of *have to*** for obligation: *I **have to** go now.* (Chapter 13)
:::

### 11.5.2 Modal Auxiliaries

***Can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must, ought (to), need, dare, used (to)*** are **modal auxiliaries**. They express ability, permission, possibility, obligation, advice and so on, and never change for the subject. Chapter 13 is devoted to them.

## 11.6 Finite and Non-Finite Verbs

A **finite verb** is limited (*finite* = limited) by the **subject's person and number** and by **tense**. It changes when the subject or tense changes. Every independent sentence must have at least one finite verb.

::: ex
- He **writes**. / They **write**. / He **wrote**. → *writes, write, wrote* are finite.
:::

A **non-finite verb** is not limited by person, number or tense. It cannot be the main verb of a sentence by itself. There are three non-finites:

| Non-finite | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Infinitive** | (*to*) + base form | *I want **to go**. He made me **laugh**.* |
| **Gerund** | base + *-ing*, used as a noun | ***Swimming** is good exercise.* |
| **Participle** | present (*-ing*), past (*-ed/-en*), perfect (*having + V3*) | *a **running** train; a **broken** cup; **Having finished**, he left.* |

::: ex
- I want **to go**. / He wants **to go**. / They wanted **to go**. → *want, wants, wanted* change (finite); *to go* does not change (non-finite).
- ✘ *Rahim going to school.* → not a sentence (no finite verb). ✔ *Rahim **is** going to school.*
- ✘ *The boy, having finished his work.* → fragment. ✔ *The boy, having finished his work, **went** out.*
:::

Chapter 14 deals with gerunds, participles and infinitives in detail.

## 11.7 Regular and Irregular Verbs

- **Regular verbs** form their past tense (V2) and past participle (V3) by adding ***-ed / -d***: *walk – walked – walked; love – loved – loved*.
- **Irregular verbs** form them in other ways: *go – went – gone; write – wrote – written; put – put – put*.

Spelling rules for *-ed*, and the full list of irregular verbs, are in Chapter 12.

## 11.8 Mood

**Mood** is the form of a verb that shows the **manner** in which the action is expressed — as a fact, a command, or a wish/imagination.

### 11.8.1 Indicative Mood

States a **fact** or asks a **question**: *The sun **rises** in the east. **Does** he live here? If it **rains**, we shall stay at home* (a real possibility).

### 11.8.2 Imperative Mood

Expresses a **command, request, advice, warning or prayer**. The subject *you* is usually understood.

::: ex
- **Shut** the door. (command)
- Please **help** me. (request)
- **Take** care of your health. (advice)
- **Don't** touch the wire. (warning/prohibition)
- **Let** us go. / **Let** him come. (proposal/permission)
- **Forgive** us our sins. (prayer)
:::

### 11.8.3 Subjunctive Mood

The **subjunctive** expresses a **wish, supposition, doubt, condition contrary to fact, purpose, or a demand/suggestion**. Its forms are special: the **base form** for all persons in the present subjunctive (*he go, she be*) and ***were*** for all persons in the past subjunctive.

**(a) Present subjunctive (base form):**

::: ex
- **Long live** Bangladesh! (✘ Long lives)
- **God bless** you! (✘ God blesses you — as a prayer)
- **God save** the King!
- **Heaven help** us!
- **Be** that as it may, … / **Come** what may, …
- After verbs of demand, suggestion, proposal, recommendation, insistence, order, request: *I **suggest** that he **go** home. The doctor **recommended** that she **take** rest. The court **ordered** that the man **be** released. It is **essential** that every citizen **pay** tax.* (British English also uses *should*: *that he **should go***.)
:::

**(b) Past subjunctive (*were* for all persons):**

::: ex
- **If I were** a bird, I would fly. (✘ If I was — informal)
- **I wish I were** rich. (✘ I wish I was — in exams)
- He talks **as if he were** the king.
- **Suppose** she **were** here, what would you say?
- **It is time** we **went** home. (*It is time + past* = it is already late)
- **I would rather** you **came** tomorrow.
:::

**(c) Past perfect subjunctive (unreal past):**

::: ex
- **If I had known**, I would have helped you.
- **I wish I had studied** harder.
- He looked **as if he had seen** a ghost.
:::

::: tip
**Exam Tip — The *suggest/insist* trap:** After *suggest, propose, recommend, insist, demand, order, request, urge, move, ask (formal)* and adjectives *essential, necessary, vital, important, imperative* + *that*, use the **base form** (or *should* + base), never the *-s* form or past form: ✘ *He insisted that she goes.* ✔ *He insisted that she **go** / **should go***. This is a top GMAT idiom too.
:::

## 11.9 Stative and Dynamic Verbs

**Dynamic (action) verbs** describe actions that can be in progress: *run, write, eat, play, build*. They can be used in **continuous** tenses.

**Stative verbs** describe **states** (mental states, feelings, senses, possession, relations). They are normally **not used in continuous tenses**.

| Group | Stative verbs |
|---|---|
| Mental states | know, believe, understand, think (= believe), suppose, remember, forget, realise, recognise, doubt, imagine, mean, mind |
| Feelings | love, like, hate, dislike, prefer, want, wish, desire, need, care, fear, adore, detest |
| Senses / perception | see, hear, smell, taste, feel, notice, perceive (when involuntary) |
| Possession & relations | have (= own), own, possess, belong, contain, consist, include, owe, depend, matter, deserve, lack |
| Being / appearance | be, seem, appear, look (= seem), resemble, cost, weigh, measure, equal, differ |

::: ex
- ✘ I am knowing him. ✔ I **know** him.
- ✘ She is having a car. ✔ She **has** a car.
- ✘ This book is belonging to me. ✔ This book **belongs** to me.
- ✘ I am understanding the problem. ✔ I **understand** the problem.
- ✘ Are you hearing me? ✔ **Can** you **hear** me? / **Do** you **hear** me?
:::

::: note
**Some stative verbs have dynamic meanings too**, and then the continuous is correct: *I **think** he is honest* (opinion) vs *I **am thinking** about the problem* (mental activity). *She **has** a car* (possession) vs *She **is having** lunch / a bath / a good time* (activity). *The soup **tastes** good* vs *The cook **is tasting** the soup*. *I **see** a bird* vs *I **am seeing** the doctor tomorrow* (meeting). *He **is** foolish* (character) vs *He **is being** foolish* (behaviour now). *I **love** music* vs *I'm **loving** it* (informal advertising — avoid in exams).
:::

## 11.10 Some Verbs Often Confused

| Verb pair | Correct use |
|---|---|
| **lie – lay – lain** (intransitive: recline) vs **lay – laid – laid** (transitive: put down) | *He **lies** on the bed. He **lay** there yesterday.* / *She **lays** the table. The hen **laid** an egg.* |
| **lie – lied – lied** (tell a lie) | *He **lied** to me.* |
| **rise – rose – risen** (intr.) vs **raise – raised – raised** (tr.) | *Prices **rise**. The sun **rose**.* / *They **raised** the price. **Raise** your hand.* |
| **sit – sat – sat** (intr.) vs **set – set – set** (tr.) | ***Sit** down.* / ***Set** the vase on the table.* |
| **fall – fell – fallen** vs **fell – felled – felled** (cut down) | *The tree **fell**.* / *They **felled** the tree.* |
| **hang – hung – hung** (objects) vs **hang – hanged – hanged** (execute) | *He **hung** the picture.* / *The murderer was **hanged**.* |
| **find – found – found** vs **found – founded – founded** (establish) | *I **found** my pen.* / *He **founded** a school.* |
| **wind – wound – wound** (turn) vs **wound – wounded – wounded** (injure) | *He **wound** the clock.* / *He was **wounded** in war.* |
| **bear – bore – borne** (carry/endure) vs **born** (birth, passive) | *She has **borne** much pain. She has **borne** three children.* / *He was **born** in 2000.* |
| **beat – beat – beaten** vs **bit/bitten** (bite) | *He was **beaten**.* / *He was **bitten** by a dog.* |
| **flee – fled – fled** (run away) vs **fly – flew – flown** | *The thief **fled**.* / *The bird **flew** away.* |
| **flow – flowed – flowed** vs **fly – flew – flown** | *The river **flowed**.* (✘ flew) |
| **borrow** (take) vs **lend** (give) | *May I **borrow** your pen? Can you **lend** me your pen?* (✘ lend me = borrow from me) |
| **learn** vs **teach** | *I **learn** English. He **teaches** me English.* (✘ He learns me) |
| **rob** (a person/place) vs **steal** (a thing) | *They **robbed** the bank / him.* / *They **stole** his money.* |
| **say** vs **tell** | *He **said** (to me) that … / He **told** me that …* (✘ He said me) |
| **bring** vs **take** | ***Bring** it here. **Take** it there.* |
| **hear** vs **listen** | *I **heard** a noise. **Listen** to me.* |
| **see** vs **look** vs **watch** | *I **saw** him. **Look** at the board. **Watch** the match.* |

## 11.11 Common Errors with Verbs

::: errs
1. ✘ Explain me this sentence. ✔ Explain this sentence **to me**.
2. ✘ He said me to go. ✔ He **told** me to go.
3. ✘ I am knowing the answer. ✔ I **know** the answer.
4. ✘ The book is lying on the table since morning. ✔ The book **has been lying** on the table since morning.
5. ✘ He laid on the bed. ✔ He **lay** on the bed.
6. ✘ The hen has lain an egg. ✔ The hen has **laid** an egg.
7. ✘ The murderer was hung. ✔ The murderer was **hanged**.
8. ✘ The price of rice has raised. ✔ The price of rice has **risen**.
9. ✘ He rose the question. ✔ He **raised** the question.
10. ✘ Please lend your book from me. ✔ Please **lend** me your book. / May I **borrow** your book?
11. ✘ He learnt me English. ✔ He **taught** me English.
12. ✘ They stole the bank. ✔ They **robbed** the bank.
13. ✘ If I was a king, I would help the poor. ✔ If I **were** a king, …
14. ✘ I suggest that he goes at once. ✔ I suggest that he **go / should go** at once.
15. ✘ Long lives our President! ✔ Long **live** our President!
16. ✘ He absented from the meeting. ✔ He absented **himself** from the meeting.
17. ✘ It is time we go home. ✔ It is time we **went** home.
18. ✘ He was born at 1990. ✔ He was born **in** 1990. (verb fine; preposition error)
19. ✘ The river flew into the sea. ✔ The river **flowed** into the sea.
20. ✘ The robber flew away. ✔ The robber **fled**.
21. ✘ She has borne in Dhaka. ✔ She was **born** in Dhaka.
22. ✘ Describe me the accident. ✔ Describe the accident **to me**.
23. ✘ This house is belonging to my uncle. ✔ This house **belongs** to my uncle.
24. ✘ He set on the chair. ✔ He **sat** on the chair.
25. ✘ I wish I was a doctor. ✔ I wish I **were** a doctor.
:::

## 11.12 Verbs in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
- **The subjunctive** is a classic GMAT idiom: *require/demand/insist/recommend **that** + base verb* — ✘ *The law requires that each employer **provides** insurance.* ✔ *… that each employer **provide** insurance.* Also ✘ *requires … **should** provide* is marked wordy in GMAT.
- **Verb vs noun (concision):** GMAT prefers strong verbs: ✘ *made an announcement of* → ✔ *announced*.
- **Sentence fragments (SAT):** every sentence needs a **finite** verb. A participle or infinitive alone is not enough: ✘ *The committee, having reviewed all the proposals carefully.* ✔ *The committee, having reviewed all the proposals carefully, **chose** the cheapest one.*
- **Transitive verb idioms:** ✘ *discuss about*, ✘ *emphasize on*, ✘ *stress on*, ✘ *request for*, ✘ *order for*, ✘ *comprise of* → ✔ ***discuss** the matter*, ***emphasize** the point*, ***request** help*, ***comprise** five parts* / ***is composed of** five parts*.
- **GRE vocabulary** includes many precise verbs: *abate, abjure, abrogate, castigate, corroborate, debunk, disparage, eschew, exacerbate, extol, foment, impugn, mitigate, obviate, placate, repudiate, stymie, undermine, vacillate, vilify*.
:::
