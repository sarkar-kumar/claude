## 2.1 What Is a Noun?

A **noun** is a word used as the **name** of a person, place, thing, animal, quality, action, state or idea. The word comes from the Latin *nomen*, meaning "name". Anything we can see, touch, hear, think about or talk about has a name, and that name is a noun.

::: ex
- **Kazi Nazrul Islam** was a great **poet**. (person)
- **Cox's Bazar** has the longest sea **beach** in the **world**. (place, thing)
- **Honesty** is the best **policy**. (quality, idea)
- His **arrival** caused great **joy**. (action, feeling)
- **Childhood** is the happiest **time** of **life**. (state)
:::

## 2.2 Kinds of Noun

Traditional grammar (Nesfield, Wren & Martin), which is followed in our exams, divides nouns into **five** main kinds:

1. **Proper Noun**
2. **Common Noun**
3. **Collective Noun**
4. **Material Noun**
5. **Abstract Noun**

The first four are **concrete nouns** (they name things that exist physically); the fifth names things we can only think of. Modern grammar adds another very useful division: **countable** and **uncountable** nouns (see Chapter 03).

### 2.2.1 Proper Noun

A **proper noun** is the name of a **particular** person, place or thing. *Proper* means "one's own". A proper noun always begins with a **capital letter**.

::: ex
- **People:** Sheikh Hasina, Rabindranath, Shakespeare, Newton
- **Places:** Dhaka, Bangladesh, the Padma, the Himalayas, Asia
- **Particular things/institutions:** the Quran, the Gita, *The Daily Star*, Dhaka University, Bangladesh Bank
- **Days, months, festivals:** Sunday, February, Eid-ul-Fitr, Pahela Baishakh (but **not** seasons: summer, winter)
- **Languages and nationalities:** Bangla, English, a Bangladeshi, the Japanese
:::

**Proper noun used as a common noun.** When a proper noun is used to mean "a person *like* that famous person", it becomes a **common noun** and takes an article:

::: ex
- He is **the Newton** of our age. (= a great scientist like Newton)
- Kalidasa is called **the Shakespeare** of India.
- Dhaka is **the Venice** of the East (historically said of Dhaka because of its canals).
- **A Daniel** come to judgement! (= a wise judge — from Shakespeare)
- He thinks he is **a Hitler**. (= a dictator)
- There are many **Mir Jafars** in our society. (= traitors)
:::

### 2.2.2 Common Noun

A **common noun** is a name **shared by every** person, place or thing of the **same kind or class**. *Common* means "shared".

::: ex
- boy, girl, teacher, city, country, river, book, bird, chair, doctor
- *Dhaka* is a proper noun, but *city* is a common noun; *Padma* is proper, *river* is common.
:::

**Common noun used as an abstract noun.** With *the*, a common noun sometimes means the **quality** associated with it:

::: ex
- **The mother** in her rose up. (= motherly love)
- **The judge** in him prevailed over **the father**. (= his sense of justice over his fatherly feeling)
- **The pen** is mightier than **the sword**. (= writing/ideas vs military force)
- **The beggar** in him was awakened.
:::

### 2.2.3 Collective Noun

A **collective noun** is the name of a **group or collection** of persons or things taken together and spoken of **as one whole**.

::: ex
- **class, team, army, family, crowd, committee, jury, audience, staff, government, parliament, crew, mob, flock, herd, fleet, bunch**
:::

**Verb with a collective noun.** If the group acts **as a single unit**, use a **singular** verb; if the members act **as individuals** (often disagreeing), use a **plural** verb. (Detailed in Chapter 23.)

::: ex
- The jury **has** given its verdict. (one unit)
- The jury **were** divided in their opinions. (individuals)
- Our team **is** the best. / The team **are** taking their seats.
- The committee **has** submitted its report.
:::

::: note
**Noun of multitude:** When a collective noun denotes the *individuals* of the group rather than the group as a whole, it is called a **noun of multitude** and takes a plural verb: *The crowd **were** throwing stones at one another.* Some words are **always plural** in sense even though they look singular: *people, police, cattle, poultry, gentry, clergy, vermin* — *The police **are** coming. Cattle **are** grazing.* (✘ cattles, ✘ polices, ✘ poultries)
:::

### 2.2.4 Material Noun

A **material noun** is the name of a **substance** or **material** out of which things are made. It is usually **uncountable** and has no plural form in its basic sense.

::: ex
- **Metals:** gold, silver, iron, copper, brass, steel
- **Food/drink:** rice, wheat, milk, water, tea, sugar, salt, oil, butter
- **Others:** wood, cotton, wool, silk, paper, glass, stone, sand, cement, coal
:::

Rules:

1. A material noun **does not take *a/an*** and usually no plural: *Gold is a precious metal* (✘ A gold is…). *Water is essential for life.*
2. It takes *the* when **particularised**: *The water of this pond is dirty. The gold in her ring is pure.*
3. To count it, use a **unit word**: *a piece of wood, a bar of gold, a loaf of bread, a cup of tea, a sheet of paper, a grain of rice, a lump of sugar, a drop of water, a bottle of milk*.
4. When a material noun names a **thing made of** that material, it becomes a **common noun** and can be counted:

| Material noun | Common noun meaning |
|---|---|
| glass (material) | a glass (drinking vessel); glasses (spectacles) |
| iron | an iron (for pressing clothes); irons (fetters, chains) |
| paper | a paper (newspaper / exam paper / research article); papers (documents) |
| copper | coppers (copper coins; BrE slang: policemen) |
| tin | a tin (a can) |
| wood | a wood / the woods (a small forest) |
| stone | a stone (a pebble; also a unit of weight = 14 pounds) |
| silver | the silver (silverware); silver (coins) |
| cloth | a cloth (a piece for cleaning); clothes (garments) |
| rubber | a rubber (an eraser, BrE) |

::: note
**Material noun as an adjective:** *a **gold** watch, a **silk** saree, a **cotton** shirt, a **stone** wall.* Some materials have special adjective forms used **figuratively**: *golden* (*golden opportunity*, *golden days*), *silken* (*silken hair*), *wooden* (*wooden expression*), *leaden* (*leaden sky*), *woollen* (*woollen garments*), *earthen* (*earthen pot*), *brazen* (*brazen lie*), *wheaten* (*wheaten bread*), *flaxen* (*flaxen hair*). ✔ *a gold ring* (made of gold) vs *a golden opportunity* (excellent).
:::

### 2.2.5 Abstract Noun

An **abstract noun** is the name of a **quality**, **action**, **state**, **feeling** or **idea** that we can only think about or feel, but cannot see or touch. *Abstract* means "drawn off" — the idea is drawn off from the object.

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Quality | goodness, honesty, beauty, wisdom, bravery, kindness, cruelty, humility |
| Action | laughter, theft, movement, judgement, arrival, flight, choice |
| State | childhood, boyhood, youth, slavery, poverty, sleep, sickness, death, freedom |
| Feeling | love, hatred, fear, anger, joy, sorrow, pity |
| Arts & sciences | grammar, music, chemistry, philosophy, mathematics |

Abstract nouns are usually **uncountable** and take **no article** when used in a general sense: *Honesty is the best policy. Knowledge is power.* (✘ The honesty is …) But they take an article when **particularised**: *the honesty **of this man***, *the wisdom **of Solomon***.

**Abstract noun used as a common noun:** *She is **a beauty*** (= a beautiful woman). *He is **a youth*** (= a young man). *He is **an authority** on economics* (= an expert). *Our **acquaintances*** (= people we know). *He is **a success*** (= a successful person). *The **kindnesses** you have shown me* (= kind acts).

## 2.4 Countable and Uncountable Nouns (Overview)

- **Countable nouns** can be counted and have singular and plural forms: *a book — two books; a child — three children*. In the singular they need a determiner (*a, the, my, this*): ✘ *I bought book.* ✔ *I bought **a** book.*
- **Uncountable nouns** cannot be counted and have only one form, which takes a **singular verb**: *water, rice, information, advice, furniture, luggage, news, knowledge*. ✘ *an advice*, ✘ *informations*. ✔ *a piece of advice*, ✔ *some information*.

The whole of Chapter 03 is devoted to the problems this distinction creates.

## 2.5 Compound Nouns

A **compound noun** is made of two or more words that together name one thing.

| Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|
| Noun + Noun | classroom, bus stop, toothbrush, football, headmaster, sunrise |
| Adjective + Noun | blackboard, greenhouse, software, high school, full moon |
| Verb + Noun | pickpocket, breakwater, scarecrow |
| Gerund + Noun | swimming pool, dining room, walking stick, waiting room |
| Noun + Gerund | sightseeing, fruit-picking, horse-riding |
| Noun + Preposition + Noun | mother-in-law, commander-in-chief, man-of-war |
| Verb + Adverb/Particle | passer-by, take-off, breakdown, drawback, outbreak, setback |
| Noun + Adjective | court-martial, attorney-general, poet laureate |

Compound nouns may be written as one word (*classroom*), with a hyphen (*mother-in-law*) or as separate words (*bus stop*). Their plurals are explained in Chapter 06 (*sons-in-law, passers-by, courts-martial, spoonfuls*).

## 2.6 Case of Nouns

**Case** is the form or position of a noun or pronoun that shows its **relation to other words** in the sentence. English nouns have three cases:

1. **Nominative (Subjective) Case** — the noun is the **subject** of a verb: ***Rahim** plays football.*
2. **Objective (Accusative) Case** — the noun is the **object** of a verb or a preposition: *I met **Rahim**. I gave it to **Rahim**.*
3. **Possessive (Genitive) Case** — the noun shows **ownership/relation**: ***Rahim's** pen.*

Nouns have the same form in the nominative and objective cases, so we identify the case only by **position**. (Pronouns change form: *he/him/his* — see Chapter 08.)

::: ex
- **The boy** hit **the ball**. → *boy*: nominative; *ball*: objective
- **The ball** hit **the boy**. → *ball*: nominative; *boy*: objective
- He gave **the beggar** **a coin**. → *beggar*: indirect object (dative); *coin*: direct object
- **Karim**, come here. → *Karim*: nominative of address (vocative)
- **The sun** having set, we returned home. → nominative absolute
:::

### 2.6.1 Forming the Possessive

| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| Singular noun → add **'s** | the boy's book, the cat's tail, Rina's dress |
| Plural ending in **-s** → add only **'** | the boys' hostel, girls' school, the teachers' room |
| Plural not ending in -s → add **'s** | children's park, men's wear, women's rights, people's demand |
| Singular ending in -s → **'s** (usual) or **'** (classical names) | James's car / James' car; Keats's poems; *Moses'*, *Jesus'*, *Socrates'* (classical) |
| Compound noun → **'s** on the **last word** | my mother-in-law's house; the commander-in-chief's order; somebody else's book |
| Joint possession → **'s** on the **last** name only | Rahim and Karim's shop (one shop owned jointly) |
| Separate possession → **'s** on **each** name | Rahim's and Karim's shops (two shops) |
| Long phrase as possessor | the Prime Minister of Bangladesh's speech → better: *the speech of the Prime Minister of Bangladesh* |

::: rule
**Sake phrases:** Before *sake*, a word ending in an **s**-sound drops the extra *s*: *for **goodness'** sake, for **conscience'** sake, for **justice'** sake*. Otherwise use *'s*: *for **God's** sake, for **heaven's** sake, for **mercy's** sake, for **pity's** sake.*
:::

### 2.6.2 When to Use 's and When to Use *of*

1. **'s** is normally used with **living beings**: *the man's hat, the dog's tail, Hasan's house*.
2. With **lifeless things**, use ***of***: *the leg **of** the table* (✘ the table's leg), *the roof **of** the house*, *the cover **of** the book*.
3. **Exceptions** — the *'s* is also used with:
   - **Personified objects:** *Nature's law, Fortune's favourite, the moon's rays, the sun's heat, England's glory, Bangladesh's economy, the earth's surface, the ship's crew, the nation's pride*.
   - **Time, distance, weight, value:** *a day's journey, a week's holiday, an hour's walk, two years' imprisonment, a stone's throw, a mile's distance, a pound's weight, a hundred taka's worth, today's paper, tomorrow's meeting*.
   - **Fixed phrases:** *at his wits' end, at one's fingers' ends, at arm's length, to one's heart's content, in his mind's eye, for mercy's sake, a hair's breadth, out of harm's way, at death's door, journey's end, the water's edge*.
   - **Organisations and places:** *the government's policy, the company's profit, the university's decision, the city's traffic*.

### 2.6.3 The Double Possessive and the Elliptical Possessive

**Double possessive** — *of* + *'s* together — is used to mean "one of several": *He is **a friend of my father's*** (= one of my father's friends). *This is **a novel of Tagore's**.* With pronouns: *a friend **of mine/yours/his/ours***. (✘ a friend of me)

**Elliptical (implied) possessive** — the noun after *'s* is understood and omitted: *I bought it at the **chemist's*** (= chemist's shop). *I am going to my **uncle's*** (= uncle's house). *We prayed at **St Paul's*** (= St Paul's Cathedral). *This book is **Rahim's**.* *My pen is better than **Karim's***.

::: note
**Possessive before a gerund (formal style, favoured by GMAT/SAT):** *I don't mind **your** coming late* (formal) / *I don't mind **you** coming late* (informal). *We were surprised at **Rahim's** winning the prize.* (✘ at Rahim winning — informal) In BCS-type error detection, the possessive form is the "correct" answer.
:::

### 2.6.4 Apposition

A noun placed **beside** another noun to explain it is in **apposition** and is in the **same case**:

::: ex
- **Mr. Rahman, our headmaster,** is ill. (*headmaster* in apposition to *Mr. Rahman* — nominative)
- I met **Rahim, the captain**. (objective)
- This is **Karim the doctor's** house. (the *'s* goes on the appositive, not on both)
:::

## 2.7 Functions of a Noun in a Sentence

| Function | Example |
|---|---|
| Subject of a verb | **Birds** fly. |
| Direct object | He wrote **a letter**. |
| Indirect object | He wrote **his mother** a letter. |
| Object of a preposition | He lives in **Khulna**. |
| Subjective complement | He became **a doctor**. |
| Objective complement | They made him **captain**. / They elected her **chairman**. |
| Apposition | Dhaka, **the capital**, is crowded. |
| Nominative of address | **Friends**, listen to me. |
| Adjective (noun adjunct) | a **village** road, a **cotton** mill |
| Adverb (adverbial object) | He walked **ten miles**. He waited **an hour**. |

## 2.8 Common Errors with Nouns

Many of these errors are discussed in detail in Chapters 03 (uncountable nouns) and 06 (number); here are the most common ones made by Bangladeshi learners.

::: errs
1. ✘ She gave me many advices. ✔ She gave me **much advice / many pieces of advice**.
2. ✘ The sceneries of Sylhet are charming. ✔ The **scenery** of Sylhet **is** charming.
3. ✘ He bought new furnitures. ✔ He bought new **furniture**.
4. ✘ I have many informations. ✔ I have **a lot of information**.
5. ✘ The cattles are grazing. ✔ The **cattle** are grazing.
6. ✘ The police has arrested the thief. ✔ The police **have** arrested the thief.
7. ✘ He is my cousin brother. ✔ He is my **cousin**.
8. ✘ She has two issues. (= children) ✔ She has two **children**. (*issue* = offspring is legal/formal; *issueless* is not standard English → use *childless*)
9. ✘ There was a pin-drop silence. ✔ There was **a silence in which one could hear a pin drop** / **complete silence**. (*pin-drop silence* is Indian/Bangladeshi English)
10. ✘ He gave me a ten-takas note. ✔ … a **ten-taka** note.
11. ✘ I had my hairs cut. ✔ I had my **hair** cut. (*hairs* = individual strands: *There are two grey hairs on his head.*)
12. ✘ The table's leg is broken. ✔ The **leg of the table** is broken.
13. ✘ This is my brother-in-law's car. — ✔ correct (the 's goes on the last word).
14. ✘ He is a friend of me. ✔ He is a friend **of mine**.
15. ✘ He came with his family members. ✔ He came with **his family** (or *members of his family*).
16. ✘ The childrens are playing. ✔ The **children** are playing.
17. ✘ I need a good accommodation. ✔ I need good **accommodation** (BrE uncountable; AmE *accommodations*).
18. ✘ My all friends came. ✔ **All my** friends came.
19. ✘ He is an M.A. in English. — ✔ correct (*an* because "M" is pronounced "em").
20. ✘ The boys' hostel's gate is closed. ✔ The **gate of the boys' hostel** is closed. (avoid double *'s* on things)
:::

## 2.9 Nouns in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
**Nominalisation (wordiness).** Turning verbs into nouns (*decide → make a decision*, *consider → give consideration to*) makes sentences heavy. SAT and GMAT prefer the **verb**: ✘ *The committee made a decision to give consideration to the proposal.* ✔ *The committee **decided** to **consider** the proposal.*

**Action nouns vs *-ing* nouns (GMAT).** When a proper noun form exists, GMAT prefers it to an *-ing* form with *the … of*: ✘ *the developing of new drugs* ✔ *the **development** of new drugs*; ✘ *the constructing of the bridge* ✔ *the **construction** of the bridge*.

**Countable vs uncountable quantifiers.** *fewer* (countable) vs *less* (uncountable); *number* (countable) vs *amount* (uncountable); *many* vs *much*. ✘ *less cars* ✔ ***fewer** cars*; ✘ *the amount of students* ✔ *the **number** of students*.

**Pronoun-noun agreement.** A collective noun like *company, team, government* is treated as **singular** in American English (the language of the SAT/GRE/GMAT): ✔ *The company announced **its** results* (✘ *their* results).
:::

::: tip
**Exam Strategy:** When asked to identify the *kind* of noun, ask in this order: (1) Is it the name of a **particular** one? → Proper. (2) Is it a **group** spoken of as one? → Collective. (3) Is it a **substance**? → Material. (4) Can it be **seen or touched**? If **not** → Abstract. (5) Otherwise → Common. Remember that **context changes the kind**: *glass* (material) vs *a glass* (common); *beauty* (abstract) vs *a beauty* (common); *Newton* (proper) vs *the Newton of our age* (common).
:::
