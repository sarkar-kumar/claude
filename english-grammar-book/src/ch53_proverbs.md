## 53.1 What Is a Proverb?

A **proverb** is a **short, well-known saying** that expresses a **general truth or piece of advice** based on common experience: *Honesty is the best policy. A stitch in time saves nine.* Unlike an idiom (which is a phrase used inside a sentence), a proverb is usually a **complete sentence** that can stand alone.

In Bangladeshi exams, proverbs are tested in three ways:

1. **Bangla equivalents** — "*The Bangla of 'Too many cooks spoil the broth' is …*" (BCS Preliminary, Bank and admission MCQs).
2. **Translation** — translating a Bangla proverb into English in the written paper (*অধিক সন্ন্যাসীতে গাজন নষ্ট* → *Too many cooks spoil the broth*).
3. **Meaning and use** — explaining or using a proverb in a sentence, paragraph or essay (amplification).

::: tip
**How to use proverbs well:** Learn the **English proverb**, its **Bangla equivalent** and its **meaning** together. When translating a Bangla proverb, **do not translate word for word** — give the established English proverb. (*নাচতে না জানলে উঠান বাঁকা* is ✔ *A bad workman quarrels with his tools*, not ✘ *If one cannot dance, the yard is crooked*.)
:::

**A bad workman quarrels with his tools.** [★]{.star} [নাচতে না জানলে উঠান বাঁকা।]{.bn} — An unskilled person blames his tools or circumstances for his failure.

**Beauty is but skin-deep.** [বাহ্যিক সৌন্দর্য ক্ষণস্থায়ী।]{.bn} — Outer beauty is superficial; character matters more.

**Beggars cannot be choosers.** [★]{.star} [ভিক্ষার চাল কাঁড়া আর আকাঁড়া।]{.bn} — People in need must accept what they are given.

**Health is wealth.** [★]{.star} [স্বাস্থ্যই সকল সুখের মূল।]{.bn} — Good health is the greatest treasure.

**Tall trees catch much wind.** [বড় গাছেই ঝড় লাগে।]{.bn} — Important people face the most criticism and danger.

**The child is father of the man.** [★]{.star} [ঊষাতেই দিনের পরিচয়।]{.bn} — Childhood shows what a person will become. (Wordsworth; similar: *Morning shows the day.*)

**The darkest hour is just before the dawn.** [রাত যত গভীর হয়, প্রভাত তত নিকটে আসে।]{.bn} — Things are worst just before they improve.

**The early bird catches the worm.** [ভোরের পাখি সুযোগ পায়।]{.bn} — Those who start early succeed.

**The face is the index of the mind.** [★]{.star} [মুখ মনের আয়না।]{.bn} — One's face shows one's thoughts and feelings.

**The grass is always greener on the other side.** [নদীর এপার কহে ছাড়িয়া নিশ্বাস, ওপারেতে সর্বসুখ আমার বিশ্বাস।]{.bn} — Other people's situations always look better than our own.

**The pen is mightier than the sword.** [★]{.star} [অসির চেয়ে মসী বড়।]{.bn} — Writing and ideas are more powerful than force.

**The pot calls the kettle black.** [★]{.star} [চালুনি বলে সুঁচ, তোর পেছনে কেন ছেঁদা।]{.bn} — Criticising others for a fault one has oneself.

**The proof of the pudding is in the eating.** [খেয়েই স্বাদ বোঝা যায়।]{.bn} — The real value of something is shown by testing it.

**There is no royal road to learning.** [শিক্ষার কোনো সহজ পথ নেই।]{.bn} — Learning needs hard work for everyone.

**There is no smoke without fire.** [★]{.star} [যা রটে তার কিছু তো বটে।]{.bn} — Every rumour has some truth in it.

**Time and tide wait for none.** [★]{.star} [সময় ও স্রোত কারও জন্য অপেক্ষা করে না।]{.bn} — Time passes for everyone; don't waste it.

**To carry coals to Newcastle.** [তেলা মাথায় তেল দেওয়া।]{.bn} — To do something unnecessary (Newcastle was famous for coal).

**To err is human, to forgive divine.** [মানুষ মাত্রই ভুল করে, ক্ষমা করা মহত্ত্ব।]{.bn} — Everyone makes mistakes; forgiving is noble. (Pope)

**Too many cooks spoil the broth.** [★]{.star} [অধিক সন্ন্যাসীতে গাজন নষ্ট।]{.bn} — Too many people managing one task ruin it.

**Too much of anything is bad.** [অতি বাড় বেড়ো না, ঝড়ে পড়ে যাবে।]{.bn} — Excess of anything, even good, is harmful.

**Truth is stranger than fiction.** [সত্য কল্পকাহিনির চেয়েও বিস্ময়কর।]{.bn} — Real events can be more surprising than stories.

**Two of a trade seldom agree.** [এক বনে দুই বাঘ থাকে না।]{.bn} — Rivals in the same business rarely get along.

**Uneasy lies the head that wears a crown.** [রাজমুকুটে সুখ নেই।]{.bn} — Responsibility brings worry. (Shakespeare)

**Union is strength.** [★]{.star} [একতাই বল।]{.bn} — Unity makes people strong.

**Walls have ears.** [★]{.star} [দেয়ালেরও কান আছে।]{.bn} — Be careful; secrets may be overheard.

**Waste not, want not.** [অপচয় না করলে অভাব হয় না।]{.bn} — If you don't waste, you won't be in need.

**We never know the worth of water till the well is dry.** [★]{.star} [দাঁত থাকতে দাঁতের মর্যাদা বোঝে না।]{.bn} — We value things only after we lose them.

**What can't be cured must be endured.** [যার প্রতিকার নেই, তা সহ্য করতেই হবে।]{.bn} — Accept what you cannot change.

**What is bred in the bone will not come out of the flesh.** [স্বভাব যায় না মলে।]{.bn} — Inborn nature cannot be changed.

**When the cat is away, the mice will play.** [বিড়াল না থাকলে ইঁদুরের উৎসব।]{.bn} — People misbehave when authority is absent.

**Where there is a will, there is a way.** [★]{.star} [ইচ্ছা থাকলে উপায় হয়।]{.bn} — Determination finds a way to succeed.

**While there is life, there is hope.** [যতক্ষণ শ্বাস, ততক্ষণ আশ।]{.bn} — Never give up hope while alive.

## 53.4 Using Proverbs in Writing

Proverbs make essays, paragraphs and letters more forceful when used **sparingly and appropriately**:

::: ex
- **Opening an essay on time management:** *"Time and tide wait for none." Students who realise this early …*
- **Concluding a paragraph on unity:** *As the proverb goes, "Union is strength"; only together can we overcome poverty.*
- **Amplifying a proverb (exam task):** Explain the meaning, give an example or story, and draw a lesson. *"A stitch in time saves nine" means that a small effort at the right time prevents a bigger problem. For example, a minor crack in a dam, if repaired early, costs little, but if ignored, it may cause a disastrous flood. So we should never postpone necessary work.*
:::

::: note
**Avoid** overusing proverbs or using them inaccurately. Never mix two proverbs (✘ *A rolling stone saves nine*), and never change their established words (✘ *Barking dogs never bite* — the proverb says *seldom*).
:::

## 53.5 Proverbs in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
Proverbs are not tested directly in international exams, but:

- **SAT/GRE reading passages** occasionally quote a proverb and then **qualify or challenge** it — understanding the proverb's literal and figurative meaning is necessary for "author's attitude" questions.
- **GRE vocabulary:** *adage, aphorism, maxim, axiom, apothegm, epigram, platitude, truism, bromide, cliché* — all are words for sayings; GRE often distinguishes **wise sayings** (*adage, maxim, aphorism*) from **overused, stale** ones (*platitude, banality, bromide, cliché, truism*).
- **GRE/GMAT essays:** a well-chosen proverb can open an argument, but it must be followed by analysis, not used as proof.
:::
