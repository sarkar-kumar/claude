## 21.1 What Is Tense?

**Tense** is the form of a verb that shows the **time** of an action or state, and also its **aspect** — whether the action is simple, in progress, or completed. The word comes from Latin *tempus*, "time".

There are **three times**: **present**, **past** and **future**. Each has **four aspects**:

1. **Indefinite (Simple)** — the action as a simple fact: *I write.*
2. **Continuous (Progressive)** — the action in progress: *I am writing.*
3. **Perfect** — the action completed (before a point of time): *I have written.*
4. **Perfect Continuous** — the action continuing over a period up to a point: *I have been writing.*

This gives the **twelve tenses** of English.

| | Indefinite | Continuous | Perfect | Perfect Continuous |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Present** | I write / He writes | I am writing | I have written | I have been writing |
| **Past** | I wrote | I was writing | I had written | I had been writing |
| **Future** | I shall/will write | I shall/will be writing | I shall/will have written | I shall/will have been writing |

### 21.1.1 Structures (Active Voice)

| Tense | Affirmative | Negative | Interrogative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present Indefinite | S + V1 (+s/es) | S + do/does not + V1 | Do/Does + S + V1? |
| Present Continuous | S + am/is/are + V-ing | S + am/is/are not + V-ing | Am/Is/Are + S + V-ing? |
| Present Perfect | S + have/has + V3 | S + have/has not + V3 | Have/Has + S + V3? |
| Present Perfect Cont. | S + have/has been + V-ing | S + have/has not been + V-ing | Have/Has + S + been + V-ing? |
| Past Indefinite | S + V2 | S + did not + V1 | Did + S + V1? |
| Past Continuous | S + was/were + V-ing | S + was/were not + V-ing | Was/Were + S + V-ing? |
| Past Perfect | S + had + V3 | S + had not + V3 | Had + S + V3? |
| Past Perfect Cont. | S + had been + V-ing | S + had not been + V-ing | Had + S + been + V-ing? |
| Future Indefinite | S + shall/will + V1 | S + shall/will not + V1 | Shall/Will + S + V1? |
| Future Continuous | S + shall/will be + V-ing | S + shall/will not be + V-ing | Shall/Will + S + be + V-ing? |
| Future Perfect | S + shall/will have + V3 | S + shall/will not have + V3 | Shall/Will + S + have + V3? |
| Future Perfect Cont. | S + shall/will have been + V-ing | S + shall/will not have been + V-ing | Shall/Will + S + have been + V-ing? |

::: note
**Time is not the same as tense.** English has only two *inflected* tenses (present *write/writes* and past *wrote*); "future" is expressed with *will/shall*, *be going to*, the present continuous, etc. The present tense can refer to the future (*The train **leaves** at 6 tomorrow*) and the past tense can refer to unreal present (*If I **were** rich …*). Traditional grammar's twelve-tense system is used in our exams, but always think about **meaning**.
:::

## 21.2 Present Indefinite (Simple Present) Tense

### 21.2.1 Uses

1. **Habitual or repeated actions** (with *always, usually, often, sometimes, never, every day, daily*): *I **get** up at six every day. She **goes** to school by bus.*
2. **Universal / general truths and facts:** *The sun **rises** in the east. Water **boils** at 100 °C. Honesty **is** the best policy.*
3. **Permanent situations:** *He **lives** in Dhaka. My father **works** in a bank.*
4. **Fixed future events (timetables, schedules):** *The train **leaves** at 7 p.m. The exam **begins** next Monday.*
5. **In clauses of time and condition referring to the future** (after *when, before, after, as soon as, until, if, unless, in case*): *I will call you **when** he **comes**. (✘ when he will come) **If** it **rains**, we will stay home.*
6. **Vivid narration (historic present) and newspaper headlines:** *Napoleon now **marches** on Moscow. "PM **inaugurates** Padma Bridge."*
7. **Quotations and what books say:** *Shakespeare **says**, "Frailty, thy name is woman." The Quran **says** …*
8. **Exclamatory sentences beginning with *Here* and *There*:** *Here **comes** the bus!*
9. **With stative verbs** (*know, believe, love, want, own, seem*) for present states: *I **know** him. She **wants** a new phone.*
10. **Instructions and demonstrations:** *First you **take** the flour, then you **add** water.*

::: tip
**Exam Tip — Universal truth in a past-tense sentence:** A universal truth stays in the **present** even after a past reporting verb: *The teacher said that the earth **moves** round the sun.* (✘ moved) *Galileo proved that the earth **is** round.*
:::

## 21.3 Present Continuous (Progressive) Tense

### 21.3.1 Uses

1. **An action happening now** (*now, at present, at the moment, right now, Look!, Listen!*): *He **is reading** now. Look! The baby **is sleeping**.*
2. **A temporary action around the present period:** *I **am reading** a novel these days. She **is staying** with her aunt this month.*
3. **Definite future arrangements:** *I **am meeting** him tomorrow. We **are going** to Cox's Bazar next week.*
4. **Changing or developing situations:** *Prices **are rising**. The climate **is getting** warmer.*
5. **Annoying repeated actions** (with *always, constantly, forever*): *He **is always complaining**. She **is constantly losing** her keys.*

::: note
**Stative verbs** (Chapter 11.9) are not normally used in the continuous: ✘ *I am knowing* ✔ *I know*; ✘ *She is having a car* ✔ *She has a car*; ✘ *This is belonging to me* ✔ *This belongs to me*.
:::

## 21.4 Present Perfect Tense

The present perfect connects the **past with the present**. The action happened at an **unspecified time** before now, and its **result or relevance** continues now.

### 21.4.1 Uses

1. **Recently completed actions (with *just, already, yet, recently, lately*):** *He **has just left**. I **have** already **finished** my homework. **Have** you **eaten** yet?*
2. **Past actions with present results:** *I **have lost** my key* (I don't have it now). *She **has broken** her leg* (it is still broken).
3. **Experiences up to now (with *ever, never, before, once, twice, so far, up to now*):** ***Have** you **ever been** to Nepal? I **have never seen** a tiger. He **has visited** Sylhet three times.*
4. **States or actions continuing from the past up to now (with *since* and *for*):** *I **have known** him **since** 2010. She **has lived** here **for** ten years.*
5. **With *this morning / today / this week / this year* (when the period is not over):** *I **have drunk** three cups of tea today.*
6. **After *It is the first / second / best … time*:** *This is **the first time** I **have been** here.*

::: rule
**Never use the present perfect with a definite past-time expression** (*yesterday, last week, ago, in 2010, when I was young, just now, the other day*). Use the **past indefinite** instead.

- ✘ I have seen him yesterday. ✔ I **saw** him yesterday.
- ✘ He has come two days ago. ✔ He **came** two days ago.
- ✘ She has graduated in 2020. ✔ She **graduated** in 2020.
- ✘ He has gone just now. ✔ He **went** just now. / ✔ He **has just gone**.
:::

### 21.4.2 *Have gone* vs *Have been*

- *He **has gone** to Dhaka.* = He is in Dhaka now (or on the way); he has not come back.
- *He **has been** to Dhaka.* = He visited Dhaka at some time and has come back.

## 21.5 Present Perfect Continuous Tense

### 21.5.1 Uses

1. **An action that began in the past and is still continuing** (emphasis on duration), with *since* and *for*: *I **have been waiting** for you **for** two hours. It **has been raining since** morning.*
2. **An action that has just stopped, with present evidence:** *Why are your eyes red? — I **have been crying**. You look tired. **Have** you **been working**?*

::: ex
- ✘ I am waiting here since morning. ✔ I **have been waiting** here since morning.
- ✘ He is living in this house for ten years. ✔ He **has been living** in this house for ten years. (or *has lived*)
- ✘ She is learning English since 2020. ✔ She **has been learning** English since 2020.
:::

::: note
**Present Perfect vs Present Perfect Continuous:** The perfect emphasises the **result / completion** (*I have written three letters* — the number is known); the perfect continuous emphasises the **activity / duration** (*I have been writing letters all morning*). With stative verbs, use the perfect: *I **have known** him for years.* (✘ have been knowing)
:::

## 21.6 Past Indefinite (Simple Past) Tense

### 21.6.1 Uses

1. **A completed action at a definite past time** (*yesterday, last year, ago, in 1971, then, when …*): *I **met** him yesterday. Bangladesh **became** independent in 1971.*
2. **A series of past actions** (in a story): *He **opened** the door, **entered** the room and **sat** down.*
3. **Past habits** (also *used to / would*): *He **walked** five miles every day when he was young.*
4. **Past states:** *He **was** a teacher. She **lived** in Khulna.*
5. **Unreal situations (subjunctive) after *if, wish, as if, it's time, would rather*:** *If I **had** money, I would buy it. I wish I **knew** the answer. It's time we **left**.*

::: ex
- **When** did you come? (✘ When have you come?) — a *when*-question asks for a definite time → past simple.
- ✘ Did he went? ✔ Did he **go**? (after *did*, use V1)
- ✘ He did not came. ✔ He did not **come**.
:::

## 21.7 Past Continuous Tense

### 21.7.1 Uses

1. **An action in progress at a particular time in the past:** *At 9 p.m. yesterday I **was reading**.*
2. **A longer action interrupted by a shorter one** (*when / while*): *I **was reading** when the light **went** out. While she **was cooking**, the phone **rang**.*
3. **Two actions in progress at the same time:** *While I **was reading**, my brother **was watching** TV.*
4. **Background description in stories:** *The sun **was shining** and the birds **were singing**.*
5. **Annoying past habits with *always*:** *He **was always borrowing** money.*

::: tip
**Exam Tip — *When* + past simple, *While* + past continuous:** *I was sleeping **when** he **came**.* / ***While** I **was sleeping**, he came.* (Both actions as simple facts in sequence: *When he came, I **opened** the door.*)
:::

## 21.8 Past Perfect Tense

### 21.8.1 Uses

1. **The earlier of two past actions** — to show that one action was completed **before** another past action: *The train **had left** before we **reached** the station. When I arrived, he **had gone** home.*
2. **With *before, after, when, as soon as, until, by the time*:** *After he **had finished** his work, he went out. By the time the police arrived, the thief **had escaped**.*
3. **With *hardly / scarcely … when*, *no sooner … than*:** *Hardly **had** he **arrived** when it began to rain.* (Chapter 16)
4. **In reported speech** (past simple / present perfect → past perfect): *He said, "I saw him." → He said that he **had seen** him.*
5. **In unreal past conditions and wishes:** *If I **had known**, I would have helped. I wish I **had studied** harder.*

::: rule
**Sequence rule:** Of two past actions, use the **past perfect** for the **earlier** and the **past simple** for the **later**. ✘ *The patient died before the doctor had come.* ✔ *The patient **had died** before the doctor **came**.*

But if the time relation is already clear from *before/after*, the past simple for both is acceptable in modern English: *He left **before** I arrived.* In exam questions, apply the sequence rule.
:::

## 21.9 Past Perfect Continuous Tense

**An action that had been continuing for some time before another past action or time:**

::: ex
- He **had been waiting** for two hours when I arrived.
- It **had been raining** since morning, so the roads were wet.
- They **had been living** in Rajshahi for ten years before they moved to Dhaka.
- ✘ I was waiting for an hour when he came. ✔ I **had been waiting** for an hour when he came.
:::

## 21.10 Future Indefinite Tense and Other Ways of Expressing the Future

### 21.10.1 *Will / Shall* + V1

1. **Simple future predictions:** *It **will** rain tomorrow. He **will** be twenty next year.*
2. **Instant decisions:** *I'm thirsty. — I **will** get you some water.*
3. **Promises, offers, threats, requests:** *I **will** help you. **Shall** I carry your bag? **Will** you open the door?*
4. **Traditional *shall/will* rule** (Chapter 13.6.1): *I/we **shall*** for simple future; *I/we **will*** for determination; *you/he/they **will*** for simple future; *you/he/they **shall*** for command/promise.

### 21.10.2 Other Future Forms

| Form | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ***be going to*** + V1 | intention / plan already made; prediction from present evidence | *I **am going to** buy a car. Look at the clouds — it **is going to** rain.* |
| Present continuous | fixed personal arrangements | *I **am flying** to Sylhet on Friday.* |
| Present simple | timetables, schedules | *The flight **leaves** at 10.* |
| ***be about to*** + V1 | immediate future | *The train **is about to** leave.* (✘ is about to leaving) |
| ***be on the point of*** + V-ing | very immediate future | *He **was on the point of leaving** when I arrived.* |
| ***be to*** + V1 | official plans, orders | *The President **is to** visit Japan.* |
| ***be likely to / be bound to / be sure to*** | probability / certainty | *He **is sure to** pass.* |

## 21.11 Future Continuous Tense

1. **An action that will be in progress at a certain future time:** *This time tomorrow I **shall be travelling** to Cox's Bazar. At 8 p.m. she **will be watching** TV.*
2. **Planned future events, as a matter of course:** *I **will be seeing** him at the office anyway.*
3. **Polite enquiries about plans:** ***Will** you **be using** the car tonight?*

## 21.12 Future Perfect Tense

**An action that will be completed before a certain future time** (often with *by, by then, by the time, before*):

::: ex
- I **shall have finished** the work **by** Friday.
- **By** the end of this year, they **will have built** the bridge.
- **By the time** you arrive, the train **will have left**.
- He **will have reached** home **before** it gets dark.
:::

## 21.13 Future Perfect Continuous Tense

**An action that will have been continuing for a period of time up to a future point:**

::: ex
- **By** next June, I **shall have been teaching** here **for** ten years.
- **By** 6 p.m., she **will have been working** for twelve hours.
:::

## 21.14 Time Expressions and the Tenses They Take

| Expression | Usual tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| every day, usually, always, often, generally, sometimes, never, seldom | Present Indefinite | *He **often** visits us.* |
| now, at present, at the moment, Look!, Listen!, these days | Present Continuous | *Look! It **is snowing**.* |
| just, already, yet, ever, never, so far, up to now, recently, lately, since, for, this week/year (unfinished) | Present Perfect | *I **have** never **seen** it.* |
| since / for (with continuing activity) | Present Perfect Continuous | *It **has been raining** for hours.* |
| yesterday, last night/week/year, ago, in 2010, the other day, just now | Past Indefinite | *I **saw** him **last week**.* |
| while, when (+ interrupting action), at that time | Past Continuous | *I **was sleeping** when he called.* |
| before, after, by the time, already (in the past), hardly … when, no sooner … than | Past Perfect | *He **had left** before I came.* |
| tomorrow, next week, in future, soon, tonight | Future Indefinite | *He **will come** tomorrow.* |
| this time tomorrow, at this time next week | Future Continuous | *I **will be sleeping** at this time tomorrow.* |
| by tomorrow, by next year, by the time | Future Perfect | *I **will have finished** by noon.* |

## 21.15 Sequence of Tenses

**Sequence of tenses** is the principle by which the tense of the verb in a **subordinate clause** follows (agrees with) the tense of the verb in the **main clause**.

### 21.15.1 Rule 1 — Past Main Clause → Past Subordinate Clause

If the verb in the main clause is in the **past**, the verb in the subordinate (especially noun) clause is normally in a **past** form.

::: ex
- He **said** that he **was** ill. (✘ is ill — in exam style)
- I **knew** that she **would** come. (✘ will come)
- She **thought** that I **had told** a lie.
- He **worked** hard so that he **might** pass. (✘ may pass)
- I **asked** him what he **wanted**.
:::

### 21.15.2 Rule 2 — Present or Future Main Clause → Any Tense in the Subordinate Clause

If the main verb is in the present or future, the subordinate verb may be in **any tense** the sense requires.

::: ex
- He **says** that he **is** ill / **was** ill / **will be** ill / **has been** ill.
- I **will tell** you what he **did** yesterday.
:::

### 21.15.3 Exceptions to Rule 1

1. **Universal truths and habitual facts** stay in the present: *He said that the sun **rises** in the east. The teacher said that honesty **is** the best policy.*
2. **After *than* (comparison)**, any tense: *He **liked** you more than he **likes** me.*
3. **Clauses of place or reason** may take any tense the sense requires: *He **lived** where his father **lives** now.*
4. **Historical facts** are often kept in the past: *The teacher said that Bangladesh **became** independent in 1971.*
5. **With *must* and *ought to*** (no past form): *He said that we **must** go.*

### 21.15.4 Purpose Clauses: *May* / *Might*

The modal in a purpose clause follows the main verb: *He **works** hard so that he **may** succeed.* / *He **worked** hard so that he **might** succeed.*

## 21.16 Special Structures with Tense

| Structure | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ***It is time / It is high time*** + past simple | the action is already late | *It is high time we **started**.* (✘ start) |
| ***I wish*** + past simple (present wish) / past perfect (past wish) | unreal | *I wish I **were** rich. I wish I **had seen** it.* |
| ***As if / As though*** + past / past perfect | unreal comparison | *He talks as if he **knew** everything.* |
| ***Would rather*** + subject + past | preference about another person | *I'd rather you **went** now.* |
| ***Since*** + past simple (main: present perfect or *It is*) | point of time | *It is five years since he **died**.* (✘ has died) / *I have known him since I **was** a child.* |
| ***Before / until / when / as soon as / if*** + present (for future) | no *will* in time/condition clause | *I will wait until he **comes**.* |
| ***It is the first time*** + present perfect | | *It's the first time I **have flown**.* |
| ***It was the first time*** + past perfect | | *It was the first time I **had flown**.* |
| ***Hardly … when / No sooner … than*** | past perfect + past simple | *No sooner **had** he **left** than she **arrived**.* |
| ***Lest*** + should + V1 | | *… lest he **should** fall.* |

## 21.17 Common Errors in Tense

::: errs
1. ✘ I have seen him yesterday. ✔ I **saw** him yesterday.
2. ✘ He has died two years ago. ✔ He **died** two years ago.
3. ✘ I am living here since 2015. ✔ I **have been living** here since 2015.
4. ✘ She is knowing the answer. ✔ She **knows** the answer.
5. ✘ When will he come, I will tell him. ✔ When he **comes**, I will tell him.
6. ✘ If it will rain, we will not go. ✔ If it **rains**, we will not go.
7. ✘ The patient died before the doctor came. (exam) ✔ The patient **had died** before the doctor came.
8. ✘ He said that he is busy. ✔ He said that he **was** busy.
9. ✘ The teacher said that the earth moved round the sun. ✔ … the earth **moves** round the sun.
10. ✘ It is time we go home. ✔ It is time we **went** home.
11. ✘ It is two years since he has gone. ✔ It is two years since he **went**.
12. ✘ I am waiting for you for two hours. ✔ I **have been waiting** for you for two hours.
13. ✘ Did you went to school? ✔ Did you **go** to school?
14. ✘ He did not came yesterday. ✔ He did not **come** yesterday.
15. ✘ When have you come? ✔ When **did** you **come**?
16. ✘ He was reading when I was entering. ✔ He was reading when I **entered**.
17. ✘ By next year, I will complete my degree. ✔ By next year, I **will have completed** my degree.
18. ✘ I had seen him yesterday. ✔ I **saw** him yesterday.
19. ✘ He worked hard so that he may pass. ✔ … so that he **might** pass.
20. ✘ Hardly he had reached when the train left. ✔ Hardly **had he** reached when the train left.
21. ✘ I am going to market every day. ✔ I **go** to market every day.
22. ✘ He has been ill since three days. ✔ He has been ill **for** three days.
23. ✘ The train will leave before we will reach. ✔ The train will leave before we **reach**.
24. ✘ She is going to school yesterday. ✔ She **went** to school yesterday.
25. ✘ We are knowing each other for years. ✔ We **have known** each other for years.
26. ✘ He will have finished the work tomorrow. ✔ He will have finished the work **by** tomorrow.
27. ✘ I wish I am a bird. ✔ I wish I **were** a bird.
28. ✘ He is about to leaving. ✔ He is about to **leave**.
29. ✘ This is the first time I see the sea. ✔ This is the first time I **have seen** the sea.
30. ✘ The sun rose in the east. (general truth) ✔ The sun **rises** in the east.
:::

## 21.18 Tense in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
- **Logical sequence (GMAT/SAT):** Tenses within a sentence must reflect the real order of events. ✘ *By the time the rescue team arrived, the fire **destroyed** the building.* ✔ *… the fire **had destroyed** the building.*
- **Unnecessary tense shifts (SAT):** In a passage written in the past, a sudden switch to the present (or vice versa) without reason is wrong.
- **Present perfect with *since*:** ✘ *Since 2010, the company **increased** its exports.* ✔ *… **has increased** …*
- **Past perfect only when needed:** GMAT does not reward the past perfect if there is no earlier past action to contrast: ✘ *The company **had announced** its results yesterday.* ✔ *… **announced** …*
- **Conditionals:** *If* + present → *will*; *if* + past → *would*; *if* + past perfect → *would have* (Chapter 25). ✘ *If he had studied, he would pass.* ✔ *… would have passed.*
- **GRE Reading:** The tense of an author's statement (*scientists believed* vs *scientists believe*) often signals a **contrast between an old view and a new one** — a common structure of GRE passages.
:::
