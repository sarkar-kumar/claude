## 16.1 What Is Inversion?

The normal word order of an English statement is **Subject + Verb** (*He came*). **Inversion** means **reversing** this order so that the **verb (or an auxiliary) comes before the subject**. It happens in questions, after certain negative or restrictive words placed at the beginning, in conditional sentences without *if*, after adverbs of place, and in some literary or emphatic structures.

There are two kinds of inversion:

1. **Subject–auxiliary inversion** — only the **auxiliary** (*do, does, did, have, has, had, be, can, will* …) moves before the subject; the main verb stays after it:
   - *He **has** never seen a tiger.* → ***Never has** he seen a tiger.*
   - *He **came** late.* → ***Seldom did** he come late.* (if there is no auxiliary, add *do/does/did*)
2. **Full (subject–verb) inversion** — the **whole verb** comes before the subject (mostly with intransitive verbs of place and movement):
   - *A bus **comes** here.* → *Here **comes** a bus.*
   - *An old temple **stands** on the hill.* → *On the hill **stands** an old temple.*

Inversion is tested in BCS, Bank and admission tests through **error detection** (*Never I have seen* ✘), **fill in the gaps** (*No sooner had he … than*) and **sentence transformation**. GRE and SAT reading passages use it constantly for emphasis, and GMAT tests it with *not only … but also* and *nor*.

## 16.2 Inversion in Questions

The basic case of subject–auxiliary inversion:

::: ex
- **Are** you ready? / **Is** she a doctor?
- **Have** you finished? / **Can** he swim?
- **Do** you know him? / **Did** she call? (*do/does/did* added)
- Where **does** he live? / Why **were** you late?
- **Who** broke the glass? → no inversion when the question word is the **subject**
- **What** happened? → no inversion (*what* is the subject) — ✘ *What did happen?* (only for emphasis)
:::

::: note
**No inversion in indirect (embedded) questions:** ✘ *I don't know where does he live.* ✔ *I don't know where **he lives**.* ✘ *Tell me what is your name.* ✔ *Tell me what **your name is**.* (Chapter 37)
:::

## 16.3 Inversion after Negative and Restrictive Expressions

When a **negative** or **restrictive** adverb or phrase is placed at the **beginning** of a sentence for emphasis, the subject and auxiliary are **inverted**. This is the most frequently tested type of inversion.

### 16.3.1 The Words and Phrases

| Group | Expressions |
|---|---|
| Negative adverbs | never, seldom, rarely, hardly, scarcely, barely, little, nowhere, neither, nor |
| Time sequence | hardly … when, scarcely … when, barely … when, no sooner … than |
| Negative phrases | at no time, in no way, on no account, under no circumstances, in no case, by no means, not once, not a single (word / person), not for a moment, not until / not till, not only … but also |
| *Only* phrases | only then, only when, only after, only by, only if, only in this way, only once, only later |
| Others | so + adjective/adverb … that; such … that; little did … |

### 16.3.2 Examples

::: ex
- **Never have I** seen such a beautiful place. (✘ Never I have seen)
- **Seldom does he** come to the office late.
- **Rarely do we** find such honest people today.
- **Little did I** know that he was a thief. (*little* = not at all)
- **Nowhere else will you** find such hospitality.
- **At no time did she** complain.
- **Under no circumstances should you** open this door.
- **On no account must you** tell him the secret.
- **In no way was he** responsible for the accident.
- **By no means is this** the end of the matter.
- **Not once did he** look back.
- **Not a single word did he** utter.
- **Not until** he came back **did I** realise the truth. (inversion in the **main** clause)
- **Only then did I** understand the problem.
- **Only after** the meeting **did he** tell us the news.
- **Only by** working hard **can you** succeed.
- **Only when** the rain stopped **did we** go out.
:::

::: tip
**Exam Tip — Where does the inversion go?** With ***not until***, ***only after***, ***only when***, ***only if***, ***only by*** the inversion comes in the **main clause**, not in the clause or phrase that follows these words: *Only **when the rain stopped** (no inversion) **did we go** (inversion) out.*
:::

### 16.3.3 *Hardly / Scarcely / Barely … when* and *No sooner … than*

These correlatives express that **one past action followed another immediately**. They are usually used with the **past perfect** in the first clause and the **past simple** in the second.

::: rule
***Hardly / Scarcely / Barely*** + *had* + subject + V3 + ***when / before*** + past simple. 
***No sooner*** + *had* + subject + V3 + ***than*** + past simple.
:::

::: ex
- **Hardly had** he reached the station **when** the train left. (✘ than)
- **Scarcely had** we sat down **when** the lights went out.
- **Barely had** she finished **when** the bell rang.
- **No sooner had** he arrived **than** it began to rain. (✘ when / then)
- **No sooner did** the teacher enter **than** the boys stood up. (past simple form, also correct)
- Without inversion (less formal): *He had **hardly** reached the station **when** the train left. He had **no sooner** arrived **than** it began to rain.*
:::

::: note
**Pairing rule:** *No sooner* is a comparative (*sooner*), so it pairs with ***than***. *Hardly, scarcely, barely* are not comparatives, so they pair with ***when*** (or *before*). ✘ *Hardly had he left than …* ✘ *No sooner had he left when …* — these are the classic exam errors.
:::

### 16.3.4 *Not only … but also*

When ***not only*** begins a sentence (and joins clauses), the **first** clause is inverted:

::: ex
- **Not only did he** steal the money, **but he also** beat the guard.
- **Not only is she** beautiful, **but she is also** intelligent.
- Without inversion: *He **not only** stole the money **but also** beat the guard.* (parallel verbs — Chapter 39)
:::

### 16.3.5 *Neither*, *Nor* and *So* (Short Answers and Additions)

To say that the same thing is true of another person, use ***so*** (positive) and ***neither / nor*** (negative) + **auxiliary** + **subject**:

::: ex
- I like tea. — **So do I**. (✘ So I do — that means "Yes, it's true, I do")
- She can swim. — **So can her brother**.
- He has finished. — **So have we**.
- I don't smoke. — **Neither / Nor do I**. (✘ Neither I do; ✘ I don't too)
- He wasn't there. — **Neither was she**.
- He did not come, **nor did he** send a message. (✘ nor he sent)
- I have never seen it, **and neither has she**.
:::

::: note
***So I do* vs *So do I*:** *So do I* = *I do too* (agreement). *So I do* = *Yes, indeed I do* (confirming a fact that someone has noticed): *"You look tired." — "**So I am**."*
:::

### 16.3.6 *So … that* and *Such … that* (Emphatic Fronting)

::: ex
- **So beautiful was** the scene **that** we could not take our eyes off it. (= The scene was so beautiful that …)
- **So fast did** he run **that** nobody could catch him.
- **Such was** his anger **that** he could not speak. (= His anger was such that …)
- **Such is** life!
:::

## 16.4 Inversion in Conditional Sentences (Omitting *If*)

In formal English, ***if*** can be dropped from a conditional clause beginning with ***had, were*** or ***should***, and the auxiliary is placed before the subject (Chapter 25).

::: ex
- If I had known → **Had I known**, I would have helped you.
- If I were you → **Were I you**, I would not go.
- If he were to come → **Were he to come**, we would welcome him.
- If you should need help → **Should you need** help, call me.
- If it had not been for your help → **Had it not been for** your help, I would have failed. (✘ Hadn't it been for)
:::

::: note
**Negative inverted conditionals** do not use the contracted form: ✘ *Hadn't I known* / ✘ *Weren't it for* → ✔ ***Had I not known*** / ✔ ***Were it not for***.
:::

## 16.5 Full Inversion after Adverbs of Place and Direction

With **intransitive verbs of movement or position** (*come, go, stand, lie, sit, run, fly, live, rise, fall*), a sentence can begin with an **adverb of place or direction**, and then the **whole verb** comes before a **noun** subject:

::: ex
- **Here comes** the bus. (✘ Here the bus comes — less natural)
- **There goes** the last train.
- **Down came** the rain.
- **Away flew** the birds.
- **In came** the headmaster.
- **Out rushed** the children.
- **Up went** the prices.
- **Off went** the soldiers.
:::

::: rule
**No inversion when the subject is a pronoun:** *Here **it** comes.* (✘ Here comes it) *There **she** goes.* *Away **they** flew.* *Down **it** came.*
:::

### 16.5.1 After Prepositional Phrases of Place

::: ex
- **On the hill stands** an old temple.
- **In the middle of the room was** a large table.
- **Among the guests were** two ministers.
- **Under the tree sat** an old beggar.
- **Beside the river lies** a small village.
- **At the top of the list is** Rahim's name.
:::

Remember that the verb agrees with the **subject that follows**: *On the hill **stand two temples**.* (Chapter 23)

## 16.6 Inversion with Participles, Adjectives and Complements

For emphasis or in literary style, a **participle**, an **adjective** or a **complement** can be placed first, followed by the verb and then the subject:

::: ex
- **Gone are** the days when a family could live on a small income.
- **Blessed are** the meek, for they shall inherit the earth.
- **Present at the meeting were** the Mayor and the Commissioner.
- **Attached is** a copy of my CV. / **Enclosed are** the documents.
- **Great was** our joy when we heard the news.
- **Lucky is** the man who has a good wife.
- **Standing at the gate was** a tall stranger.
:::

## 16.7 Inversion in Wishes, Exclamations and Literary Language

::: ex
- **May you live** long! **May Allah bless** you! (wish/prayer)
- **Long live** Bangladesh! (subjunctive)
- **How sweet is** the song of the cuckoo! (literary; normal: *How sweet the song of the cuckoo is!*)
- **Come** what may, I will stand by you. (= Whatever happens)
- **Be** that as it may, … (= Even if that is true)
- **Try as he might**, he could not open the door. (concessive)
- **Much as I** like him, I cannot support him. (= Although I like him very much)
- **Strange as it may seem**, he refused the offer.
:::

::: note
***Adjective/adverb + as + subject + verb*** (*Strange as it may seem; Tired as he was; Much as I admire him*) expresses **concession** (= *although*). There is no inversion of subject and verb here; the adjective is fronted. ✘ *As strange as it may seem* is common informally but the exam form is ***Strange as it may seem***.
:::

## 16.8 Inversion after *As* and *Than* (Formal Comparison)

In formal writing, after ***as*** and ***than***, the auxiliary may come before a **noun** subject:

::: ex
- She is very hard-working, **as are** her sisters.
- Rahim scored higher than **did** Karim.
- Bangladesh exports more garments than **does** Vietnam (in some years).
:::

## 16.9 Inversion in Reporting Direct Speech

In stories, the **reporting verb** may come before a **noun** subject after a quotation:

::: ex
- "I am tired," **said the boy**. (also: *the boy said*)
- "Come in," **replied the teacher**.
- With pronouns, normal order is preferred: *"I am tired," **he said**.* (*said he* is old-fashioned)
:::

## 16.10 Summary Table of Inversion Patterns

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Negative adverb + aux + S + V | *Never **have I** seen it.* |
| *Hardly/Scarcely* + *had* + S + V3 + *when* | *Hardly **had he** left **when** she came.* |
| *No sooner* + *had* + S + V3 + *than* | *No sooner **had he** left **than** she came.* |
| *Only* + phrase/clause + aux + S + V | *Only then **did he** agree.* |
| *Not until* + clause, aux + S + V | *Not until he left **did I** relax.* |
| *Not only* + aux + S + V, *but* S *also* V | *Not only **did he** sing, but he also danced.* |
| *So / Neither / Nor* + aux + S | *So **do I**. Nor **can he**.* |
| *So* + adj + V + S + *that* | *So great **was the noise** that …* |
| *Had / Were / Should* + S (conditional) | ***Had I** known, …* |
| Adverb of place + V + noun S | *Here **comes the bus**.* |
| Prepositional phrase of place + V + S | *On the hill **stands a temple**.* |
| Participle/adjective + *be* + S | *Gone **are the days** …* |
| *May* + S + V (wish) | ***May you** succeed!* |

## 16.11 Common Errors with Inversion

::: errs
1. ✘ Never I have seen such a sight. ✔ Never **have I** seen such a sight.
2. ✘ Seldom he comes here. ✔ Seldom **does he** come here.
3. ✘ Hardly he had reached when it rained. ✔ Hardly **had he** reached **when** it rained.
4. ✘ Hardly had he gone than she came. ✔ Hardly had he gone **when** she came.
5. ✘ No sooner had he come when the bell rang. ✔ No sooner had he come **than** the bell rang.
6. ✘ No sooner he had come than the bell rang. ✔ No sooner **had he** come than the bell rang.
7. ✘ Not only he is clever but also honest. ✔ **Not only is he** clever, **but he is also** honest. / He is **not only** clever **but also** honest.
8. ✘ Little he knew about the danger. ✔ Little **did he** know about the danger.
9. ✘ Only then I understood. ✔ Only then **did I** understand.
10. ✘ Not until he left I relaxed. ✔ Not until he left **did I** relax.
11. ✘ I don't like tea. — Neither I do. ✔ **Neither do I.**
12. ✘ He can't swim and I can't too. ✔ He can't swim, and **neither can I**.
13. ✘ He did not come, nor he sent a letter. ✔ … **nor did he** send a letter.
14. ✘ Here comes he. ✔ Here **he comes**.
15. ✘ Hadn't he helped me, I would have failed. ✔ **Had he not** helped me, I would have failed.
16. ✘ Were I knew the truth, I would tell you. ✔ **Had I known** / **If I knew** the truth, …
17. ✘ Under no circumstances you should go there. ✔ Under no circumstances **should you** go there.
18. ✘ So loud the music was that we couldn't sleep. ✔ So loud **was the music** that we couldn't sleep.
19. ✘ On the table were lying a book. ✔ On the table **was lying a book**.
20. ✘ I don't know where does he live. ✔ I don't know where **he lives**. (no inversion in embedded questions)
:::

## 16.12 Inversion in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
- **Agreement in inverted sentences (SAT/GMAT):** find the subject **after** the verb: ✘ *Among the most important factors **is** cost and quality.* ✔ *Among the most important factors **are** cost and quality.*
- **GMAT *nor*:** ✘ *The policy did not reduce costs, nor it improved quality.* ✔ *… **nor did it** improve quality.*
- **GMAT *not only … but also*** must be **parallel**: ✘ *not only in Asia but also Africa* ✔ *not only **in Asia** but also **in Africa***.
- **GRE/SAT Reading:** inverted sentences (*Only recently have scientists discovered …; Rarely has a theory provoked such debate …*) signal **emphasis** and often contain the author's main point; read them carefully when answering "primary purpose" questions.
:::
