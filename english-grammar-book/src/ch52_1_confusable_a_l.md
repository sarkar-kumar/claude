## 52.1 Why Words Get Confused

Many English words are confused because they:

- **sound the same** (homophones): *principal / principle, stationary / stationery*;
- **look similar** (paronyms): *affect / effect, eminent / imminent, adapt / adopt*;
- **have related meanings** but different uses: *economic / economical, historic / historical, continual / continuous*;
- are **false friends** of Bangla expressions: *cousin brother, lend / borrow*.

"**Fill in the blank with the correct word**" (*The ___ of the college is strict — principal / principle*) and "**Distinguish between the pairs**" are common in BCS Written, Bank written tests and admission tests; MCQs use these pairs as distractors. The **SAT** tests them as "word choice/usage" errors, and the **GRE** loves near-synonyms with different shades.

**Key to the list:** each set gives the words, their **part of speech**, the **Bangla meaning**, the **English meaning** and **examples**.

**Alternate / Alternative** — *alternate* (adj) [একান্তর]{.bn} = every second; (v) take turns; *alternative* (n/adj) [বিকল্প]{.bn} = another choice. *Ex:* He visits us on **alternate** days. / We have no **alternative**.
