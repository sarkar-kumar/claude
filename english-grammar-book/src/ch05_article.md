## 5.1 What Is an Article?

***A***, ***an*** and ***the*** are called **articles**. They are the most commonly used words in English, and they are really a kind of **determiner** (traditional grammar calls them *demonstrative adjectives*). They come before nouns and tell us whether the noun is **general** or **particular**.

- ***A / An*** — the **indefinite article**: it points to **any one** (not a particular one) person or thing. *Give me **a** pen* (any pen).
- ***The*** — the **definite article**: it points to a **particular** person or thing that both speaker and listener know. *Give me **the** pen* (the one we both know about).

Bangla has no articles in the English sense (we use *ṭa, ṭi, khana* or nothing), so Bangladeshi learners often omit articles or use them where English does not. Article questions appear in almost every BCS, Bank and admission test as fill-in-the-gaps and error-detection items.

## 5.2 *A* or *An*? — The Sound Rule

::: rule
The choice between *a* and *an* depends on the **sound** of the next word, **not its spelling**. Use ***an*** before a word that begins with a **vowel sound** (*a, e, i, o, u* sounds), and ***a*** before a word that begins with a **consonant sound** — including the "y" sound (/j/) and the "w" sound (/w/).
:::

### 5.2.1 Vowel Letters with Consonant Sounds → *A*

| Word begins with … | Sound | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| *u* pronounced "you" | /j/ | **a** university, **a** union, **a** uniform, **a** unique, **a** unit, **a** useful book, **a** usual, **a** utensil, **a** U-turn, **a** UN official, **a** US citizen |
| *eu* pronounced "you" | /j/ | **a** European, **a** eulogy, **a** euphemism, **a** eunuch |
| *ewe* | /j/ | **a** ewe (female sheep) |
| *o* pronounced "wa" | /w/ | **a** one-eyed man, **a** one-taka coin, **a** one-way road, **a** once-famous actor |

### 5.2.2 Consonant Letters with Vowel Sounds → *An*

| Word begins with … | Sound | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Silent *h* | vowel | **an** hour, **an** honest man, **an** honour, **an** honourable person, **an** heir, **an** heiress, **an** honorarium, **an** hourly bus |
| Letters *F, H, L, M, N, R, S, X* said as letters | "ef, aitch, el, em, en, ar, es, ex" | **an** M.A., **an** MBBS doctor, **an** MP, **an** SSC candidate, **an** HSC examinee, **an** LLB, **an** FIR, **an** X-ray, **an** NGO, **an** S.I., **an** L-shaped room, **an** honours student, **an** RAB officer (if spelled out as R-A-B) |

### 5.2.3 Consonant Letters with Consonant Sounds → *A*

| Example | Reason |
|---|---|
| **a** house, **a** horse, **a** hotel, **a** hospital, **a** hero, **a** historic event | *h* is pronounced (*an hotel / an historic* are old-fashioned British forms) |
| **a** B.A., **a** B.Sc., **a** BCS cadre, **a** CNG, **a** DC, **a** VIP, **a** JSC examinee | the letter names begin with consonant sounds ("bee, see, dee, vee, jay") |
| **a** NATO base, **a** UNESCO report | acronyms read as words begin with consonant sounds ("nay-toe", "you-nes-co") |

::: ex
- **An** honest man is respected everywhere.
- He is **an** M.A. in English and **a** B.A. in history.
- She is **a** university teacher.
- I waited for **an** hour.
- He is **a** one-eyed man.
- **An** NGO worker came to the village.
- It is **a** useful book. / It is **an** unusual book. / It is **an** umbrella.
- **A** European came to visit **an** Asian country.
:::

::: tip
**Exam Tip:** Say the word aloud. If your mouth starts with a vowel sound, use *an*; otherwise, *a*. The most tested items are: ***an hour, an honest, an heir, an honour, an M.A., an MP, an SSC, an X-ray, an FIR*** and ***a university, a European, a one-eyed, a union, a useful, a uniform, a U-turn***.
:::

## 5.3 Uses of the Indefinite Article (*A / An*)

### 5.3.1 Main Rules

1. **Before a singular countable noun mentioned for the first time** or not identified: *I saw **a** tiger in the zoo. **The** tiger was sleeping.* (first mention → *a*; second mention → *the*)
2. **In the sense of "one":** ***a** dozen eggs, **a** hundred taka, **a** thousand people, **a** day or two, **a** week*. ✘ *Wait one minute* is acceptable; ✔ *Wait **a** minute* is more natural.
3. **To show profession, nationality, religion or class (as a complement):** *He is **a** doctor. She is **an** engineer. He is **a** Bangladeshi. He is **a** Muslim.* (✘ He is doctor.)
4. **In the sense of "per / each":** *Taka 60 **a** kilo; twice **a** day; 80 kilometres **an** hour; three times **a** week.*
5. **To represent a whole class (any one example):** ***A** cow is a useful animal* (= any cow). ***A** child needs love.*
6. **In exclamations with *what* and *such* + singular countable:** ***What a** lovely garden! He is **such a** fool!*
7. **Before a proper noun to mean "a certain" or "one like":** ***A** Mr. Karim came to see you* (= a man called Mr. Karim, unknown to me). *He is **a** second Hatem Tai* (= a generous man like Hatem Tai).
8. **With *many*, *quite*, *rather*, *half*:** ***Many a** man **has** done it* (singular noun and verb). *It was **quite a** good film. It was **rather a** long day. **Half an** hour.*
9. **With illnesses that are countable:** ***a** cold, **a** headache, **a** fever, **a** toothache, **a** stomachache, **a** heart attack, **a** sore throat*. (✘ I have headache.) Uncountable illnesses: *diabetes, cancer, malaria, measles* (no *a*).
10. **With an uncountable or abstract noun qualified by an adjective** (Chapter 03.6): ***a** good knowledge of, **a** sound education, **a** happy life, **a** warm welcome*.

### 5.3.2 Fixed Expressions with *A / An*

Learn these phrases; examiners often drop the article and ask you to supply it.

| Phrase | Phrase | Phrase |
|---|---|---|
| a lot of | a great deal of | a great many |
| a few / a little | a couple of | a number of |
| in a hurry | at a loss | as a rule |
| in a nutshell | at a stretch | at a glance |
| all of a sudden | at a time | in a body |
| make a noise | make a mistake | make a fortune |
| take a seat | take a walk | take a bath |
| have a look | have a mind to | have a good time |
| catch a cold | tell a lie | keep a secret |
| a short while ago | a long time | It is a pity / a shame |
| come to an end | put an end to | a matter of time |
| to a great extent | on an average | in a way |

::: note
Some words in this list cannot be used without *a*: ✘ *in hurry*, ✘ *at loss*, ✘ *as rule*, ✘ *make noise* (acceptable informally, but *make **a** noise* is standard in exams), ✘ *catch cold* (BrE also accepts *catch cold*). And *on an average* is common in South Asia; standard British/American English says ***on average***.
:::

## 5.4 When *A / An* Is NOT Used

::: ex
1. **Before plural nouns:** ✘ *a books* → ✔ *books* / *some books*.
2. **Before uncountable nouns:** ✘ *an advice, a furniture, a good news* (Chapter 03).
3. **Before names of meals** (in general): *We have **breakfast** at 8. Lunch is ready.* But with an adjective: *We had **a** heavy **lunch**.*
4. **After *kind of / sort of / type of*** (formal): *What kind of **man** is he?* (*What kind of **a** man* is informal.)
5. **Before titles and unique positions** after *elect, appoint, make, choose, become, crown, name*: *They elected him **chairman**. He was appointed **headmaster**. She became **Prime Minister**.*
6. **Before *Mr, Mrs, Dr* + known name:** ✘ *a Dr Rahman* (unless meaning "a certain").
7. **In some fixed phrases:** *at first sight, by mistake, in fact, on foot, on purpose, at night, by heart*.
:::

## 5.5 Uses of the Definite Article (*The*)

### 5.5.1 General Rules

1. **Particular or already mentioned:** *This is **the** book I told you about. I met a man. **The** man was blind.*
2. **When both speaker and listener know which one is meant (shared knowledge):** *Close **the** door. Where is **the** teacher? Did you feed **the** dog?*
3. **Before a noun made particular by a phrase or clause:** ***The** water of this well is pure. **The** history of Bangladesh. **The** boy who came yesterday is my cousin.*
4. **Before unique things (only one of its kind):** ***the** sun, **the** moon, **the** earth, **the** sky, **the** world, **the** universe, **the** equator, **the** North Pole, **the** horizon, **the** atmosphere*.
5. **With superlatives:** ***the** best, **the** tallest, **the** most beautiful*.
6. **With ordinal numbers and *only, same, very, main, last, next, whole, following*:** ***the** first, **the** second, **the** only child, **the** same mistake, **the** very man, **the** main road, **the** whole day, **the** following year*.
7. **With an adjective to denote a class of people (plural verb):** ***The** rich **are** not always happy. **The** poor **need** help. **The** blind, **the** deaf, **the** dead, **the** young, **the** old, **the** unemployed, **the** wounded.*
8. **With a singular noun to represent a whole class (formal, generic use):** ***The** cow is a domestic animal. **The** tiger is in danger of extinction. **The** rose is the sweetest of all flowers. **The** computer has changed our lives.*
9. **With nationalities ending in *-sh, -ch, -ese, -ss*:** ***the** English, **the** French, **the** Chinese, **the** Japanese, **the** Swiss*. (Others take plural -s: ***the** Bangladeshis, **the** Americans*.)
10. **With family names in the plural (whole family):** ***the** Khans, **the** Chowdhurys, **the** Tagores*.
11. **With comparatives in parallel structure:** ***The** more you read, **the** more you learn. **The** sooner, **the** better. **The** higher you go, **the** cooler it is.*
12. **With comparative + *of the two*:** *Rahim is **the** taller **of the two** brothers.*
13. **Before a proper noun qualified by an adjective or used as a common noun:** ***the** great Akbar, **the** immortal Nazrul, **the** Newton of our age, **the** Shakespeare of Bengal*. And as an epithet after a name: *Alexander **the** Great, Akbar **the** Great*.
14. **With body parts in a prepositional phrase after a verb + object:** *He struck me **on the** head. She pulled him **by the** ear.*
15. **For a measurement unit with *by*:** *Eggs are sold **by the** dozen. Cloth is sold **by the** metre. Workers are paid **by the** hour.*
16. **With decades and centuries:** *in **the** 1970s, in **the** nineteenth century*.
17. **With inventions and musical instruments:** ***The** telephone was invented by Bell. She plays **the** violin / **the** harmonium / **the** tabla.* (but games: *He plays **football*** — no article)
18. **With *the* + adjective in abstract sense (singular):** ***The** good, **the** true, **the** beautiful*.
19. **With certain institutions and media:** ***the** government, **the** police, **the** army, **the** public, **the** press, **the** cinema, **the** theatre, **the** radio*. (but ***television*** usually without *the*: *watch television*)

### 5.5.2 *The* with Geographical and Other Proper Names

This is a high-frequency exam area. Learn the table with its exceptions.

| Use ***the*** | Examples | Do NOT use *the* | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rivers | **the** Padma, **the** Meghna, **the** Jamuna, **the** Nile, **the** Thames | Single lakes | Lake Kaptai, Lake Victoria |
| Seas, oceans, gulfs, bays | **the** Bay of Bengal, **the** Red Sea, **the** Pacific, **the** Persian Gulf | Single mountains / peaks | Mount Everest, Keokradong, Mount Fuji |
| Canals | **the** Suez Canal, **the** Panama Canal | Single islands | Bhola, Sri Lanka, Sicily |
| Deserts | **the** Sahara, **the** Thar, **the** Gobi | Continents | Asia, Africa, Europe |
| Mountain ranges | **the** Himalayas, **the** Alps, **the** Andes | Most countries | Bangladesh, India, France, China |
| Groups of islands | **the** Maldives, **the** Andamans, **the** Philippines | Cities, towns, villages | Dhaka, Paris, London |
| Countries with *republic, kingdom, states, union* or plural names | **the** USA, **the** UK, **the** UAE, **the** Netherlands, **the** People's Republic of China | States, provinces, districts | Texas, Punjab, Sylhet |
| Holy books | **the** Quran, **the** Bible, **the** Gita, **the** Vedas, **the** Tripitaka | Streets, roads, squares (mostly) | Mirpur Road, Times Square |
| Famous buildings, monuments | **the** Taj Mahal, **the** Shaheed Minar, **the** Eiffel Tower, **the** Pyramids | Planets (as names) | Mars, Jupiter (but **the** earth/**the** Earth) |
| Newspapers & magazines | **The** Daily Star, **The** Times, **the** Prothom Alo | Universities named after places/people | Dhaka University, Harvard University (but **the** University of Dhaka) |
| Ships, trains, planes | **the** Titanic, **the** Subarna Express | Airports, stations, parks | Hazrat Shahjalal International Airport, Kamalapur Station, Ramna Park |
| Hotels, museums, cinemas, theatres | **the** Sonargaon Hotel, **the** National Museum, **the** Louvre | Acronyms read as words | NATO, UNESCO, UNICEF, OPEC |
| Organisations (initials read letter by letter) | **the** UN, **the** WHO, **the** EU, **the** BBC, **the** IMF | Magazines named after a person | — |
| Regions and directions | **the** Middle East, **the** Far East, **the** north of Bangladesh | Compass points after *in* + proper adjective | in northern Bangladesh, in South Asia |

## 5.6 When *The* Is NOT Used

1. **Before most proper nouns** (persons, countries, cities, continents): ✘ *the Dhaka*, ✘ *the Rahim*, ✘ *the Asia*.
2. **Before material, abstract and uncountable nouns used in a general sense:** ***Gold** is costly. **Honesty** is the best policy. **Rice** is our staple food. **Life** is short.* But particularised: ***The** gold of this ring; **the** honesty of this man; **the** life of Nazrul.*
3. **Before plural nouns used in a general sense:** ***Books** are our best friends. **Dogs** are faithful.* But: ***The** books on the table are mine.*
4. **Before languages:** *She speaks **English** and **Bangla**.* But: ***the** English language*, ***the** Bangla language*.
5. **Before names of meals in general:** *We have **dinner** at nine.* But: ***The** dinner given by the Mayor was grand.*
6. **Before games and sports:** *They play **cricket**.* (✘ the cricket)
7. **Before most diseases:** *He suffers from **diabetes / cancer / malaria***. (Optional with a few: *(the) flu, (the) measles*.)
8. **Before days, months and seasons in general:** *on **Friday**, in **March**, in **winter*** (*in **the** winter of 1971* — particular).
9. **Before *school, college, university, hospital, church, mosque, temple, prison, jail, court, bed, market, sea, work, class* when used for their primary purpose:**

::: ex
- He goes to **school** every day. (as a student) / His father went to **the school** to meet the headmaster. (as a visitor)
- She is in **hospital**. (as a patient — BrE) / I went to **the hospital** to see her.
- He was sent to **prison**. (as a prisoner) / The lawyer went to **the prison**.
- We go to **bed** at ten. / He sat on **the bed**.
- He went to **sea** (became a sailor). / We went to **the sea** for a holiday.
:::

10. **With *by* + means of transport and communication:** *by **bus**, by **train**, by **air**, by **sea**, by **post**, by **phone**, by **email*** (but *in **the** bus*, *on **the** train*, *in **my** car*).
11. **Before *man* (mankind) and *woman* in a general sense:** ***Man** is mortal. **Woman** is the better half of man.*
12. **Before *home*** in common phrases: *go **home**, at **home**, leave **home***.
13. **Before *next* and *last* in time expressions measured from now:** ***next** week, **last** year, **next** Friday*. But measured from another time: *He arrived on Monday and left **the next** day.*
14. **Before titles used as complements or in apposition:** *He was elected **Chairman**. Dr Kamal, **Principal** of the college, gave a speech.*
15. **In pairs of nouns joined by a preposition or *and*:** *husband and wife, father and son, day and night, from head to foot, face to face, hand in hand, arm in arm, side by side, from door to door, from morning till evening*.
16. **Before *nature, society, space, life, history, television*** in general sense: *the beauty of **nature**; **Society** has changed; travel into **space**; I watched **television***.
17. **Before *most* meaning "the majority":** ***Most** students passed* (✘ The most students …). But superlative: *She is **the most** intelligent.*
18. **In many fixed phrases:** *at night, at noon, at dawn, at sunset, by day, in time, on time, at present, at last, in fact, on foot, by heart, by chance, in debt, at sea, catch fire, lose heart, take place, set sail, give way, send word, take part, keep pace, in front of, on behalf of, in case of*.

## 5.7 Repeating the Article

The repetition (or not) of an article can change the **meaning** and therefore the **verb**.

::: ex
- **A** black **and white** cow is grazing. (one cow, black-and-white)
- **A** black **and a** white cow **are** grazing. (two cows)
- **The** Secretary **and** Treasurer **has** come. (one person holding two posts)
- **The** Secretary **and the** Treasurer **have** come. (two persons)
- **The** poet **and** philosopher **is** dead. (one person — e.g. Iqbal)
- **The** poet **and the** philosopher **are** dead. (two persons)
- I have **a** red **and a** blue pen. (two pens) / I have **a** red **and** blue pen. (one pen with two colours)
:::

## 5.8 The Position of the Article

Usually the article comes before the adjective (*a good boy*). But after certain words the article moves **after the adjective**:

| Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|
| ***such / what / many / half / quite / rather*** + **a** + (adj) + noun | *such **a** fool; what **a** pity; many **a** man; half **an** hour; quite **a** surprise; rather **a** cold day* (also *a rather cold day*) |
| ***so / too / as / how / however*** + **adj** + **a** + noun | *so good **a** man (= such a good man); too heavy **a** load; as brave **a** soldier as any; how clever **a** boy; however hard **a** task* |
| ***all / both / double / twice*** + **the** + noun | *all **the** boys; both **the** brothers; double **the** price; twice **the** amount* |

::: ex
- It is **too difficult a** problem for me. (✘ a too difficult problem)
- He is **as honest a** man **as** his father.
- I have never seen **so beautiful a** place.
- **Many a** student **has** failed in this subject. (*many a* + singular noun + singular verb)
:::

## 5.9 Articles with *Few* and *Little*

*Few/little* (= almost none, negative), *a few/a little* (= some, positive), *the few/the little* (= all of the small number/amount) — explained fully in Chapter 03.9.1.

::: ex
- **Few** people can keep a secret. (negative)
- **A few** people came to the party. (positive)
- **The few** books that he has are all old. (all of his books)
- He has **little** knowledge of science. / He has **a little** knowledge of science.
:::

## 5.10 Articles with Proper Nouns: Special Cases

::: ex
- **The** Rahman you met yesterday is my uncle. (a particular Rahman, defined by a clause)
- I know three **Rahims** in my class. (proper noun used as common noun, plural)
- She is **a** Florence Nightingale. (= a devoted nurse)
- **The** Sundarbans is the largest mangrove forest in the world. (plural-form name)
- **The** Hague, **the** Vatican, **the** Gambia (fixed exceptions among names of places)
- He visited **Dhaka** and **the** Bay of Bengal.
:::

## 5.11 Common Errors with Articles

::: errs
1. ✘ He is honest man. ✔ He is **an** honest man.
2. ✘ She is an university student. ✔ She is **a** university student.
3. ✘ I waited for a hour. ✔ I waited for **an** hour.
4. ✘ He is a M.A. ✔ He is **an** M.A.
5. ✘ The honesty is the best policy. ✔ **Honesty** is the best policy.
6. ✘ Sun rises in east. ✔ **The** sun rises in **the** east.
7. ✘ Padma is a big river. ✔ **The** Padma is a big river.
8. ✘ He is best boy in the class. ✔ He is **the** best boy in the class.
9. ✘ The Mount Everest is the highest peak. ✔ **Mount** Everest is the highest peak.
10. ✘ He goes to the school every day (as a student). ✔ He goes to **school** every day.
11. ✘ He went by the bus. ✔ He went **by bus**.
12. ✘ I have headache. ✔ I have **a** headache.
13. ✘ He plays the football. ✔ He plays **football**.
14. ✘ She plays harmonium. ✔ She plays **the** harmonium.
15. ✘ The rich is not always happy. ✔ **The** rich **are** not always happy.
16. ✘ He was elected the Chairman. ✔ He was elected **Chairman**.
17. ✘ Quran is the holy book of Muslims. ✔ **The** Quran is the holy book of **the** Muslims / of Muslims.
18. ✘ What a fine weather! ✔ **What fine weather!**
19. ✘ He is a too good man to do this. ✔ He is **too good a** man to do this.
20. ✘ Many a students have failed. ✔ **Many a student has** failed.
21. ✘ I saw an one-eyed man. ✔ I saw **a** one-eyed man.
22. ✘ He gave me an useful advice. ✔ He gave me **useful advice / a useful piece of advice**.
23. ✘ The man is mortal. ✔ **Man** is mortal.
24. ✘ He came at the night. ✔ He came **at night**.
25. ✘ We had the breakfast at seven. ✔ We had **breakfast** at seven.
26. ✘ He is the Newton of age. ✔ He is **the** Newton of **our** age.
27. ✘ I bought the rice and the fish from market. ✔ I bought rice and fish from **the** market.
28. ✘ Taka 50 the kilo. ✔ Taka 50 **a** kilo.
29. ✘ The more you work, more you earn. ✔ **The** more you work, **the** more you earn.
30. ✘ He is the taller of three brothers. ✔ He is **the tallest** of **the** three brothers. (*taller* for two only)
:::

## 5.12 Articles in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
Articles are rarely the sole tested point in international tests, but they appear in **idioms** and **meaning**:

- ***A number of*** (= many, plural) vs ***the number of*** (= the count, singular).
- ***The*** + superlative or *only* signals uniqueness: GMAT may test logic such as ✘ *She is one of the only candidates* (illogical) → ✔ *She is one of the **few** candidates*.
- ***A*** before a countable singular noun is required in standard written English; omitted articles in answer choices are an easy elimination clue.
- In GRE Reading, ***the*** vs ***a*** can change the claim: *"**The** cause of the decline"* (the only cause) vs *"**a** cause of the decline"* (one of several causes). Questions on what the author "suggests" often turn on this difference.
:::

::: tip
**A Three-Question Method for Any Article Blank:** (1) Is the noun **countable singular**? — then it needs *a/an/the* or another determiner. (2) Is it **particular/known/unique** to both speaker and listener? — use *the*. (3) If it is **general** and **plural or uncountable**, use **no article**. Then check the special lists (geographical names, institutions, meals, games, instruments, fixed phrases) for exceptions.
:::
