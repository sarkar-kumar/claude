## 1.1 What Is a Part of Speech?

Every word in an English sentence does a particular **job**. Some words name things, some show action, some describe, some join, and some show relationships. The **job** a word does in a sentence is called its **part of speech**. Traditional grammar — the grammar followed in BCS, Bank and university admission question papers — divides English words into **eight parts of speech**:

| No. | Part of Speech | Its Job | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Noun | names a person, place, thing, idea | Rahim, Dhaka, book, honesty |
| 2 | Pronoun | stands in place of a noun | he, she, it, they, who, this |
| 3 | Adjective | describes / qualifies a noun or pronoun | good, tall, five, every, this |
| 4 | Verb | shows action, being or possession | run, write, is, have, seem |
| 5 | Adverb | modifies a verb, adjective or another adverb | quickly, very, often, here |
| 6 | Preposition | shows relation of a noun/pronoun to another word | in, on, at, by, with, from |
| 7 | Conjunction | joins words, phrases or clauses | and, but, or, because, though |
| 8 | Interjection | expresses sudden feeling | Alas! Hurrah! Oh! Bravo! |

Modern grammars (and the SAT, GRE and GMAT) also treat **determiners** (*a, an, the, this, my, some, every*) as a separate class. Traditional grammar calls them *adjectives* (articles are called "demonstrative adjectives" or simply "articles"). In Bangladeshi exams, if an option list has no "determiner", choose **adjective** for words such as *this, every, some, my* when they come before a noun.

::: rule
**The Golden Rule of Identification:** A word has **no fixed part of speech**. What a word *is* depends on what it *does* in that particular sentence. *"We cannot tell what part of speech a word is until we see it in a sentence."* — this principle (stated by J. C. Nesfield and repeated in every standard grammar) is the key to the whole chapter.
:::

::: ex
- I bought a **round** table. (adjective — describes *table*)
- The boxer won the first **round**. (noun — names a thing)
- The ship will **round** the cape tomorrow. (verb — action)
- The earth moves **round** the sun. (preposition — relates *earth* and *sun*)
- The wheel turned **round**. (adverb — modifies *turned*)
:::

### 1.1.1 Content Words and Function Words

It helps to divide the eight classes into two big groups:

- **Content words (open classes):** nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs. They carry the main meaning; new ones are created all the time (*selfie, google, unfriend*).
- **Function words (closed classes):** pronouns, prepositions, conjunctions, determiners and auxiliary verbs. They build grammar; the list is almost fixed, and they are tested heavily in error-detection questions.

## 1.2 The Eight Parts of Speech in Detail

### 1.2.1 Noun

A **noun** is a word used as the **name** of a person, place, thing, animal, quality, action or idea.

::: ex
- **Person:** Nazrul, teacher, farmer, girl
- **Place:** Sylhet, village, market, Asia
- **Thing:** pen, river, computer, rice
- **Animal:** tiger, cow, hilsa
- **Quality / Idea:** honesty, beauty, freedom, wisdom
- **Action / State:** arrival, laughter, sleep, childhood
:::

A noun can work as the **subject** (*The **boy** runs*), the **object** of a verb (*I saw the **boy***), the **object of a preposition** (*Give it to the **boy***), the **complement** (*He is a **doctor***) or an **apposition** (*Rahim, the **captain**, scored*). Full details are in Chapter 02.

### 1.2.2 Pronoun

A **pronoun** is a word used **instead of a noun** to avoid repetition.

::: ex
- Rina is a student. **She** reads in class ten. (*She* = Rina)
- The book is old, but **it** is useful.
- **Who** broke the glass? (interrogative pronoun)
- This is the man **who** helped me. (relative pronoun)
- **This** is my pen. (demonstrative pronoun — stands alone)
:::

::: note
**Pronoun or adjective?** *This, that, these, those, each, either, neither, some, any, many, few, all, both, one, what, which* are **pronouns** when they stand **alone** and **adjectives (determiners)** when they come **before a noun**. *This is good* (pronoun) / *This book is good* (adjective). *Each got a prize* (pronoun) / *Each boy got a prize* (adjective).
:::

### 1.2.3 Adjective

An **adjective** **qualifies** (adds to the meaning of) a noun or pronoun. It answers *What kind? Which one? How many? How much?*

::: ex
- She is a **clever** girl. (what kind — before the noun: *attributive* use)
- The girl is **clever**. (after a linking verb: *predicative* use)
- I have **three** pens. (how many)
- He has **little** money. (how much)
- **That** house is ours. (which one)
:::

### 1.2.4 Verb

A **verb** says something about the subject: it shows **action** (*run, eat*), **state of being** (*be, seem, become*) or **possession** (*have, own*). Without a verb there is no sentence.

::: ex
- The baby **cries**.
- He **is** honest.
- They **have** a car.
- She **seems** tired.
- The meeting **will be held** tomorrow. (verb phrase: auxiliaries + main verb)
:::

### 1.2.5 Adverb

An **adverb** modifies (adds to the meaning of) a **verb**, an **adjective**, another **adverb**, a **preposition**, a **conjunction** or a **whole sentence**. It answers *How? When? Where? How often? How much?*

::: ex
- He runs **fast**. (modifies the verb *runs*)
- She is **very** beautiful. (modifies the adjective *beautiful*)
- He ran **very** fast. (modifies the adverb *fast*)
- The bird flew **just** over the house. (modifies the preposition *over*)
- He came **only** because he was invited. (modifies the conjunction *because*)
- **Fortunately**, nobody was hurt. (modifies the whole sentence — sentence adverb)
:::

### 1.2.6 Preposition

A **preposition** is placed **before** a noun or pronoun to show its relation to some other word in the sentence (place, time, direction, cause, manner etc.). The noun or pronoun after it is called its **object**.

::: ex
- The cat is **under** the table.
- He was born **in** 1990.
- She went **to** school **by** bus.
- I am fond **of** music.
- He sat **between** you and me. (✘ between you and I — the object of a preposition must be in the objective case)
:::

### 1.2.7 Conjunction

A **conjunction** **joins** words, phrases, clauses or sentences.

::: ex
- Rahim **and** Karim are friends. (joins words)
- He is poor **but** honest.
- I stayed at home **because** it was raining. (joins clauses)
- **Though** he is rich, he is not happy.
- **Either** you **or** he is guilty. (correlative conjunction)
:::

### 1.2.8 Interjection

An **interjection** is a word that expresses a **sudden feeling or emotion**. It has no grammatical connection with the rest of the sentence and is usually followed by an exclamation mark.

::: ex
- **Alas!** He is dead. (grief)
- **Hurrah!** We have won. (joy)
- **Bravo!** Well done. (approval)
- **Oh!** I forgot my keys. (surprise)
- **Hush!** The baby is sleeping. (call for silence)
:::

## 1.3 How to Identify Parts of Speech: Four Reliable Tests

Exam questions usually underline a word and ask *"What part of speech is the underlined word?"* Use these four tests one after another.

### 1.3.1 Test 1 — The Function Test (Most Important)

Ask what the word is **doing**:

1. Is it **naming** something? → Noun.
2. Is it standing **in place of** a noun? → Pronoun.
3. Is it **describing / limiting** a noun? → Adjective.
4. Is it saying what the subject **does or is**? → Verb.
5. Is it telling **how / when / where / how much** about a verb, adjective or adverb? → Adverb.
6. Does it have a **noun/pronoun object after it** and show a relation? → Preposition.
7. Does it **join** two clauses or words? → Conjunction.
8. Is it a **cry** of feeling? → Interjection.

### 1.3.2 Test 2 — The Position (Slot) Test

English has a fairly fixed word order, so the **position** of a word gives a strong clue.

| Slot (frame sentence) | Part of speech that fits | Example |
|---|---|---|
| The ___ is/was good. | Noun | The **book** is good. |
| a/the very ___ person | Adjective | a very **kind** person |
| He/They will ___ (it). | Verb | They will **win**. |
| He did it ___. | Adverb | He did it **carefully**. |
| ___ the table (shows place) | Preposition | **under** the table |

**Clue words:** a word right after *a, an, the, my, his, this, some* is usually a **noun** or an **adjective + noun**. A word right after *to* (infinitive) or after a modal (*can, will, must*) is a **verb**. A word right after *very, too, so, quite, rather* is an **adjective or adverb**.

### 1.3.3 Test 3 — The Suffix (Word Ending) Test

Many words carry endings that signal their class. This test is helpful but **not perfect** (see the traps in 1.4).

| Class | Typical suffixes | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | -tion, -sion, -ment, -ness, -ity, -ty, -ance, -ence, -ship, -hood, -dom, -ism, -ist, -er, -or, -ee, -age, -al, -th, -ure, -cy | information, decision, movement, kindness, ability, cruelty, importance, silence, friendship, childhood, freedom, heroism, scientist, teacher, actor, employee, marriage, arrival, growth, failure, privacy |
| Adjective | -ous, -ful, -less, -able, -ible, -al, -ic, -ical, -ive, -ish, -y, -ant, -ent, -ary, -ory, -some, -ly (from nouns) | famous, beautiful, careless, readable, visible, national, heroic, historical, active, childish, rainy, important, different, necessary, compulsory, troublesome, friendly |
| Verb | -ize/-ise, -ify, -en, -ate | modernise, beautify, strengthen, activate |
| Adverb | -ly (from adjectives), -ward(s), -wise | quickly, homeward(s), clockwise, otherwise |

### 1.3.4 Test 4 — The Substitution Test

Replace the word with a word whose class you are sure of. If the sentence still works grammatically, the two words belong to the same class.

::: ex
- He came **after** the meeting. → He came **before** / **during** the meeting. (both prepositions → *after* is a preposition)
- He came **after** we had left. → He came **when** we had left. (*when* is a conjunction → *after* is a conjunction)
- He came **after**. → He came **later**. (*later* is an adverb → *after* is an adverb)
:::

## 1.4 The Same Word as Different Parts of Speech: Exam Favourites

These words are the most frequently tested in BCS and admission tests. Learn each pattern. (Chapter 42 gives a larger list.)

### 1.4.1 *That*

::: ex
- **That** is my house. → **Demonstrative pronoun** (stands alone)
- **That** house is mine. → **Adjective/Determiner** (before a noun)
- This is the book **that** I bought. → **Relative pronoun** (refers to *book*, introduces an adjective clause)
- I know **that** he is honest. → **Conjunction** (introduces a noun clause)
- He was **that** tired that he slept at once. → **Adverb** (= so; informal)
- I am so tired **that** I cannot walk. → **Conjunction** (of result)
:::

::: tip
**Exam Tip — Relative pronoun or conjunction?** If *that* can be replaced by *which/who/whom*, it is a **relative pronoun**: *the book that (= which) I bought*. If it cannot, and the clause after it is complete in itself, it is a **conjunction**: *I know that he is honest* (✘ I know which he is honest).
:::

### 1.4.2 *But*

::: ex
- He is poor **but** honest. → **Conjunction**
- All **but** him were present. → **Preposition** (= except; note the objective *him*)
- He is **but** a child. → **Adverb** (= only)
- There is no one **but** loves his country. → **Relative pronoun** (= who … not; literary)
- No **buts**, please. / Ifs and **buts**. → **Noun**
- **But** me no buts. → **Verb** (archaic; rarely tested)
:::

### 1.4.3 *Since, Before, After, Till/Until*

These four can be **prepositions** (followed by a noun/pronoun), **conjunctions** (followed by a clause with a subject and verb) or **adverbs** (followed by nothing).

::: ex
- I have been here **since** Monday. → Preposition
- It is two years **since** he died. → Conjunction
- I have not seen him **since**. → Adverb
- **Since** you are tired, you may go. → Conjunction (= because)
- He came **before** me. → Preposition
- He came **before** I left. → Conjunction
- I have seen him **before**. → Adverb
:::

### 1.4.4 *Like*

::: ex
- I **like** mangoes. → Verb
- He runs **like** a deer. → Preposition
- We shall not see his **like** again. → Noun
- They are of **like** minds. → Adjective (= similar)
- He talks **like** he knows everything. → Conjunction (informal; formal English prefers *as if*)
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — *Like* vs *As*:** In GMAT Sentence Correction, *like* is accepted only as a **preposition** (followed by a noun/pronoun) and *as* is used before a **clause**. ✘ *Like I said, the plan failed.* ✔ *As I said, the plan failed.* ✘ *She sings like her mother does.* ✔ *She sings as her mother does* / *She sings like her mother.* Also, *like* compares things (*Like Dhaka, Karachi is crowded*), while *such as* gives examples (*cities such as Dhaka and Karachi*).
:::

### 1.4.5 *What*

::: ex
- **What** do you want? → Interrogative pronoun
- **What** book is this? → Interrogative adjective
- I know **what** you mean. → Relative pronoun (= that which)
- **What**! You failed again? → Interjection
- **What** a beautiful scene! → Adjective (exclamatory)
- **What** with illness and **what** with poverty, he was ruined. → Adverb (= partly)
:::

### 1.4.6 *Well*

::: ex
- She sings **well**. → Adverb
- I am quite **well** now. → Adjective (= in good health)
- The village **well** has dried up. → Noun
- **Well**, what can I do for you? → Interjection
- Tears **welled** up in her eyes. → Verb
:::

### 1.4.7 *Down, Up, In, Out, Near, Round, Off*

::: ex
- He fell **down** the stairs. → Preposition
- Sit **down**. → Adverb
- the **down** train → Adjective
- He has had many ups and **downs**. → Noun
- They **downed** tools. → Verb
- The house is **near** the river. → Preposition
- The day is **near**. → Adjective
- Come **near**. → Adverb
- The ship **neared** the coast. → Verb
:::

### 1.4.8 *Fast, Hard, Late, Early, High, Long, Straight, Daily*

These are both **adjectives** and **adverbs** with the same form.

::: ex
- He is a **fast** runner. (adj) / He runs **fast**. (adv) / He **fasts** in Ramadan. (verb) / He broke his **fast**. (noun)
- This is a **hard** question. (adj) / He works **hard**. (adv)
- He came by a **late** train. (adj) / He came **late**. (adv)
- I take an **early** walk. (adj) / I get up **early**. (adv)
- *The Daily Star* is a **daily**. (noun) / a **daily** newspaper (adj) / It is published **daily**. (adv)
:::

::: note
**Do not confuse:** *hard* (= with effort) ≠ *hardly* (= scarcely); *late* (= not on time) ≠ *lately* (= recently); *high* (= at a high level) ≠ *highly* (= very much); *near* (= close) ≠ *nearly* (= almost); *short* ≠ *shortly* (= soon); *free* (= without paying) ≠ *freely* (= without restriction); *deep* (= far down) ≠ *deeply* (= greatly). *He works **hard*** ✔ / *He hardly works* = he works very little.
:::

### 1.4.9 Words Ending in *-ly* That Are NOT Adverbs

Many students think every *-ly* word is an adverb. The following are **adjectives** (formed from nouns + *-ly*):

*friendly, lovely, lonely, lively, ugly, silly, costly, deadly, cowardly, manly, womanly, motherly, brotherly, scholarly, heavenly, holy, orderly, elderly, likely, unlikely, timely, homely, worldly, jolly, chilly, hilly, bodily, kingly, beastly, leisurely, sickly, surly, burly.*

::: ex
- She is a **friendly** girl. (adj) — We cannot say ✘ *She behaved friendly*. ✔ *She behaved **in a friendly manner/way***.
- He gave a **cowardly** reply. ✔ / He replied **in a cowardly way**.
- It was a **costly** mistake.
- *Daily, weekly, monthly, yearly, early, kindly, only, likely* can be both adjectives and adverbs: *a **kindly** old man* (adj) / *Kindly help me* (adv).
:::

### 1.4.10 Nouns Used as Adjectives and Adjectives Used as Nouns

A **noun** placed before another noun to describe it works as an **adjective** (sometimes called a *noun adjunct*). Such a noun normally stays **singular**.

::: ex
- a **gold** watch, a **village** school, a **cotton** shirt, a **college** student, a **ten-taka** note, a **five-year** plan, a **shoe** shop (✘ shoes shop), a **two-mile** walk (✘ two-miles walk)
:::

An **adjective** preceded by *the* can work as a **plural noun** for a class of people: *the rich, the poor, the old, the young, the blind, the dead, the brave, the wise, the honest*. Such a phrase takes a **plural verb** and has **no -s**.

::: ex
- **The rich are** not always happy. (✘ The rich is / ✘ The riches are)
- **The poor need** our help.
- **The brave deserve** the fair.
- *The unknown* / *the beautiful* (abstract idea) → singular: **The beautiful is** not always good.
:::

### 1.4.11 *-ing* Words: Verb, Noun (Gerund) or Adjective (Participle)?

::: ex
- He **is swimming** now. → **Verb** (part of the verb phrase *is swimming*)
- **Swimming** is a good exercise. → **Noun (Gerund)** — subject
- I like **swimming**. → **Noun (Gerund)** — object
- Look at the **swimming** fish. → **Adjective (Present Participle)** — describes *fish*
- a **swimming** pool → **Gerund** used as an adjective (= a pool *for* swimming, not a pool that swims)
:::

::: tip
**Exam Tip — Gerund or participle before a noun?** Ask: *Is the noun itself doing the action?* A **sleeping** child = a child who is sleeping → **participle (adjective)**. A **sleeping** bag = a bag *for* sleeping → **gerund** (a noun used to form a compound). Similarly: *a walking stick, a dining table, a reading room, drinking water, a frying pan, a swimming pool* are gerunds; *a barking dog, a rising sun, a falling leaf, a running train* are participles.
:::

### 1.4.12 Past Participle: Verb or Adjective?

::: ex
- The window **was broken** by the boys. → **Verb** (passive)
- He looked at the **broken** window. → **Adjective** (describes *window*)
- I am **tired**. / She looks **worried**. → **Adjective** (after linking verbs)
- **Written** in simple English, the book is popular. → **Participle** (adjectival; describes *book*)
:::

### 1.4.13 Words Whose Stress Changes the Class

Some two-syllable words are **nouns/adjectives** with stress on the **first** syllable and **verbs** with stress on the **second**:

| Noun / Adjective (stress 1st) | Verb (stress 2nd) |
|---|---|
| **RE**cord (a record) | re**CORD** (to record) |
| **PRE**sent (a gift; now) | pre**SENT** (to give) |
| **OB**ject (a thing) | ob**JECT** (to oppose) |
| **CON**duct (behaviour) | con**DUCT** (to lead) |
| **PRO**duce (crops) | pro**DUCE** (to make) |
| **PER**mit (a licence) | per**MIT** (to allow) |
| **CON**tract (an agreement) | con**TRACT** (to shrink) |
| **IN**sult / **PRO**test / **RE**bel / **CON**flict / **SUB**ject | in**SULT** / pro**TEST** / re**BEL** / con**FLICT** / sub**JECT** |

### 1.4.14 Other Frequently Tested Words

::: ex
- **Enough:** I have **enough** money (adj). / He is old **enough** (adv). / I have had **enough** (noun/pronoun).
- **Very:** He is **very** good (adv). / This is the **very** book I wanted (adj = exact).
- **Much:** **Much** water (adj). / He is **much** better (adv). / **Much** has been done (noun/pronoun).
- **Only:** He is an **only** child (adj). / He **only** smiled (adv). / I would go, **only** I am busy (conj = but).
- **Still:** The water is **still** (adj = calm). / He is **still** here (adv). / the **still** of the night (noun). / **Still**, I love her (conj/adv = nevertheless).
- **Yet:** He has not come **yet** (adv). / He is poor, **yet** he is honest (conj).
- **For:** He works **for** money (prep). / He was sad, **for** his son had died (conj = because).
- **As:** He is **as** tall **as** I (adv … conj). / **As** he was ill, he stayed home (conj). / He worked **as** a clerk (prep). / Such men **as** are honest (relative pronoun).
- **Either:** **Either** pen will do (adj). / **Either** of them will do (pronoun). / **Either** you or I (conj). / He does not like it **either** (adv).
- **Back:** My **back** aches (noun). / Come **back** (adv). / the **back** door (adj). / They **backed** the plan (verb).
- **Last:** the **last** boy (adj) / He came **last** (adv) / He was the **last** to leave (noun/pronoun) / The food will **last** a week (verb).
- **Over:** The plane flew **over** the city (prep). / The game is **over** (adj/adv). / an **over** in cricket (noun).
- **Off:** He fell **off** the horse (prep). / Switch **off** the light (adv/particle). / an **off** day (adj).
- **Before / After / Since** — see 1.4.3.
- **Love, Work, Name, Book, Water, Hand, Head, Face, Eye, Fish:** each can be noun or verb: *Please **book** two tickets. **Water** the plants. She **headed** the team. He **eyed** me suspiciously.*
:::

## 1.5 Phrases and Clauses Can Also Act as Parts of Speech

A **group** of words can do the job of a single part of speech. This idea links Chapter 01 with Chapters 30 and 31.

::: ex
- **To tell lies** is a sin. → noun phrase (= *Lying* is a sin)
- He is a man **of great wisdom**. → adjective phrase (= a *wise* man)
- He did the work **with great care**. → adverb phrase (= *carefully*)
- **What he said** is true. → noun clause
- This is the house **where I was born**. → adjective clause
- I will wait **till you return**. → adverb clause
- **In front of**, **on account of**, **in spite of**, **because of** → phrase prepositions
- **As soon as**, **as long as**, **so that**, **in order that**, **even if** → phrase conjunctions
- **Alas!**, **Good heavens!**, **Oh dear!** → interjection phrases
:::

:::

::: note
**Participle prepositions:** Some *-ing* or *-ed* words have become prepositions: *regarding, concerning, considering, during, pending, barring, excepting, including, notwithstanding, touching, owing to, given, provided*. *During the war* = preposition (✘ not a gerund or participle in this use).
:::

## 1.7 Common Errors in the Use of Parts of Speech

::: errs
1. ✘ He is a very good runner and runs very good. ✔ … runs very **well**. (*good* = adjective; *well* = adverb for manner)
2. ✘ She dresses beautiful. ✔ She dresses **beautifully**.
3. ✘ I feel badly about the matter. ✔ I feel **bad** about the matter. (*feel* is a linking verb here → adjective)
4. ✘ The food tastes deliciously. ✔ The food tastes **delicious**.
5. ✘ He behaved friendly with me. ✔ He behaved **in a friendly manner** with me.
6. ✘ He came lately to the class. ✔ He came **late** to the class.
7. ✘ He is enough strong to lift it. ✔ He is **strong enough** to lift it. (*enough* follows an adjective/adverb but precedes a noun: *enough money*)
8. ✘ The poors are suffering. ✔ **The poor are** suffering.
9. ✘ Between you and I, he is wrong. ✔ Between you and **me** …
10. ✘ He is more cleverer than his brother. ✔ He is **cleverer** than his brother.
11. ✘ He ran quick to the station. ✔ He ran **quickly** to the station.
12. ✘ She is real happy. ✔ She is **really** happy. (adverb needed to modify an adjective)
13. ✘ I can't hardly see. ✔ I can **hardly** see. (*hardly* is already negative)
14. ✘ This is a two-days programme. ✔ This is a **two-day** programme.
15. ✘ He is an university student. ✔ He is **a** university student. (sound, not spelling, decides *a/an*)
:::

## 1.8 Connecting to the SAT, GRE and GMAT

International tests do not ask "Name the part of speech", but every verbal question assumes you can recognise it instantly:

- **SAT Writing & Language / Standard English Conventions:** questions on *adjective vs adverb* (*a remarkably* (✔) *clear explanation*), *pronoun case*, and *parallel structure* (verbs must be parallel with verbs, nouns with nouns).
- **GRE Text Completion & Sentence Equivalence:** the blank must be filled with a word of the right part of speech. If the blank is preceded by *a/an/the* and followed by a noun, an **adjective** is needed; if followed by an object, a **verb**. Eliminating options of the wrong class is the fastest first step.
- **GMAT Sentence Correction:** tests *modifier* placement (Chapter 29), *noun vs verb* forms (*the ability **to** do* ✔; *the ability **of** doing* ✘), and *like vs as* (1.4.4).

::: tip
**Final Advice:** Never memorise a word's class in isolation. Always ask **"What is this word doing here?"** If it names → noun; replaces a noun → pronoun; describes a noun → adjective; acts/is → verb; describes the action/quality → adverb; relates → preposition; joins → conjunction; exclaims → interjection. This single question solves almost every identification problem.
:::
