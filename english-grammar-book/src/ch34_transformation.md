## 34.1 What Is Transformation?

**Transformation of sentences** means changing the **form** of a sentence **without changing its meaning**. A sentence can be transformed from one **kind** to another (assertive ↔ interrogative ↔ imperative ↔ exclamatory ↔ optative), from one **structure** to another (simple ↔ complex ↔ compound), from **affirmative to negative**, from one **degree of comparison** to another, from **active to passive** and from **direct to indirect** speech.

Transformation is a standard question in SSC, HSC, university admission tests and BCS Written, and it trains the flexibility needed for GMAT Sentence Correction and SAT Writing, where the same idea must be recognised in different forms.

::: rule
**Golden rule:** The **meaning and the tense** must remain the same. Only the **form** changes.
:::

## 34.2 Affirmative into Negative (and Vice Versa)

The basic method is to **negate the opposite**: *He is rich* → *He is **not poor***. Learn the following patterns.

| Rule | Affirmative | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| Use an **antonym** + *not* | *He is honest.* | *He is **not dishonest**.* |
| | *I remember you.* | *I do **not forget** you.* |
| | *He was present.* | *He was **not absent**.* |
| | *She is always punctual.* | *She is **never late**.* |
| ***Only / alone*** (person) → ***none but*** | *Only Rahim can do it.* | ***None but** Rahim can do it.* |
| ***Only*** (thing) → ***nothing but*** | *He drinks only milk.* | *He drinks **nothing but** milk.* |
| ***Only*** (number/age) → ***not more than / not above*** | *He is only ten.* | *He is **not more than** ten.* |
| ***Must*** → ***cannot but / cannot help + -ing*** | *We must obey our parents.* | *We **cannot but** obey our parents. / We **cannot help obeying** …* |
| ***Every / everybody / all*** → ***There is no … but / not … any*** | *Everybody loves flowers.* | ***There is no one who does not** love flowers. / **There is nobody but** loves flowers.* |
| | *Every mother loves her child.* | ***There is no mother who does not** love her child.* |
| ***All*** → ***none … not / not … any*** | *All the boys were present.* | ***None** of the boys was absent.* |
| ***Always*** → ***never*** | *He always speaks the truth.* | *He **never** tells a lie.* |
| ***Sometimes*** → ***not always*** | *Sometimes he comes.* | *He does **not always** come.* |
| ***As … as*** → ***not less … than*** | *Rahim is as tall as Karim.* | *Rahim is **not less** tall **than** Karim. / Karim is **not taller than** Rahim.* |
| ***Too … to*** → ***so … that … cannot*** | *He is too weak to walk.* | *He is **so** weak **that** he **cannot** walk.* |
| ***Both … and*** → ***not only … but also*** | *Both Rahim and Karim came.* | ***Not only** Rahim **but also** Karim came.* |
| ***Many*** → ***not a few*** | *He has many friends.* | *He has **not a few** friends.* |
| ***Much*** → ***not a little*** | *He has much money.* | *He has **not a little** money.* |
| ***A few / a little*** → ***not many / not much*** (reverse) | *He has a few books.* | *He does **not** have **many** books.* |
| ***Few / little*** → ***not many / not much*** | *Few people are honest.* | ***Not many** people are honest.* |
| ***As soon as*** → ***no sooner … than*** | *As soon as he came, the bell rang.* | ***No sooner had** he come **than** the bell rang.* |
| **Superlative** → ***No other … so/as … as*** | *He is the best boy in the class.* | ***No other** boy in the class is **as good as** he.* |
| **Comparative** → ***not so … as*** | *Rahim is taller than Karim.* | *Karim is **not so tall as** Rahim.* |
| ***Doubt*** → ***not sure*** | *I doubt whether he will come.* | *I am **not sure** whether he will come.* |
| ***Hate*** → ***not like / not love*** | *I hate liars.* | *I **do not like** liars.* |
| ***Fail*** → ***not succeed*** | *He failed in the exam.* | *He **did not succeed** in the exam.* |
| ***Sometimes … sometimes*** | *He was sometimes kind.* | *He was **not always** cruel.* |
| ***Universal truth*** | *Man is mortal.* | *Man is **not immortal**.* |
| ***Love*** → ***not hate*** | *I love my country.* | *I do **not hate** my country.* |

### 34.2.1 Negative into Affirmative

Reverse the rules above:

::: ex
- *He is not dishonest.* → *He is **honest**.*
- *None but the brave deserves the fair.* → ***Only** the brave deserve the fair.*
- *There is no rose without a thorn.* → ***Every** rose has a thorn.*
- *I cannot but laugh.* → *I **must** laugh.*
- *He never fails to do his duty.* → *He **always** does his duty.*
- *No sooner had he seen me than he ran away.* → ***As soon as** he saw me, he ran away.*
- *There is no one but loves his country.* → ***Everybody** loves his country.*
- *Not a few people attended.* → ***Many** people attended.*
:::

## 34.3 Assertive into Interrogative (and Vice Versa)

An assertive sentence is changed into a **rhetorical question**. **Affirmative** assertive → **negative** interrogative; **negative** assertive → **affirmative** interrogative.

| Assertive | Interrogative |
|---|---|
| *He is honest.* | ***Isn't** he honest?* |
| *He is not honest.* | ***Is** he honest?* |
| *I can do it.* | ***Can't** I do it?* |
| *It is no use crying.* | ***What is the use of** crying?* |
| *There is no use crying over spilt milk.* | ***What is the use of** crying over spilt milk?* |
| *Everybody loves his country.* | ***Who does not** love his country?* |
| *Nobody can do this.* | ***Who can** do this?* |
| *I never saw such a sight.* | ***Did** I **ever** see such a sight?* |
| *He never tells a lie.* | ***Does** he **ever** tell a lie?* |
| *Nothing can please him.* | ***What** can please him?* |
| *There is none so foolish.* | ***Who** is so foolish?* |
| *I shall never forget you.* | ***Shall** I **ever** forget you?* |
| *It does not matter if we fail.* | ***What does it matter** if we fail?* |
| *All are mortal.* | ***Who** is not mortal?* |
| *There is no need to hurry.* | ***Is there any need** to hurry?* |

::: tip
**Exam Tip — Word pairs for this transformation:** *everybody / everyone / all* → ***who … not***; *nobody / no one / none* → ***who***; *never* → ***ever***; *nothing* → ***what***; *it is no use* → ***what is the use of***; *there is no* → ***is there any***; *it does not matter* → ***what does it matter***.
:::

## 34.4 Assertive into Exclamatory (and Vice Versa)

Assertive sentences with ***very, great, a matter of joy/sorrow, I wish*** can be turned into exclamatory sentences with ***What, How, Hurrah, Alas, Would that, If only***.

| Assertive | Exclamatory |
|---|---|
| *It is a very beautiful scene.* | ***What a** beautiful scene it is!* |
| *The scene is very beautiful.* | ***How** beautiful the scene is!* |
| *He is a great fool.* | ***What a** fool he is!* |
| *She sings very sweetly.* | ***How** sweetly she sings!* |
| *They are very lovely flowers.* | ***What** lovely flowers they are!* |
| *It is a matter of joy that we have won.* | ***Hurrah!** We have won.* |
| *It is a matter of sorrow that he has failed.* | ***Alas!** He has failed.* |
| *I wish I were a bird.* | ***Would that** I were a bird! / **If only** I were a bird!* |
| *It is very kind of you.* | ***How kind of you!*** |
| *I wish I could be young again.* | ***Oh, that** I could be young again!* |

Exclamatory into assertive — the reverse:

::: ex
- *What a nice dress it is!* → *It is a **very** nice dress.*
- *How happy we are!* → *We are **very** happy.*
- *Alas! I am undone.* → *It is **a matter of sorrow** that I am undone.*
- *Bravo! You have done well.* → *It is **praiseworthy** that you have done well.*
:::

## 34.5 Assertive into Imperative (and Vice Versa)

| Assertive | Imperative |
|---|---|
| *You should obey your parents.* | ***Obey** your parents.* |
| *You must not smoke here.* | ***Don't smoke** here.* |
| *I request you to help me.* | ***Please help** me.* |
| *I order you to leave.* | ***Leave**.* |
| *You should never tell a lie.* | ***Never tell** a lie.* |
| *I propose that we should go for a walk.* | ***Let us go** for a walk.* |
| *It is wrong to waste time.* | ***Don't waste** time.* |
| *Everybody should do his duty.* | ***Let everybody do** his duty.* / ***Do** your duty.* |
| *If you work hard, you will succeed.* | ***Work hard, and** you will succeed.* |
| *If you do not work hard, you will fail.* | ***Work hard, or** you will fail.* |

Imperative into assertive:

::: ex
- *Shut the door.* → *You **are ordered to** shut the door. / I **order you to** shut the door.*
- *Please give me a pen.* → *I **request you to** give me a pen.*
- *Never do it again.* → *You **should never** do it again.*
- *Let us start.* → *I **propose that we should** start.*
:::

## 34.6 Assertive into Optative (and Vice Versa)

| Assertive | Optative |
|---|---|
| *I wish that you may live long.* | ***May** you live long!* |
| *I pray that Allah may bless you.* | ***May** Allah bless you!* |
| *We wish our country long life.* | ***Long live** our country!* |
| *I wish you a happy journey.* | ***Have a** happy journey! / **Wish** you a happy journey!* |

## 34.7 Interrogative into Assertive

Reverse 34.3: a **positive** rhetorical question → **negative** statement; a **negative** question → **positive** statement.

::: ex
- *Who does not love flowers?* → ***Everybody** loves flowers.*
- *Can the leopard change its spots?* → *The leopard **cannot** change its spots.*
- *Is there any man who does not love his country?* → *There is **no** man who does not love his country.*
- *What is the use of studying without understanding?* → ***It is no use** studying without understanding.*
- *Didn't he help you?* → *He **helped** you.*
- *When can we forget those days?* → *We can **never** forget those days.*
:::

## 34.8 Simple, Complex and Compound: Inter-Transformation

This is the largest and most frequently tested area of transformation. The tables show the **same idea** in all three structures.

### 34.8.1 Time

| Simple | Complex | Compound |
|---|---|---|
| ***Seeing** the police, the thief ran away.* | ***When** the thief saw the police, he ran away.* | *The thief saw the police **and** ran away.* |
| ***On hearing** the news, she fainted.* | ***As soon as** she heard the news, she fainted.* | *She heard the news **and** (immediately) fainted.* |
| ***Having finished** his work, he went home.* | ***After** he had finished his work, he went home.* | *He finished his work **and** went home.* |
| ***At the time of** my arrival, he was sleeping.* | ***When** I arrived, he was sleeping.* | *I arrived, **and** at that time he was sleeping.* |
| ***Before** his death, he made a will.* | ***Before** he died, he made a will.* | *He made a will **and** then died.* |

### 34.8.2 Reason / Cause

| Simple | Complex | Compound |
|---|---|---|
| ***Being** ill, he could not come.* | ***As / Since / Because** he was ill, he could not come.* | *He was ill, **so** he could not come.* |
| ***Because of / Owing to** illness, he could not come.* | ***Because** he was ill, he could not come.* | *He was ill **and therefore** could not come.* |
| *He was punished **for his laziness**.* | *He was punished **because he was lazy**.* | *He was lazy, **and so** he was punished.* |

### 34.8.3 Condition

| Simple | Complex | Compound |
|---|---|---|
| ***By working hard**, you will succeed.* | ***If** you work hard, you will succeed.* | *Work hard, **and** you will succeed.* |
| ***Without working hard**, you will fail.* | ***If** you do **not** work hard / **Unless** you work hard, you will fail.* | *Work hard, **or** you will fail.* |
| ***In case of** rain, the match will be cancelled.* | ***If** it rains, the match will be cancelled.* | *It may rain, **and then** the match will be cancelled.* |
| ***But for** his help, I would have failed.* | ***If** he **had not** helped me, I would have failed.* | *He helped me, **or else** I would have failed.* |

### 34.8.4 Concession / Contrast

| Simple | Complex | Compound |
|---|---|---|
| ***In spite of / Despite** his poverty, he is honest.* | ***Though / Although** he is poor, he is honest.* | *He is poor, **but** he is honest.* / *He is poor, **yet** he is honest.* |
| ***Notwithstanding** his efforts, he failed.* | ***Though** he tried hard, he failed.* | *He tried hard, **but** he failed.* |
| *He is poor **but** honest.* (one clause) | ***Although** he is poor, he is honest.* | *He is poor, **nevertheless** he is honest.* |

### 34.8.5 Purpose

| Simple | Complex | Compound |
|---|---|---|
| *He works hard **to pass** the exam.* | *He works hard **so that he may pass** the exam.* | *He wants to pass the exam, **and so** he works hard.* |
| *He came here **for help**.* | *He came here **so that he might get help**.* | *He wanted help, **and so** he came here.* |
| *Walk fast **to catch** the bus.* | *Walk fast **so that you may catch** the bus.* | *Walk fast, **or** you will miss the bus.* |

### 34.8.6 Result

| Simple | Complex | Compound |
|---|---|---|
| *He is **too** weak **to walk**.* | *He is **so** weak **that** he cannot walk.* | *He is very weak, **and so** he cannot walk.* |
| *He is **strong enough to lift** the box.* | *He is **so** strong **that** he can lift the box.* | *He is very strong, **and so** he can lift the box.* |
| *The tea is **too hot to drink**.* | *The tea is **so** hot **that** it cannot be drunk / that we cannot drink it.* | *The tea is very hot, **so** we cannot drink it.* |

### 34.8.7 Noun Phrase ↔ Noun Clause

| Simple | Complex | Compound |
|---|---|---|
| *I know **his address**.* | *I know **where he lives**.* | *He lives somewhere, **and** I know the place.* |
| *He confessed **his guilt**.* | *He confessed **that he was guilty**.* | *He was guilty, **and** he confessed it.* |
| *I believe **him to be honest**.* | *I believe **that he is honest**.* | *He is honest, **and** I believe it.* |
| *His **success** is certain.* | *It is certain **that he will succeed**.* | *He will succeed, **and** this is certain.* |
| *Tell me **the time of his arrival**.* | *Tell me **when he will arrive**.* | *He will arrive at a certain time; tell me that time.* |

### 34.8.8 Adjective Phrase ↔ Adjective Clause

| Simple | Complex | Compound |
|---|---|---|
| *I saw a **wounded** soldier.* | *I saw a soldier **who was wounded**.* | *I saw a soldier, **and** he was wounded.* |
| *The man **standing at the gate** is my uncle.* | *The man **who is standing at the gate** is my uncle.* | *A man is standing at the gate, **and** he is my uncle.* |
| *A **rich** man is not always happy.* | *A man **who is rich** is not always happy.* | *A man may be rich, **but** he is not always happy.* |
| *This is **the house of my birth**.* | *This is the house **where I was born**.* | *I was born in a house, **and** this is that house.* |

### 34.8.9 *Not only … but also* and *Both … and*

::: ex
- **Simple:** *Besides being a poet, he was a painter.*
- **Complex:** *He was a poet **who was also** a painter.*
- **Compound:** *He was **not only** a poet **but also** a painter.*
:::

## 34.9 Transformation Using Other Structures

### 34.9.1 *Too … to* / *So … that* / *Enough*

::: ex
- *He is too short to reach the shelf.* = *He is so short that he cannot reach the shelf.* = *He is not tall enough to reach the shelf.*
- *The question is so easy that anyone can answer it.* = *The question is easy enough for anyone to answer.*
:::

### 34.9.2 *As soon as* / *No sooner … than* / *Hardly … when*

::: ex
- *As soon as he saw me, he left.* = *No sooner had he seen me than he left.* = *Hardly had he seen me when he left.* = *On seeing me, he left.*
:::

### 34.9.3 *Unless* / *If … not* / *Or* / *Otherwise*

::: ex
- *Unless you hurry, you will be late.* = *If you do not hurry, you will be late.* = *Hurry, or you will be late.* = *Hurry; otherwise you will be late.*
:::

### 34.9.4 Changing Parts of Speech (Noun ↔ Adjective ↔ Adverb ↔ Verb)

::: ex
- *He is very **honest**.* → *His **honesty** is great.* (adjective → noun)
- *She sang **sweetly**.* → *Her singing was **sweet**.* (adverb → adjective)
- *He **succeeded** in life.* → *He achieved **success** in life. / He was **successful** in life.*
- *He **decided** to leave.* → *He took a **decision** to leave.*
- *She **behaved** politely.* → *Her **behaviour** was polite.*
- *He spoke **angrily**.* → *He spoke **in anger**. / He was **angry** when he spoke.*
:::

### 34.9.5 Emphatic Transformation (*It is … that*)

::: ex
- *Rahim broke the glass.* → ***It was Rahim who** broke the glass.* / ***It was the glass that** Rahim broke.*
- *We need peace.* → ***It is peace that** we need.* / ***What we need is** peace.*
:::

## 34.10 Model Transformations: Mixed Practice Examples

::: errs
1. *Only a fool can say so.* → (Negative) *None but a fool can say so.*
2. *Everyone admires a good man.* → (Interrogative) *Who does not admire a good man?*
3. *What a beautiful bird it is!* → (Assertive) *It is a very beautiful bird.*
4. *Never tell a lie.* → (Assertive) *You should never tell a lie.*
5. *May Allah bless you!* → (Assertive) *I pray that Allah may bless you.*
6. *In spite of his wealth, he is unhappy.* → (Complex) *Though he is wealthy, he is unhappy.* → (Compound) *He is wealthy, but he is unhappy.*
7. *He is too proud to beg.* → (Complex) *He is so proud that he will not beg.*
8. *Being tired, she went to bed.* → (Complex) *As she was tired, she went to bed.* → (Compound) *She was tired and went to bed.*
9. *I know the man who is sitting there.* → (Simple) *I know the man sitting there.*
10. *If you do not run, you will miss the train.* → (Simple) *Without running, you will miss the train.* → (Compound) *Run, or you will miss the train.*
11. *As soon as he reached home, it started raining.* → (Negative) *No sooner had he reached home than it started raining.*
12. *He must admit his fault.* → (Negative) *He cannot but admit his fault.*
13. *There is no man but loves his motherland.* → (Affirmative) *Every man loves his motherland.*
14. *He confessed his guilt.* → (Complex) *He confessed that he was guilty.*
15. *Who can bear such an insult?* → (Assertive) *No one can bear such an insult.*
16. *It is a matter of great joy that you have passed.* → (Exclamatory) *Hurrah! You have passed.*
17. *The boy is very clever.* → (Exclamatory) *How clever the boy is!*
18. *He was poor, but he was honest.* → (Simple) *In spite of his poverty, he was honest.*
19. *Work hard to shine in life.* → (Complex) *Work hard so that you may shine in life.*
20. *I am sure of his success.* → (Complex) *I am sure that he will succeed.*
:::

## 34.11 Common Errors in Transformation

::: errs
1. ✘ *Only Rahim can do it* → *Nobody but Rahim cannot do it.* ✔ *None but Rahim **can** do it.* (no double negative)
2. ✘ *Though he is poor, but he is honest.* ✔ *Though he is poor, he is honest.*
3. ✘ *He is too weak that he cannot walk.* ✔ *He is **so** weak that he cannot walk.*
4. ✘ *No sooner he came than …* ✔ *No sooner **did he come / had he come** than …*
5. ✘ *Who does not loves his country?* ✔ *Who does not **love** his country?*
6. ✘ *What a beautiful bird is it!* ✔ *What a beautiful bird **it is**!*
7. ✘ *Unless you do not work …* ✔ *Unless you work …*
8. ✘ *Seeing the police, the thief was ran away.* ✔ *Seeing the police, the thief **ran** away.*
9. ✘ Changing the tense: *He worked hard to pass* → *He worked hard so that he **may** pass.* ✔ … so that he **might** pass.
10. ✘ Changing the meaning: *Few people came* → *Many people did not come.* ✔ *Not many people came.*
:::

## 34.12 Transformation in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
Transformation skill is exactly what **GMAT Sentence Correction** demands: five answer choices express the same meaning in different structures, and you must recognise which one is **grammatical, logical and concise**. Typical "transformations" between choices include:

- clause ↔ participial phrase (*because it was built in 1900* ↔ *built in 1900*);
- active ↔ passive;
- *so … that* ↔ *too … to* ↔ *enough … to*;
- *although* ↔ *despite* ↔ *but*;
- noun ↔ verb forms (*the decision to expand* ↔ *decided to expand*).

The **meaning must not change**: GMAT often includes an answer that is grammatically perfect but **changes the meaning** of the original — that answer is wrong. In **GRE Sentence Equivalence**, two different words must produce sentences of the **same meaning** — another form of meaning-preserving transformation.
:::
