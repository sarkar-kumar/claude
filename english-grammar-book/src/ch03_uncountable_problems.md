## 3.1 Countable and Uncountable Nouns

English nouns fall into two grammatical groups:

- **Countable nouns** (count nouns) name things we can count one by one: *a book, two books; a boy, ten boys; an idea, many ideas*.
- **Uncountable nouns** (non-count or mass nouns) name things we think of as a **mass** or a **whole** that cannot be divided into separate units: *water, rice, sugar, information, advice, furniture, luggage, news*.

The trouble is that the division is **grammatical, not logical**. In Bangla we can say *anek tottho* ("many informations") or *ekta upodesh* ("an advice"), and in many languages *furniture* or *luggage* are countable. In English they are not. That is why examiners in BCS, Bank and admission tests set at least one question on uncountable nouns almost every year — the so-called **"uncountable problems"**.

## 3.2 The Five Grammar Rules of Uncountable Nouns

::: rule
An uncountable noun:

1. **never takes *a* or *an***: ✘ *an advice*, ✘ *a furniture*, ✘ *a good news*;
2. **has no plural form**: ✘ *advices, furnitures, informations, luggages, sceneries*;
3. **always takes a singular verb**: *The news **is** good. The furniture **was** expensive.*;
4. is used with **singular pronouns**: *I got the information and passed **it** on* (✘ *them*);
5. takes quantifiers like ***much, little, a little, a great deal of, an amount of, less***, not *many, few, a few, a number of, fewer*.
:::

::: ex
- ✘ He gave me an advice. ✔ He gave me **some advice / a piece of advice**.
- ✘ The informations are correct. ✔ The **information is** correct.
- ✘ I have many works to do. ✔ I have **a lot of work** to do.
- ✘ How many luggages do you have? ✔ **How much luggage** do you have? / **How many pieces of luggage** …?
- ✘ The sceneries of Bangladesh are beautiful. ✔ The **scenery** of Bangladesh **is** beautiful.
:::

## 3.4 The Most Tested Uncountable Nouns and How to "Count" Them

To show a single unit or a specific quantity of an uncountable noun, we use a **partitive** (unit) expression: ***a piece of, an item of, a bit of, a slice of, a loaf of, a cup of, a drop of*** etc.

| Uncountable noun | ✘ Never say | ✔ Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| advice | an advice, advices | a piece of advice, a word of advice, some advice |
| information | an information, informations | a piece / an item of information, some information |
| news | a news | a piece / an item of news, some news |
| furniture | a furniture, furnitures | a piece / an article / an item of furniture |
| luggage / baggage | a luggage, luggages | a piece of luggage; (bags / suitcases) |
| equipment | an equipment, equipments | a piece of equipment |
| machinery | a machinery, machineries | a machine, a piece of machinery |
| scenery | a scenery, sceneries | a scene, a view, a landscape, a sight |
| poetry | a poetry, poetries | a poem, a piece of poetry |
| jewellery | a jewellery, jewelleries | a piece of jewellery, a jewel |
| stationery | stationeries | an item of stationery |
| work | a work (= a task), works | a job, a task, a piece of work |
| homework | a homework, homeworks | a homework assignment, a piece of homework |
| money | a money, moneys | a note, a coin, a sum of money |
| evidence | an evidence, evidences | a piece of evidence |
| research | a research, researches (avoid) | a study, a research project, a piece of research |
| progress | a progress, progresses | some progress, a step forward |
| knowledge | knowledges | a piece of knowledge; *a good knowledge of* ✔ (see 3.6) |
| bread | a bread, breads | a loaf / slice / piece of bread |
| weather | a fine weather | fine weather; *What fine weather!* |
| travel | a travel (= a trip) | a journey, a trip, a tour, a voyage |
| accommodation | an accommodation (BrE) | a room, a flat, a place to stay |
| permission | a permission | permission; a permit (document) |
| traffic | a traffic, traffics | a vehicle; heavy traffic |
| behaviour | a behaviour, behaviours (general use) | behaviour; a way of behaving |
| lightning | a lightning | a flash of lightning |
| thunder | a thunder | a clap / peal of thunder |
| luck | a good luck | good luck; a stroke of luck |
| applause | an applause | a round of applause |
| grass | a grass, grasses (lawn) | a blade of grass |
| rain | a rain | a drop of rain, a shower |
| chess | a chess | a game of chess |
| music | a music | a piece of music, a song, a tune |
| clothing | a clothing | an article / item of clothing; a garment |
| soap | a soap | a bar / cake of soap |
| chalk | a chalk | a piece / stick of chalk |
| fun | a fun | some fun; *It was great fun* (✘ a great fun) |
| employment | an employment | a job |
| damage | a damage | some damage; *damages* = compensation money |
| vocabulary | vocabularies (for one person's words) | a word, a term; *a wide vocabulary* ✔ |
| slang | a slang | a slang word / expression |
| mail / post | a mail | a letter, a parcel |
| garbage / rubbish | a rubbish | a piece of rubbish |
| hair (on head) | hairs (all hair on head) | hair; *a hair* = one strand ✔ |
| wood (material) | a wood | a piece of wood; *a wood* = small forest ✔ |
| toast | a toast (bread) | a slice / piece of toast; *a toast* = a drink to honour someone ✔ |
| strength | — | *strengths* ✔ = strong points |
| health, wealth | a good health | good health (✘ a good health) |

::: tip
**Exam Tip — The "big eleven":** Learn these eleven as the most frequently tested in Bangladesh: ***advice, information, news, furniture, luggage/baggage, equipment, machinery, scenery, poetry, jewellery, stationery***. If any of them appears with *a/an*, a plural *-s*, *many*, *few*, *several* or a plural verb, the sentence is wrong. Also remember: ***alphabet*** is countable but means the whole set of letters: ✘ *There are 26 alphabets in English.* ✔ *There are 26 **letters** in the English alphabet.*
:::

## 3.5 Countable in One Sense, Uncountable in Another

Many nouns change their grammar when their meaning changes. These pairs are favourites of question-setters, because the "wrong-looking" form may actually be right.

| Uncountable meaning | Countable meaning |
|---|---|
| **experience** = knowledge/skill from doing: *He has **a lot of experience** in banking.* | **an experience** = an event: *Visiting Sundarbans was **an** exciting **experience**.* |
| **time** = the dimension: *I have no **time**.* | **a time / times** = occasions; periods: *I have told you **three times**. We had **a** good **time**. In **ancient times**.* |
| **paper** = material: *a sheet of **paper*** | **a paper** = newspaper, research article, exam paper: *He read **a paper** at the seminar.* **papers** = documents |
| **glass** = material | **a glass** = drinking vessel; **glasses** = spectacles |
| **iron** = metal | **an iron** = appliance for pressing clothes; **irons** = fetters |
| **light** = brightness: *There is not enough **light**.* | **a light** = a lamp: *Turn off **the lights**.* |
| **hair** = all hair on the head: *She has long black **hair**.* | **a hair / hairs** = individual strands: *There is **a hair** in my soup.* |
| **business** = trade in general: ***Business** is slow.* | **a business** = a company: *He runs **two businesses**.* |
| **room** = space: *Is there **room** for me?* | **a room** = a part of a house: *a house with **five rooms*** |
| **work** = labour, a job: *He is at **work**.* | **a work / works** = creative product; factory: *the **works** of Tagore; a steel **works*** |
| **noise** = general sound: *Don't make **noise**.* | **a noise** = a particular sound: *I heard **a** strange **noise**.* |
| **coffee / tea** = the drink | **a coffee / two teas** = a cup / cups of it (informal) |
| **chicken / fish / lamb** = meat | **a chicken / fish** = the animal; ***fishes*** = species of fish |
| **stone** = material | **a stone** = a pebble; a unit of weight |
| **wood** = material | **a wood / the woods** = small forest |
| **language** = human communication | **a language** = Bangla, English etc. |
| **education** = learning in general | **a good education** = a particular person's learning ✔ |
| **success / failure** in general | **a success / a failure** = a successful/failed thing or person |
| **crime** = illegal activity in general | **a crime** = a particular illegal act |
| **sound** = what we hear in general | **a sound** = a particular sound |
| **cake** = the substance: *some **cake*** | **a cake** = a whole cake |
| **democracy** = the system | **a democracy** = a democratic country |
| **youth** = the period of being young | **a youth** = a young man |
| **beauty** = the quality | **a beauty** = a beautiful woman/thing |
| **kindness** = the quality | **a kindness** = a kind act |
| **lunch / dinner** (general): *We had **lunch**.* | **a** good **lunch** (with adjective) ✔ |

## 3.6 *A / An* + Adjective + Abstract Noun

A special and tricky rule: some abstract uncountable nouns **can take *a/an*** when they are **qualified by an adjective** or followed by a defining phrase (*of …*) and refer to a particular **type or instance**.

::: ex
- She has **a good knowledge of** English. ✔ (✘ She has good knowledges)
- He received **a** good **education** at Dhaka University. ✔
- We live **a** quiet **life**. ✔
- He has **a** great **love of** music. ✔
- She showed **a** remarkable **patience**. ✔ (acceptable; *remarkable patience* is also correct)
- They gave us **a** warm **welcome**. ✔
- There was **a** long **silence**. ✔
- He has **a** strong **dislike** of cats. ✔
- They have **a** high **standard of living**. ✔
- He fell into **a** deep **sleep**. ✔
:::

::: note
**But this rule does NOT apply to the "big eleven" and some others.** Even with an adjective, you cannot say ✘ *a good advice*, ✘ *a useful information*, ✘ *a good news*, ✘ *a heavy luggage*, ✘ *a fine weather*, ✘ *a great progress*, ✘ *a strong evidence*, ✘ *a valuable furniture*, ✘ *a good luck*, ✘ *a heavy traffic*, ✘ *a great fun*, ✘ *a good health*, ✘ *a bad behaviour*, ✘ *a hard work*, ✘ *a permission*. Use them without *a/an*: *good advice, useful information, good news, heavy luggage, fine weather, great progress*.
:::

## 3.7 Nouns That Are Always Plural

The opposite problem: some nouns exist **only in the plural** and take a **plural verb**. They have no singular form in that meaning.

| Type | Examples | Correct use |
|---|---|---|
| Two-part things (tools, clothes) | scissors, spectacles, glasses, trousers, pants, jeans, shorts, pyjamas, tights, pliers, tongs, binoculars, scales | *My scissors **are** blunt.* To count: ***a pair of** scissors, **two pairs of** trousers* |
| Plural-only nouns | goods, thanks, riches, premises, outskirts, surroundings, belongings, savings, earnings, wages, remains, contents, clothes, proceeds, congratulations, credentials, valuables, archives, annals, dregs, ashes (of the dead), arms (weapons), amends, alms, tidings, entrails, eaves | *The goods **have** arrived. My belongings **are** in the bag. Many thanks **are** due to you.* |
| Collective plurals without -s | people, police, cattle, poultry, vermin, gentry, clergy, peasantry | *The police **are** investigating. Cattle **are** grazing. People **are** waiting.* |

::: ex
- ✘ This scissor is sharp. ✔ These **scissors are** sharp. / This **pair of scissors is** sharp. (verb agrees with *pair*)
- ✘ He wears a spectacle. ✔ He wears **spectacles** / **a pair of spectacles**.
- ✘ The premise of the school is clean. ✔ The **premises** of the school **are** clean. (*premise* singular = a basic idea in an argument)
- ✘ The outskirt of the city ✔ the **outskirts** of the city
- ✘ He offered a thank. ✔ He offered **his thanks**. / ✔ **Thanks are** due to you.
- ✘ The cattles / polices / peoples are … ✔ The **cattle / police / people** are …
:::

::: note
**People vs peoples:** *people* = persons (plural): *Ten **people** were injured.* *A people / peoples* = a nation / nations: *the **peoples** of Asia* (= the different nations). Both are correct in the right meaning. **Arms** (weapons) is plural-only; **arm** (body part) is normal: *two arms*. **Means** is the same in singular and plural: *a means of transport / all possible means*.
:::

## 3.8 Nouns That Look Plural but Are Singular (Uncountable)

These end in *-s* but take a **singular** verb (details in Chapters 06 and 23):

- **News:** *The news **is** good.* (✘ The news are …)
- **Subjects:** *mathematics, physics, economics, politics, ethics, statistics, linguistics, phonetics, civics, electronics* — *Mathematics **is** my favourite subject.* (When they mean practical matters/qualities, they can be plural: *His politics **are** confusing. The statistics **show** a rise.*)
- **Diseases:** *measles, mumps, rickets, diabetes, shingles* — *Measles **is** infectious.*
- **Games:** *billiards, darts, draughts, dominoes* — *Billiards **is** played on a table.*
- **Others:** *innings, gallows, series, species, means, summons, works (factory), headquarters, crossroads* — *An innings **was** played. A summons **was** issued.*

## 3.9 Quantifiers: Countable vs Uncountable

This table is the heart of the chapter. Errors in *much/many, few/little, fewer/less, number/amount* appear in every type of exam.

| With countable plurals | With uncountables | With both |
|---|---|---|
| many | much | some, any |
| few, a few, very few | little, a little, very little | a lot of, lots of, plenty of |
| fewer, fewest | less, least | enough, no, all, most |
| a number of, a large number of | an amount of, a large amount of, a great deal of, a good deal of | more, most |
| several, both, a couple of | a quantity of (also with plural) | half of, none of |
| each, every (+ singular) | a bit of, a piece of | the rest of |
| how many? | how much? | |

::: ex
- How **many** books? / How **much** money?
- **Many** students / **Much** effort
- **A number of** people **were** absent. / **A great deal of** time **was** wasted.
- **Fewer** accidents / **Less** pollution
- **Several** chairs / **A piece of** furniture
:::

### 3.9.1 *Few, A Few, The Few* / *Little, A Little, The Little*

This trio is tested very often. The difference is in **attitude**:

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **few** (countable) | almost none — **negative** | ***Few** people attended the meeting.* (= hardly anyone; disappointing) |
| **a few** (countable) | some, a small number — **positive** | *I have **a few** friends in Dhaka.* (= some friends; enough) |
| **the few** (countable) | the small number that exists — **all of them** | *I spent **the few** taka I had.* (= all the little money I had) |
| **little** (uncountable) | almost none — **negative** | *There is **little** hope of his recovery.* (= almost no hope) |
| **a little** (uncountable) | some, a small amount — **positive** | *There is **a little** milk in the jug.* |
| **the little** (uncountable) | the small amount that exists — **all of it** | *He spent **the little** money he had.* |

::: ex
- ✘ A little knowledge is a dangerous thing — ✔ correct proverb (some knowledge, but not enough, is dangerous).
- He has **few** friends, so he is lonely. (negative)
- He has **a few** friends, so he is not lonely. (positive)
- I gave him **the few** books I had. (all my books, though not many)
- **Little** water is left; we must save it. / There is **little** time left.
- ✘ He has a little friends. ✔ He has **a few** friends.
- ✘ There are few water in the bottle. ✔ There is **little** water in the bottle.
:::

::: tip
**Exam Tip — Negative test:** *Few/little* can be replaced by *hardly any / not many / not much*: *Few came* = *Hardly anyone came*. *A few/a little* can be replaced by *some*: *A few came* = *Some came*. If the sentence has a **"but / so / therefore"** that shows disappointment, choose *few/little*: *He tried hard, but **few** listened.* If it shows something positive, choose *a few/a little*: *Don't worry — we still have **a little** time.*
:::

### 3.9.2 *Fewer* vs *Less*; *Number* vs *Amount*

::: rule
***Fewer*** and ***number*** are for **countable** nouns; ***less*** and ***amount*** are for **uncountable** nouns.
:::

::: ex
- ✘ Less people came this year. ✔ **Fewer** people came this year.
- ✔ There is **less** traffic on Fridays.
- ✘ The amount of students has increased. ✔ The **number** of students has increased.
- ✔ The **amount** of rainfall has decreased.
- ✘ He made less mistakes. ✔ He made **fewer** mistakes.
- Exception by convention: with **time, money, distance, weight** seen as one sum, *less* is used: ***less than** 10 kilometres; **less than** 500 taka; **less than** two hours.* Also *less than 5 per cent*.
:::

### 3.9.3 *Much* and *Many* in Positive Sentences

In modern English, *much* and *many* are most natural in **questions** and **negatives**; in positive statements, *a lot of / lots of / plenty of / a great deal of* sounds more natural. *Much* in positive statements is formal: *Much has been written about the war. I owe him much.* But *very much*, *too much*, *so much*, *as much* are normal everywhere: *I like it **very much**.*

## 3.10 Agreement with Quantity Expressions

::: ex
- **A number of** students **are** absent. (= many; plural verb)
- **The number of** students **is** increasing. (the number itself; singular verb)
- **A lot of** water **is** wasted. / **A lot of** people **are** waiting. (verb follows the noun after *of*)
- **Plenty of** food **is** available. / **Plenty of** chairs **are** available.
- **Some of** the money **was** stolen. / **Some of** the boys **were** late.
- **None of** the information **is** useful. / **None of** the students **is/are** present (formal: *is*).
- **Most of** the furniture **is** new. / **Most of** the chairs **are** new.
- **Half of** the cake **has** been eaten. / **Half of** the eggs **are** rotten.
:::

## 3.11 Common Errors with Uncountable Nouns

::: errs
1. ✘ He gave me a good advice. ✔ He gave me **good advice / a good piece of advice**.
2. ✘ We bought many furnitures. ✔ We bought **a lot of furniture** / **many pieces of furniture**.
3. ✘ The news are true. ✔ The news **is** true.
4. ✘ Informations are given below. ✔ **Information is** given below. / **Details are** given below.
5. ✘ Our luggages have been lost. ✔ Our **luggage has** been lost.
6. ✘ The sceneries of Rangamati are beautiful. ✔ The **scenery** of Rangamati **is** beautiful.
7. ✘ She bought many jewelleries. ✔ She bought **a lot of jewellery**.
8. ✘ He has written many poetries. ✔ He has written **many poems / a lot of poetry**.
9. ✘ The machineries are old. ✔ The **machinery is** old. / The **machines are** old.
10. ✘ I have no more works today. ✔ I have no more **work** today.
11. ✘ He has done many researches. ✔ He has done **a lot of research** / **many studies**.
12. ✘ We had a fine weather. ✔ We had **fine weather**.
13. ✘ She has long hairs. ✔ She has long **hair**.
14. ✘ He made a great progress. ✔ He made **great progress**.
15. ✘ The police has come. ✔ The police **have** come.
16. ✘ There are less students today. ✔ There are **fewer** students today.
17. ✘ The amount of cars is huge. ✔ The **number** of cars is huge.
18. ✘ I have a few money. ✔ I have **a little** money.
19. ✘ He did a lot of mischiefs. ✔ He did **a lot of mischief**.
20. ✘ I want an information. ✔ I want **some information / a piece of information**.
21. ✘ Give me a paper to write on. ✔ Give me **a sheet / piece of paper**.

## 3.12 Uncountable Nouns in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
GMAT Sentence Correction regularly tests **quantity words**: *fewer/less*, *number/amount*, *many/much*, and the use of *numbers* with uncountables. Typical traps:

- ✘ *The new law will result in less traffic accidents.* ✔ … ***fewer** traffic accidents* (accidents are countable).
- ✘ *An increasing amount of consumers prefer online shopping.* ✔ *An increasing **number** of consumers …*
- ✘ *The company has reduced the number of waste it produces.* ✔ … *the **amount** of waste …*
- *Increase / decrease* with numbers: *The **number** of accidents **increased*** ✔ (✘ *The accidents increased in number* is wordy but acceptable).
- **Data**: in formal American usage, *data* is often treated as **plural** (*The data **are** reliable*) though singular is now common. **Criteria** and **phenomena** are always plural (*these criteria*; singular: *criterion, phenomenon*). See Chapter 06.

In GRE Reading Comprehension, the difference between *few* (negative) and *a few* (positive) can decide the **author's attitude** — a frequent question type.
:::
