## 10.1 What Are Latin Adjectives?

English has borrowed thousands of words from **Latin**. Among them is a small but very important group of adjectives that were **comparative-degree forms in Latin** — they already contain the meaning of "more" or "less". In grammar books of Bangladesh these are called **Latin Adjectives** or **Latin Comparatives**. They end in ***-or*** (and one, *preferable*, in *-able*).

| Latin adjective | Meaning | Opposite |
|---|---|---|
| **superior** | higher in rank, quality or ability; better | inferior |
| **inferior** | lower in rank, quality or ability; worse | superior |
| **senior** | older in age; higher in rank or service | junior |
| **junior** | younger in age; lower in rank or service | senior |
| **prior** | earlier in time or order | posterior |
| **anterior** | situated before / in front; earlier | posterior |
| **posterior** | situated behind; later | anterior / prior |
| **major** | greater, more important | minor |
| **minor** | lesser, less important | major |
| **exterior** | outer | interior |
| **interior** | inner | exterior |
| **ulterior** | lying beyond; hidden (motive) | — |
| **preferable** | more desirable | — |

## 10.2 The Golden Rule: *To*, Not *Than*

::: rule
The Latin comparatives ***superior, inferior, senior, junior, prior, anterior, posterior*** and ***preferable*** (and the verb ***prefer***) are followed by ***to***, **not *than***. Because they are already comparative in meaning, they are **never** used with ***more*** or ***most***.
:::

::: ex
- He is **superior to** me in intelligence. (✘ superior than)
- This cloth is **inferior to** that. (✘ inferior than)
- Rahim is **senior to** Karim by three years. (✘ senior than)
- She is **junior to** me in service. (✘ junior than)
- He had left the office **prior to** my arrival. (✘ prior than)
- Death is **preferable to** dishonour. (✘ preferable than; ✘ more preferable)
- I **prefer** tea **to** coffee. (✘ prefer tea than coffee)
- The events of chapter 3 are **anterior to** those of chapter 4.
:::

::: tip
**Exam Tip — Double comparison trap:** ✘ *more superior*, ✘ *more preferable*, ✘ *most superior*, ✘ *more senior than* are all wrong in a Bangladeshi exam. To strengthen them, use ***much, far, by far, greatly, considerably***: *This model is **far superior to** the old one. Walking is **much preferable to** sitting all day.*
:::

## 10.3 Each Latin Adjective in Detail

### 10.3.1 *Superior* and *Inferior*

::: ex
- Our team is **superior to** theirs in every respect.
- Local products are not always **inferior to** foreign ones.
- He thinks himself **superior to** others. (arrogant)
- **Superior** and **inferior** can be used without comparison: *a **superior** officer* (= of higher rank), *goods of **inferior** quality*, *the Mother **Superior*** (head of a convent), *an **inferiority** complex*.
- As nouns: *He is my **superior** in office. He treats his **inferiors** kindly.*
:::

### 10.3.2 *Senior* and *Junior*

::: ex
- He is **senior to** me by five years. / He is five years **senior to** me. / He is **my senior by** five years.
- Rina is **junior to** Mina in service.
- As adjectives without comparison: *a **senior** officer, a **junior** clerk, **senior** citizens, a **junior** school.*
- As nouns: *He is **my senior**. The **juniors** respect the **seniors**.*
- After names: *Martin Luther King **Jr.** (Junior), John Smith **Sr.** (Senior).*
:::

::: note
***Elder / older* vs *senior*:** *He is **older than** I* / *He is **senior to** me* (age or rank). ✘ *He is elder than me.* — *Elder* is not followed by *than* (Chapter 09).
:::

### 10.3.3 *Prior*, *Anterior* and *Posterior*

::: ex
- **Prior to** the meeting, the chairman met the press. (= before; formal)
- Applications must be submitted **prior to** 30 June.
- A **prior** engagement prevented him from coming. (= earlier; no comparison)
- The **anterior** part of the brain controls speech. (= front)
- The **posterior** legs of a frog are longer. (= hind)
- A **posterior** event is one that comes after another.
:::

### 10.3.4 *Major* and *Minor*

*Major* and *minor* are Latin comparatives in origin, but in modern English they are used as **ordinary (positive) adjectives** meaning *important / unimportant* or *greater / lesser*. They are **not followed by *to* or *than***.

::: ex
- Unemployment is a **major** problem in Bangladesh.
- It was only a **minor** mistake.
- the **major** part of the population; **minor** injuries
- As nouns: *He is a **minor*** (= under 18, not legally adult). *She is a history **major*** (AmE: main subject of study). *a **major** in the army* (rank).
- ✘ This problem is major than that. ✔ This problem is **more serious than** that / **greater than** that.
:::

### 10.3.5 *Exterior*, *Interior* and *Ulterior*

These also are used as **positive adjectives**, not followed by *to*:

::: ex
- The **exterior** walls need painting. / the **interior** of the country / **interior** decoration
- He has an **ulterior** motive. (= a hidden, usually selfish, reason)
- As nouns: *The **exterior** of the house is old, but the **interior** is modern.*
:::

### 10.3.6 *Preferable* and *Prefer*

***Preferable*** (adjective) and ***prefer*** (verb) take ***to***:

::: ex
- Poverty with honour is **preferable to** wealth with dishonour.
- I **prefer** walking **to** cycling. (*prefer* + gerund + *to* + gerund)
- I **prefer** milk **to** tea.
- With infinitives, use ***rather than***: *I **prefer to walk rather than** (to) cycle.* (✘ I prefer to walk than cycle)
- ✘ I prefer tea than coffee. ✔ I prefer tea **to** coffee.
- ✘ This is more preferable. ✔ This is **preferable**.
:::

## 10.4 Other Comparatives and Expressions That Take *To*

Apart from the Latin adjectives, the following words also take ***to*** in comparison:

| Word | Example |
|---|---|
| prefer | *I prefer rice **to** bread.* |
| preference | *He showed a **preference for** rice **to** bread / **over** bread.* |
| compare | *Life is often **compared to** a journey* (likeness). *Compare this copy **with** the original* (examining differences). |
| similar | *Your pen is **similar to** mine.* (✘ similar with) |
| equal | *Two plus two is **equal to** four.* |
| identical | *This copy is **identical to / with** the original.* |
| opposite | *His house is **opposite (to)** mine.* |
| contrary | *His actions are **contrary to** his words.* |
| previous | *He had resigned **previous to** the election.* (formal) |
| subsequent | ***Subsequent to** the war, prices rose.* (formal) |
| owing / due | *The delay was **due to** heavy rain.* |

::: note
***Different from / than / to:*** British English uses *different **from*** (also *different **to*** informally); American English uses *different **from*** and *different **than***. In Bangladeshi exams and formal writing, ***different from*** is always safe: *This book is **different from** that one.*
:::

## 10.5 Comparing with Latin Adjectives — Model Sentences

::: ex
- Gold is **superior to** silver in value.
- This medicine is **inferior to** that in effect.
- My father is **senior to** my uncle by two years.
- The new clerk is **junior to** all of us.
- Prayer is **preferable to** sleep. (from the call to *Fajr* prayer)
- The visa must be obtained **prior to** departure.
- Health is **preferable to** wealth.
- The Mughal period was **posterior to** the Sultanate period.
- Our tea is **far superior to** imported tea.
- He is **much senior to** me in experience.
:::

## 10.7 Common Errors with Latin Adjectives

::: errs
1. ✘ He is senior than me. ✔ He is **senior to** me.
2. ✘ This pen is superior than that. ✔ This pen is **superior to** that.
3. ✘ Death is more preferable than dishonour. ✔ Death is **preferable to** dishonour.
4. ✘ I prefer tea than coffee. ✔ I prefer tea **to** coffee.
5. ✘ He came prior than me. ✔ He came **before** me. / He arrived **prior to** my arrival.
6. ✘ My brother is junior than me. ✔ My brother is **junior to** me.
7. ✘ She is more superior to her classmates. ✔ She is **far / much superior to** her classmates.
8. ✘ He is inferior than his brother in mathematics. ✔ He is **inferior to** his brother in mathematics.
9. ✘ I prefer to read than to play. ✔ I prefer **reading to playing**. / I prefer **to read rather than play**.
10. ✘ This is the most superior quality. ✔ This is of **superior** / the **highest** quality.
11. ✘ His motive was ulterior than his words. ✔ He had an **ulterior** motive.
12. ✘ My house is similar with yours. ✔ My house is **similar to** yours.
13. ✘ He is senior from me by two years. ✔ He is **senior to** me by two years.
14. ✘ This plan is major than that one. ✔ This plan is **more important than** that one.
15. ✘ He is two years senior than his wife. ✔ He is two years **senior to** his wife.
:::

## 10.8 Latin Adjectives in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
- **GMAT idioms:** *prefer A **to** B*; *preferable **to***; *superior **to***; *prior **to*** (often replaced by *before* for concision). GMAT also tests ***rather than*** with parallel forms: *She prefers **to walk rather than to drive*** ✔.
- **Redundancy (GMAT/SAT):** *more superior, more preferable, more senior than* are both ungrammatical and redundant.
- **GRE vocabulary:** the descriptive Latin adjectives in 10.6 (*aquiline, leonine, bovine, asinine, avuncular, uxorious, puerile, senile, quotidian, vernal, insular, mundane, sanguine, bellicose, pacific, somnolent, loquacious*) appear regularly in GRE Text Completion and Reading. *Sanguine* is a classic trap: literally "of blood", it now means **optimistic**.
:::
