## 19.1 What Is a Conjunction?

A **conjunction** is a word that **joins** words, phrases, clauses or sentences. (*Con* = together, *junction* = joining.)

::: ex
- Rahim **and** Karim are brothers. (joins two words)
- He is poor **but** honest. (joins two adjectives)
- You can go by bus **or** by train. (joins two phrases)
- I stayed at home **because** it was raining. (joins two clauses)
- She worked hard; **however**, she failed. (*however* links ideas but is a conjunctive **adverb** — see 19.6)
:::

A conjunction differs from a **relative pronoun** (*who, which*) and a **relative adverb** (*where, when*), which also join clauses but have an additional job. It also differs from a **preposition**, which takes a noun/pronoun object, not a clause: *He came **because of** the rain* (preposition phrase) / *He came **because** it was raining* (conjunction).

## 19.2 Kinds of Conjunctions

There are three structural kinds:

1. **Coordinating conjunctions** — join elements of **equal rank** (two words, two phrases, two independent clauses).
2. **Subordinating conjunctions** — join a **dependent (subordinate) clause** to a **main clause**.
3. **Correlative conjunctions** — **pairs** of conjunctions used together.

## 19.3 Coordinating Conjunctions

The seven basic coordinating conjunctions can be remembered as **FANBOYS**: ***For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So***. Traditional grammar classifies coordinators by meaning into four groups:

| Kind | Meaning | Conjunctions | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cumulative / Copulative** | adds one fact to another | and, both … and, as well as, not only … but also, also, too, moreover, furthermore | *He is clever **and** hard-working. **Both** Rina **and** Mina came. He **as well as** his brother is guilty.* |
| **Adversative** | contrasts two facts | but, yet, still, however, nevertheless, whereas, while, only | *He is rich, **but** he is unhappy. He is poor, **yet** he is honest. You may go, **only** come back soon.* |
| **Alternative / Disjunctive** | gives a choice between alternatives | or, either … or, neither … nor, otherwise, else, or else, nor | *Work hard, **or** you will fail. **Either** you **or** he must go. Hurry up, **otherwise** you will miss the bus.* |
| **Illative / Causal** | shows inference, result or reason | so, therefore, hence, consequently, for, thus | *He was ill, **so** he did not come. He must be ill, **for** he is absent.* |

::: note
***For*** as a conjunction means *because* but is **coordinating** and formal; it introduces a reason **as an afterthought** and cannot begin a sentence: *He felt no fear, **for** he was a brave man.* ✘ *For he was brave, he felt no fear.* (Use *As/Because/Since* at the beginning.)
:::

## 19.4 Subordinating Conjunctions

Subordinating conjunctions introduce **adverb clauses** (and *that, whether, if* introduce **noun clauses**). They are classified by meaning:

| Meaning | Conjunctions | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | when, whenever, while, as, before, after, till, until, since, as soon as, as long as, once, by the time, now that, the moment | *I will wait **till** you come. **As soon as** he saw me, he ran away. It is five years **since** he died.* |
| **Place** | where, wherever | *Go **wherever** you like.* |
| **Cause / Reason** | because, since, as, now that, seeing that, inasmuch as, in that | ***As** it was raining, we stayed home. **Since** you are tired, you may rest.* |
| **Purpose** | that, so that, in order that, lest | *He works hard **so that** he may succeed. Walk fast **lest** you should miss the train.* |
| **Result / Consequence** | so … that, such … that, so that (result) | *He is **so** weak **that** he cannot walk. It was **such** a hot day **that** we stayed in.* |
| **Condition** | if, unless, provided (that), providing, on condition that, in case, as long as, so long as, supposing, suppose, whether … or, but that, once | *I will go **if** he invites me. You will fail **unless** you work hard. You can go **provided** you return early.* |
| **Concession / Contrast** | though, although, even though, even if, while, whereas, however, no matter how/what, whatever, much as, as (in *Rich as he is*) | ***Though** he is poor, he is honest. **Even if** it rains, I will go.* |
| **Comparison** | than, as … as, so … as, the … the | *He is taller **than** I. She is **as** clever **as** her sister. **The** more, **the** merrier.* |
| **Manner** | as, as if, as though, the way | *Do **as** I tell you. He talks **as if** he knew everything.* |
| **Noun clause** | that, whether, if | *I know **that** he is honest. I doubt **whether** he will come.* |

## 19.5 Correlative Conjunctions

Correlative conjunctions are used **in pairs**. The two elements they join must be **grammatically parallel** (Chapter 39).

| Pair | Example |
|---|---|
| **either … or** | ***Either** you **or** I must go.* |
| **neither … nor** | *He is **neither** rich **nor** handsome.* (✘ neither … or) |
| **both … and** | ***Both** Rahim **and** Karim passed.* (✘ both … as well as) |
| **not only … but also** | *She is **not only** beautiful **but also** intelligent.* |
| **whether … or** | *I don't know **whether** he will come **or** not.* |
| **though / although … yet** | ***Though** he is poor, **yet** he is honest.* (traditional; *yet* is optional; ✘ though … but) |
| **so … that** | *He ran **so** fast **that** he won.* |
| **such … that** | *It was **such** a good film **that** we saw it twice.* |
| **such … as** | ***Such** men **as** are honest are respected.* |
| **so … as** | *He is not **so** tall **as** his brother.* |
| **as … as** | *She is **as** wise **as** her mother.* |
| **the same … as** | *This is **the same** pen **as** I lost.* |
| **no sooner … than** | ***No sooner** had he come **than** it rained.* |
| **hardly / scarcely … when** | ***Hardly** had he come **when** it rained.* |
| **lest … should** | *Work hard **lest** you **should** fail.* |
| **rather … than** | *I would **rather** die **than** beg.* |
| **if … then** | ***If** x = 2, **then** 2x = 4.* |
| **not … but** | *It is **not** you **but** I who am to blame.* |
| **not so much … as** | *He is **not so much** a scholar **as** a teacher.* |
| **whether … or (no)** | ***Whether** you like it **or not**, you must go.* |

::: rule
**Parallel placement:** Put each half of a correlative **immediately before** the matching elements: ✘ *He **not only** lost his money **but also** his reputation.* ✔ *He lost **not only** his money **but also** his reputation.* ✘ *She **either** will go by bus **or** by train.* ✔ *She will go **either** by bus **or** by train.*
:::

## 19.6 Conjunctions vs Conjunctive Adverbs

Words such as ***however, therefore, moreover, furthermore, nevertheless, nonetheless, consequently, hence, thus, besides, otherwise, accordingly, still, also, then*** connect ideas but are grammatically **adverbs**. They cannot join two independent clauses with only a comma.

::: ex
- ✘ He studied hard, however he failed. (comma splice)
- ✔ He studied hard; **however**, he failed.
- ✔ He studied hard. **However**, he failed.
- ✔ He studied hard, **but** he failed. (real conjunction with a comma)
:::

## 19.7 Important Rules and Usage Points

### 19.7.1 *Both … and*, *As well as*

***Both*** pairs only with ***and***. ***As well as*** is not a correlative of *both*, and the verb agrees with the **first** subject (Chapter 23).

::: ex
- ✘ **Both** Rahim **as well as** Karim came. ✔ **Both** Rahim **and** Karim came.
- Rahim, **as well as** his brothers, **is** coming. (verb agrees with *Rahim*)
- ✘ Both of them are not present. (ambiguous) ✔ **Neither** of them is present. / **Only one** of them is present.
:::

### 19.7.2 *Either … or*, *Neither … nor*

::: ex
- ✘ **Neither** he **or** his friend came. ✔ **Neither** he **nor** his friend came.
- ✘ He did not come **nor** write. → ✔ He **neither** came **nor** wrote. / He did not come **or** write.
- The verb agrees with the **nearer** subject: ***Either** the teacher **or** the students **are** responsible.* ***Neither** the students **nor** the teacher **is** responsible.*
:::

### 19.7.3 *Though / Although* — Never with *But*

***Though/Although*** already shows contrast; adding ***but*** is a double conjunction error. *Yet* or *still* may be used for emphasis (traditional style).

::: ex
- ✘ **Though** he is rich, **but** he is not happy.
- ✔ **Though** he is rich, he is not happy.
- ✔ **Though** he is rich, **yet** he is not happy. (traditional)
- ✔ He is rich, **but** he is not happy.
- ***Although*** is slightly more formal and normally begins a sentence; ***though*** can also come at the end as an adverb: *He is rich. He is not happy, **though**.*
:::

### 19.7.4 *Lest … should*

***Lest*** means "*for fear that / so that … not*". It is followed by ***should*** + base form (or the bare subjunctive in American English) and **never by *not***:

::: ex
- Walk carefully **lest** you **should** fall. (✘ lest you should not fall; ✘ lest you will fall; ✘ lest you fall not)
- He ran fast **lest** he **should** miss the train.
:::

### 19.7.5 *Unless* and *Until* — Already Negative

***Unless*** = *if … not*; ***until*** = *up to the time when*. They must **not** be followed by *not* (in the sense of double negation).

::: ex
- ✘ **Unless** you do **not** work hard, you will fail. ✔ **Unless** you work hard, you will fail. (= If you do not work hard …)
- ✘ Wait here **until** I do **not** return. ✔ Wait here **until** I return.
- ***Until / till*** concerns **time**; ***unless*** concerns **condition**: ✘ *Don't go until he permits you* (acceptable) / ✔ *You can't go **unless** he permits you.*
:::

### 19.7.6 *Since*, *Because*, *As*, *For*

| Conjunction | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ***because*** | the **strongest**, gives the **direct cause**; answers *why?*; usually after the main clause | *He failed **because** he was lazy.* — *Why did he fail? **Because** he was lazy.* |
| ***since*** | a **known or obvious** reason; often at the start | ***Since** you are busy, I will come later.* |
| ***as*** | a reason that is **less important** or already known | ***As** it was late, we went home.* |
| ***for*** | an **afterthought** reason (formal, never first) | *He must be poor, **for** he wears torn clothes.* |
| ***because of*** | **preposition** — followed by a noun, not a clause | *He was absent **because of** illness.* (✘ because of he was ill) |

::: note
***Since*** also expresses **time** (*It is ten years **since** he left*). With the "time" meaning, the main clause normally takes the **present perfect** or *It is …*: *I **have** known him **since** he **was** a child.*
:::

### 19.7.7 *The reason … is that* (not *because*)

***The reason*** already means *because*, so ***the reason … because*** is redundant:

::: ex
- ✘ **The reason** he failed is **because** he was lazy.
- ✔ **The reason** he failed is **that** he was lazy.
- ✔ He failed **because** he was lazy.
- ✘ The reason why … is because … ✔ The reason why … is that …
:::

### 19.7.8 *Than* — after Comparatives Only

***Than*** follows a **comparative** (*-er / more / less*) and *other, else, rather, sooner*:

::: ex
- ✘ He is more cleverer **than** his brother. ✔ He is cleverer **than** his brother.
- ✘ He is senior **than** me. ✔ He is senior **to** me. (Latin comparatives — Chapter 10)
- ✘ He has no other friend **but** you. ✔ He has no other friend **than** you. (*other … than*)
- ✘ He prefers to die **than** beg. ✔ He would **rather** die **than** beg.
- ✘ Scarcely had he left **than** … ✔ … **when** … (Chapter 16)
:::

### 19.7.9 *As … as*, *So … as*

***As … as*** is used in **positive** comparisons; ***so … as*** (or *as … as*) in **negative** ones (traditional rule):

::: ex
- He is **as** tall **as** his father.
- He is not **so** tall **as** his father. (also *not as tall as*)
- ✘ He is so tall as his father. ✔ He is **as** tall **as** his father.
:::

### 19.7.10 *If* vs *Whether*

Both introduce indirect yes/no questions (*I don't know **if/whether** he will come*), but only ***whether*** can be used:

1. **after a preposition:** *It depends **on whether** he agrees.* (✘ on if)
2. **before a *to*-infinitive:** *I can't decide **whether to** go.* (✘ if to go)
3. **at the beginning of a sentence (as subject):** ***Whether** he will come is uncertain.* (✘ If he will come is uncertain)
4. **with *or not* immediately after it:** *I don't know **whether or not** he will come.* (✘ if or not)
5. **after *the question*, *the issue*, *discuss*:** *We discussed **whether** to go.*

### 19.7.11 *As if* / *As though*

These conjunctions introduce an **unreal comparison** and take the **past subjunctive** (*were*) or past perfect:

::: ex
- He talks **as if** he **were** a king. (✘ as if he is a king — in exams)
- She behaved **as though** nothing **had happened**.
- He looks **as if** he **is** ill. (here it may be true — real possibility; present tense acceptable)
:::

### 19.7.12 *That* (Noun Clause and Purpose)

***That*** introduces **noun clauses** and can often be **omitted** after common reporting verbs (*say, think, know, believe, hope*): *I think (**that**) he is right.* It cannot be omitted when the clause is the **subject**: ***That** he is honest is clear.* (✘ He is honest is clear.)

::: ex
- ✘ He said **that** "I am ill." (direct speech has no *that*) ✔ He said, "I am ill." / ✔ He said **that** he was ill.
- ✘ I asked him **that** where he lived. ✔ I asked him **where** he lived. (*that* is not used before a question word)
:::

### 19.7.13 *While* and *Whereas*

***While*** = (1) *during the time that* (*He arrived **while** I was eating*), (2) *although / whereas* (contrast): ***While** I like the colour, I don't like the design.* ***Whereas*** = in contrast (comparing two facts): *Rahim is tall, **whereas** Karim is short.*

### 19.7.14 *In case*, *Provided*, *As long as*, *Now that*, *Once*

::: ex
- Take an umbrella **in case** it rains. (as a precaution; ✘ in case it will rain)
- You can go **provided (that)** you finish your work. (= only if)
- You may stay **as long as** you keep quiet. (condition) / I'll remember it **as long as** I live. (time)
- ***As far as*** = to the extent that / distance: ***As far as** I know, he is honest.*
- **Now that** the exams are over, we can relax. (= because now)
- **Once** you learn to ride a bicycle, you never forget. (= as soon as / after)
:::

### 19.7.15 *So that* (purpose) vs *So* (result)

::: ex
- He studied hard **so that** he **could** pass. (purpose — usually with *can/could/may/might/will/would*)
- He studied hard, **so** he passed. (result — comma, no modal needed)
:::

### 19.7.16 *Such … that* vs *So … that*

***So*** + adjective/adverb + *that*; ***such*** + (a/an) + adjective + noun + *that*:

::: ex
- It was **so** cold **that** we stayed inside.
- It was **such** a cold day **that** we stayed inside.
- They are **such** good friends **that** they never quarrel.
- ✘ It was such cold that … ✘ It was so cold day that …
:::

### 19.7.17 Some Fixed Conjunctive Expressions

***Seldom or never*** (✘ seldom or ever), ***little or nothing***, ***few if any***: *He **seldom or never** goes to the cinema. He knows **little or nothing** about it.*

## 19.8 Beginning a Sentence with *And* or *But*

Traditional grammar says a sentence should not begin with *And* or *But*. Modern writers do it for emphasis, and it is grammatically acceptable. In formal exam answers, prefer *Moreover / Furthermore* and *However* at the beginning: ***However**, the plan failed.*

## 19.9 Common Errors with Conjunctions

::: errs
1. ✘ Though he is poor but he is honest. ✔ Though he is poor, he is honest.
2. ✘ Both Rahim as well as Karim came. ✔ Both Rahim **and** Karim came.
3. ✘ Neither he or I was there. ✔ Neither he **nor** I was there.
4. ✘ Unless you do not work hard, you will fail. ✔ Unless you work hard, you will fail.
5. ✘ Wait until I do not come. ✔ Wait until I come.
6. ✘ Walk carefully lest you should not fall. ✔ Walk carefully lest you **should fall**.
7. ✘ No sooner had he arrived when the rain started. ✔ … **than** the rain started.
8. ✘ Hardly had I left than he came. ✔ … **when** he came.
9. ✘ The reason he was late is because he missed the bus. ✔ … is **that** he missed the bus.
10. ✘ He is so weak that he cannot to walk. ✔ He is so weak that he cannot walk.
11. ✘ It was so a good film that … ✔ It was **such** a good film that …
12. ✘ I doubt if or not he will come. ✔ I doubt **whether or not** he will come.
13. ✘ If he will come or not is uncertain. ✔ **Whether** he will come or not is uncertain.
14. ✘ He not only lost his watch but also his bag. ✔ He lost **not only** his watch **but also** his bag.
15. ✘ He was absent because of he was ill. ✔ He was absent **because** he was ill / **because of** illness.
16. ✘ He talks as if he is the boss. (unreal) ✔ He talks as if he **were** the boss.
17. ✘ Scarcely I had reached when it rained. ✔ Scarcely **had I** reached when it rained.
18. ✘ He asked me that what my name was. ✔ He asked me **what** my name was.
19. ✘ He said that, "I am busy." ✔ He said, "I am busy." / He said **that** he was busy.
20. ✘ He was so tired as he slept at once. ✔ He was **so** tired **that** he slept at once.
21. ✘ He is not as tall like his brother. ✔ He is not **as / so** tall **as** his brother.
22. ✘ He has no other friend but you. ✔ He has no other friend **than** you.
23. ✘ Since he was ill, so he did not come. ✔ **Since** he was ill, he did not come. / He was ill, **so** he did not come.
24. ✘ Take your umbrella in case it will rain. ✔ … in case it **rains**.
25. ✘ He seldom or ever comes here. ✔ He **seldom or never** comes here.
26. ✘ I would rather die than to beg. ✔ I would rather die than **beg**.
27. ✘ Because he was ill, therefore he did not come. ✔ **Because** he was ill, he did not come.
28. ✘ He worked hard so that he passed. (meaning result) ✔ He worked hard, **so** he passed.
29. ✘ He is both a good teacher and writes well. ✔ He is **both** a good teacher **and** a good writer.
30. ✘ Either you can stay or go. ✔ You can **either** stay **or** go.
:::

## 19.10 Conjunctions in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
- **Correlative parallelism (GMAT):** *both X **and** Y*, *either X **or** Y*, *neither X **nor** Y*, *not only X **but also** Y*, *not X **but** Y*, *X rather than Y*, *whether X or Y* — X and Y must have the **same grammatical form**. ✘ *The plan was not only expensive but also took too long.* ✔ *The plan **not only was** expensive **but also took** too long* / *was not only **expensive** but also **slow***.
- ***Whether* vs *if*** — GMAT prefers ***whether*** for alternatives: *He asked **whether** the report was finished.*
- ***The reason … is that*** — GMAT marks *the reason … is because* as redundant.
- ***So … as to*** / ***so … that***: *The noise was **so** loud **as to** be unbearable* / *so loud **that** it was unbearable.* ✘ *so loud **as** it was unbearable*.
- **GRE Text Completion — logic words:** contrast (*but, yet, although, however, whereas, while, despite*); continuation (*and, moreover, furthermore, indeed, in fact*); cause/effect (*because, since, therefore, thus, hence, consequently*). Spotting the conjunction first tells you whether the blank should **agree with** or **oppose** the other half of the sentence.
:::
