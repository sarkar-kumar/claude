## 35.1 What Is "Pin Point Error"?

In a **Pin Point Error** (also called **Error Detection** or **Spotting the Error**) question, a sentence is divided into **underlined parts** (usually A, B, C, D, and sometimes E = "No error"). You must find the **one part** that contains a grammatical, idiomatic or usage error.

::: ex
- *The number of students (A) / in our college (B) / are increasing (C) / day by day. (D)* → Error in **(C)**: *The number of* + singular → ***is** increasing*.
- *He is one of the best (A) / player (B) / that our country (C) / has ever produced. (D)* → Error in **(B)**: *one of the best **players***.
:::

This item appears in nearly every **BCS Preliminary**, **Bank** (officer, senior officer, AD), **university admission** and **job** recruitment test in Bangladesh, and it is the core of **SAT Writing** ("Identifying Sentence Errors" in the older SAT) and **GMAT Sentence Correction**. Pin point errors draw on **every chapter** of Part A; this chapter gives a systematic **checklist** and a classified bank of typical errors.

## 35.2 A Ten-Point Checklist for Spotting Errors

Read the sentence once for meaning, then check these points **in order**. Most errors are found in the first five.

::: rule
1. **Subject–verb agreement** (Chapter 23): find the true subject; check *is/are, has/have, was/were*, *-s* verbs.
2. **Tense** (Chapter 21): time words (*since, ago, yesterday, by the time*), sequence of tenses, *if*-clauses.
3. **Pronoun** (Chapter 08): case (*I/me*), agreement (*each … his*), reference (ambiguous *it/they*), *who/whom*, *one … one's*.
4. **Verb form** (Chapters 12–14, 22): V2/V3, gerund vs infinitive, modals + V1, *to* as preposition, *suggest that he go*.
5. **Preposition and idiom** (Chapters 17–18): *married **to***, *congratulate **on***, *discuss* (no preposition), *prefer … **to***.
6. **Article and determiner** (Chapters 04–05): *a/an/the*, *each/every*, *few/a few*, *much/many*, *less/fewer*.
7. **Adjective/adverb and comparison** (Chapters 09, 15, 24): double comparatives, *than any other*, *-ed/-ing*, *hard/hardly*.
8. **Nouns and number** (Chapters 02–03, 06): *informations, sceneries, two dozens, one of the best player*.
9. **Word order, conjunctions, inversion** (Chapters 16, 19): *though … but*, *no sooner … than*, *hardly … when*, *lest … should*, *scarcely had he*.
10. **Parallelism, redundancy, modifiers** (Chapters 29, 38, 39): *not only … but also* placement, *return back*, dangling *-ing* phrases.
:::

::: tip
**Exam Tip — Read the whole sentence, then read each part separately.** Many errors are visible only when you connect two parts (subject in A, verb in C). If you cannot find any error after checking all ten points, the answer may be "No error" — but in Bangladeshi MCQs "No error" is the correct answer much less often than in the SAT.
:::

## 35.3 Error Bank: Subject–Verb Agreement

::: errs
1. ✘ The quality of the mangoes **are** good. ✔ … **is** good.
2. ✘ Each of the boys **have** a pen. ✔ … **has** a pen.
3. ✘ Neither of the two sisters **are** married. ✔ … **is** married.
4. ✘ The news **are** true. ✔ The news **is** true.
5. ✘ Bread and butter **are** my breakfast. ✔ … **is** my breakfast.
6. ✘ The Prime Minister, along with his ministers, **are** coming. ✔ … **is** coming.
7. ✘ Either the teacher or the students **is** responsible. ✔ … **are** responsible.
8. ✘ A number of students **was** absent. ✔ … **were** absent.
9. ✘ The number of accidents **are** rising. ✔ … **is** rising.
10. ✘ Ten kilometres **are** a long distance. ✔ … **is** a long distance.
11. ✘ Physics **are** a difficult subject. ✔ … **is** …
12. ✘ The cattle **is** grazing. ✔ The cattle **are** grazing.
13. ✘ Everyone of the students **were** present. ✔ Every one of the students **was** present.
14. ✘ There **is** many books on the shelf. ✔ There **are** many books …
15. ✘ He is one of those who **has** succeeded. ✔ … who **have** succeeded.
16. ✘ The committee **have** submitted its report. ✔ The committee **has** submitted its report.
17. ✘ Many a man **have** died. ✔ Many a man **has** died.
18. ✘ Mathematics and physics **is** my favourite subjects. ✔ … **are** my favourite subjects.
:::

## 35.10 Error Bank: Conjunctions, Inversion and Structure

::: errs
1. ✘ **Though** he is poor, **but** he is honest. ✔ Though he is poor, he is honest.
2. ✘ **No sooner** had he come **when** it rained. ✔ … **than** it rained.
3. ✘ **Hardly** had he come **than** it rained. ✔ … **when** it rained.
4. ✘ **Never I have** seen it. ✔ **Never have I** seen it.
5. ✘ **Unless** you do **not** work hard, you will fail. ✔ Unless you work hard, …
6. ✘ Walk fast **lest** you **will** miss the bus. ✔ … lest you **should** miss the bus.
7. ✘ **Both** Rahim **as well as** Karim came. ✔ Both Rahim **and** Karim came.
8. ✘ **Neither** he **or** I ✔ Neither he **nor** I
9. ✘ The reason he failed is **because** he was lazy. ✔ … is **that** he was lazy.
10. ✘ He asked me **that where** I lived. ✔ He asked me **where** I lived.
11. ✘ I don't know where **does he live**. ✔ … where **he lives**.
12. ✘ He **not only** lost his money **but also** his reputation. ✔ He lost **not only** his money **but also** his reputation.
13. ✘ He is **so** weak **to walk**. ✔ He is **too** weak to walk.
14. ✘ **Scarcely** I had reached when … ✔ **Scarcely had I** reached when …
:::

## 35.11 Error Bank: Redundancy, Modifiers and Word Choice

::: errs
1. ✘ He **returned back** home. ✔ He **returned** home.
2. ✘ Please **repeat** it **again**. ✔ Please **repeat** it.
3. ✘ The **reason why** he failed is **because** … ✔ The reason he failed is **that** …
4. ✘ **Walking** in the garden, **a snake bit** him. ✔ Walking in the garden, **he was bitten** by a snake.
5. ✘ He is a **ten years** old boy. ✔ He is a **ten-year-old** boy.
6. ✘ **Each and every** students ✔ Each and every **student**
7. ✘ The **cousin brother** ✔ my **cousin**
8. ✘ He **said me** that … ✔ He **told me** that …
9. ✘ **Explain me** the rule. ✔ **Explain** the rule **to me**.
10. ✘ He is **too much** intelligent. ✔ He is **very** intelligent.
11. ✘ I **besides** the others went there. ✔ I, **along with** the others, went there.
12. ✘ The **principle** of the college ✔ the **principal** of the college
13. *He has a **dearth** of knowledge.* — **No error** (*dearth* = shortage).
14. *He is **senior to** me **by** two years.* — **No error**.
15. ✘ She **lended** me her book. ✔ She **lent** me her book.
:::

::: note
Items 13 and 14 above are **correct** sentences: exam papers deliberately include sentences that look wrong but are right. Always check against a rule before deciding.
:::

## 35.12 Pin Point Errors in the SAT, GRE and GMAT

::: gre
- **SAT (Writing & Language / earlier "Identifying Sentence Errors"):** the most frequent errors are subject–verb agreement, pronoun agreement and case, verb tense, idiom, comparison, parallelism, adjective/adverb confusion, and redundancy. About one in five old-SAT items was "No error".
- **GMAT Sentence Correction** is a "pin point error" test in reverse: the error is somewhere in the underlined part, and you must pick the version that removes it **without introducing a new error or changing the meaning**. Learn to **"split"** answer choices: compare the first words of each choice (*is/are*, *which/that*, *like/as*) to eliminate several at once.
- **GRE** has no grammar section, but recognising grammatical structure helps parse complex Reading Comprehension sentences.
:::
