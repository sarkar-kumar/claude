[Part A · Grammar]{.chpart}

## 1.1 What Is a Part of Speech?

Every word in an English sentence does a particular **job**. Some words name things, some describe them, some show action, some join other words, and so on. The **part of speech** of a word is simply the name of the job that the word is doing **in that sentence**.

Traditional grammar recognises **eight parts of speech**:

| No. | Part of speech | Job it does | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Noun | names a person, place, thing, idea | Rahim, Dhaka, book, honesty |
| 2 | Pronoun | stands in place of a noun | he, she, it, they, who, this |
| 3 | Adjective | describes / qualifies a noun or pronoun | good, tall, five, some, Bangladeshi |
| 4 | Verb | shows action or state | run, write, is, seem, become |
| 5 | Adverb | modifies a verb, adjective or another adverb | quickly, very, often, here |
| 6 | Preposition | shows relation of a noun/pronoun to another word | in, on, at, by, with, of |
| 7 | Conjunction | joins words, phrases or clauses | and, but, or, because, although |
| 8 | Interjection | expresses sudden feeling | Alas! Hurrah! Oh! Bravo! |

Modern grammars often add a ninth class, the **determiner** (a, an, the, this, my, some, every, each), which earlier grammars put under adjectives. Many Bangladeshi exam papers still follow the eight-part system and call *a, an, the* "articles" and *this, my, some* "adjectives". Know both systems (see Chapters 04 and 05).

::: rule
**The golden rule:** *A word's part of speech depends on its function in the sentence, not on the word itself.* The same word can be a noun in one sentence, a verb in another and an adjective in a third.
:::

::: ex
- He went **round** the field. (preposition)
- The earth is **round**. (adjective)
- The doctor is on his **round**. (noun)
- The ship **rounded** the cape. (verb)
- Winter comes **round** again. (adverb)
:::

This is why exam questions say, *"Identify the part of speech of the underlined word"* — you must look at what the word is **doing**, not merely at what it looks like. Chapter 42 gives a long list of such words.

## 1.2 Content Words and Function Words

It helps to divide the parts of speech into two broad groups:

- **Content (lexical / open-class) words** carry the main meaning: nouns, main verbs, adjectives and adverbs. New words of these types enter the language all the time (*selfie, google, upload*).
- **Function (grammatical / closed-class) words** hold the sentence together: pronouns, prepositions, conjunctions, determiners/articles and auxiliary verbs. Their number is fixed; English rarely gets a new preposition.

In a telegram or a newspaper headline we drop the function words and keep the content words: *"Minister visits flood area"* = *The Minister visits the flood area.*

## 1.3 The Noun

A **noun** is a word used as the name of a person, place, thing, animal, quality, action, idea or feeling.

::: ex
- **Person:** Nazrul, teacher, farmer, sister
- **Place:** Sylhet, village, school, Asia
- **Thing / animal:** pen, river, tiger, computer
- **Quality / idea:** honesty, beauty, freedom, wisdom
- **Action / state:** movement, arrival, childhood, sleep
:::

### 1.3.1 How to Identify a Noun

1. **It can follow an article or determiner:** *a* **book**, *the* **river**, *this* **idea**, *my* **sister**.
2. **It can be made plural or possessive:** *book → books; boy → boy's.*
3. **It acts as subject, object or complement:** **Rahim** plays. I saw **Rahim**. He is a **doctor**.
4. **It can follow a preposition:** in **Dhaka**, of **gold**, with **care**.
5. **Typical noun suffixes:** -tion (*action*), -sion (*decision*), -ment (*movement*), -ness (*kindness*), -ity (*ability*), -hood (*childhood*), -ship (*friendship*), -dom (*freedom*), -ance/-ence (*importance, silence*), -er/-or (*teacher, actor*), -ist (*artist*), -ism (*patriotism*), -age (*marriage*), -th (*growth*), -cy (*accuracy*).

::: note
**Gerunds are nouns too.** A verb + *-ing* used as a noun is called a *gerund*: **Swimming** is good exercise. I like **reading**. (Chapter 14.) A whole clause can also work as a noun: **What he said** is true. (noun clause, Chapter 31.)
:::

## 1.4 The Pronoun

A **pronoun** is a word used **instead of a noun** to avoid repetition.

::: ex
- Rahim is a good boy. **He** is honest. (*he* = Rahim)
- The book is on the table. Give **it** to me.
- **Who** is knocking at the door?
- **This** is my pen. (demonstrative pronoun — it stands alone)
:::

**Identification:** a pronoun **stands alone** in place of a noun. If the same word stands *before* a noun, it is not a pronoun but an adjective/determiner:

::: ex
- **This** is my house. → pronoun (stands alone)
- **This** house is mine. → adjective/determiner (qualifies *house*)
- **Which** do you want? → pronoun
- **Which** book do you want? → adjective
- **Some** of the students were absent. → pronoun
- **Some** students were absent. → adjective
:::

## 1.5 The Adjective

An **adjective** describes, qualifies or limits a noun or pronoun. It answers *What kind? Which one? How many? How much?*

::: ex
- She is a **clever** girl. (what kind?)
- I bought **five** mangoes. (how many?)
- He has **little** money. (how much?)
- **That** boy is my cousin. (which one?)
- The sky is **blue**. (adjective after a linking verb — *predicative* use)
:::

### 1.5.1 Attributive and Predicative Adjectives

- **Attributive:** stands before the noun — *a* **red** *rose*, *a* **tall** *man*.
- **Predicative:** comes after a linking verb (be, seem, look, become, feel, grow, appear, taste, smell, sound) — *The rose is* **red**. *He looks* **tired**. *The milk tastes* **sour**.

Some adjectives are only predicative: *afraid, alive, alone, asleep, awake, aware, ashamed, ablaze, alike, content, unable.* ✘ *an asleep child* ✔ *a sleeping child* / *The child is asleep.*

Some are only attributive: *main, chief, principal, mere, sheer, utter, very (= exact), former, latter, elder, eldest, inner, outer, upper.* ✘ *The reason is main.* ✔ *The main reason is…*

### 1.5.2 How to Identify an Adjective

1. It can come between *a/the* and a noun: *the* **old** *house*.
2. It can follow *very / too / so / quite*: **very** *old*. (Not always: *very main* is wrong.)
3. It has comparative/superlative forms: *old – older – oldest; beautiful – more beautiful – most beautiful.*
4. **Typical adjective suffixes:** -ful (*useful*), -less (*careless*), -ous (*famous*), -ive (*active*), -able/-ible (*readable, visible*), -al (*national*), -ic (*historic*), -ish (*childish*), -y (*healthy*), -ant/-ent (*important, different*), -ary (*necessary*), -some (*handsome*), -like (*childlike*), -ly (*friendly, lovely, manly*).

::: tip
**Exam Tip — *-ly* is not always an adverb.** *Friendly, lovely, lonely, ugly, silly, costly, manly, womanly, brotherly, elderly, cowardly, timely, deadly, likely, orderly, holy, kindly, daily, weekly, early* can be adjectives. *She is a* **lovely** *girl* (adj). *He gave a* **friendly** *smile* (adj). Since these have no adverb form in -lyly, we say *in a friendly way / manner*: ✘ *He spoke friendly.* ✔ *He spoke in a friendly manner.*
:::

## 1.6 The Verb

A **verb** is a word that says something about the subject — what it **does**, what **happens** to it, or what it **is**.

::: ex
- The baby **cries**. (action)
- The glass **was broken**. (something happens to the subject)
- She **is** a doctor. (state of being)
- He **seems** happy. (linking)
- They **have** a car. (possession)
:::

Every complete sentence (clause) must contain a **finite verb** — a verb that shows tense and agrees with its subject (*goes, went, is, was, has gone*). Infinitives (*to go*), gerunds (*going*) and participles (*going, gone*) are **non-finite** and cannot make a sentence on their own: ✘ *He going to school.* ✔ *He is going to school.*

### 1.6.1 Kinds of Verbs at a Glance (detailed in Chapter 11)

- **Main (principal / lexical) verbs:** *eat, write, think, run.*
- **Auxiliary (helping) verbs:** *be, have, do* (primary) and *can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must, ought to, need, dare, used to* (modal).
- **Transitive verbs** take an object: *He* **wrote** *a letter.*
- **Intransitive verbs** do not take an object: *The baby* **slept**.
- **Linking (copular) verbs:** *be, become, seem, appear, look, feel, grow, turn, remain, taste, smell, sound.*

### 1.6.2 How to Identify a Verb

1. It changes with tense: *walk – walked – walking.*
2. It can follow a subject pronoun: *I* **go**, *they* **sing**.
3. It can follow a modal or *to*: *can* **swim**, *to* **swim**.
4. **Typical verb suffixes:** -ise/-ize (*realise*), -ify (*simplify*), -en (*widen, strengthen*), -ate (*activate*); prefixes en-/em- (*enrich, empower, enlarge, endanger*), be- (*befriend, belittle*).

## 1.7 The Adverb

An **adverb** modifies (adds to the meaning of) a **verb**, an **adjective**, another **adverb**, a **preposition**, a **conjunction** or a **whole sentence**. It tells *how, when, where, how often, how much* or *to what degree*.

::: ex
- He runs **fast**. (modifies verb — how?)
- She is **very** beautiful. (modifies adjective)
- He runs **too** fast. (modifies adverb)
- The bird flew **right** over the house. (modifies preposition)
- I came **simply** because you called me. (modifies conjunction)
- **Fortunately**, nobody was hurt. (modifies whole sentence)
:::

### 1.7.1 How to Identify an Adverb

1. It answers *how? when? where? how often? to what extent?*
2. It is often movable within the sentence: **Suddenly** the light went out. / The light **suddenly** went out. / The light went out **suddenly**.
3. Many adverbs = adjective + *-ly*: *quick → quickly, happy → happily, careful → carefully.*
4. Many common adverbs have no *-ly*: *fast, hard, late, early, well, soon, now, then, here, there, often, never, always, very, too, quite, almost, still, yet, already, again.*

::: note
**Hard / hardly, late / lately, near / nearly, high / highly** have different meanings. *He works* **hard** (= with effort). *He* **hardly** *works* (= almost never). *He came* **late**. *I haven't seen him* **lately** (= recently). See Chapter 15.
:::

## 1.8 The Preposition

A **preposition** is a word placed **before a noun or pronoun** to show its relation to some other word in the sentence. The noun/pronoun after it is called its **object**.

::: ex
- The book is **on** the table. (place)
- He came **at** 10 o'clock. (time)
- She went **to** school **by** bus. (direction, means)
- This gift is **for** you. (purpose)
- He is fond **of** music. (fixed combination)
:::

**Identification:** a preposition must have an **object**. If the same word has no object, it is usually an **adverb**:

::: ex
- He walked **down** the hill. (preposition — object: *the hill*)
- Please sit **down**. (adverb — no object)
- He is **in** the room. (preposition) / Come **in**. (adverb)
- I have seen him **before**. (adverb) / I came **before** him. (preposition) / I came **before** he did. (conjunction)
:::

Prepositions may be simple (*in, on, at, by, with*), compound (*inside, within, without, beneath, across*), double (*from behind, from under, out of*), participial (*concerning, regarding, considering, during, pending*) or phrasal (*in spite of, on account of, because of, in front of, with regard to, by means of*). See Chapters 17 and 18.

## 1.9 The Conjunction

A **conjunction** joins words, phrases, clauses or sentences.

::: ex
- Rahim **and** Karim are friends. (joins words)
- He is poor **but** honest.
- Hurry up, **or** you will miss the train.
- I stayed at home **because** it was raining.
- **Although** he is rich, he is not happy.
- **Either** you **or** he must go. (correlative conjunction)
:::

**Coordinating conjunctions** join equal parts: the seven **FANBOYS** — *For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So*. **Subordinating conjunctions** join a dependent clause to a main clause: *because, since, as, although, though, if, unless, when, while, until, after, before, that, so that, lest.* **Correlatives** come in pairs: *either…or, neither…nor, both…and, not only…but also, whether…or, no sooner…than, hardly…when, scarcely…when.*

**Identification:** a conjunction joins; it does not govern a noun as an object. Compare:

::: ex
- He came **after** dinner. → preposition (object: dinner)
- He came **after** we had finished dinner. → conjunction (joins two clauses)
- He came **after**. → adverb
:::

## 1.10 The Interjection

An **interjection** is a word or phrase that expresses a sudden feeling or emotion — joy, sorrow, surprise, pain, approval — and has no grammatical connection with the rest of the sentence. It is usually followed by an exclamation mark.

::: ex
- **Hurrah!** We have won the match. (joy)
- **Alas!** He is no more. (sorrow)
- **Oh!** What a surprise! (surprise)
- **Ouch!** That hurts. (pain)
- **Bravo!** Well played. (approval)
- **Hush!** The baby is sleeping. (call for silence)
- **Fie!** You should be ashamed. (disgust)
:::

## 1.11 Step-by-Step Method for Identifying the Part of Speech

When an exam asks for the part of speech of an underlined word, follow these steps:

1. **Read the whole sentence.** Never judge from the word alone.
2. **Find the main verb and subject.** This shows you the skeleton.
3. **Ask what the word does:** Is it naming? (noun) Replacing a noun? (pronoun) Describing a noun? (adjective) Showing action/state? (verb) Telling how/when/where/how much? (adverb) Linking a noun to another word? (preposition) Joining clauses? (conjunction) Exclaiming? (interjection)
4. **Check its neighbours.** After *a/the/my* → probably noun (or adjective + noun). After a modal or *to* → verb. Before a noun → adjective/determiner. Before a noun phrase showing relation → preposition. Before a clause (subject + verb) → conjunction.
5. **Check whether it can be replaced.** Replace it with a typical member of the class: *He ran* **fast** → *He ran* **quickly** (adverb ✔). *It is a* **fast** *train* → *It is a* **quick** *train* (adjective ✔).

## 1.13 *As* — A Word with Many Jobs

::: ex
- **As** he was ill, he could not come. (conjunction — reason)
- He works **as** a clerk. (preposition — in the role of)
- He is **as** tall as his brother. (first *as* = adverb, second *as* = conjunction)
- Such men **as** are honest are respected. (relative pronoun)
- **As** you sow, so you reap. (conjunction — manner)
:::

## 1.14 *What* — Pronoun, Adjective, Interjection

::: ex
- **What** do you want? (interrogative pronoun)
- **What** book is this? (interrogative adjective)
- **What!** You failed again? (interjection)
- I know **what** you mean. (relative pronoun = that which)
- **What** a beautiful scene! (exclamatory adjective)
:::

## 1.15 Phrases and Clauses Doing the Job of a Part of Speech

A group of words can also do the job of a single part of speech:

| Group | Example | Works as |
|---|---|---|
| Noun phrase | **To err** is human. / I like **playing chess**. | Noun |
| Adjective phrase | A man **of wealth** came. (= a wealthy man) | Adjective |
| Adverb phrase | He spoke **in a loud voice**. (= loudly) | Adverb |
| Noun clause | **That he is honest** is known to all. | Noun |
| Adjective clause | The man **who came here** is my uncle. | Adjective |
| Adverb clause | I will go **if you come**. | Adverb |
| Prepositional phrase | **In spite of** his illness, he came. | Preposition |
| Conjunctional phrase | He worked hard **so that** he might pass. | Conjunction |

These are treated in detail in Chapters 30 and 31.

## 1.16 Common Errors Linked to Wrong Parts of Speech

::: errs
1. ✘ He sings very **good**. ✔ He sings very **well**. (*good* is an adjective; a verb needs an adverb.)
2. ✘ She looks **beautifully**. ✔ She looks **beautiful**. (*look* here is a linking verb, so it takes an adjective.)
3. ✘ The rose smells **sweetly**. ✔ The rose smells **sweet**.
4. ✘ He is a **foolish** fellow and behaves **foolish**. ✔ …behaves **foolishly**.
5. ✘ This is a **real** good book. ✔ This is a **really** good book. (adverb needed to modify the adjective *good*)
6. ✘ He gave me an **advise**. ✔ He gave me a piece of **advice**. (*advice* = noun; *advise* = verb)
7. ✘ I **practice** football every day (BrE). ✔ I **practise** football every day. (BrE: *practice* = noun, *practise* = verb.)
8. ✘ He is **sick** of fever. ✔ He is **suffering from** fever. / He is **ill** with fever.
9. ✘ The **beautify** of the scene charmed us. ✔ The **beauty** of the scene charmed us.
10. ✘ He **succeed** in life through hard work. ✔ He **succeeded** … / Hard work brings **success**. / He is **successful**.
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Word Form Errors:** The SAT Writing & Language section and GMAT Sentence Correction often test the "wrong word form": an adjective where an adverb is needed (*The committee acted* **quick** → **quickly**), a noun where a verb is needed, or an *-ing* form where a finite verb is needed (*The scientists* **having discovered** *a new species* → **have discovered**). Train your eye to ask: *What job is this word doing, and is it the right form for that job?*
:::

## 1.17 Practice Identification (Worked Examples)

Study the reasoning in each example.

::: ex
- "He is a **hard** worker." — describes the noun *worker* → **adjective**.
- "He works **hard**." — tells *how* he works → **adverb**.
- "The **poor** are often neglected." — *the* + adjective used as a plural noun (= poor people) → **noun**.
- "Better **late** than never." — tells *when* → **adverb**.
- "He **bettered** his record." → **verb**; "Respect your **betters**." → **noun**.
- "Every **dog** has his day." → **noun**; "The police **dogged** him." (followed closely) → **verb**.
- "I have **no** money." → **adjective/determiner**; "He is **no** better today." → **adverb**; "Answer yes or **no**." → **noun**.
- "**Only** he passed." → **adverb** (focusing adverb); "He is the **only** son." → **adjective**; "You may go, **only** come back early." → **conjunction** (= but).
- "He has gone **out**." → **adverb**; "He jumped **out of** the window." → **preposition** (double preposition).
- "The plane flew **above** the clouds." → **preposition**; "the sky **above**" → **adverb**; "the **above** statement" → **adjective**; "Read **the above** carefully." → **noun**.
:::
