# Uncountable Problems {#ch03}

[Part A · Grammar]{.chpart}
:::

## 3.1 Why "Uncountable Problems"?

Learners in Bangladesh make the same mistakes with a group of English nouns year after year: *informations, furnitures, an advice, sceneries, many luggages, a good news, poetries*. These errors arise because in Bangla we can count almost anything (*একটি উপদেশ, অনেক তথ্য*), but English treats certain nouns as **uncountable** — they cannot be counted with numbers, cannot take *a/an*, cannot be made plural, and take a **singular verb**. Examiners know this weakness well; therefore questions on these nouns appear in almost every BCS Preliminary, Bank and admission paper, usually as *error detection*, *correction* or *fill in the gaps*.

This chapter lists those "problem" nouns, explains the rules that govern them, and shows the correct ways to express quantity with them.

## 3.2 The Five Golden Rules of Uncountable Nouns

::: rule
**Rule 1 — No *a/an*:** ✘ *an information*, ✘ *an advice*, ✘ *a furniture*.

**Rule 2 — No plural form:** ✘ *informations*, ✘ *advices*, ✘ *furnitures*, ✘ *sceneries*.

**Rule 3 — Singular verb and singular pronoun:** *The information* **is** *correct; I will check* **it**.

**Rule 4 — Use uncountable quantifiers:** *much, little, a little, a great deal of, a large amount of, less, least* — not *many, few, a few, a number of, several, fewer.*

**Rule 5 — To count, use a "unit" (partitive) word:** *a piece of advice, two items of news, an article of furniture.*
:::

## 3.3 The Master List of Problem Uncountable Nouns

Learn this list thoroughly. Each of these nouns is **uncountable in normal English**.

| Noun | ✘ Wrong | ✔ Right |
|---|---|---|
| **advice** | an advice, advices | some advice, a piece of advice, a word of advice |
| **information** | an information, informations | some information, a piece/bit of information |
| **furniture** | a furniture, furnitures | some furniture, a piece/an article of furniture |
| **luggage** | a luggage, luggages | some luggage, a piece of luggage, three items of luggage |
| **baggage** | baggages | a piece of baggage |
| **equipment** | equipments | a piece of equipment, some equipment |
| **machinery** | machineries | a piece of machinery, machines |
| **scenery** | sceneries, a scenery | the scenery, a beautiful scene / view |
| **poetry** | poetries, a poetry | a poem, a piece of poetry, poems |
| **news** | a news, the news are | a piece/an item of news, the news is |
| **knowledge** | knowledges | some knowledge, *a good knowledge of* (accepted idiom) |
| **work** (labour) | works (in this sense) | some work, a piece of work, a job |
| **homework / housework** | homeworks | some homework, a homework assignment |
| **bread** | a bread, breads | a loaf / slice / piece of bread |
| **money** | moneys (except in legal/financial "moneys/monies") | some money, a sum of money, a coin, a note |
| **traffic** | traffics | heavy traffic, a traffic jam |
| **weather** | a fine weather | fine weather, a spell of fine weather |
| **evidence** | evidences, an evidence | a piece of evidence, some evidence |
| **research** | researches (avoid in exams) | some research, a piece of research, studies |
| **progress** | progresses, a progress | much progress, some progress |
| **damage** (harm) | damages (= harm) | much damage; *damages* = money paid as compensation |
| **mischief** | mischiefs | a piece of mischief, much mischief |
| **jewellery** (AmE jewelry) | jewelleries | a piece of jewellery, jewels |
| **stationery** | stationeries | some stationery, items of stationery |
| **hair** (on head) | hairs (in this sense) | her hair is long; *a hair / two hairs* = individual strands |
| **crockery / cutlery** | crockeries | pieces of crockery |
| **clothing** | clothings | an item/article of clothing |
| **software / hardware** | softwares | a piece of software, software programs |
| **feedback** | feedbacks | some feedback |
| **wildlife** | wildlives | — |
| **behaviour** | behaviours (in general sense) | his behaviour is good |
| **health / wealth** | healths | good health |
| **fun** | a fun | great fun, a lot of fun |
| **accommodation** (BrE) | accommodations | some accommodation, a place to stay |
| **slang / vocabulary** | slangs | some slang, a slang word; a large vocabulary (countable sense) |
| **applause / laughter** | applauses | a round of applause, a burst of laughter |
| **litter / rubbish / garbage** | rubbishes | a piece of litter |
| **paper** (material) | papers (material) | a sheet of paper; *papers* = documents/newspapers |
| **dust / sand / grass / rice** | sands (normally), rices | a grain of sand, a blade of grass, a grain of rice |
| **lightning / thunder** | a lightning | a flash of lightning, a clap of thunder |
| **produce** (farm products) | produces | fresh produce |
| **percentage / data** | — | see notes below |

::: tip
**Exam Tip — the most tested eight:** *advice, information, furniture, luggage, scenery, poetry, news, machinery.* If any of these appears with *a/an*, with an *-s* ending, with *many/few*, or with a plural verb, the sentence is almost certainly wrong.
:::

## 3.4 Rules Illustrated with Examples

### 3.4.1 Advice

::: ex
- ✘ He gave me **an advice**. ✔ He gave me **a piece of advice**.
- ✘ Your **advices are** valuable. ✔ Your **advice is** valuable.
- ✘ I need **many advices**. ✔ I need **a lot of advice**.
- **Advise** (verb, pronounced with *z*) vs **advice** (noun, pronounced with *s*): *I* **advise** *you to work hard. — That is good* **advice**.
:::

### 3.4.2 Information

::: ex
- ✘ I have **an important information**. ✔ I have **an important piece of information** / **some important information**.
- ✘ **These informations are** wrong. ✔ **This information is** wrong.
- ✘ He gave **few informations**. ✔ He gave **little information**.
:::

### 3.4.3 Furniture

::: ex
- ✘ The room is full of **furnitures**. ✔ The room is full of **furniture**.
- ✘ He bought **a new furniture**. ✔ He bought **a new piece of furniture** (a table, a sofa).
- ✘ **The furniture were** old. ✔ **The furniture was** old.
:::

### 3.4.4 Luggage / Baggage

::: ex
- ✘ How many **luggages** do you have? ✔ How many **pieces of luggage** / **bags** do you have?
- ✘ My **luggages have** been lost. ✔ My **luggage has** been lost.
:::

### 3.4.5 Scenery

*Scenery* means the general appearance of the natural features of a place. It is uncountable. A single view is a **scene**.

::: ex
- ✘ The **sceneries** of Rangamati **are** beautiful. ✔ The **scenery** of Rangamati **is** beautiful.
- ✘ I saw **a beautiful scenery**. ✔ I saw **a beautiful scene / view**.
- ✔ The **scenes** of the film were shot in Bandarban. (*scene* is countable)
:::

### 3.4.6 Poetry and Poem

*Poetry* = poems in general, the art (uncountable). *Poem* = one composition (countable).

::: ex
- ✘ He has written **many poetries**. ✔ He has written **many poems** / **a lot of poetry**.
- ✘ Nazrul wrote **a poetry** named *Bidrohi*. ✔ Nazrul wrote **a poem** named *Bidrohi*.
- Similar pairs: *machinery / machine, scenery / scene, jewellery / jewel, clothing / garment, laughter / laugh, vocabulary (the stock) / word.*
:::

### 3.4.7 News

*News* looks plural (it ends in *-s*), but it is **uncountable and singular**.

::: ex
- ✘ **The news are** very good. ✔ **The news is** very good.
- ✘ I heard **a sad news**. ✔ I heard **a sad piece of news** / **some sad news**.
- ✘ **These news have** shocked us. ✔ **This news has** shocked us.
- ✔ Here **is** the news. (BBC style)
:::

### 3.4.8 Work vs Works

- *Work* (= labour, employment) is uncountable: *I have* **a lot of work** *today. He is looking for* **work** (✘ *a work*). Say *a job* for one position.
- *Works* (countable plural) has other meanings: (a) creations of art/literature: *the* **works** *of Shakespeare*; (b) a factory: *a gas* **works**, *steel* **works** (singular or plural in sense); (c) building or engineering projects: *public* **works**, *road* **works**.

::: ex
- ✘ I have **many works** to do. ✔ I have **a lot of work** / **many tasks** to do.
- ✔ *Gitanjali* is one of the best-known **works** of Tagore.
:::

### 3.4.9 Hair

::: ex
- ✘ Her **hairs are** black. ✔ Her **hair is** black. (hair on the head as a mass)
- ✔ I found **two grey hairs** on my head. (individual strands — countable)
- ✔ There is **a hair** in my soup.
:::

### 3.4.10 Damage vs Damages

::: ex
- ✔ The flood caused **much damage** to the crops. (✘ many damages)
- ✔ The court ordered him to pay **damages** of Tk 5 lakh. (*damages* = compensation money)
:::

### 3.4.11 Knowledge

*Knowledge* is uncountable, but the idiom **"a good / sound / thorough knowledge of"** is accepted: *He has* **a good knowledge of** *English.* ✘ *He has* **knowledges** *in English.* ✘ *He has* **a knowledge in** *English.*

### 3.4.12 Bread

::: ex
- ✘ I ate **two breads**. ✔ I ate **two slices of bread** / **two loaves of bread**.
- ✔ **Bread is** made from flour.
:::

### 3.4.13 Money and Its Partners

*Money, cash, change* are uncountable, but *taka, dollar, coin, note, rupee, pound* are countable.

::: ex
- ✘ How **many money** do you have? ✔ How **much money** do you have?
- ✔ I have **five hundred taka**. (*taka* has the same form in singular and plural.)
- ✘ Have you got **a change**? ✔ Have you got **any change**?
:::

## 3.5 Partitives — Expressing Quantity with Uncountable Nouns

A **partitive** (unit noun) lets you count a part of an uncountable mass. Learn these collocations — they also appear in vocabulary questions.

| Partitive | Used with |
|---|---|
| a **piece of** | advice, information, news, furniture, luggage, equipment, cake, paper, chalk, land, music, evidence, work, jewellery |
| an **item / article of** | news, furniture, clothing, luggage |
| a **bit of** | information, advice, luck, trouble, fun |
| a **loaf / slice / piece of** | bread |
| a **bar of** | soap, chocolate, gold |
| a **cake of** | soap |
| a **lump of** | sugar, coal, clay |
| a **cube of** | sugar, ice |
| a **grain of** | rice, sand, salt, wheat, truth |
| a **blade of** | grass |
| a **sheet / ream of** | paper (ream = 500 sheets) |
| a **drop of** | water, rain, oil, blood |
| a **cup / glass / bottle of** | tea, water, milk, juice |
| a **spoonful of** | sugar, medicine |
| a **pinch of** | salt, snuff |
| a **sack / bag of** | rice, flour, cement |
| a **tube of** | toothpaste |
| a **jar of** | jam, honey |
| a **pane of** | glass |
| a **plot of** | land |
| a **stroke of** | luck, genius, bad luck |
| a **flash of** | lightning, inspiration |
| a **clap / peal of** | thunder |
| a **gust / breath of** | wind, fresh air |
| a **fit of** | anger, laughter, coughing |
| a **burst of** | applause, laughter, gunfire |
| a **round of** | applause, talks, drinks |
| a **spell of** | fine weather, rain, bad luck |
| a **state of** | confusion, war, emergency |
| a **means of** | transport, livelihood |
| a **word of** | advice, warning, praise |
| a **pair of** | trousers, scissors, spectacles (see Chapter 06) |

## 3.6 Nouns That Are Countable in One Sense and Uncountable in Another

Many nouns change their meaning when they change their countability. These are favourites of the SAT, GRE and GMAT as well.

| Noun | Uncountable meaning | Countable meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **paper** | material: *Write on* **paper**. | newspaper / document / essay: *I bought* **a paper**. *He presented* **a paper** *at the seminar.* |
| **glass** | material: *The window is made of* **glass**. | a drinking vessel: *a* **glass** *of water*; **glasses** = spectacles |
| **iron** | metal: *Iron is useful.* | an instrument for pressing clothes: *an* **iron** |
| **light** | brightness: *Light travels fast.* | a lamp: *Turn off* **the lights**. |
| **experience** | knowledge gained over time: *He has five years'* **experience**. | an event: *It was* **a strange experience**. |
| **time** | the abstract idea: *Time is money.* | an occasion: *I have been there* **three times**. *We had* **a good time**. |
| **room** | space: *Is there* **room** *for me?* | a part of a house: *a big* **room** |
| **business** | trade in general: *Business is good.* | a company: *He runs* **a small business**. |
| **work** | labour, job | a creation: *the* **works** *of Nazrul* |
| **hair** | all the hair on the head | a single strand: *a* **hair** |
| **chicken / fish / lamb** | meat: *I like* **chicken**. | the animal: *three* **chickens**; *two* **fishes** (different species) / *two fish* |
| **coffee / tea / beer** | the drink: *I like* **coffee**. | a cup/serving: *Two* **coffees**, *please*. (informal) |
| **cake** | the food: *Have some* **cake**. | a whole one: *She baked* **a cake**. |
| **stone** | material: *a house of* **stone** | a piece: *He threw* **a stone**. |
| **language** | the human ability: *Language is a means of communication.* | a particular one: *Bangla is* **a sweet language**. |
| **noise** | noise in general: *Don't make* **noise**. | a particular sound: *I heard* **a strange noise**. |
| **sound** | — | always countable: *a* **sound** |
| **education** | in general: *Education is the backbone of a nation.* | *a* **good education** (accepted with an adjective) |
| **knowledge** | in general | *a good knowledge of* (idiom) |
| **success / failure** | in general | *The event was* **a great success**. (a successful event/person) |
| **youth** | the period: *in his* **youth** | a young man: *a* **youth** *of twenty*; **the youth** = young people |

## 3.7 Plural-looking Nouns That Are Singular

These nouns end in *-s* but are singular in meaning and take a singular verb:

- **News** — *The news is good.*
- **Subjects of study:** *mathematics, physics, economics, statistics, politics, ethics, linguistics, phonetics, civics, electronics* — *Physics* **is** *my favourite subject.* (But when they mean *qualities / activities / figures*, they can be plural: *His* **politics are** *questionable. The* **statistics show** *a rise in crime.*)
- **Diseases:** *measles, mumps, rickets, diabetes, rabies, herpes* — *Measles* **is** *infectious.*
- **Games:** *billiards, darts, draughts, dominoes, cards* (when referring to the game) — *Billiards* **is** *an indoor game.*
- **Names of countries/books:** *the United States* **is** *a big country; "Gulliver's Travels"* **is** *a satire; the Netherlands* **is** *in Europe; the Philippines* **is** *an archipelago.*
- **Means / series / species / headquarters / crossroads / innings** — same form singular and plural: *a* **means** *of transport / many* **means**; *a* **series** *of lectures / two* **series**; *this* **species** *is rare / these* **species** *are rare.*

## 3.8 Singular-looking Nouns That Are Always Plural

These have no *-s* but are always plural and take a plural verb:

- **Cattle, people (= persons), police, poultry, vermin, gentry, clergy, peasantry, livestock (usually singular), folk** — *The* **cattle are** *grazing. The* **police have** *come. The* **poultry are** *fed twice a day.*
- ✘ *a cattle* ✔ *a cow, a head of cattle*; ✘ *a police* ✔ *a policeman / police officer*; ✘ *a poultry* ✔ *a hen*.
- *People* (= persons) is plural: *Many* **people were** *present.* (But *a people* = a nation is countable: *The Bengalis are* **a brave people**. *The* **peoples** *of Asia.*)

## 3.9 Nouns Always Plural in Form and Meaning

- **Tools and garments with two parts:** *scissors, tongs, pliers, spectacles, glasses, binoculars, trousers, pants, jeans, pyjamas, shorts, tights* — *My* **trousers are** *new.* Use *a pair of* to make them singular: *A pair of scissors* **is** *on the table.*
- Others: *alms, thanks, riches, wages* (often), *premises, proceeds, outskirts, surroundings, belongings, savings, goods, arrears, archives, annals, congratulations, credentials, earnings, lodgings, odds, remains, stairs, contents, valuables, environs, amends, assets*.

::: ex
- **Thanks are** due to the organisers.
- His **riches have** not made him happy.
- The **premises are** under CCTV surveillance.
- The **proceeds** of the concert **were** donated to flood victims.
- **Alms are** given to the poor.
:::

## 3.10 Data, Media, Criteria — Latin and Greek Plurals

These are plural forms borrowed from Latin/Greek. Formal English (and exam keys) usually treat them as plural:

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| datum | **data** |
| medium | **media** |
| criterion | **criteria** |
| phenomenon | **phenomena** |
| bacterium | **bacteria** |
| curriculum | **curricula** (or curriculums) |
| memorandum | **memoranda** |
| stratum | **strata** |

::: ex
- The **data are** reliable. (formal; *data is* is common in modern usage)
- The **criteria are** clear. ✘ *a criteria*
- This **phenomenon is** strange. These **phenomena are** strange.
- The **media have** a great influence on society.
:::

## 3.11 Using *the* with Uncountable Nouns

- No article when the noun is used **in a general sense**: *Honesty is the best policy. Milk is good for health. Gold is costly.*
- *The* is used when the noun is **particular / specified**: *The milk in this jug is sour. The honesty of the clerk impressed us. The information you gave me was useful.*

::: errs
1. ✘ **The honesty** is the best policy. ✔ **Honesty** is the best policy.
2. ✘ **The water** is necessary for life. ✔ **Water** is necessary for life.
3. ✘ **Gold** of this ring is pure. ✔ **The gold** of this ring is pure.
:::

## 3.12 Quantifiers with Countable and Uncountable Nouns

| Countable only | Uncountable only | Both |
|---|---|---|
| many, few, a few, fewer, fewest, several, a number of, a couple of, each, every, either, neither, one, two… | much, little, a little, less, least, a great deal of, a large amount of, a large quantity of | some, any, no, a lot of, lots of, plenty of, enough, all, most, more |

::: ex
- **Few** students / **little** water / **fewer** mistakes / **less** sugar
- **A number of** books / **an amount of** money
- **Many** chairs / **much** furniture
:::

::: note
**Less vs Fewer.** Strict usage: *fewer* with plural countables, *less* with uncountables — *fewer cars, less traffic*. But *less* is accepted with amounts of time, money and distance treated as a single quantity: *less than 10 kilometres, less than 500 taka, less than two hours*; and in *one less*.
:::

## 3.13 Common Errors — Consolidated Practice

::: errs
1. ✘ The **furnitures** of this room **are** costly. ✔ The **furniture** of this room **is** costly.
2. ✘ He gave me **many advices**. ✔ He gave me **a lot of advice**.
3. ✘ I have **an urgent work**. ✔ I have **some urgent work** / **an urgent job**.
4. ✘ **A large number of** money was stolen. ✔ **A large amount of** money was stolen.
5. ✘ **The sceneries** of Sylhet **attract** tourists. ✔ **The scenery** of Sylhet **attracts** tourists.
6. ✘ He has **little friends**. ✔ He has **few friends**.
7. ✘ There were **less** people at the meeting. ✔ There were **fewer** people at the meeting.
8. ✘ **The cattle is** grazing. ✔ **The cattle are** grazing.
9. ✘ Give me **an information** about the train. ✔ Give me **some information** about the train.
10. ✘ **Mathematics are** difficult. ✔ **Mathematics is** difficult.
11. ✘ I bought **a new trouser**. ✔ I bought **a new pair of trousers** / **new trousers**.
12. ✘ **The scissor is** blunt. ✔ **The scissors are** blunt.
13. ✘ He has **many luggages**. ✔ He has **much luggage** / **many bags**.
14. ✘ We need **some equipments**. ✔ We need **some equipment**.
15. ✘ **His hairs are** grey. ✔ **His hair is** grey.
16. ✘ The flood caused **many damages**. ✔ The flood caused **much damage**.
17. ✘ He has **a good command over** English. ✔ He has **a good command of** English.
18. ✘ **Poultries are** kept in this farm. ✔ **Poultry are** kept on this farm.
19. ✘ He told me **a news**. ✔ He told me **a piece of news**.
20. ✘ I have **no much** time. ✔ I do not have **much** time. / I have **little** time.
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner:** GMAT Sentence Correction frequently pairs countable nouns with the wrong quantifier: ✘ *The* **amount** *of* **students** *enrolled has risen.* ✔ *The* **number** *of students…* ✘ *Less* **cars** ✔ *Fewer cars.* Note also: ✔ *"The number of applicants* **is** *rising"* (singular — *the number*), but ✔ *"A number of applicants* **are** *waiting"* (plural — *a number of* = many).
:::
