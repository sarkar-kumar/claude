# Interjection {#ch20}

[Part A · Grammar]{.chpart}

## 20.1 Definition

An **interjection** is a word or phrase that expresses a **sudden feeling or emotion** — joy, sorrow, surprise, pain, anger, approval, disgust, fear, attention, greeting. It is "thrown in" (Latin *inter* = between + *jacere* = to throw) and has **no grammatical connection** with the rest of the sentence. If you remove it, the sentence remains complete.

::: ex
- **Hurrah!** We have won the match.
- **Alas!** He is dead.
- **Oh!** I have cut my finger.
- **Bravo!** You have done well.
:::

An interjection is usually followed by an **exclamation mark (!)**. When the feeling is mild, a **comma** may be used, and the exclamation mark is placed at the end of the sentence: *Oh, what a beautiful sight!*

## 20.2 Interjections Classified by Feeling

| Feeling | Interjections | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Joy / Delight** | Hurrah! Hurray! Hooray! Wow! Yippee! Ah! Aha! | **Hurrah!** We are on holiday. |
| **Sorrow / Grief** | Alas! Ah! Oh! Ah me! Woe! Alack! | **Alas!** Our leader is no more. |
| **Surprise / Wonder** | Oh! What! Ha! Wow! Indeed! Gosh! My goodness! Good heavens! Really! | **What!** You have failed again? |
| **Approval / Praise** | Bravo! Well done! Excellent! Splendid! Good! | **Bravo!** What a fine shot! |
| **Disapproval / Contempt / Disgust** | Fie! Pooh! Pshaw! Ugh! Yuck! Tut! Tut-tut! Shame! Boo! | **Fie!** You should be ashamed of yourself. |
| **Pain** | Ouch! Ow! Oh! Ah! | **Ouch!** That hurt. |
| **Calling / Attention** | Hello! Hallo! Hey! Ho! Lo! Look! Listen! Ahoy! | **Hey!** Come here. |
| **Silence** | Hush! Sh! Hist! | **Hush!** The baby is sleeping. |
| **Greeting / Parting** | Hi! Hello! Good morning! Goodbye! Farewell! Welcome! | **Welcome!** Come in. |
| **Relief** | Phew! Whew! At last! Thank God! | **Phew!** That was close. |
| **Doubt / Hesitation** | Hmm! Er! Um! Well! | **Hmm**, I'm not sure. |
| **Warning** | Look out! Watch out! Beware! Careful! | **Look out!** A car is coming. |
| **Regret / Apology** | Oops! Sorry! Pardon! Alas! | **Oops!** I dropped the glass. |
| **Prayer / Wish** | Amen! Alas! God bless! Would that…! O that…! | **Would that** I were young again! |

## 20.3 Interjections Formed from Other Parts of Speech

Many ordinary words and phrases become interjections when used exclamatorily:

| Part of speech | As interjection |
|---|---|
| Nouns | **Heavens!** **Goodness!** **Shame!** **Nonsense!** **Help!** **Fire!** **Silence!** **Mercy!** |
| Adjectives | **Good!** **Excellent!** **Great!** **Fine!** **Splendid!** **Strange!** **Dear me!** |
| Verbs | **Look!** **Listen!** **Hark!** **Behold!** **Hush!** **Welcome!** **Bless you!** |
| Adverbs | **Well!** **Indeed!** **Why!** **How!** **Away!** **Off!** **Back!** |
| Phrases | **Good heavens!** **My goodness!** **Dear me!** **Well done!** **For shame!** **What a pity!** **Thank goodness!** **Oh my God!** **Good gracious!** |

::: ex
- **Well!** I never expected this. (surprise)
- **Why!** It's you! (surprise)
- **Hark!** The birds are singing. (listen — literary)
- **Behold!** The sun is rising. (look — literary)
- **Help!** I'm drowning.
- **Dear me!** What a mess!
:::

::: note
**The same word can be an interjection or another part of speech.** *Well* is an adverb in *He sings* **well**, an adjective in *I am* **well**, a noun in *a deep* **well**, a verb in *Tears* **welled** *up*, and an interjection in **Well**, *what do you want?* Similarly *What!* (interjection) vs *What is your name?* (pronoun). See Chapter 42.
:::

## 20.4 *O* and *Oh*

- **O** is used before a noun in **direct address** (vocative), mostly in poetry, prayer and formal style; it is always a capital letter and is **not** followed directly by an exclamation mark: **O** *God, help us!* **O** *Wind, if Winter comes, can Spring be far behind?* (Shelley) **O** *Captain! my Captain!* (Whitman)
- **Oh** expresses emotion (surprise, pain, fear, wish); it is followed by a comma or exclamation mark: **Oh!** *How painful!* **Oh**, *I see.*

## 20.5 Interjections in Exclamatory Sentences

Interjections are closely linked to **exclamatory sentences** (Chapter 32). In transformation exercises, exclamatory sentences beginning with interjections are converted into assertive sentences by replacing the interjection with a suitable phrase:

| Interjection | Assertive replacement |
|---|---|
| **Alas!** | It is a matter of sorrow / I am sorry that / It is sad that |
| **Hurrah!** | It is a matter of joy / We are glad that |
| **Bravo!** | It is praiseworthy that / You have done well |
| **Oh!** / **Ah!** | I am surprised / It is painful that (according to context) |
| **Fie!** | It is shameful that |
| **What a…!** / **How…!** | It is a very… / It is very… |
| **Would that / O that** | I wish that |

::: ex
- **Alas!** He is dead. → *It is a matter of great sorrow that he is dead.*
- **Hurrah!** We have won the match. → *It is a matter of great joy that we have won the match.*
- **Bravo!** You have done well. → *You have done very well, and it is praiseworthy.*
- **What a beautiful scene it is!** → *It is a very beautiful scene.*
- **How sweetly she sings!** → *She sings very sweetly.*
- **Would that** I were a bird! → *I wish I were a bird.*
- **Fie!** You are a coward. → *It is shameful that you are a coward.*
:::

(Full transformation rules: Chapter 34.)

## 20.6 Punctuation and Capitalisation with Interjections

1. A strong interjection stands alone with an **exclamation mark**, and the next word begins with a **capital**: **Hurrah!** *We won.*
2. A mild interjection is followed by a **comma**, and the sentence continues in small letters: **Oh**, *how lovely!*
3. Do not use more than one exclamation mark in formal writing: ✘ *Alas!!!*
4. Interjections are informal; avoid them in formal letters, applications and reports (except in quoted speech).

## 20.7 Interjections in Literature (Famous Examples)

::: quote
"**Alas**, poor Yorick! I knew him, Horatio." — Shakespeare, *Hamlet*

"**O** Romeo, Romeo! wherefore art thou Romeo?" — Shakespeare, *Romeo and Juliet* (here *wherefore* means *why*)

"**Ah**, love, let us be true / To one another!" — Matthew Arnold, *Dover Beach*

"**O** Captain! my Captain! our fearful trip is done." — Walt Whitman

"**Hail** to thee, blithe Spirit! / Bird thou never wert." — P. B. Shelley, *To a Skylark*

"**Behold** her, single in the field, / Yon solitary Highland Lass!" — William Wordsworth, *The Solitary Reaper*
:::

## 20.8 Common Errors with Interjections

::: errs
1. ✘ **Alas**! We have won the match. ✔ **Hurrah!** We have won the match. (wrong feeling)
2. ✘ **Hurrah!** My father has died. ✔ **Alas!** My father has died.
3. ✘ **Bravo!** You have failed again. ✔ **Fie!** / **Shame!** You have failed again.
4. ✘ **Oh** God, help me! (prayer, formal) ✔ **O** God, help me!
5. ✘ **Alas!!!** He is gone. ✔ **Alas!** He is gone.
6. ✘ **Alas**, what a pleasant surprise! ✔ **Oh**, what a pleasant surprise!
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner:** Interjections are not tested directly in the SAT, GRE or GMAT, but they matter in **reading comprehension**: an author's "Alas" or "Indeed" reveals **tone** (regret, irony, emphasis). GRE tone questions often hinge on such small words. In SAT Writing, the formal register expected in essays means interjections are usually inappropriate — a "style and tone" item may ask you to remove a casual "Wow!" from an academic passage.
:::
