# Dangling Modifier {#ch29}

[Part A · Grammar]{.chpart}

## 29.1 What Is a Modifier?

A **modifier** is a word, phrase or clause that **describes** or gives more information about another word in the sentence. Adjectives, adverbs, participial phrases, infinitive phrases, prepositional phrases and relative clauses are all modifiers.

::: ex
- The **tall** boy (adjective modifying *boy*)
- **Running fast**, he caught the bus. (participial phrase modifying *he*)
- **To pass the exam**, you must study. (infinitive phrase modifying *you*)
:::

A modifier must be **clearly attached** to the word it describes. When it is not, two kinds of errors occur: **dangling modifiers** and **misplaced modifiers**. Both produce sentences that are unclear and often absurd.

## 29.2 Dangling Modifiers

A **dangling modifier** is a phrase (usually at the beginning of a sentence) whose **logical subject is missing** from the sentence. It "dangles" because there is nothing in the sentence for it to modify sensibly; the reader attaches it to the nearest noun — usually the subject of the main clause — and the result is illogical.

::: ex
- ✘ **Walking along the road**, a car hit him. (The car was not walking!)
- ✘ **Having finished the homework**, the TV was turned on. (The TV did not finish the homework.)
- ✘ **To pass the exam**, hard work is necessary. (Hard work does not pass exams.)
- ✘ **At the age of five**, my father died. (Was the father five?)
- ✘ **While cooking dinner**, the smoke alarm went off.
:::

::: rule
**The rule:** When a sentence begins with a modifying phrase that has no subject of its own (a participle, gerund, infinitive or elliptical phrase), the **subject of the main clause** must be the **doer** of the action in that phrase.
:::

### 29.2.1 Kinds of Dangling Modifiers

| Kind | Dangling (✘) | Correct (✔) |
|---|---|---|
| **Present participle** | **Entering the room**, the light was switched on. | **Entering the room**, *he* switched on the light. |
| **Past participle** | **Exhausted by the journey**, the bed looked inviting. | **Exhausted by the journey**, *she* found the bed inviting. |
| **Perfect participle** | **Having lost his wallet**, the police were informed. | **Having lost his wallet**, *he* informed the police. |
| **Gerund (after a preposition)** | **After reading the letter**, the envelope was thrown away. | **After reading the letter**, *I* threw the envelope away. |
| **Infinitive** | **To win the match**, practice is essential. | **To win the match**, *the team* must practise. |
| **Elliptical clause** (subject and verb omitted) | **When ten years old**, my family moved to Dhaka. | **When I was ten years old**, my family moved to Dhaka. |
| **Prepositional phrase** | **As a student**, the library was my second home. | **As a student**, *I* treated the library as my second home. |
| **Appositive phrase** | **A brilliant scientist**, the award was given to Dr. Hasan. | **A brilliant scientist**, *Dr. Hasan* received the award. |

### 29.2.2 Two Ways to Correct a Dangling Modifier

**Method 1 — Make the doer the subject of the main clause:**

::: ex
- ✘ **Running to catch the bus**, my bag fell down. → ✔ **Running to catch the bus**, **I** dropped my bag.
- ✘ **Having read the novel**, the film seemed boring. → ✔ **Having read the novel**, **I** found the film boring.
:::

**Method 2 — Turn the modifier into a full clause with its own subject:**

::: ex
- ✘ **Running to catch the bus**, my bag fell down. → ✔ **While I was running to catch the bus**, my bag fell down.
- ✘ **Being a rainy day**, we stayed at home. → ✔ **As it was a rainy day**, we stayed at home. / ✔ **It being a rainy day**, we stayed at home. (absolute construction)
:::

### 29.2.3 Accepted Exceptions (Absolute and Disjunct Phrases)

Some participial expressions are accepted as **independent (disjunct)** phrases; they comment on the whole sentence and do not need a subject:

*generally speaking, strictly speaking, judging from, considering, taking everything into account, given, provided, supposing, owing to, according to, regarding, concerning, talking of, granted that.*

::: ex
- ✔ **Generally speaking**, Bangladeshis are hospitable.
- ✔ **Considering** his age, he did very well.
- ✔ **Judging from** his accent, he is from Sylhet.
- ✔ **Strictly speaking**, a tomato is a fruit.
:::

Also accepted is the **absolute construction**, where the participle has its **own subject**:

::: ex
- ✔ **The sun having set**, we returned home.
- ✔ **Weather permitting**, the match will start at ten.
:::

## 29.3 Misplaced Modifiers

A **misplaced modifier** has a subject in the sentence but is placed **too far from** the word it modifies, so it appears to modify the wrong word.

::: ex
- ✘ I saw a dead snake **riding my bicycle**. (The snake was riding?) ✔ **Riding my bicycle**, I saw a dead snake.
- ✘ He bought a horse for his son **with a long tail**. ✔ He bought a horse **with a long tail** for his son.
- ✘ She served sandwiches to the children **on paper plates**. ✔ She served the children sandwiches **on paper plates**.
- ✘ The teacher said **on Monday** he would give a test. (Did he say it on Monday, or will the test be on Monday?) ✔ **On Monday** the teacher said … / The teacher said he would give a test **on Monday**.
- ✘ There is a mobile phone in the room **which costs ten thousand taka**. ✔ In the room there is a mobile phone **which costs ten thousand taka**.
:::

### 29.3.1 Misplaced Single-Word Modifiers (*only, almost, just, even, nearly, merely, hardly, scarcely*)

These limiting words modify the word **immediately after** them. Moving them changes the meaning (see also Chapter 15).

::: ex
- ✘ He **almost** ate the whole cake. (He nearly started eating but didn't?) ✔ He ate **almost** the whole cake.
- ✘ I **only** have five hundred taka. (strict style) ✔ I have **only** five hundred taka.
- ✘ She **nearly** earned a lakh last month (meaning she earned slightly less). ✔ She earned **nearly** a lakh last month.
- ✘ He **just** needs a pen, not a notebook. ✔ He needs **just** a pen, not a notebook.
:::

### 29.3.2 Squinting Modifiers

A **squinting modifier** sits between two words and could modify either one.

::: ex
- ✘ Students who study **often** get good marks. (study often? or often get good marks?) ✔ Students who **often** study get good marks. / Students who study get good marks **often**.
- ✘ The man who was walking **slowly** crossed the road. (walking slowly? or crossed slowly?) ✔ The man who was walking crossed the road **slowly**. / The man who was **slowly** walking crossed the road.
:::

## 29.4 Dangling Modifiers in Exam Questions

Bangladeshi exams usually test this as **error detection** or "Which sentence is correct?". The test is simple: **read the opening phrase and ask "Who is doing it?" — then check the subject after the comma.**

::: ex
- (A) Being a hot day, I stayed inside. (B) It being a hot day, I stayed inside. → **B** (absolute construction)
- (A) Having finished the work, the office was closed. (B) Having finished the work, they closed the office. → **B**
- (A) While walking in the park, a dog bit me. (B) While I was walking in the park, a dog bit me. → **B**
- (A) Reaching the station, the train had left. (B) Reaching the station, I found that the train had left. → **B**
- (A) To be a good student, discipline is required. (B) To be a good student, one must be disciplined. → **B**
:::

## 29.5 Common Errors — Consolidated

::: errs
1. ✘ **Being ill**, the office was closed for him. ✔ **Being ill**, he did not go to the office.
2. ✘ **Opening the door**, a strange sight met my eyes. ✔ **Opening the door**, I saw a strange sight.
3. ✘ **Born in a poor family**, education was a dream for him. ✔ **Born in a poor family**, he could only dream of education.
4. ✘ **After finishing the meal**, the plates were washed. ✔ **After finishing the meal**, we washed the plates.
5. ✘ **Hoping to win**, the prize was not given to him. ✔ **Hoping to win**, he was disappointed when the prize went to another.
6. ✘ **While crossing the road**, a bus hit the old man. ✔ **While the old man was crossing the road**, a bus hit him.
7. ✘ **At the age of six**, my grandfather taught me to swim. ✔ **When I was six**, my grandfather taught me to swim.
8. ✘ **Having been warned**, the money was hidden. ✔ **Having been warned**, he hid the money.
9. ✘ **To make tea**, water must first be boiled. ✔ **To make tea**, you must first boil water.
10. ✘ **Flying over Dhaka**, the buildings looked tiny. ✔ **Flying over Dhaka**, we saw that the buildings looked tiny.
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — The #1 Modifier Rule:** Modifier errors are among the most frequent on the GMAT and SAT. The test-makers' rule: **an introductory modifier must be immediately followed by the noun it modifies.** ✘ *"Founded in 1921, students from all over the country study at Dhaka University."* ✔ *"Founded in 1921, Dhaka University attracts students from all over the country."* The GMAT also applies the **"touch rule"** to relative pronouns: *which / who* should refer to the noun right before it. ✘ *"He sold the car to the dealer, which was ten years old"* → ✔ *"He sold the ten-year-old car to the dealer."*
:::
