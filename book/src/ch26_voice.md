[Part A · Grammar]{.chpart}

## 26.1 What Is Voice?

**Voice** is the form of a verb that shows whether the **subject does the action** or **receives the action**.

- **Active voice** — the subject **does** the action: *Rahim* **wrote** *a letter.*
- **Passive voice** — the subject **receives** the action (it is acted upon): *A letter* **was written** *by Rahim.*

::: ex
- Active: The cat **killed** the rat. → Passive: The rat **was killed** by the cat.
- Active: Tagore **wrote** *Gitanjali*. → Passive: *Gitanjali* **was written** by Tagore.
:::

### 26.1.1 When Do We Use the Passive?

1. When the **doer is unknown**: *My bicycle* **has been stolen**.
2. When the **doer is obvious or unimportant**: *English* **is spoken** *all over the world.* / *The thief* **was arrested**.
3. When the **action/receiver is more important** than the doer: *The Padma Bridge* **was opened** *in 2022.*
4. In **scientific, official and formal writing**: *The solution* **was heated** *to 80°C. Applications* **are invited** *for the post of…*
5. To **avoid blame or responsibility**: *Mistakes* **were made**.

## 26.2 Basic Rules for Changing Active into Passive

::: rule
1. The **object** of the active verb becomes the **subject** of the passive verb.
2. The **subject** of the active verb becomes the **agent**, usually introduced by **by** (and is often omitted).
3. The verb takes the form **be (in the same tense) + past participle (V3)**.
4. The tense of the sentence **does not change**.
5. Pronouns change case: *I → me, we → us, he → him, she → her, they → them, who → whom* (and vice versa).
6. **Only transitive verbs** (verbs with an object) can be changed into the passive.
:::

| Active subject | Passive agent | Active object | Passive subject |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | by me | me | I |
| we | by us | us | we |
| you | by you | you | you |
| he | by him | him | he |
| she | by her | her | she |
| they | by them | them | they |
| it | by it | it | it |
| who | by whom | whom | who |

## 26.3 Passive Forms in Different Tenses

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Present Indefinite | He **writes** a letter. | A letter **is written** by him. |
| Present Continuous | He **is writing** a letter. | A letter **is being written** by him. |
| Present Perfect | He **has written** a letter. | A letter **has been written** by him. |
| Past Indefinite | He **wrote** a letter. | A letter **was written** by him. |
| Past Continuous | He **was writing** a letter. | A letter **was being written** by him. |
| Past Perfect | He **had written** a letter. | A letter **had been written** by him. |
| Future Indefinite | He **will write** a letter. | A letter **will be written** by him. |
| Future Perfect | He **will have written** a letter. | A letter **will have been written** by him. |
| Modal | He **can / must / should write** a letter. | A letter **can / must / should be written** by him. |
| Modal perfect | He **should have written** a letter. | A letter **should have been written** by him. |
| *Be going to* | He **is going to write** a letter. | A letter **is going to be written** by him. |

::: note
**Present perfect continuous, past perfect continuous, future continuous and future perfect continuous** have no commonly used passive forms. In exams, these are said to have "no passive" (or are changed using an equivalent perfect form when the meaning allows).
:::

::: ex
- They **are building** a new bridge. → A new bridge **is being built** (by them).
- She **has finished** the work. → The work **has been finished** by her.
- The police **had arrested** the thief before I came. → The thief **had been arrested** by the police before I came.
- We **shall celebrate** Victory Day. → Victory Day **will be celebrated** by us.
- You **must obey** the rules. → The rules **must be obeyed** (by you).
:::

## 26.4 Passive of Interrogative Sentences

### 26.4.1 Yes/No Questions (beginning with an auxiliary)

Keep the question form: **Auxiliary + new subject + be-form + V3 + by + agent?**

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| **Do** you **know** him? | **Is** he **known** to you? (*known to* — see 26.10) |
| **Did** he **write** the letter? | **Was** the letter **written** by him? |
| **Is** she **singing** a song? | **Is** a song **being sung** by her? |
| **Have** you **done** the work? | **Has** the work **been done** by you? |
| **Will** they **help** us? | **Shall / Will** we **be helped** by them? |
| **Can** you **solve** this problem? | **Can** this problem **be solved** by you? |
| **Does** he **not like** tea? | **Is** tea **not liked** by him? |

### 26.4.2 Wh-Questions

**(a) *Who* (subject):** *Who* → *By whom*

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| **Who** wrote this letter? | **By whom was** this letter **written**? |
| **Who** is calling me? | **By whom am** I **being called**? |
| **Who** has broken the glass? | **By whom has** the glass **been broken**? |

**(b) *Whom* (object):** *Whom* → *Who*

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| **Whom** did you see? | **Who was seen** by you? |
| **Whom** do you want? | **Who is wanted** by you? |

**(c) *What* (object):**

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| **What** are you doing? | **What is being done** by you? |
| **What** did he say? | **What was said** by him? |

**(d) *Why, where, when, how* (adverbs — keep them at the start):**

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| **Why** did you beat the dog? | **Why was** the dog **beaten** by you? |
| **When** will you finish the work? | **When will** the work **be finished** by you? |
| **How** did he solve the problem? | **How was** the problem **solved** by him? |
| **Where** did you buy this pen? | **Where was** this pen **bought** by you? |

## 26.5 Passive of Imperative Sentences

**Structure:** **Let + object + be + V3** (for commands/requests), or *You are ordered / requested / advised / forbidden + to + V1*.

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| **Shut** the door. | **Let** the door **be shut**. |
| **Do** it at once. | **Let** it **be done** at once. |
| **Help** the poor. | **Let** the poor **be helped**. |
| **Don't** insult the poor. | **Let** the poor **not be** insulted. / Let not the poor be insulted. |
| **Please help** me. | You **are requested to** help me. |
| **Obey** your parents. | You **are advised to** obey your parents. / Let your parents be obeyed. |
| **Don't** smoke. | You **are forbidden to** smoke. / Smoking **is prohibited**. |
| **Get out** of the room. (intransitive) | You **are ordered to** get out of the room. |
| **Let** him **do** the work. | **Let** the work **be done** by him. |
| **Let** us **sing** a song. | **Let** a song **be sung** by us. |

## 26.6 Passive of Verbs with Two Objects

Verbs like *give, tell, show, teach, lend, send, offer, pay, promise, buy, ask* have an **indirect object** (person) and a **direct object** (thing). Either can become the subject of the passive; using the **personal object** is more common.

| Active | Passive 1 (person as subject) | Passive 2 (thing as subject) |
|---|---|---|
| He **gave** me a book. | I **was given** a book by him. | A book **was given** to me by him. |
| The teacher **taught** us English. | We **were taught** English by the teacher. | English **was taught** to us by the teacher. |
| She **told** me a story. | I **was told** a story by her. | A story **was told** to me by her. |
| They **offered** him a job. | He **was offered** a job. | A job **was offered** to him. |
| He **asked** me a question. | I **was asked** a question by him. | A question **was asked** of me by him. |

## 26.7 Passive of Verbs with Object Complement (Factitive Verbs)

The **object** becomes the subject; the **complement** stays after the verb.

::: ex
- They **made** him **captain**. → He **was made captain** (by them).
- We **elected** her **chairman**. → She **was elected chairman** by us.
- They **called** him **a liar**. → He **was called a liar**.
- The news **made** her **sad**. → She **was made sad** by the news.
- They **painted** the house **white**. → The house **was painted white**.
:::

## 26.8 Passive of Prepositional and Phrasal Verbs

The preposition/particle **stays with the verb**.

::: ex
- Everybody **laughed at** him. → He **was laughed at** by everybody.
- She **looks after** the child. → The child **is looked after** by her.
- They **put off** the meeting. → The meeting **was put off**.
- The police **are looking into** the matter. → The matter **is being looked into** by the police.
- We must **send for** a doctor. → A doctor must **be sent for**.
- They **took care of** the patient. → The patient **was taken care of**. / Care **was taken of** the patient.
- He **made fun of** me. → I **was made fun of** by him.
- They **paid attention to** the speech. → The speech **was paid attention to**. / Attention **was paid to** the speech.
:::

## 26.9 Passive with Infinitives, Bare Infinitives and Gerunds

**(a) Make / let / see / hear / help + object + bare infinitive** → in the passive, **to** returns (except *let*):

::: ex
- They **made** him **work** hard. → He **was made to work** hard.
- I **saw** him **go** out. → He **was seen to go** out.
- We **heard** her **sing**. → She **was heard to sing**.
- They **let** him **go**. → He **was allowed to go**. (✘ He was let go — informal)
:::

**(b) Infinitive with object:**

::: ex
- I want **you to do** it. → I want **it to be done** by you.
- He wants **someone to repair** the car. → He wants **the car to be repaired**.
- There is no time **to waste**. → There is no time **to be wasted**.
- It is time **to close** the shop. → It is time **for the shop to be closed**.
:::

**(c) Gerunds:**

::: ex
- I remember **them taking** me to the zoo. → I remember **being taken** to the zoo (by them).
- He hates **people calling** him a coward. → He hates **being called** a coward.
:::

## 26.10 Prepositions Other Than *by* in the Passive

Some verbs (mostly of feeling and state) take a preposition **other than *by***:

| Verb | Preposition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| know | **to** | He is **known to** everybody. (✘ by everybody) |
| please | **with** | I am **pleased with** your work. |
| satisfy | **with** | We are **satisfied with** the result. |
| surprise / astonish | **at** | We were **surprised at** the news. |
| shock | **at / by** | We were **shocked at** his death. |
| alarm / frighten | **at / of** | She was **frightened of** the dog. |
| interest | **in** | He is **interested in** music. |
| contain | **in** | Much milk is **contained in** the jug. |
| fill | **with** | The glass is **filled with** water. |
| cover | **with** | The ground is **covered with** snow. |
| crowd | **with** | The hall was **crowded with** people. |
| disgust | **with** | I am **disgusted with** his conduct. |
| annoy | **with / at** | He was **annoyed with** me. |
| marry | **to** | She was **married to** a doctor. |
| devote | **to** | He was **devoted to** his work. |
| involve | **in** | He was **involved in** the crime. |
| engage | **in** | They are **engaged in** a dispute. |
| oblige | **to** | I am **obliged to** you. |
| vex | **with / at** | He was **vexed with** me / **at** my conduct. |
| worry | **about** | She is **worried about** her son. |
| absorb | **in** | He was **absorbed in** study. |
| tire | **of** | I am **tired of** his talk. |
| line | **with** | The road is **lined with** trees. |
| make | **of / from** | The table is **made of** wood. |
| kill | **with** (instrument) / **by** (agent) | The snake was **killed with** a stick **by** him. |

::: ex
- Active: His conduct **surprised** me. → Passive: I **was surprised at** his conduct.
- Active: The work **pleased** him. → Passive: He **was pleased with** the work.
- Active: Everybody **knows** him. → Passive: He **is known to** everybody.
- Active: The bottle **contains** milk. → Passive: Milk **is contained in** the bottle.
:::

## 26.11 Impersonal (Reporting) Passive: *It is said that…*

With verbs like *say, believe, think, know, report, consider, expect, suppose, hope, rumour, allege*, there are two passive patterns:

| Active | Passive 1 (*It* + passive + *that*) | Passive 2 (Subject + passive + *to*-infinitive) |
|---|---|---|
| People **say** that he is honest. | **It is said** that he is honest. | He **is said to be** honest. |
| People **believe** that he was killed. | **It is believed** that he was killed. | He **is believed to have been killed**. |
| They **think** that she is a spy. | **It is thought** that she is a spy. | She **is thought to be** a spy. |
| People **know** that he lives here. | **It is known** that he lives here. | He **is known to live** here. |

::: note
Use the **perfect infinitive** (*to have + V3*) when the reported action happened **before** the reporting: *He is said* **to have left** *the country last year.*
:::

## 26.12 Passive with *Need*, *Want*, *Deserve*, *Require*, *Be worth*

These verbs + gerund already have a **passive meaning**:

::: ex
- My shirt **needs washing**. (= needs to be washed)
- The garden **wants watering**.
- He **deserves praising**. / He deserves **to be praised**.
- This book **is worth reading**. (✘ worth being read)
:::

## 26.13 Quasi-Passive (Active in Form, Passive in Meaning)

Some verbs are active in form but passive in meaning:

::: ex
- Honey **tastes** sweet. → Honey **is** sweet **when it is tasted**.
- The rose **smells** sweet. → The rose is sweet when it is smelt.
- This cloth **feels** soft.
- The book **sells** well. → The book **is sold** well.
- The house **is building**. (archaic) → The house **is being built**.
- The cake **cuts** easily.
:::

## 26.14 Passive to Active

Reverse the process: the agent (after *by*) becomes the subject; the passive subject becomes the object; the verb returns to active form in the same tense. When there is **no agent**, supply a suitable subject (*someone, people, they, we*).

::: ex
- The letter was posted **by me**. → **I** posted the letter.
- My watch has been stolen. → **Someone** has stolen my watch.
- English is spoken all over the world. → **People** speak English all over the world.
- Let the flag be hoisted. → **Hoist** the flag.
- By whom was the window broken? → **Who** broke the window?
- He is known to all. → **All know** him.
- I was surprised at his behaviour. → His behaviour **surprised** me.
:::

## 26.15 Verbs That Cannot Be Put into the Passive

1. **Intransitive verbs** (no object): *sleep, come, go, die, arrive, happen, occur, exist, rise, fall* — ✘ *He was died.* ✘ *An accident was happened.* ✔ *An accident* **happened**.
2. **Stative verbs of possession / relation** (even if transitive): *have (= own), resemble, lack, suit, fit, become (= suit), cost, weigh, contain (in some uses)* — ✘ *His father is resembled by him.* ✔ *He* **resembles** *his father.* ✘ *A car is had by me.*
3. **Reflexive objects / reciprocal pronouns**: ✘ *Himself was hurt by him.*
4. **Cognate objects** rarely: *He lived a happy life* → (*A happy life was lived by him* — avoid).

## 26.16 Common Errors in Voice

::: errs
1. ✘ The letter **was wrote** by him. ✔ The letter **was written** by him.
2. ✘ The work **has been did**. ✔ The work **has been done**.
3. ✘ He is known **by** everybody. ✔ He is known **to** everybody.
4. ✘ I was surprised **by** his rudeness (exam key prefers *at*). ✔ I was surprised **at** his rudeness.
5. ✘ The snake was killed **by** a stick. ✔ The snake was killed **with** a stick.
6. ✘ An accident **was happened** yesterday. ✔ An accident **happened** yesterday.
7. ✘ He **was died** of cholera. ✔ He **died** of cholera.
8. ✘ The house **is being build**. ✔ The house **is being built**.
9. ✘ By whom **this book was** written? ✔ By whom **was this book** written?
10. ✘ He was made **work** hard. ✔ He was made **to work** hard.
11. ✘ Let the door **is** shut. ✔ Let the door **be** shut.
12. ✘ **The meeting was put** by them **off**. ✔ The meeting **was put off** by them.
13. ✘ He was laughed by all. ✔ He was laughed **at** by all.
14. ✘ The book is worth **being read**. ✔ The book is worth **reading**.
15. ✘ It is said that he **is left** the country. ✔ He is said **to have left** the country. / It is said that he **has left** the country.
16. ✘ By whom **was broken the window**? ✔ By whom **was the window broken**?
17. ✘ The bridge **was constructing** last year. ✔ The bridge **was being constructed** last year.
18. ✘ I **am interested at** music. ✔ I am **interested in** music.
19. ✘ The glass is **filled by** water. ✔ The glass is **filled with** water.
20. ✘ The car **will repaired** tomorrow. ✔ The car **will be repaired** tomorrow.
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Active Is (Usually) Better:** The GMAT and SAT prefer the **active voice** when it is clearer and more concise: ✘ *"The decision to expand was made by the board in 2020"* → ✔ *"The board decided in 2020 to expand."* But the passive is **not grammatically wrong**; it is correct when the doer is unknown or unimportant. A real GMAT error is the **mixed or dangling passive**: ✘ *"Using new methods, the bridge was completed early"* — the bridge did not use the methods. ✔ *"Using new methods, the engineers completed the bridge early."* (See Chapter 29.)
:::
