## 34.1 What Is Transformation?

**Transformation of sentences** means changing the **form** (structure or type) of a sentence **without changing its meaning**. It shows mastery of grammar and is tested in SSC, HSC, university admission and BCS/bank written examinations, while the same skill is needed for "Which sentence has the same meaning?" items in MCQ tests and in GMAT Sentence Correction.

The main types of transformation are:

1. **Simple ↔ Complex ↔ Compound** (by structure)
2. **Assertive ↔ Interrogative ↔ Exclamatory ↔ Imperative ↔ Optative** (by meaning)
3. **Affirmative ↔ Negative**
4. **Degrees of comparison** (Chapter 24)
5. **Active ↔ Passive voice** (Chapter 26)
6. **Direct ↔ Indirect speech** (Chapter 27)

::: rule
**Golden rule:** The meaning and the tense must remain the same. Only the structure changes.
:::

## 34.2 Simple to Complex and Complex to Simple

A simple sentence has phrases; a complex sentence has subordinate clauses. So **Simple → Complex = expand a phrase into a clause**, and **Complex → Simple = reduce a clause to a phrase**.

### 34.2.1 Participle / Gerund Phrase ↔ Adverb Clause of Time or Reason

| Simple | Complex |
|---|---|
| **Seeing the police**, the thief ran away. | **When the thief saw the police**, he ran away. / **As soon as** the thief saw the police, he ran away. |
| **Being ill**, he could not attend the class. | **As / Since he was ill**, he could not attend the class. |
| **Having finished the work**, he went home. | **After he had finished the work**, he went home. |
| **On reaching** home, I found him waiting. | **When I reached** home, I found him waiting. |
| **At the age of five**, he lost his father. | **When he was five years old**, he lost his father. |
| **The sun having set**, we returned home. | **When the sun had set**, we returned home. |
| **In my childhood** I lived in a village. | **When I was a child**, I lived in a village. |

### 34.2.2 *In spite of / Despite* ↔ *Though / Although* (Concession)

| Simple | Complex |
|---|---|
| **In spite of** his poverty, he is honest. | **Though** he is poor, he is honest. |
| **Despite** being rich, he is not happy. | **Although** he is rich, he is not happy. |
| **Notwithstanding** his efforts, he failed. | **Though** he made efforts, he failed. |

### 34.2.3 *Because of / Owing to / Due to* ↔ *Because / Since / As* (Reason)

| Simple | Complex |
|---|---|
| He could not come **because of illness**. | He could not come **because he was ill**. |
| **Owing to heavy rain**, the match was cancelled. | **As it rained heavily**, the match was cancelled. |

### 34.2.4 Infinitive ↔ *So that / In order that* (Purpose)

| Simple | Complex |
|---|---|
| He works hard **to prosper** in life. | He works hard **so that he may prosper** in life. |
| We eat **to live**. | We eat **so that we may live**. |
| He went to Dhaka **to see** his ailing father. | He went to Dhaka **so that he might see** his ailing father. |

### 34.2.5 *Too … to* ↔ *So … that … cannot* (Result)

| Simple | Complex |
|---|---|
| He is **too weak to walk**. | He is **so weak that he cannot walk**. |
| The tea was **too hot to drink**. | The tea was **so hot that it could not be drunk** / **that I could not drink it**. |
| She is **too proud to accept** help. | She is **so proud that she will not accept** help. |

::: note
**Enough … to ↔ so … that … can:** *He is* **rich enough to buy** *a car.* → *He is* **so rich that he can buy** *a car.*
:::

### 34.2.6 *By / Without* + Gerund ↔ *If / Unless* (Condition)

| Simple | Complex |
|---|---|
| **By working hard**, you will succeed. | **If you work hard**, you will succeed. |
| **Without working hard**, you cannot succeed. | **Unless you work hard**, you cannot succeed. / **If you do not work hard**, … |
| **In case of** rain, the match will be postponed. | **If it rains**, the match will be postponed. |
| **But for** your help, I would have failed. | **If you had not helped me**, I would have failed. |

### 34.2.7 Noun / Infinitive Phrase ↔ Noun Clause

| Simple | Complex |
|---|---|
| I know **his address**. | I know **where he lives**. |
| I know **him to be honest**. | I know **that he is honest**. |
| **His honesty** is known to all. | **That he is honest** is known to all. / It is known to all **that he is honest**. |
| He confessed **his guilt**. | He confessed **that he was guilty**. |
| I don't know **the time of his arrival**. | I don't know **when he will arrive**. |
| He seems **to be ill**. | It seems **that he is ill**. |
| Tell me **your age**. | Tell me **how old you are**. |
| I believe **in his innocence**. | I believe **that he is innocent**. |

### 34.2.8 Adjective / Participle Phrase ↔ Adjective (Relative) Clause

| Simple | Complex |
|---|---|
| The man **standing at the gate** is my uncle. | The man **who is standing at the gate** is my uncle. |
| I saw **a wounded** bird. | I saw a bird **which was wounded**. |
| He is **a man of honesty**. | He is a man **who is honest**. |
| The **rich** should help the poor. | Those **who are rich** should help the poor. |
| This is **my birthplace**. | This is the place **where I was born**. |
| He was **the first to come**. | He was the first person **who came**. |
| I have no money **to spend**. | I have no money **that I can spend**. |
| **Barking** dogs seldom bite. | Dogs **that bark** seldom bite. |

### 34.2.9 Special Patterns

| Simple | Complex |
|---|---|
| **Every** man loves his country. | There is no man **who does not love** his country. |
| He is **the best** boy in the class. | There is no boy in the class **who is as good as** he. |
| He **must** work hard **to pass**. | He must work hard **if he wants to pass**. |
| He was **sure of success**. | He was sure **that he would succeed**. |
| It is **time to go**. | It is time **that we went**. |

## 34.3 Simple to Compound and Compound to Simple

A **compound** sentence joins independent clauses with coordinating conjunctions (*and, but, or, so, yet, not only … but also, either … or, neither … nor*). To go from **simple to compound**, expand the phrase into an independent clause and join with a coordinator; to go from **compound to simple**, reduce one clause to a phrase.

| Simple | Compound |
|---|---|
| **Seeing the police**, the thief ran away. | The thief **saw** the police **and** ran away. |
| **Being ill**, he could not come. | He **was** ill, **so** he could not come. / He was ill **and therefore** could not come. |
| **In spite of** his poverty, he is honest. | He is poor, **but** he is honest. / He is poor, **yet** he is honest. |
| **By working hard**, you will succeed. | Work hard, **and** you will succeed. |
| **Without working hard**, you will fail. | Work hard, **or** you will fail. / You must work hard, **otherwise** you will fail. |
| He is **too weak to walk**. | He is very weak, **so** he cannot walk. / He is very weak **and** cannot walk. |
| **Besides** being a poet, he was a painter. | He was **not only** a poet **but also** a painter. |
| **Having finished** the work, he went home. | He finished the work **and** went home. |
| He came **to see** me. | He wanted to see me, **so** he came. |
| **Taking off** his shoes, he entered the room. | He took off his shoes **and** entered the room. |
| **Instead of** going to school, he went to the cinema. | He did not go to school **but** went to the cinema. |
| **Both** Rahim **and** Karim came. | Rahim came, **and** Karim came too. |

## 34.4 Complex to Compound and Compound to Complex

| Complex | Compound |
|---|---|
| **As** he was ill, he could not come. | He was ill, **so** he could not come. |
| **Though** he is poor, he is honest. | He is poor, **but** he is honest. |
| **If** you work hard, you will succeed. | Work hard, **and** you will succeed. |
| **Unless** you work hard, you will fail. | Work hard, **or** you will fail. |
| **When** he saw the police, he ran away. | He saw the police **and** ran away. |
| He is **so** weak **that** he cannot walk. | He is very weak **and** (so) he cannot walk. |
| **Since** it was raining, we stayed inside. | It was raining, **so** we stayed inside. |
| The man **who** came here is my uncle. | The man came here, **and** he is my uncle. |
| I know **that** he is honest. | He is honest, **and** I know it. |
| **No sooner** had he come **than** she left. | He came, **and** she left at once. |

## 34.5 Assertive ↔ Interrogative

### 34.5.1 Affirmative Assertive → Negative Interrogative (and vice versa)

::: rule
An **affirmative** assertive becomes a **negative** interrogative; a **negative** assertive becomes an **affirmative** interrogative. (The question is rhetorical — it expects the answer that confirms the original statement.)
:::

| Assertive | Interrogative |
|---|---|
| He is honest. | **Is he not** honest? / **Isn't** he honest? |
| He is not honest. | **Is** he honest? |
| He works hard. | **Doesn't** he work hard? |
| He never tells a lie. | **Does** he **ever** tell a lie? |
| Everybody loves his country. | **Who does not** love his country? |
| Nobody loves a liar. | **Who** loves a liar? |
| There is no use of crying. | **What is the use of** crying? |
| It is no use waiting. | **What is the use of** waiting? |
| I shall never forget you. | **Shall I ever** forget you? |
| No one can bear such an insult. | **Who can** bear such an insult? |
| All men are mortal. | **Who is not** mortal? / **Are not** all men mortal? |
| Everybody knows Tagore. | **Who does not** know Tagore? |
| Nothing can be gained by idleness. | **What can be** gained by idleness? |
| It does not matter whether you come or not. | **What does it matter** whether you come or not? |
| None can deny it. | **Who can** deny it? |
| You can do it. | **Can't you** do it? |

## 34.6 Assertive ↔ Exclamatory

### 34.6.1 Assertive → Exclamatory

Replace *very / great / so* with *what* or *how*; use exclamatory word order; interjection words for feelings (*It is a matter of joy → Hurrah!*).

| Assertive | Exclamatory |
|---|---|
| It is a **very** beautiful bird. | **What a** beautiful bird it is! |
| The bird is **very** beautiful. | **How** beautiful the bird is! |
| She sings **very** sweetly. | **How** sweetly she sings! |
| It was a **great** victory. | **What a** victory it was! |
| It is a matter of **great joy** that we have won. | **Hurrah!** We have won. |
| It is a matter of **great sorrow** that he is dead. | **Alas!** He is dead. |
| I wish I were a bird. | **Would that** I were a bird! / **Had I** the wings of a bird! |
| It is very sad that he died young. | **Alas!** He died young. |
| You have done very well. | **Bravo!** You have done very well. |
| He is a **great** fool. | **What a** fool he is! |
| It is shameful that you told a lie. | **Fie!** You told a lie. |

### 34.6.2 Exclamatory → Assertive

Reverse the process (replace *what/how* with *very/great*, interjections with "It is a matter of …").

| Exclamatory | Assertive |
|---|---|
| **What a** fine day it is! | It is a **very** fine day. |
| **How** clever the boy is! | The boy is **very** clever. |
| **Alas!** I am undone. | **I am sorry that** I am undone. / It is a matter of sorrow that I am undone. |
| **Would that** I could be a child again! | **I wish** I could be a child again. |
| **O that** I were young again! | **I wish** I were young again. |
| **What** a piece of work is man! | Man is a **wonderful** piece of work. |

## 34.7 Imperative ↔ Assertive

| Imperative | Assertive |
|---|---|
| Shut the door. | You are **ordered / told to** shut the door. / You **should** shut the door. |
| Please help me. | You are **requested to** help me. / I **request** you to help me. |
| Never tell a lie. | You **should never** tell a lie. |
| Do not smoke. | You **should not** smoke. / You are **forbidden to** smoke. |
| Let us go for a walk. | **We should** go for a walk. / I **propose that we should** go for a walk. |
| Work hard to succeed. | You **must** work hard to succeed. |
| Be quiet. | You **should be** quiet. |

## 34.8 Imperative ↔ Interrogative

| Imperative | Interrogative |
|---|---|
| Please open the window. | **Will you please** open the window? / **Would you mind** opening the window? |
| Help me. | **Can't you** help me? / **Will you** help me? |
| Do not make a noise. | **Why are you** making a noise? / **Won't you** stop making a noise? |
| Keep quiet. | **Will you** keep quiet? |

## 34.9 Optative ↔ Assertive

| Optative | Assertive |
|---|---|
| **May** you be happy! | I **wish that** you may be happy. / I **pray that** you be happy. |
| **May** Allah bless you! | I **pray that** Allah may bless you. |
| **Long live** Bangladesh! | We **wish** Bangladesh a long life. / We **pray that** Bangladesh may live long. |
| **Would that** I were rich! | I **wish** I were rich. |

## 34.10 Affirmative ↔ Negative (Without Changing Meaning)

| Affirmative | Negative |
|---|---|
| He is **always** punctual. | He is **never** late. / He is **never** unpunctual. |
| He is **honest**. | He is **not dishonest**. |
| Rahim is **taller than** Karim. | Karim is **not so tall as** Rahim. |
| **Everybody** loves his country. | **There is no one who does not** love his country. |
| I **doubt** whether he will come. | I **am not sure** whether he will come. |
| He **failed** in the exam. | He **did not pass** the exam. |
| He is **sometimes** late. | He is **not always** punctual. |
| **Only** a fool can say this. | **None but** a fool can say this. |
| **Only** Rahim can do it. | **None but** Rahim can do it. / **No one other than** Rahim can do it. |
| He is **the best** player. | **No other** player is **as good as** he. |
| As soon as he saw me, he left. | **No sooner** had he seen me **than** he left. |
| I **remember** the day. | I **do not forget** the day. |
| He **was** the **greatest** poet. | **No other** poet **was so great as** he. |
| **Every** mother loves her child. | **There is no mother but loves** her child. / **There is no mother who does not love** her child. |
| He is **too** weak to walk. | He is **so weak that he cannot** walk. |
| He **will** come. | He **will not fail** to come. |
| She **is** **sometimes** right. | She is **not always** wrong. |
| He **loves** the poor. | He **does not hate** the poor. |
| **All** that glitters is **not** gold. | **Not all** that glitters is gold. (same meaning) |

## 34.11 Transformation Using "Too … to" / "So … that" / "Enough" / "Not only … but also" / "As soon as" (Mixed Practice)

::: ex
- He is too poor to buy a car. (simple) → He is so poor that he cannot buy a car. (complex) → He is very poor, so he cannot buy a car. (compound)
- In spite of being rich, he is not happy. (simple) → Though he is rich, he is not happy. (complex) → He is rich, but he is not happy. (compound)
- Seeing me, he fled. (simple) → As soon as he saw me, he fled. (complex) → He saw me and fled. (compound)
- He confessed his guilt. (simple) → He confessed that he was guilty. (complex) → He was guilty and he confessed it. (compound)
- Besides being a scholar, he was a poet. (simple) → He was not only a scholar but also a poet. (compound)
- He must work hard to pass. (simple) → He must work hard so that he may pass. (complex) → He must work hard, or he will not pass. (compound)
:::

## 34.12 Common Errors in Transformation

::: errs
1. ✘ **Though** he is poor, **but** he is honest. (complex + compound markers together) ✔ **Though** he is poor, he is honest.
2. ✘ He is **too** weak **that** he cannot walk. ✔ He is **so** weak that he cannot walk. / He is **too** weak **to** walk.
3. ✘ He is so weak that he cannot walk → **He is too weak that he can walk**. (meaning changed) ✔ He is too weak to walk.
4. ✘ Who does not love his country? → **Everybody does not love his country.** (wrong meaning) ✔ Everybody loves his country.
5. ✘ What a beautiful bird **is it**! ✔ What a beautiful bird **it is**!
6. ✘ If you work hard, you will succeed → **Work hard or you will succeed.** ✔ Work hard **and** you will succeed.
7. ✘ Unless you work hard, you will fail → **Work hard and you will fail.** ✔ Work hard **or** you will fail.
8. ✘ Seeing the police the thief ran away → **When the thief saw the police, so he ran away.** ✔ When the thief saw the police, he ran away.
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Equivalence of Meaning:** GMAT Sentence Correction answer choices are essentially transformations of one sentence; the right one keeps the **original meaning** with the **clearest structure**. Be suspicious of choices that change the logical relationship (cause ↔ contrast: *because* vs *although*; condition ↔ time: *if* vs *when*), or that turn a definite statement into a vague one. GRE Sentence Equivalence asks for two words that produce sentences "alike in meaning" — the same equivalence skill applied to vocabulary.
:::
