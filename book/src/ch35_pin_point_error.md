## 35.1 What Is "Pin Point Error"?

In a **Pin Point Error** (or *Error Detection / Spotting the Error*) question, a sentence is divided into parts — usually (A), (B), (C), (D) and sometimes (E) "No error" — and you must **identify the part that contains a grammatical, idiomatic or usage error**. It is one of the most important item types in BCS Preliminary, Bank recruitment (officer, cash officer, AD), university admission, and in the SAT Writing and GMAT Sentence Correction sections.

Unlike *Correction*, you do not have to rewrite the sentence; you have to **locate** the error. But to locate it with confidence, you must know *why* the part is wrong. This chapter gives a systematic checklist and many worked examples organised by error type.

## 35.2 A Ten-Point Checklist

Read the whole sentence once for meaning. Then check, in this order:

1. **Subject–verb agreement** (find the real subject; ignore phrases between) — Chapter 23.
2. **Verb tense and form** (time clues, sequence of tenses, *since/for*, conditionals, V2 vs V3) — Chapters 21–22.
3. **Pronoun case and agreement** (*between you and I*, *each … their*, *who/whom*) — Chapter 08.
4. **Articles and determiners** (*a/an*, *the* with rivers/superlatives, *few/a few*, *each/every*) — Chapters 04–05.
5. **Prepositions** (*married with*, *discuss about*, *senior than*) — Chapters 17–18.
6. **Adjective vs adverb** (*sings good*, *looks beautifully*) — Chapters 09, 15.
7. **Comparison** (*more better*, *than any boy*, *senior than*) — Chapters 10, 24.
8. **Conjunctions and correlatives** (*although … but*, *neither … or*, *no sooner … when*) — Chapter 19.
9. **Parallelism, modifiers and word order** (*not only … but also*, dangling participles, inversion) — Chapters 16, 29, 39.
10. **Redundancy and word choice** (*return back*, *cousin brother*, *advice/advise*) — Chapters 38, 52.

If none applies, the answer may be "No error" — but test-makers rarely use it more than once or twice per paper.

::: tip
**Exam Tip — The errors hide in the small words.** Most errors are in prepositions, articles, verb endings (*-s, -ed*), pronouns and comparison words. Read slowly, pronounce every ending in your mind, and pay special attention to the part that sounds "almost right".
:::

## 35.3 Worked Examples by Error Type

In each example, the erroneous part is shown in **bold**, followed by the correction and the reason.

### 35.3.1 Subject–Verb Agreement

::: ex
- The quality of the mangoes **are** excellent. → **is** (subject: *quality*).
- One of my friends **live** in Canada. → **lives** (*one of* + plural → singular verb).
- Each of the students **have** submitted the assignment. → **has**.
- Neither the teacher nor the students **was** present. → **were** (verb agrees with the nearer subject *students*).
- The Prime Minister, with all her ministers, **were** present. → **was** (*with* does not add to the subject).
- Bread and butter **are** my favourite breakfast. → **is** (one dish).
- The number of unemployed youths **are** increasing. → **is** (*the number of* → singular).
- Many a student **have** failed this year. → **has**.
- Physics **are** a difficult subject. → **is**.
- The cattle **is** grazing in the field. → **are**.
- Ten thousand taka **are** a big amount for him. → **is** (sum of money as one unit).
- Every boy and every girl **were** given a prize. → **was**.
:::

### 35.3.2 Tense and Verb Form

::: ex
- I **have seen** him yesterday. → **saw**.
- He **has been** absent since three days. → *for* three days (error in *since*).
- No sooner had he reached the station **when** the train left. → **than**.
- If I **was** you, I would accept the offer. → **were**.
- He **is working** here since 2015. → **has been working**.
- It is high time we **start** working. → **started**.
- The train **left** before we **had reached** the station. → **had left … reached**.
- He told me that he **will** come. → **would**.
- I **am knowing** him for years. → **have known**.
- The criminal was **hung** yesterday. → **hanged**.
- He **lay** the book on the table. → **laid**.
- The prices of essentials have **raised** sharply. → **risen**.
- If he **would have** studied, he would have passed. → **had**.
:::

### 35.3.3 Pronoun Errors

::: ex
- This is between you and **I**. → **me**.
- Every student must bring **their** own pen. → **his** (or *his or her*).
- He is taller than **me**. → **I** (formal: *than I am*).
- **Whom** do you think is the best player? → **Who**.
- Let **we** go there. → **us**.
- One should respect **his** elders. → **one's**.
- Rahim and **myself** will attend the meeting. → **I**.
- The book is **her's**. → **hers**.
- I, who **is** your friend, will help you. → **am**.
- He enjoyed **at** the party. → **enjoyed himself** at the party.
:::

### 35.3.4 Articles and Determiners

::: ex
- He is **a** honest man. → **an**.
- **Padma** is a mighty river. → **The Padma**.
- She is **an** university teacher. → **a**.
- He is **best** student in the class. → **the best**.
- There is **little** books on the shelf. → **few** (and *are*).
- **Every** students must come. → **student**.
- He gave me **an** advice. → **a piece of** advice / **some** advice.
- **The** honesty is the best policy. → **Honesty**.
- Both of them **are not** present. → **Neither of them is** present.
- He has **less** friends than his brother. → **fewer**.
:::

### 35.3.5 Prepositions

::: ex
- She is married **with** a doctor. → **to**.
- We discussed **about** the problem. → **discussed the problem** (no preposition).
- He is senior **than** me. → **to**.
- He died **from** cholera. → **of**.
- I prefer tea **than** coffee. → **to**.
- He congratulated me **for** my success. → **on**.
- He is suffering **with** fever. → **from**.
- Divide the money **among** the two brothers. → **between**.
- He has been living here **from** 2010. → **since**.
- He reached **at** the station late. → **reached the station** (no *at*).
- He is good **in** English. → **at**.
- He jumped **in** the river from the bridge. → **into** (movement from outside to inside).
:::

### 35.3.6 Adjective/Adverb Confusion

::: ex
- She sings very **good**. → **well**.
- The rose smells **sweetly**. → **sweet**.
- He looks **happily**. → **happy**.
- He drives very **careful**. → **carefully**.
- This is a **real** good idea. → **really**.
- He is **enough** strong to lift it. → **strong enough**.
- He is **too much** tired. → **very** tired.
:::

### 35.3.7 Comparison

::: ex
- He is **more stronger** than I. → **stronger**.
- Rahim is taller than **any boy** in the class. → **any other boy**.
- She is the **cleverest** of the two sisters. → **cleverer**.
- He is **elder** than me. → **older**.
- This is the **most unique** opportunity. → **a unique**.
- The climate of Sylhet is better than **Rajshahi**. → **that of Rajshahi**.
- He is **very** taller than me. → **much** taller.
:::

### 35.3.8 Conjunctions and Correlatives

::: ex
- Although he is poor, **but** he is honest. → remove **but**.
- **Neither** he **or** his brother came. → **nor**.
- **Both** Rahim **as well as** Karim came. → **and**.
- Unless you **do not** work hard, you will fail. → **work** (remove *not*).
- Walk fast lest you **should not** miss the bus. → **should** (remove *not*).
- Hardly had I reached home **than** it started raining. → **when**.
- He is **not only** a poet **but** a painter. → **but also**.
- It was **such** a hot day **so** we stayed inside. → **that**.
- Wait here **till** I **do not** return. → **till I return**.
:::

### 35.3.9 Parallelism and Word Order

::: ex
- He likes swimming, reading and **to play** chess. → **playing**.
- She is beautiful, intelligent and **has kindness**. → **kind**.
- He **not only** reads English **but also** French. → reads **not only** English **but also** French.
- **Never I have** seen such a sight. → **Never have I**.
- I asked him where **was he** going. → **he was**.
- Do you know what **is his name**? → **his name is**.
:::

### 35.3.10 Modifier Errors

::: ex
- **Walking along the road**, a snake bit him. → **While he was walking along the road**, a snake bit him.
- **Being a rainy day**, we stayed at home. → **It being** a rainy day …
- **Reaching the station**, the train had left. → **When I reached** the station, …
:::

### 35.3.11 Redundancy and Word Choice

::: ex
- Please **return back** the book. → **return**.
- He is my **cousin brother**. → **cousin**.
- The reason of his failure is **because** he was lazy. → **that**.
- I **will advice** you. → **advise**.
- He gave me some good **advise**. → **advice**.
- The **sceneries** of Sylhet are beautiful. → **scenery** … **is**.
- Each and every students **were** present. → **student was**.
- I **myself personally** believe it. → **I personally** / **I myself**.
- He repeated the same thing **again**. → remove **again**.
- He **returned back** home. → **returned**.
:::

### 35.3.12 Idioms and Fixed Expressions

::: ex
- He is **fond to** music. → **fond of**.
- I am looking forward to **meet** you. → **meeting**.
- He **made** me **to** laugh. → **made me laugh**.
- He insisted **to** go. → **on going**.
- He prevented me **to go**. → **from going**.
- Would you mind **to open** the window? → **opening**.
- He **availed** the opportunity. → **availed himself of**.
- He **dared** not **to** go there. → **dared not go** / **dare not go**.
:::

## 35.4 Mixed Worked Examples (Full Sentences)

Study how each sentence is analysed. The error part is marked.

::: errs
1. The committee **have** submitted **its** report. — Inconsistent: if the committee acts as one unit, use **has** … **its**; if as individuals, **have** … **their**. ✔ The committee **has** submitted **its** report.
2. Scarcely had the guests arrived, **than** the lights went out. → **when**.
3. The furniture **were** arranged neatly in the room. → **was**.
4. I have been waiting for you **since** two hours. → **for**.
5. He is one of the best students who **has** ever studied here. → **have**.
6. The teacher, as well as the students, **were** present in the meeting. → **was**.
7. If he **will come**, I shall go with him. → **comes**.
8. The poor **is** always neglected in society. → **are**.
9. The Sundarbans **are** the largest mangrove forest in the world. → **is** (one forest; a name).
10. Neither of the two candidates **are** suitable for the post. → **is**.
11. My father **as well as** my uncles **are** doctors. → **is a doctor** (agreement with *father*).
12. He has **got** a good knowledge **in** English. → **of**.
13. **Being** a holiday, the office was closed. → **It being** a holiday …
14. I prefer **to read** than **to write**. → **reading to writing** / **to read rather than write**.
15. The news **are** too good to be true. → **is**.
16. He **don't** know the answer. → **doesn't**.
17. A large number of students **was** absent. → **were**.
18. He is not **so** intelligent **than** his brother. → **as**.
19. Each of the boys **were** given **their** certificate. → **was** … **his**.
20. He **has gone** to Cox's Bazar last month. → **went**.
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Error Spotting Strategy:** The old SAT "Identifying Sentence Errors" format was exactly this item type, and its lessons apply directly: (1) the error is **always in an underlined part**; (2) about one item in five has **no error**, so don't invent one; (3) the most frequent errors are agreement, pronoun, idiom (preposition), verb tense, comparison and parallelism. On the GMAT the same errors appear inside longer, more complex sentences — the checklist above is your best tool for both.
:::
