## 4.1 What Is a Determiner?

A **determiner** is a word placed **before a noun** (or before the adjectives that describe the noun) to **limit or specify** it. It tells us *which one, whose, how many* or *how much*. It does not describe a quality the way an ordinary adjective does.

::: ex
- **the** book, **a** pen, **an** apple (articles)
- **this** boy, **those** houses (demonstratives)
- **my** father, **their** school (possessives)
- **some** water, **many** students, **few** friends (quantifiers)
- **each** player, **every** citizen, **either** side (distributives)
- **three** books, **the first** day (numerals)
- **which** book? **whose** car? (interrogatives)
:::

Compare an **adjective** and a **determiner**: in *the old house*, *old* tells us the **quality** of the house (adjective) while *the* tells us **which** house (determiner). Determiners come **first** in a noun phrase: *these* **two** *beautiful old* houses.

::: note
**Determiner vs Pronoun.** Many determiners can also stand alone as pronouns. When the word is followed by a noun, it is a determiner; when it stands alone, it is a pronoun: **This** *book is mine* (determiner) / **This** *is mine* (pronoun). **Some** *boys came* (determiner) / **Some** *came* (pronoun). Exceptions: *every, no, the, a/an, my, your, her, our, their* can only be determiners (their pronoun forms are *everyone, none, mine, yours, hers, ours, theirs*).
:::

## 4.2 Classes of Determiners

| Class | Members |
|---|---|
| Articles | a, an, the |
| Demonstratives | this, that, these, those |
| Possessives | my, your, his, her, its, our, their, whose, one's; noun + 's (*Rahim's*) |
| Quantifiers | some, any, no, much, many, more, most, little, a little, few, a few, less, fewer, enough, several, all, both, half, a lot of, plenty of, a number of, a great deal of |
| Distributives | each, every, either, neither |
| Numerals | cardinal (one, two, three…) and ordinal (first, second, last, next…) |
| Interrogatives | which, what, whose |
| Pre-determiners | all, both, half, double, twice, such, quite, rather, what (exclamative) |
| Others | another, other, such, certain, own (after possessive) |

## 4.3 Order of Determiners

When more than one determiner is used, they follow a fixed order:

**Pre-determiner → Central determiner → Post-determiner → Adjective(s) → Noun**

| Pre | Central | Post | Adjective | Noun |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| all | the | — | — | students |
| both | my | — | elder | brothers |
| half | the | — | — | class |
| — | the | first | three | chapters |
| — | these | two | old | houses |
| — | my | many | good | friends |
| twice | the | — | — | price |
| such | a | — | lovely | day |
| what | a | — | beautiful | scene |

::: ex
- ✔ **All the** students were present. ✘ *The all students*
- ✔ **Both my** brothers are doctors. ✘ *My both brothers*
- ✔ **The first two** chapters are easy. (In modern English *the first two* is preferred to *the two first*.)
- ✔ **Half the** class was absent. / **Half of the** class…
- ✔ It costs **twice the** price.
- ✔ It was **such a** lovely day. ✘ *a such lovely day*
- ✔ It was **quite a** good show. / **a quite** good show (both accepted; *quite a* is more common)
:::

## 4.4 Articles

Articles (*a, an, the*) are the most common determiners. They are treated in full detail in **Chapter 05**.

## 4.5 Demonstratives: *this, that, these, those*

| Near | Far |
|---|---|
| **this** (singular) | **that** (singular) |
| **these** (plural) | **those** (plural) |

::: ex
- **This** book is mine; **that** book is yours.
- **These** mangoes are sweet; **those** mangoes are sour.
- ✘ **This** news **are** good. ✔ **This** news **is** good.
- ✘ **These kind of** books are useless. ✔ **This kind of** book is useless. / **These kinds of** books are useless.
- ✘ **That** sort of men are dangerous. ✔ **That sort of man is** dangerous. / **Men of that sort are** dangerous.
:::

**Those** is used to avoid repeating a plural noun in comparisons: *The streets of Dhaka are busier than* **those** *of Rajshahi.* (✘ *than Rajshahi*). **That** replaces a singular noun: *The climate of Sylhet is wetter than* **that** *of Rajshahi.*

## 4.6 Possessive Determiners

*my, your, his, her, its, our, their, whose, one's* (and nouns in the possessive case). They show ownership.

::: ex
- **My** pen, **her** bag, **its** tail, **their** house, **Rahim's** car
- ✘ **It's** wings are broken. ✔ **Its** wings are broken.
- ✘ Everyone should do **their** duty. (informal) ✔ Everyone should do **his or her** duty / **his** duty (traditional exam answer). (See Chapter 08 and Chapter 23.)
- ✘ One should respect **his** parents. ✔ One should respect **one's** parents. (Traditional British usage; *his* is accepted in American English.)
:::

## 4.7 Quantifiers

### 4.7.1 *Some* and *Any*

| *Some* | *Any* |
|---|---|
| Affirmative sentences: *I have* **some** *money.* | Negative sentences: *I don't have* **any** *money.* |
| Offers and requests (expecting *yes*): *Would you like* **some** *tea? Could you lend me* **some** *money?* | Questions (neutral): *Do you have* **any** *questions?* |
| — | Conditional: *If you need* **any** *help, call me.* |
| — | Affirmative meaning "it does not matter which": **Any** *child can do this.* *Take* **any** *pen you like.* |

::: ex
- ✘ I don't have **some** friends here. ✔ I don't have **any** friends here.
- ✔ **Some** people believe in ghosts. (= certain people)
- ✔ **Some** boy has broken the window. (= an unknown boy — singular use)
- ✔ It happened **some** twenty years ago. (= about — adverb)
- ✘ I have not **no** money. (double negative) ✔ I have **no** money. / I do not have **any** money.
:::

### 4.7.2 *Much* and *Many*

- **Many** + plural countable: *many books, many people.*
- **Much** + uncountable: *much water, much time.*
- In affirmative sentences, *much* sounds formal; *a lot of / plenty of / a great deal of* is more natural: *He has* **a lot of** *money.* In negatives and questions *much* is normal: *I don't have* **much** *time. Is there* **much** *traffic?*

::: ex
- ✘ **Much** people attended. ✔ **Many** people attended.
- ✘ **Many** furniture ✔ **Much** furniture
- ✔ **Many a** student **has** failed. (*many a* + singular noun + singular verb — see Chapter 23)
- ✔ **A great many** people came. (*a great many* + plural)
:::

### 4.7.3 *Few, A Few, The Few* and *Little, A Little, The Little*

This is one of the **most frequently tested** areas in Bangladeshi exams.

| Word | Used with | Meaning | Feeling |
|---|---|---|---|
| **few** | countable plural | almost none, not many | negative |
| **a few** | countable plural | some, a small number | positive |
| **the few** | countable plural | the not many that exist — all of them | "all of the small number" |
| **little** | uncountable | almost nothing, hardly any | negative |
| **a little** | uncountable | some, a small amount | positive |
| **the little** | uncountable | the small amount that exists — all of it | "all of the small amount" |

::: ex
- **Few** people are honest nowadays. (= hardly anyone — negative)
- **A few** people came to help. (= some came — positive)
- **The few** friends he had deserted him. (= all of his small number of friends)
- There is **little** hope of his recovery. (= almost no hope)
- There is **a little** hope of his recovery. (= some hope)
- **The little** money he had was stolen. (= all of his small amount of money)
- ✔ "**A little** learning is a dangerous thing." (Alexander Pope, *An Essay on Criticism*) — here *a little* = some, a small amount.
:::

::: tip
**Exam Tip — Spot the feeling.** If the sentence is negative in feeling, choose *few/little*; if it is positive, choose *a few/a little*; if the sentence speaks of *all* of a small quantity (usually followed by a relative clause such as *he had*), choose *the few/the little*.

- *He has* **____** *friends, so he feels lonely.* → **few**
- *He has* **____** *friends who help him in danger.* → **a few**
- *He spent* **____** *money he had.* → **the little**
:::

::: note
**Quite a few** (= a fairly large number) and **not a few** (= many) are positive: *Quite a few students got GPA 5.* **Only a few / only a little** = very few / very little, but used with *a*: ✔ *Only a few people came.* ✘ *Only few people came* (informal).
:::

### 4.7.4 *Fewer / Less* and *More / Most*

- **Fewer** (comparative of *few*) + plural countable: *fewer mistakes, fewer students.*
- **Less** (comparative of *little*) + uncountable: *less sugar, less noise.*
- **More / most** with both: *more books, more water; most people, most of the time.*

::: ex
- ✘ **Less** students passed this year. ✔ **Fewer** students passed this year.
- ✘ **Most of** students are poor. ✔ **Most students** are poor. / **Most of the** students are poor.
- ✘ **The most** people like music. ✔ **Most** people like music.
:::

### 4.7.5 *Enough*

*Enough* comes **before** a noun but **after** an adjective or adverb:

::: ex
- We have **enough** money / **enough** chairs.
- He is **old enough** to vote. ✘ *enough old*
- She did not run **fast enough**.
:::

### 4.7.6 *Several, A Number of, A Lot of, Plenty of*

- **Several** (= more than two but not many) + plural countable: *several times, several books.*
- **A number of** + plural countable + plural verb: *A number of students* **were** *absent.*
- **The number of** + plural noun + **singular** verb: *The number of students* **is** *increasing.*
- **A lot of / lots of / plenty of** + countable or uncountable; verb agrees with the noun: *A lot of* **water is** *wasted. A lot of* **people are** *waiting.*
- **A great deal of / a large amount of** + uncountable: *a great deal of* **money**.

### 4.7.7 *All, Both, Half*

- **All** (whole quantity) + plural or uncountable: *all the students, all the milk, all day.* ✔ *All of us / all of them* (with pronouns, *of* is required). ✘ *All we* → ✔ *We all* / *All of us*.
- **Both** (= the two together) + plural: *both the boys / both boys / both of the boys / both of them.* ✘ *Both of them are not present* (ambiguous) → ✔ *Neither of them is present.*
- **Half** + *(of) the* + noun: *half the class, half an hour* (✘ *a half hour* is AmE), *half of them.*

::: ex
- ✘ **Both** Rahim **as well as** Karim came. ✔ **Both** Rahim **and** Karim came.
- ✘ **The both** brothers are clever. ✔ **Both the** brothers are clever.
- ✘ **All** of students passed. ✔ **All (of) the** students passed. / **All** students passed.
:::

### 4.7.8 *No* and *None*

- **No** is a determiner (needs a noun): *There is* **no** *water. He has* **no** *friends.*
- **None** is a pronoun (stands alone): **None** *of them came.* *I wanted some milk, but there was* **none**.
- ✘ *None student came.* ✔ *No student came. / None of the students came.*
- *None* takes a singular verb in formal style when it means *not one* (**None of them is** *present*), but a plural verb is also accepted with plural nouns (**None of them are** *present*). Exam keys usually prefer the singular. (See Chapter 23.)

## 4.8 Distributives: *each, every, either, neither*

Distributives refer to members of a group **one at a time**.

| Word | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **each** | two or more, considered **individually** | *Each* boy has a book. / *Each of* the two boys… |
| **every** | three or more, considered **as a whole group, one by one** | *Every* student must attend. |
| **either** | one or the other **of two** | You may take *either* road. |
| **neither** | not one nor the other **of two** | *Neither* answer is correct. |

::: rule
All four take a **singular noun** and a **singular verb**: *Each student* **has**…, *Every citizen* **is**…, *Either answer* **is** *correct*, *Neither of the boys* **was** *present.*
:::

::: ex
- ✘ **Every** students **are** present. ✔ **Every** student **is** present.
- ✘ **Each of** the girls **have** a pen. ✔ **Each of** the girls **has** a pen.
- ✘ **Every of** the students ✔ **Every one of** the students / **Each of** the students (*every* cannot be followed directly by *of*).
- ✘ **Either of** the three boys can go. ✔ **Any of** the three boys can go. / **Any one** of the three… (*either/neither* for two only)
- ✘ **Neither of** the four… ✔ **None of** the four…
- ✔ **Each** and **every** member **was** consulted. (emphatic; singular verb)
- ✔ He comes **every** other day (= on alternate days). / **every** few days / **every** three hours (*every* + number + plural noun = interval).
- ✔ There are trees on **either side** of the road. (= on both sides — *either side* takes a singular noun)
:::

::: note
**Each** can come after the subject: *The boys* **each** *have a pen* (here the verb agrees with *the boys* — plural). Compare: **Each of the boys has** *a pen.* Also: *They gave us ten taka* **each**.
:::

## 4.9 Numerals as Determiners

- **Cardinal numbers** (*one, two, three, hundred*) tell **how many**: *two pens, five hundred taka.*
- **Ordinal numbers** (*first, second, third, last, next*) tell **position/order**: *the first boy, the third chapter.* They normally take *the*.

::: ex
- ✘ He was **first** to come. → ✔ He was **the first** to come.
- ✘ **The** chapter **ten** → ✔ **Chapter ten** / **the tenth** chapter (no *the* with "noun + cardinal"): *page 10, chapter 3, room 12, gate 2, World War II.*
- ✘ **The first two** boys / **the two first** boys → modern standard: **the first two** boys (the first two in order).
- ✔ **Hundreds of** people / **two hundred** people (✘ two hundreds people)
- ✔ **Dozens of** eggs / **three dozen** eggs (✘ three dozens eggs)
- ✔ **One and a half** hours / **an hour and a half**
:::

## 4.10 Interrogative Determiners: *which, what, whose*

- **Which** — choice from a limited, known set: **Which** *colour do you prefer, red or blue?*
- **What** — choice from an open/unlimited set: **What** *colour is your car?*
- **Whose** — possession: **Whose** *pen is this?*

## 4.11 *Other, Another, Others, The Other*

| Form | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **another** | one more / a different one (singular countable) | Have **another** cup of tea. Give me **another** pen. |
| **other** | + plural or uncountable noun | **Other** people may disagree. |
| **the other** | the remaining one(s) of a definite set | I have two pens; one is red, **the other** is blue. |
| **others** | pronoun = other people/things | Some like tea, **others** like coffee. |
| **the others** | the remaining ones (pronoun) | Rahim stayed; **the others** left. |
| **each other** | between two (traditionally) | The two brothers love **each other**. |
| **one another** | among more than two (traditionally) | The students helped **one another**. |

::: ex
- ✘ I need **another** books. ✔ I need **other** books / **another** book / **more** books.
- ✘ Help the **others** people. ✔ Help **other** people. / Help **others**.
- ✔ *Wait* **another two days**. (*another* + number + plural noun is correct when the period is treated as one unit.)
- ✔ **Every other** day (= alternate days); **one after the other**; **on the other hand**.
:::

## 4.12 *Such* and *What* (Exclamative / Pre-determiner)

::: ex
- It was **such a** good film that I saw it twice.
- They are **such** good friends.
- **What a** beautiful day! / **What** lovely flowers! / **What** nonsense!
- ✘ **Such** a beautiful **a** girl. ✔ **Such a** beautiful girl. / **So** beautiful **a** girl (formal).
:::

## 4.13 *Own*

*Own* is used after a possessive to emphasise ownership: *my own house, his own car.* ✘ *an own house* ✔ *a house of his own*.

## 4.14 Common Errors with Determiners

::: errs
1. ✘ **Every** boys must come. ✔ **Every** boy must come.
2. ✘ He has **less** friends than his brother. ✔ He has **fewer** friends than his brother.
3. ✘ **Both of** them are not present. ✔ **Neither of** them is present.
4. ✘ I have **little** books. ✔ I have **few** books.
5. ✘ **Each** of the student **were** given a prize. ✔ **Each** of the students **was** given a prize.
6. ✘ **Either of** the ten applicants may be selected. ✔ **Any** of the ten applicants…
7. ✘ He gave me **a few** advice. ✔ He gave me **a little** advice.
8. ✘ **All the both** boys came. ✔ **Both the** boys came.
9. ✘ I don't have **no** time. ✔ I don't have **any** time. / I have **no** time.
10. ✘ **The most** of the people are poor. ✔ **Most** of the people are poor.
11. ✘ He is **enough strong** to lift it. ✔ He is **strong enough** to lift it.
12. ✘ **These** kind of mistakes **are** common. ✔ **This** kind of mistake **is** common.
13. ✘ I met him **the last** week. ✔ I met him **last** week. (no article with *last/next* + time word in relation to now)
14. ✘ **None** student was present. ✔ **No** student was present.
15. ✘ I bought **three dozens** pens. ✔ I bought **three dozen** pens.
16. ✘ The house has **much** rooms. ✔ The house has **many** rooms.
17. ✘ **Little** knowledge is dangerous. ✔ **A little** knowledge is a dangerous thing.
18. ✘ The bus leaves **every two hour**. ✔ The bus leaves **every two hours**. (*every* + number + plural noun)
19. ✘ He is one of **my** friend. ✔ He is one of **my friends**. (*one of* + plural noun)
20. ✘ I have read **this both** books. ✔ I have read **both these** books.
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Quantifier Logic:** The GMAT tests *the number of / the amount of*, *fewer/less*, and *each/every* agreement. Example: ✘ *"Each of the proposals* **have** *merit, but neither* **are** *practical."* ✔ *"Each of the proposals* **has** *merit, but neither* **is** *practical."* GRE Reading passages exploit *few* (negative) vs *a few* (positive) to reverse the author's attitude — "Few critics have praised the novel" means the novel was largely **not** praised.
:::
