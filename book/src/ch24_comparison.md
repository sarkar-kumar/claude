# Comparison & Degree of Comparison {#ch24}

[Part A · Grammar]{.chpart}

## 24.1 What Is Degree of Comparison?

Adjectives and adverbs of quality change their form to show **how much** of a quality a person or thing has compared with others. These forms are called **degrees of comparison**. There are three degrees:

| Degree | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive** | the simple quality; no comparison, or comparison of **equality** (*as … as*) | Rahim is **tall**. / Rahim is **as tall as** Karim. |
| **Comparative** | compares **two** persons/things (or one with the rest of a group) | Rahim is **taller than** Karim. |
| **Superlative** | compares **more than two**; shows the highest degree | Rahim is **the tallest** boy in the class. |

## 24.2 Formation of Comparatives and Superlatives

### 24.2.1 Regular Formation

| Rule | Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|---|
| One-syllable words: add *-er, -est* | tall, great, small, rich | taller, greater, smaller, richer | tallest, greatest, smallest, richest |
| Ending in *-e*: add *-r, -st* | wise, brave, large, fine | wiser, braver, larger, finer | wisest, bravest, largest, finest |
| Short vowel + single consonant: double the consonant | big, hot, fat, thin, sad, wet | bigger, hotter, fatter, thinner, sadder, wetter | biggest, hottest, fattest, thinnest, saddest, wettest |
| Consonant + *y*: *y → i* + *-er, -est* | happy, easy, heavy, dry, busy | happier, easier, heavier, drier, busier | happiest, easiest, heaviest, driest, busiest |
| Vowel + *y*: add *-er, -est* | gay, grey, coy | gayer, greyer, coyer | gayest, greyest, coyest |
| Two-syllable words ending in *-er, -ow, -le, -y*: often *-er, -est* | clever, narrow, simple, gentle, pretty | cleverer, narrower, simpler, gentler, prettier | cleverest, narrowest, simplest, gentlest, prettiest |
| Most words of two or more syllables: *more, most* | beautiful, careful, intelligent, famous, useful, honest | more beautiful, more careful… | most beautiful, most careful… |
| Adjectives in *-ful, -less, -ous, -ive, -ing, -ed, -ish, -al, -ic* | hopeful, useless, famous, active, boring, tired | more hopeful … | most hopeful … |
| Adverbs in *-ly* | quickly, carefully | more quickly | most quickly |

::: note
**Both forms possible:** *common (commoner / more common), polite, quiet, pleasant, stupid, handsome, cruel, clever, narrow, simple*. Never mix the two methods: ✘ *more taller*, ✘ *most cleverest*, ✘ *more better*.
:::

### 24.2.2 Irregular Comparison

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| good / well | better | best | |
| bad / ill / evil / badly | worse | worst | |
| much / many | more | most | *much* (quantity), *many* (number) |
| little | less / lesser | least | *less* (quantity); *lesser* (smaller in importance: *the lesser evil*) |
| few | fewer | fewest | number |
| far | farther / further | farthest / furthest | distance / additional |
| late | later / latter | latest / last | time / order |
| old | older / elder | oldest / eldest | general / family members |
| near | nearer | nearest / next | distance / order |
| fore | former | foremost / first | |
| in | inner | inmost / innermost | |
| out | outer / utter | outmost / outermost / utmost / uttermost | |
| up | upper | upmost / uppermost | |
| top | — | topmost | |
| hind | hinder | hindmost | |

### 24.2.3 Pairs with Different Meanings

| Pair | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **later / latter** | *later* = after in time; *latter* = second of two | He came **later**. / Of tea and coffee, I prefer **the latter**. |
| **latest / last** | *latest* = most recent; *last* = final in a series | the **latest** news / the **last** chapter / his **last** novel (he wrote no more) |
| **older / elder** | *older* (persons & things, with *than*); *elder* (family members, before a noun, never with *than*) | He is **older than** me. / my **elder** brother |
| **oldest / eldest** | same distinction | the **oldest** building / my **eldest** son |
| **farther / further** | *farther* = distance; *further* = additional (and distance in BrE) | Dhaka is **farther** than Gazipur. / **further** information |
| **nearest / next** | *nearest* = distance; *next* = order | the **nearest** hospital / the **next** stop |
| **less / fewer** | quantity / number | **less** water / **fewer** books |
| **less / lesser** | *less* = quantity; *lesser* = less important | **less** time / the **lesser** of two evils |

## 24.3 Absolute Adjectives — No Comparison

Some adjectives express a quality that cannot be greater or less: *unique, perfect, complete, round, square, empty, full, dead, universal, eternal, infinite, supreme, extreme, ideal, chief, matchless, impossible, excellent, entire, total, unanimous, absolute, final, fatal, golden, wooden* (material adjectives).

::: ex
- ✘ This is **the most unique** idea. ✔ This is a **unique** idea.
- ✘ This circle is **more round** than that. ✔ This circle is **more nearly round** than that.
- ✘ He is **the most perfect** man. ✔ He is a **perfect** man.
- ✘ It was **the most fatal** accident. ✔ It was a **fatal** accident.
:::

## 24.4 Rules for Using the Three Degrees

### 24.4.1 Positive Degree

**(a) Equality — *as + positive + as*:**

::: ex
- Rahim is **as tall as** Karim.
- She sings **as sweetly as** a nightingale.
- He is **as good a player as** his brother. (*as + adj + a + noun + as*)
:::

**(b) Inequality — *not so / not as + positive + as*:**

::: ex
- Karim is **not so tall as** Rahim. (also *not as tall as*)
:::

**(c) Multiples — *twice / three times / half as … as*:**

::: ex
- This room is **twice as large as** that one. (✘ twice larger than — avoid in exam keys)
- He earns **three times as much as** I do.
:::

### 24.4.2 Comparative Degree

**(a) Comparative + *than*:**

::: ex
- Rahim is **taller than** Karim.
- Gold is **more precious than** silver.
- He runs **faster than** I (do).
:::

**(b) Comparing one with the rest of the group — use *any other / all other / any of the other*:**

::: rule
When a member is compared with **its own group**, add **other** (or *else*); otherwise the thing is compared with itself.

✘ *Rahim is taller than any boy in the class.* (Rahim is also a boy in the class.)

✔ *Rahim is taller than* **any other** *boy in the class.*

✔ *Rahim is taller than* **all the other** *boys in the class.*

✔ *Dhaka is bigger than* **any other** *city in Bangladesh.*

✔ *Dhaka is bigger than* **any** *city in India.* (no *other* — different group)
:::

**(c) *The* + comparative + *of the two*:**

::: ex
- Rahim is **the taller of the two** brothers. (✘ the tallest of the two)
- This is **the better of the two** plans.
:::

**(d) Parallel increase — *the + comparative …, the + comparative*:**

::: ex
- **The more** you read, **the more** you learn.
- **The higher** you climb, **the colder** it gets.
- **The sooner, the better.**
:::

**(e) Gradual increase — *comparative + and + comparative*:**

::: ex
- It is getting **colder and colder**.
- Prices are rising **higher and higher**.
- The story became **more and more** interesting.
:::

**(f) Intensifiers with comparatives: *much, far, a lot, a little, slightly, still, even, by far* (not *very*):**

::: ex
- ✘ He is **very** better now. ✔ He is **much / far / a lot** better now.
- ✔ This one is **even** cheaper. / **slightly** taller / **a little** older.
:::

**(g) Latin comparatives with *to* (Chapter 10):** *senior to, junior to, superior to, inferior to, prior to, prefer to.*

**(h) Comparatives that do not take *than*:** *elder, former, latter, inner, outer, upper* — these are used only before nouns.

### 24.4.3 Superlative Degree

**(a) *The* + superlative + *of / in*:**

::: ex
- Rahim is **the tallest** boy **in** the class.
- Everest is **the highest** peak **in** the world.
- He is **the best of** all the students.
- ✘ He is **tallest** boy in the class. ✔ He is **the tallest** boy …
:::

**(b) *One of the* + superlative + plural noun:**

::: ex
- Dhaka is **one of the most populous cities** in the world. (✘ one of the most populous city)
- Nazrul was **one of the greatest poets** of Bengal.
:::

**(c) *Most* without *the* = very:**

::: ex
- It is **a most** interesting book. (= very interesting)
- You are **most** welcome.
:::

**(d) *By far / much / quite / the very* with superlatives:**

::: ex
- She is **by far the best** student. / This is **the very best** quality.
:::

**(e) Superlative + relative clause with *that* + present perfect + *ever*:**

::: ex
- This is **the best film that** I **have ever seen**.
:::

## 24.5 Transformation of Degrees (Changing One Degree into Another)

This is a regular item in SSC, HSC, admission and BCS written tests. The meaning must remain **exactly the same**. Learn the following patterns.

### 24.5.1 Superlative → Comparative → Positive

**Pattern A — Superlative with *the … in/of* (one thing is the highest in its group):**

| Degree | Sentence |
|---|---|
| Superlative | Rahim is **the tallest** boy in the class. |
| Comparative | Rahim is **taller than any other** boy in the class. / Rahim is **taller than all other** boys in the class. |
| Positive | **No other** boy in the class is **as tall as** Rahim. |

**Pattern B — *One of the* + superlative (one of the highest):**

| Degree | Sentence |
|---|---|
| Superlative | Dhaka is **one of the biggest** cities in Asia. |
| Comparative | Dhaka is **bigger than most other** cities in Asia. |
| Positive | **Very few** cities in Asia are **as big as** Dhaka. |

**Pattern C — Superlative with *not the* (not the highest):**

| Degree | Sentence |
|---|---|
| Superlative | Rahim is **not the best** boy in the class. |
| Comparative | Rahim is **not better than some other** boys in the class. / **Some** boys are **better than** Rahim. |
| Positive | Rahim is **not so good as some other** boys in the class. |

### 24.5.2 Comparative ↔ Positive (Two Persons/Things)

| Comparative | Positive |
|---|---|
| Rahim is **taller than** Karim. | Karim is **not so tall as** Rahim. |
| Gold is **more precious than** iron. | Iron is **not so precious as** gold. |
| He is **not taller than** his brother. | His brother is **at least as tall as** he. |
| Shakib is **better than** Tamim. | Tamim is **not so good as** Shakib. |

### 24.5.3 Positive with *as … as* ↔ Comparative

| Positive | Comparative |
|---|---|
| Karim is **as tall as** Rahim. | Rahim is **not taller than** Karim. |
| He is **as wise as** Solomon. | Solomon is **not wiser than** he. |

### 24.5.4 Special Patterns

| Sentence type | Example (all three degrees) |
|---|---|
| With *very few* | **Positive:** Very few poets are as great as Tagore. → **Comparative:** Tagore is greater than most other poets. → **Superlative:** Tagore is one of the greatest poets. |
| With *no other* | **Positive:** No other metal is as useful as iron. → **Comparative:** Iron is more useful than any other metal. → **Superlative:** Iron is the most useful metal. |
| With *at least* | **Positive:** A is at least as good as B. → **Comparative:** B is not better than A. |
| Negative superlative | **Superlative:** He is not the richest man in the village. → **Comparative:** Some men in the village are richer than he. → **Positive:** He is not so rich as some other men in the village. |
| With *some other* / *few other* | **Comparative:** Mango is sweeter than many other fruits. → **Superlative:** Mango is one of the sweetest fruits. → **Positive:** Very few fruits are as sweet as mango. |

::: tip
**Exam Tip — Three checks in transformation:** (1) Keep the meaning identical (especially *one of the* → *very few* / *most other*). (2) Use *other* when comparing within the same group. (3) Use *so … as* in negative positive sentences (*not so tall as*), *as … as* in affirmative ones.
:::

## 24.6 Comparing Like with Like (Faulty Comparison)

Two things being compared must be **of the same kind**. This is the most important comparison rule in the SAT, GRE and GMAT, and it is widely tested in Bangladesh as well.

::: ex
- ✘ The climate of Sylhet is wetter than Rajshahi. (climate vs city) ✔ The climate of Sylhet is wetter than **that of** Rajshahi.
- ✘ The streets of Dhaka are busier than Khulna. ✔ … than **those of** Khulna.
- ✘ His handwriting is better than his brother. ✔ His handwriting is better than **his brother's**.
- ✘ The salary of a doctor is higher than a teacher. ✔ … than **that of** a teacher. / … than **a teacher's**.
- ✘ Like Rahim, Karim's car is red. ✔ Like Rahim's car, Karim's car is red. / Like Rahim, Karim has a red car.
:::

## 24.7 Case of Pronouns after *than* and *as*

The pronoun after *than / as* takes the case it would have if the sentence were completed:

::: ex
- He is taller than **I** (am). ✔ (formal) / *than me* (informal)
- She is as clever as **he** (is).
- I like you better than **he** (likes you). → meaning: he likes you less.
- I like you better than **him** (than I like him). → meaning: I like him less.
:::

## 24.8 Common Errors in Comparison

::: errs
1. ✘ He is **more taller** than I. ✔ He is **taller** than I.
2. ✘ She is **the most cleverest** girl. ✔ She is **the cleverest** girl.
3. ✘ Rahim is **taller than any** boy in the class. ✔ … **taller than any other** boy …
4. ✘ He is **the tallest of the two**. ✔ He is **the taller of the two**.
5. ✘ He is **senior than** me. ✔ He is **senior to** me.
6. ✘ I prefer tea **than** coffee. ✔ I prefer tea **to** coffee.
7. ✘ He is **very** taller than his brother. ✔ He is **much** taller than his brother.
8. ✘ This is the **most unique** opportunity. ✔ This is a **unique** opportunity.
9. ✘ She is **one of the best student** in the class. ✔ … **one of the best students** …
10. ✘ Karim is **not as tall than** Rahim. ✔ Karim is **not so (as) tall as** Rahim.
11. ✘ The population of Dhaka is larger than Chattogram. ✔ … larger than **that of** Chattogram.
12. ✘ He is **elder than** me. ✔ He is **older than** me.
13. ✘ This is the **latest** chapter of the book (= final). ✔ This is the **last** chapter of the book.
14. ✘ Please give me **farther** information. ✔ … **further** information.
15. ✘ He has **less** friends than me. ✔ He has **fewer** friends than me.
16. ✘ The **more** you work, **more** you earn. ✔ **The more** you work, **the more** you earn.
17. ✘ He is **more wiser** than his brother. ✔ He is **wiser** than his brother.
18. ✘ Of the two plans, this is **the best**. ✔ … this is **the better**.
19. ✘ He is **as tall like** his father. ✔ He is **as tall as** his father.
20. ✘ This room is **twice larger** than that. ✔ This room is **twice as large as** that.
21. ✘ He is the **most perfect** man I know. ✔ He is the **most nearly perfect** man I know. / a **perfect** man
22. ✘ No other boy in the class is **so tall than** Rahim. ✔ … **as tall as** Rahim.
23. ✘ He works **more harder** than me. ✔ He works **harder** than me.
24. ✘ Rice is **cheaper** in Bangladesh **than India**. ✔ Rice is cheaper in Bangladesh **than in** India.
25. ✘ He is **superior than** me in rank. ✔ He is **superior to** me in rank.
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Comparisons:** The GMAT's favourite comparison errors are (1) **illogical comparison** (✘ *"Unlike the economy of Japan, Bangladesh relies on garments"* → ✔ *Unlike Japan, Bangladesh…* or *Unlike Japan's economy, Bangladesh's economy…*), (2) **like vs as** (*like* + noun; *as* + clause: ✔ *"Like her mother, she is a doctor"*, ✔ *"She works hard, as her mother did"*), (3) **ambiguous comparison** (✘ *"I like him more than my brother"* — than my brother likes him or than I like my brother?), and (4) **twice as … as / more … than** idioms. On the GRE, comparative words like *more, less, rather than, unlike* are key "contrast signals" in Text Completion.
:::
