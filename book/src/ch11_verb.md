## 11.1 Definition

A **verb** is a word that says something about a person or thing — what it **does**, what **is done** to it, or what it **is** (its state). The word comes from the Latin *verbum*, "word", because the verb is the most important word in a sentence: **no sentence is complete without a finite verb**.

::: ex
- The baby **cries**. (action)
- The thief **was caught**. (something done to the subject)
- Allah **is** merciful. (state of being)
- She **has** two cars. (possession)
- He **seems** tired. (linking)
:::

## 11.2 Finite and Non-finite Verbs

| Finite verb | Non-finite verb |
|---|---|
| Limited by the **subject** (number, person) and by **tense** | Not limited by subject or tense |
| Can form a sentence by itself with a subject | Cannot form a sentence by itself |
| *He* **goes** *to school. They* **went** *home. She* **is** *reading.* | **To go** (infinitive), **going** (gerund / present participle), **gone** (past participle) |

::: ex
- He **wants** (finite) **to learn** (non-finite) English.
- I **saw** (finite) him **crossing** (non-finite) the road.
- **Having finished** (non-finite) the work, he **went** (finite) home.
- ✘ He **going** to school. ✔ He **is going** / He **goes** to school.
:::

Non-finite verbs (infinitive, gerund, participle) are treated fully in **Chapter 14**.

## 11.3 Principal (Main) and Auxiliary (Helping) Verbs

- **Principal verbs** carry the main meaning: *eat, go, write, think, play.*
- **Auxiliary verbs** help principal verbs to form tenses, voice, mood, questions and negatives.

| Auxiliary type | Members | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary auxiliaries** | *be* (am, is, are, was, were, be, being, been), *have* (have, has, had), *do* (do, does, did) | continuous tenses (*is going*), passive (*was written*), perfect tenses (*has gone*), questions/negatives/emphasis (*Do you know? I did not go. I do love her.*) |
| **Modal auxiliaries** | can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must, ought to, need, dare, used to | ability, permission, possibility, obligation, etc. (Chapter 13) |

::: note
*Be, have* and *do* can also be **principal verbs**: *He* **is** *a doctor* (main verb). *I* **have** *a car* (main verb). *He* **does** *his homework* (main verb). In *He* **has done** *it*, *has* is auxiliary and *done* is principal.
:::

## 11.4 Transitive and Intransitive Verbs

### 11.4.1 Transitive Verbs

A **transitive verb** passes its action from the subject to an **object**. Ask *"what?"* or *"whom?"* after the verb; if there is an answer, the verb is transitive.

::: ex
- He **wrote** a letter. (wrote what? → a letter)
- The police **arrested** the thief. (arrested whom? → the thief)
- She **loves** her parents.
:::

**Double object (ditransitive):** some verbs take two objects — an **indirect object** (usually a person, *to/for whom*) and a **direct object** (usually a thing): *give, lend, send, show, tell, teach, buy, offer, promise, pay, bring, ask, write.*

::: ex
- He **gave me** (IO) **a book** (DO). = He gave a book **to me**.
- She **bought her son** a bicycle. = She bought a bicycle **for her son**.
- The teacher **asked him** a question.
:::

### 11.4.2 Intransitive Verbs

An **intransitive verb** does not take an object; the action stays with the subject.

::: ex
- The baby **sleeps**. / The sun **rises**. / Birds **fly**. / He **died** yesterday. / She **laughed** loudly.
- (*loudly, yesterday* are adverbs, not objects.)
:::

**Intransitive verbs cannot be put into the passive voice:** ✘ *The baby was slept.*

### 11.4.3 Verbs Used Both Transitively and Intransitively

| Verb | Transitive | Intransitive |
|---|---|---|
| ring | He **rang** the bell. | The bell **rang**. |
| open | She **opened** the door. | The door **opened**. |
| break | He **broke** the glass. | The glass **broke**. |
| move | They **moved** the table. | The table **moved**. |
| stop | The driver **stopped** the bus. | The bus **stopped**. |
| burn | He **burnt** the papers. | The candle **burns**. |
| fly | The boy **flies** a kite. | Birds **fly**. |
| run | He **runs** a business. | He **runs** fast. |
| grow | Farmers **grow** rice. | Rice **grows** in Bangladesh. |
| sell | They **sell** books. | These books **sell** well. (= are sold) |
| read | I **read** the newspaper. | This book **reads** well. (= reads pleasantly) |
| walk | He **walks** his dog. | He **walks** every morning. |

### 11.4.4 Intransitive Verbs Used Transitively

1. **Cognate object** — an object with the same meaning (or same root) as the verb:

::: ex
- He **lived a happy life**. / She **died a peaceful death**. / He **fought a good fight**. / She **sang a sweet song**. / He **slept a sound sleep**. / They **ran a race**. / She **smiled a sweet smile**. / He **dreamt a strange dream**.
:::

2. **Causative use** — the subject *causes* someone/something to do the action:

::: ex
- The horse **walks**. → He **walks the horse**. (makes it walk)
- The boat **floats**. → They **float the boat**.
- He **ran** fast. → He **runs a factory**.
- Birds **fly**. → Children **fly kites**.
:::

3. **With a preposition** (prepositional verbs): *laugh at, look at, look for, listen to, wait for, depend on, think of* — the whole group is transitive: *Everybody* **laughed at** *him* → *He* **was laughed at** *by everybody.*

### 11.4.5 Transitive Verbs Used Intransitively

::: ex
- The cake **cuts** easily. / These clothes **wash** well. / The door **won't shut**. / The book **sells** well.
:::

## 11.5 Linking (Copular) Verbs and Complements

A **linking verb** does not show action; it **links** the subject to a word that describes or renames it — the **complement**.

*be, become, seem, appear, look, feel, sound, smell, taste, grow, turn, get, remain, stay, keep, prove, fall (asleep/ill).*

::: ex
- He **is** a doctor. (noun complement)
- She **looks** beautiful. (adjective complement)
- The milk **turned** sour. / He **fell** ill. / The weather **grew** cold.
- He **remained** silent. / The food **tastes** delicious.
:::

::: rule
**Adjective, not adverb, after a linking verb:** ✘ *She looks* **beautifully**. ✔ *She looks* **beautiful**. ✘ *The rose smells* **sweetly**. ✔ *The rose smells* **sweet**. ✘ *I feel* **badly**. ✔ *I feel* **bad**.
:::

**Verbs of incomplete predication** need a complement to complete their meaning:

- **Intransitive verbs of incomplete predication** (need a subjective complement): *He became* **king**. *She seems* **happy**.
- **Factitive verbs** (transitive verbs that need an **objective complement**): *make, elect, appoint, call, name, choose, think, consider, crown, declare, find, keep, paint, render.*

::: ex
- They **made** him **captain**. (*him* = object; *captain* = objective complement)
- The people **elected** her **President**.
- We **call** him **Babu**.
- The news **made** her **sad**.
- He **painted** the house **white**.
- They **found** the door **open**.
- I **consider** him **honest**. (✘ consider him **as** honest — see Chapter 38 on redundancy)
:::

## 11.6 Regular and Irregular Verbs

- **Regular (weak) verbs** form their past tense and past participle by adding **-ed / -d / -t**: *walk – walked – walked; love – loved – loved; dwell – dwelt – dwelt.*
- **Irregular (strong) verbs** change the inner vowel or the whole word: *sing – sang – sung; go – went – gone; cut – cut – cut.*

### 11.6.1 Spelling Rules for Adding *-ed*

1. Most verbs: add *-ed* — *work → worked, play → played*.
2. Ending in *-e*: add *-d* — *love → loved, agree → agreed*.
3. Consonant + *y*: change *y* to *i* + *-ed* — *carry → carried, study → studied, try → tried*. (Vowel + *y*: *play → played, obey → obeyed*.) Exceptions: *pay → paid, lay → laid, say → said*.
4. One-syllable verb ending in **single vowel + single consonant**: double the consonant — *stop → stopped, plan → planned, rob → robbed, beg → begged*.
5. Two-syllable verb **stressed on the last syllable**: double the consonant — *prefer → preferred, occur → occurred, admit → admitted, refer → referred, permit → permitted, regret → regretted, commit → committed*. (Stress on first syllable — no doubling: *visit → visited, offer → offered, open → opened, happen → happened, benefit → benefited, target → targeted*.)
6. British English doubles final *-l* after a short vowel regardless of stress: *travel → travelled, cancel → cancelled, quarrel → quarrelled, label → labelled, model → modelled* (AmE: *traveled, canceled*).
7. Ending in *-c*: add *-k* — *picnic → picnicked, panic → panicked, mimic → mimicked, traffic → trafficked*.

## 11.7 Stative (State) Verbs and Dynamic (Action) Verbs

**Dynamic verbs** describe actions and can be used in continuous tenses: *run, eat, write, play.* **Stative verbs** describe states — feelings, thoughts, possession, senses — and are **normally not used in continuous tenses**.

| Category | Stative verbs |
|---|---|
| Senses / perception | see, hear, smell, taste, feel, notice, recognise |
| Feelings / emotions | love, like, hate, dislike, prefer, want, wish, desire, adore, detest, mind, need, care |
| Thinking / opinion | know, believe, think (= believe), understand, realise, suppose, mean, remember, forget, doubt, imagine (= suppose), agree, disagree |
| Possession | have (= own), own, possess, belong, owe |
| Being / qualities | be, seem, appear, look (= seem), resemble, consist, contain, cost, weigh, measure, deserve, matter, fit, equal, include, lack, depend |

::: ex
- ✘ I **am knowing** him. ✔ I **know** him.
- ✘ She **is having** a car. ✔ She **has** a car.
- ✘ This book **is belonging** to me. ✔ This book **belongs** to me.
- ✘ I **am seeing** a bird in the tree. ✔ I **see** / **can see** a bird in the tree.
- ✘ He **is resembling** his father. ✔ He **resembles** his father.
- ✘ I **am understanding** the lesson. ✔ I **understand** the lesson.
:::

::: note
**Dual-use verbs.** Some stative verbs can be continuous when they express an **action**: *I* **am having** *breakfast* (= eating). *She* **is seeing** *a doctor* (= consulting). *I* **am thinking** *of going* (= considering). *He* **is tasting** *the soup* (= testing its taste). *They* **are looking** *at the picture* (action). *I* **am feeling** *better today* (accepted). See also Chapter 21.
:::

## 11.8 Causative Verbs: *make, have, get, let, help*

These verbs show that the subject **causes** someone else to do something.

| Verb | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **make** (force) | make + object + **bare infinitive** | The teacher **made** me **write** it again. (✘ to write) |
| **let** (allow) | let + object + **bare infinitive** | **Let** him **go**. (✘ to go) |
| **have** (arrange) | have + person + **bare infinitive** | I **had** the mechanic **repair** my car. |
| **have** (passive sense) | have + thing + **past participle** | I **had** my car **repaired**. / She **had** her hair **cut**. |
| **get** (persuade) | get + person + **to-infinitive** | I **got** him **to sign** the paper. |
| **get** (passive sense) | get + thing + **past participle** | I **got** my watch **mended**. |
| **help** | help + object + (to) infinitive | He **helped** me (**to**) **carry** the bag. |

::: ex
- ✘ He made me **to laugh**. ✔ He made me **laugh**.
- ✔ In the passive, *to* returns: *I* **was made to** *write it again.* (✘ I was made write)
- ✘ Let him **to** go. ✔ Let him go.
- ✘ I had my car **repair**. ✔ I had my car **repaired**.
- ✘ I got him **sign** the paper. ✔ I got him **to sign** the paper.
:::

**Other verbs followed by object + bare infinitive:** verbs of perception — *see, hear, watch, notice, feel, observe*: *I* **saw** *him* **cross** *the road* (whole action) / *I saw him* **crossing** *the road* (action in progress). Also *bid*: *He* **bade** *me* **go**.

## 11.9 Mood of Verbs

**Mood** is the form of the verb that shows the **manner** in which the action is expressed.

1. **Indicative mood** — states a fact or asks a question: *He* **goes** *to school. Did you* **see** *him?*
2. **Imperative mood** — gives a command, request, advice or prayer: **Shut** *the door.* **Please help** *me.* **Do not smoke**. *May Allah* **bless** *you* (optative).
3. **Subjunctive mood** — expresses a wish, supposition, condition contrary to fact, purpose or demand. It is the trickiest and most tested.

### 11.9.1 The Subjunctive — Forms and Uses

| Use | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Unreal wish (present) | *wish* + past (with *were* for all persons) | I wish I **were** a bird. (✘ was) / I wish I **knew** the answer. |
| Unreal wish (past) | *wish* + past perfect | I wish I **had seen** him. |
| Imaginary condition | *if* + past (*were*) | If I **were** you, I would accept it. |
| *As if / as though* (unreal) | past / past perfect | He talks **as if** he **were** a king. / He looked **as though** he **had seen** a ghost. |
| *It is (high) time* | + past simple | **It is high time** we **left**. (✘ leave) |
| *Would rather / sooner* + another subject | + past | I **would rather** you **went** home now. |
| Demand / suggestion / necessity (mandative) | **bare infinitive** for all persons | I **suggest** that he **go** at once. / It is **necessary** that she **be** present. / The doctor **recommended** that he **take** rest. |
| Prayer / wish (formulaic) | bare form | **Long live** Bangladesh! **God bless** you! **Be** that as it **may**. **Come** what **may**. |
| Purpose with *lest* | *lest* + **should** + verb (or bare form) | Walk carefully **lest** you **should fall**. (✘ lest you should not fall) |

::: tip
**Exam Tip — Mandative subjunctive.** After *suggest, propose, recommend, insist, demand, order, request, urge, require, move* (in a meeting) and *it is necessary / essential / important / vital / imperative that*, the verb in the *that*-clause is the **base form** for all subjects (or *should* + base form in BrE): *He* **insisted** *that she* **attend** *the meeting* (✘ attends). This is also a favourite GMAT rule.
:::

## 11.10 Phrasal Verbs and Prepositional Verbs (Introduction)

A verb + adverb particle or preposition can form a new meaning: *give up* (= quit), *look after* (= take care of), *put off* (= postpone), *bring up* (= rear), *carry on* (= continue). These are listed in **Chapter 47**.

::: ex
- **Separable:** *Put off* the meeting. / *Put* the meeting *off*. / *Put* **it** *off* (✘ put off it — pronoun must go between).
- **Inseparable:** *Look after* the child (✘ look the child after). / *Look after* **him**.
:::

## 11.11 Verbs Followed by Infinitive, Gerund or Both

This is treated in detail in Chapter 14, but the key lists are needed often:

| Followed by **to-infinitive** | Followed by **gerund (-ing)** | Either (often different meaning) |
|---|---|---|
| afford, agree, aim, appear, arrange, attempt, choose, claim, decide, demand, deserve, expect, fail, hesitate, hope, intend, learn, manage, offer, plan, pretend, promise, refuse, seem, threaten, want, wish, would like | admit, appreciate, avoid, consider, delay, deny, dislike, enjoy, escape, finish, forgive, give up, imagine, involve, keep, mind, miss, postpone, practise, quit, recall, recommend, resent, resist, risk, suggest, tolerate, understand, can't help, can't stand, it's no use, it's worth, look forward to, be used to, object to | begin, start, continue, like, love, hate, prefer (little change); **remember, forget, stop, try, regret, mean, go on, need** (meaning changes) |

::: ex
- He **agreed to help** me. / He **avoided meeting** me.
- ✘ I enjoy **to swim**. ✔ I enjoy **swimming**.
- ✘ I look forward to **meet** you. ✔ I look forward to **meeting** you.
- ✘ He suggested **to go** there. ✔ He suggested **going** there. / He suggested **that we (should) go** there.
- He **stopped smoking** (gave up). / He **stopped to smoke** (paused in order to smoke).
- I **remember posting** the letter (I did it earlier). / **Remember to post** the letter (don't forget in future).
:::

## 11.12 Common Errors with Verbs

::: errs
1. ✘ He **didn't went** there. ✔ He **didn't go** there. (After *did*, use the base form.)
2. ✘ **Does** he **plays** football? ✔ **Does** he **play** football?
3. ✘ He **can sings**. ✔ He **can sing**.
4. ✘ The criminal was **hung**. ✔ The criminal was **hanged**.
5. ✘ He **laid** on the grass. ✔ He **lay** on the grass.
6. ✘ The price of rice has **raised**. ✔ The price of rice has **risen**.
7. ✘ He **made** me **to work**. ✔ He **made** me **work**.
8. ✘ I **am knowing** the answer. ✔ I **know** the answer.
9. ✘ I wish I **was** rich. (exam key) ✔ I wish I **were** rich.
10. ✘ It is time we **go** home. ✔ It is time we **went** home.
11. ✘ He **has broke** the glass. ✔ He **has broken** the glass.
12. ✘ She **has wrote** a letter. ✔ She **has written** a letter.
13. ✘ The sun **has rose**. ✔ The sun **has risen**.
14. ✘ He **sung** a song yesterday. ✔ He **sang** a song yesterday.
15. ✘ The ship **sunk** in the storm. ✔ The ship **sank** in the storm. (*sunk* = past participle)
16. ✘ I **seen** him yesterday. ✔ I **saw** him yesterday.
17. ✘ He **drunk** a glass of milk. ✔ He **drank** a glass of milk.
18. ✘ He **founded** the lost book. ✔ He **found** the lost book.
19. ✘ He **enjoyed** at the party. ✔ He **enjoyed himself** at the party.
20. ✘ The doctor suggested that he **takes** rest. ✔ … that he **take** rest / **should take** rest.
21. ✘ He was **borne** in 1971. ✔ He was **born** in 1971. (*borne* = carried: *The cost will be borne by the company.*)
22. ✘ The bird has **flew** away. ✔ The bird has **flown** away.
23. ✘ He **beated** the dog. ✔ He **beat** the dog.
24. ✘ We **discussed about** the matter. ✔ We **discussed** the matter. (*discuss* is transitive — no *about*)
25. ✘ He **ordered for** a cup of tea. ✔ He **ordered** a cup of tea.
26. ✘ He **resembles to** his father. ✔ He **resembles** his father.
27. ✘ He **reached at** the station. ✔ He **reached** the station.
28. ✘ He **entered into** the room. ✔ He **entered** the room. (But *enter into an agreement* ✔)
29. ✘ He **attacked on** the enemy. ✔ He **attacked** the enemy.
30. ✘ Please **return back** the book. ✔ Please **return** the book.

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Verb Form and Mood:** The GMAT tests (1) the **mandative subjunctive** (*require that he* **be**, not *is*), (2) **would vs will** sequence (*He said that he* **would** *come*), (3) correct **past participles** (✘ *had swam* ✔ *had swum*; ✘ *has went* ✔ *has gone*), and (4) **lie/lay** distinctions. The SAT checks that the verb is **finite** in a main clause: ✘ *"The museum, which* **housing** *rare manuscripts, attracts…"* ✔ *"…which* **houses** *rare manuscripts…"*.
:::
