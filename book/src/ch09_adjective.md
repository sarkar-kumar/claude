# Adjective {#ch09}

[Part A · Grammar]{.chpart}

## 9.1 Definition

An **adjective** is a word that **qualifies** (describes, limits or adds to the meaning of) a **noun or pronoun**. The word comes from the Latin *adjectivum*, "something added".

::: ex
- Rahim is a **clever** boy. (*clever* describes *boy*)
- I have **five** pens. (*five* tells how many)
- **This** house is ours. (*this* points out which house)
- He is **happy**. (*happy* describes the pronoun *he*)
:::

## 9.2 Kinds of Adjectives

### 9.2.1 Adjectives of Quality (Descriptive Adjectives)

They show the **kind or quality** of a person or thing and answer *What kind?*

*good, bad, honest, brave, tall, beautiful, red, round, large, wise, cruel, hot, soft, rich, poor, intelligent.*

::: ex
- Dhaka is a **large** city. / He is an **honest** man. / The **red** rose is **beautiful**.
:::

**Proper adjectives** (formed from proper nouns) are a sub-group; they begin with a capital letter: *Bangladeshi, English, Shakespearean, Indian, Chinese, Islamic, Victorian, Elizabethan, Socratic, Herculean (task), Napoleonic.*

### 9.2.2 Adjectives of Quantity

They show **how much** of a thing (used with uncountable nouns) and answer *How much?*

*much, little, a little, no, some, any, enough, sufficient, all, whole, half, great, a lot of.*

::: ex
- He has **much** patience. / There is **little** hope. / I ate **some** rice. / I have **enough** money. / He spent the **whole** day reading.
:::

### 9.2.3 Adjectives of Number (Numeral Adjectives)

They show **how many** or **in what order** and answer *How many?* They are of three types:

1. **Definite numeral adjectives** — show an exact number.
   - *Cardinals:* one, two, three, hundred…
   - *Ordinals:* first, second, third, last…
   - *Multiplicatives:* single, double, triple, twofold, threefold…
2. **Indefinite numeral adjectives** — do not show an exact number: *all, many, few, a few, several, some, any, no, enough, certain, sundry, numerous.*
3. **Distributive numeral adjectives** — refer to each one of a number: *each, every, either, neither.*

::: ex
- I have **two** sisters. / He was the **first** boy. / A **double** bed.
- **Many** people came. / **Several** books are missing. / **Few** students passed.
- **Each** boy got a prize. / **Every** citizen must pay tax. / **Neither** answer is correct.
:::

::: note
Some words (*some, any, enough, all, no, more, most*) can be adjectives of **quantity** or of **number** depending on the noun:

- I ate **some** rice. (quantity — uncountable) / **Some** boys came. (number — countable)
- He has **enough** money. (quantity) / We have **enough** chairs. (number)
- **All** the milk (quantity) / **All** the boys (number)
:::

### 9.2.4 Demonstrative Adjectives

They **point out** which person or thing is meant: *this, that, these, those, such, same, yonder, the former, the latter.*

::: ex
- **This** pen is mine. / **Those** boys are naughty. / I have never seen **such** a sight. / He made the **same** mistake again.
:::

### 9.2.5 Interrogative Adjectives

*What, which, whose* used **with nouns** to ask questions.

::: ex
- **What** colour is it? / **Which** book do you want? / **Whose** car is this?
:::

### 9.2.6 Possessive Adjectives

*my, our, your, his, her, its, their* — show possession (modern grammar: possessive determiners).

::: ex
- **My** house / **Their** school / **Its** colour
:::

### 9.2.7 Emphasizing Adjectives

*own, very* — used for emphasis.

::: ex
- I saw it with my **own** eyes. / This is the **very** book I wanted. / He is the **very** man.
:::

### 9.2.8 Exclamatory Adjectives

*What* used in exclamations: **What** *a genius!* **What** *folly!* **What** *a beautiful scene!*

## 9.3 Position of Adjectives

### 9.3.1 Attributive (before the noun)

*a* **tall** *man, a* **beautiful** *garden, the* **old** *house.*

### 9.3.2 Predicative (after a linking verb)

*The man is* **tall**. *The garden looks* **beautiful**. *He became* **famous**. *The food smells* **good**.

### 9.3.3 Postpositive (immediately after the noun)

Adjectives come **after** the noun in these cases:

1. **With indefinite pronouns** (*something, anything, nothing, someone, everyone*): *something* **new**, *nothing* **important**, *someone* **reliable**. (✘ *new something*)
2. **In some fixed titles and phrases** (from French/Latin): *Heir* **apparent**, *Prince* **Royal**, *Poet* **Laureate**, *Governor* **General**, *Attorney* **General**, *Secretary* **General**, *President* **elect**, *court* **martial**, *God* **Almighty**, *the body* **politic**, *time* **immemorial**, *sum* **total**, *notary* **public**, *things* **Bangladeshi**.
3. **When the adjective has its own complement**: *a man* **proud of his wealth**, *a basket* **full of mangoes**, *a box* **too heavy to lift**.
4. **Adjectives ending in -able/-ible** after superlatives or *every/only*: *the best hotel* **available**, *every means* **possible**, *the only person* **responsible**.
5. **For emphasis or in poetry**: *Mountains* **high**, *rivers* **deep**. / *A man* **brave and honest**.
6. **Measurement**: *ten feet* **tall**, *five years* **old**, *two metres* **wide**.
7. Words like *present, concerned, involved, responsible* change meaning with position: *the* **present** *members* (current) / *the members* **present** (attending); *the* **concerned** *mother* (worried) / *the people* **concerned** (affected).

### 9.3.4 Adjectives Used Only Predicatively

*afraid, alike, alive, alone, ashamed, asleep, awake, aware, ablaze, afloat, akin, content, glad (mostly), ill (BrE in sense of "sick"), well (healthy), unable, worth.*

::: ex
- ✘ an **asleep** baby ✔ a **sleeping** baby / The baby is **asleep**.
- ✘ an **alone** man ✔ a **lonely** man / He lives **alone**.
- ✘ **alike** brothers ✔ **similar** brothers / The brothers are **alike**.
- ✘ an **afraid** child ✔ a **frightened** child / The child is **afraid**.
- ✘ an **alive** fish ✔ a **live** fish / a **living** creature / The fish is **alive**.
:::

### 9.3.5 Adjectives Used Only Attributively

*main, chief, principal, mere, sheer, utter, elder, eldest, former, latter, inner, outer, upper, lower (in sense of position), only, very, live (= living), drunken, sole, total, entire, damn.*

::: ex
- ✘ The reason is **main**. ✔ This is the **main** reason.
- ✘ My brother is **elder** than me. ✔ My brother is **older** than me. / He is my **elder** brother.
- ✘ It was **sheer**. ✔ It was **sheer** luck.
:::

## 9.4 Order of Adjectives

When several adjectives describe one noun, they follow a natural order. Remember **"OSASCOMP"**:

| Order | Category | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **O**pinion | beautiful, nice, ugly, lovely, delicious |
| 2 | **S**ize | big, small, tall, tiny, huge |
| 3 | **A**ge | old, new, young, ancient |
| 4 | **S**hape | round, square, oval, flat |
| 5 | **C**olour | red, blue, white, black |
| 6 | **O**rigin (nationality) | Bangladeshi, Japanese, Chinese, Indian |
| 7 | **M**aterial | wooden, cotton, silk, plastic, gold |
| 8 | **P**urpose (often a noun/gerund) | sleeping (bag), writing (desk), running (shoes) |

Determiners and numbers come **before** all of these: *the two …*

::: ex
- a **beautiful old Italian** car (opinion – age – origin)
- a **big round wooden** table (size – shape – material)
- a **lovely little white** cat
- **two small red plastic** balls
- an **expensive new black leather** bag
- ✘ a **wooden big round** table ✔ a **big round wooden** table
- ✘ a **silk red beautiful** saree ✔ a **beautiful red silk** saree
:::

::: tip
**Exam Tip:** In "Which order is correct?" questions, check first that **opinion** comes before fact, and **material/purpose** comes last, right before the noun. Also: *first, last, next* + **number**: *the* **first two** *pages*, *the* **last three** *days* (✘ the two first).
:::

**Coordinate adjectives** (of the same category) are separated by commas or *and*: *a* **long, boring** *speech*; *a* **kind and honest** *man*. Test: if you can reverse them or put *and* between them, use a comma.

## 9.5 Adjectives Used as Nouns

Some adjectives are used as nouns when preceded by *the*:

1. **Plural noun meaning a class of persons**: *the rich, the poor, the old, the young, the sick, the dead, the blind, the deaf, the wise, the learned, the brave, the virtuous, the wicked, the unemployed* — always plural verb: *The rich* **are** *not always happy.*
2. **Singular abstract idea**: *the good, the beautiful, the true, the unknown, the impossible* — *The beautiful* **is** *not always the good.*
3. **Nationality ending in -sh, -ch, -ese, -ss**: *the English, the French, the Dutch, the Chinese, the Japanese, the Swiss, the Irish* — plural meaning: *The English* **are** *a practical people.*
4. **Some adjectives became full nouns**: *a native, the natives, a criminal, a savage, an intellectual, a liberal, Christians, Muslims, the elders, our superiors, valuables, sweets, greens, eatables, drinkables, the ancients, the moderns, the blacks, the whites, the Rohingyas.*
5. **Fixed phrases**: *in short, in general, in private, in public, at best, at present, for good (= for ever), at large, in particular, from bad to worse, through thick and thin, in the dark, at last, before long.*

::: ex
- ✘ **The poor is** neglected. ✔ **The poor are** neglected.
- ✘ **A rich** should help **a poor**. ✔ **The rich** should help **the poor**. / **A rich man** should help **a poor man**.
- ✘ He helped **the poors**. ✔ He helped **the poor**.
:::

## 9.6 Nouns Used as Adjectives

A noun placed before another noun works as an adjective (noun adjunct). It is normally **singular**:

*a* **gold** *watch, a* **cotton** *shirt, a* **village** *school, the* **Dhaka** *University, a* **summer** *day, a* **bus** *stop, a* **ten-taka** *note, a* **five-year** *plan, a* **two-mile** *walk, a* **three-storey** *building, a* **six-day** *week.*

::: ex
- ✘ a **five-years** plan ✔ a **five-year** plan
- ✘ a **ten-takas** note ✔ a **ten-taka** note
- ✘ a **two-miles** walk ✔ a **two-mile** walk
- ✘ a **three-storeys** building ✔ a **three-storey(ed)** building
:::

## 9.7 Participial Adjectives: *-ing* vs *-ed*

Present and past participles of verbs work as adjectives, and the two forms have different meanings:

| -ing (cause of the feeling — describes the thing/situation) | -ed (person who feels it) |
|---|---|
| The film was **boring**. | I was **bored**. |
| The news is **surprising**. | We were **surprised** at the news. |
| The lecture was **interesting**. | The students were **interested**. |
| The result was **disappointing**. | He was **disappointed** with the result. |
| The journey was **tiring**. | We were **tired**. |
| The situation is **confusing**. | I am **confused**. |
| It was a **frightening** experience. | The child was **frightened**. |
| His behaviour is **annoying**. | I am **annoyed** with him. |
| The story is **exciting**. | The children are **excited**. |
| The weather is **depressing**. | She felt **depressed**. |

::: ex
- ✘ I am **interesting** in music. ✔ I am **interested** in music.
- ✘ The match was very **excited**. ✔ The match was very **exciting**.
- ✘ He was **boring** in the class. (unless you mean he was dull for others) ✔ He was **bored** in the class.
:::

Other participial adjectives: *a* **running** *train, a* **rolling** *stone, a* **broken** *chair, a* **fallen** *tree, a* **retired** *officer, a* **learned** *(learn-id) man, a* **drunken** *man, a* **sunken** *ship, a* **shrunken** *face, a* **molten** *metal, a* **swollen** *river.*

## 9.8 Compound Adjectives

Two or more words joined (usually by hyphens) to act as one adjective **before** a noun:

| Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|
| number + noun | a **five-star** hotel, a **ten-year-old** boy, a **two-hour** journey |
| adjective + noun + -ed | a **kind-hearted** man, **blue-eyed**, **left-handed**, **long-legged**, **absent-minded** |
| noun + -ing participle | **heart-breaking**, **time-consuming**, **mouth-watering**, **eye-catching**, **peace-loving** |
| adjective/adverb + -ing | **good-looking**, **hard-working**, **easy-going**, **far-reaching**, **long-lasting** |
| noun/adjective/adverb + past participle | **man-made**, **hand-written**, **well-known**, **badly-paid**, **sun-dried**, **home-made**, **state-owned** |
| adjective + noun | **full-time**, **last-minute**, **high-level**, **low-cost**, **first-class** |
| others | **up-to-date**, **out-of-date**, **day-to-day**, **face-to-face**, **door-to-door**, **once-in-a-lifetime** |

::: note
Compound adjectives are hyphenated **before** a noun but usually not after a linking verb: *a* **well-known** *writer* / *The writer is* **well known**. *An* **up-to-date** *list* / *The list is* **up to date**.
:::

## 9.9 Formation of Adjectives

| From | Suffix | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Nouns | -ful | beauty → beautiful, use → useful, care → careful |
| Nouns | -less | care → careless, home → homeless, hope → hopeless |
| Nouns | -ous | danger → dangerous, fame → famous, courage → courageous |
| Nouns | -al | nation → national, nature → natural, culture → cultural |
| Nouns | -ic | hero → heroic, science → scientific, history → historic |
| Nouns | -y | health → healthy, wealth → wealthy, dirt → dirty |
| Nouns | -ly | friend → friendly, man → manly, cost → costly |
| Nouns | -ish | child → childish, fool → foolish, self → selfish |
| Nouns | -en | gold → golden, wood → wooden, wool → woollen |
| Nouns | -some | trouble → troublesome, quarrel → quarrelsome |
| Nouns | -like | child → childlike, life → lifelike |

## 9.10 Degrees of Comparison (Brief)

Adjectives of quality have three degrees:

- **Positive:** *tall, beautiful, good*
- **Comparative:** *taller, more beautiful, better* (comparing two)
- **Superlative:** *tallest, most beautiful, best* (comparing more than two)

The full rules — formation, irregular forms, *than/to* (Latin comparatives), double comparatives, transformation — are given in **Chapter 24**, and Latin adjectives (*senior to, superior to, prefer to*) in **Chapter 10**.

## 9.11 Some Adjectives Often Confused

| Pair | Use |
|---|---|
| **little / small** | *little* (quantity: little water; also size with emotion: a little girl) / *small* (size: a small house) |
| **elder / older** | *elder*: family members, only before a noun, no *than* (my elder brother) / *older*: persons and things, used with *than* (He is older than me) |
| **eldest / oldest** | same distinction |
| **later / latter** | *later*: time (He came later) / *latter*: second of two (Of tea and coffee, I prefer the latter) |
| **latest / last** | *latest*: most recent (the latest news) / *last*: final (the last chapter) |
| **farther / further** | *farther*: distance / *further*: additional (further information) — also distance in BrE |
| **nearest / next** | *nearest*: distance / *next*: order or position (the next house) |
| **less / fewer** | *less*: quantity / *fewer*: number |
| **many / much** | *many*: countable / *much*: uncountable |
| **each / every** | *each*: two or more individually / *every*: three or more as a group |
| **some / any** | affirmative / negative-question (Chapter 04) |
| **verbal / oral** | *verbal*: in words (spoken or written) / *oral*: spoken |
| **economic / economical** | *economic*: of economics (economic policy) / *economical*: thrifty (an economical car) |
| **historic / historical** | *historic*: famous in history (a historic day — 16 December 1971) / *historical*: related to history (a historical novel) |
| **childish / childlike** | *childish*: silly (negative) / *childlike*: innocent (positive) |
| **healthy / healthful** | *healthy*: in good health / *healthful*: good for health (also *healthy food* is accepted) |
| **official / officious** | *official*: authorised / *officious*: meddlesome |
| **respectable / respectful / respective** | worthy of respect / showing respect / relating to each |
| **sensible / sensitive / sensual / sensuous** | wise / easily affected / bodily pleasure / pleasing to the senses |
| **industrial / industrious** | of industry / hard-working |
| **imaginary / imaginative / imaginable** | not real / creative / possible to imagine |
| **momentary / momentous** | lasting a moment / very important |
| **luxurious / luxuriant** | expensive and comfortable / growing richly (luxuriant hair/vegetation) |
| **ingenious / ingenuous** | clever, inventive / innocent, frank |
| **contemptible / contemptuous** | deserving contempt / showing contempt |
| **credible / credulous / creditable** | believable / too ready to believe / praiseworthy |
| **judicial / judicious** | of a court / wise |
| **alternate / alternative** | every other / another choice |
| **continual / continuous** | repeated with breaks / without a break |
| **definite / definitive** | clear / final, authoritative |
| **exceptional / exceptionable** | unusual, outstanding / objectionable |

(More such pairs: Chapter 52.)

## 9.12 Common Errors with Adjectives

::: errs
1. ✘ He is **more stronger** than I. ✔ He is **stronger** than I.
2. ✘ She is **most cleverest**. ✔ She is **the cleverest**.
3. ✘ He is my **older** brother. ✔ He is my **elder** brother.
4. ✘ My brother is **elder than** me. ✔ My brother is **older than** me.
5. ✘ I want **new something**. ✔ I want **something new**.
6. ✘ He is a **five years** old boy. ✔ He is a **five-year-old** boy.
7. ✘ The **poor is** suffering. ✔ The **poor are** suffering.
8. ✘ I am **boring** with this class. ✔ I am **bored** with this class.
9. ✘ She is **senior than** me. ✔ She is **senior to** me.
10. ✘ He has **less** friends. ✔ He has **fewer** friends.
11. ✘ This is a **historical** day for the nation. ✔ This is a **historic** day for the nation.
12. ✘ Of the two plans, the **later** is better. ✔ … the **latter** is better.
13. ✘ Please give me **farther** details. ✔ Please give me **further** details.
14. ✘ He looks **sadly**. ✔ He looks **sad**.
15. ✘ The flower smells **sweetly**. ✔ The flower smells **sweet**.
16. ✘ He is an **alone** man. ✔ He is a **lonely** man.
17. ✘ She bought a **silk beautiful red** saree. ✔ She bought a **beautiful red silk** saree.
18. ✘ He is **too much** tired. ✔ He is **very (much)** tired. / **too** tired to walk.
19. ✘ Rahim is **more taller** of the two. ✔ Rahim is **the taller** of the two.
20. ✘ This is the **most unique** opportunity. ✔ This is a **unique** opportunity. (*unique, perfect, complete, round, square, ideal, extreme, chief, universal, entire* are absolute — not compared.)
21. ✘ He is **very** stronger than me. ✔ He is **much** stronger than me.
22. ✘ It is a **mouth watering** dish. ✔ It is a **mouth-watering** dish.
23. ✘ The **concerned** officer was absent (= the officer in charge). ✔ The officer **concerned** was absent.
24. ✘ I have **a few** water. ✔ I have **a little** water.
25. ✘ He is **economic** in his habits. ✔ He is **economical** in his habits.
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Adjective vs Adverb after Linking Verbs:** *Feel, look, seem, smell, taste, sound, appear, become, grow, remain, stay, turn, prove* take an **adjective** when they describe the subject: *She felt* **bad** *(✘ badly) about the mistake. The plan sounds* **good**. But when the same verb describes an action, use an **adverb**: *She looked* **angrily** *at him* (the act of looking was angry). GRE Text Completion often uses precise adjective pairs — **sanguine/sanguinary, credible/credulous, ingenious/ingenuous, luxurious/luxuriant** — so knowing exact meanings pays off.
:::
