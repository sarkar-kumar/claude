## 25.1 What Is a Conditional Sentence?

A **conditional sentence** says that one thing happens (or would happen) **only if** another thing happens. It has two parts:

1. **The *if*-clause (condition / protasis):** *If it rains, …*
2. **The main clause (result / apodosis):** *…, we shall stay at home.*

Either clause may come first. When the *if*-clause comes first, it is followed by a **comma**: *If it rains,* **we shall stay at home.** / *We shall stay at home* **if it rains.** (no comma)

Conditionals are tested every year in Bangladesh through *Right Form of Verbs*, error detection and sentence completion, and they are a core GMAT and SAT topic (✘ *If I would have known…*).

## 25.2 The Four (Five) Types of Conditionals

| Type | Situation | *If*-clause | Main clause | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Zero** | general truth, scientific fact, habit (always true) | present simple | present simple | If you heat ice, it **melts**. |
| **First** | real / possible in the present or future | present simple | *will / shall / can / may* + V1 | If it **rains**, we **will stay** in. |
| **Second** | unreal / imaginary / unlikely in the present or future | past simple (*were*) | *would / could / might* + V1 | If I **were** rich, I **would help** the poor. |
| **Third** | unreal in the past (did not happen) | past perfect | *would / could / might have* + V3 | If he **had worked** hard, he **would have passed**. |
| **Mixed** | past condition → present result, or present condition → past result | past perfect / past | *would* + V1 / *would have* + V3 | If I **had studied** medicine, I **would be** a doctor now. |

## 25.3 Zero Conditional

Used for things that are **always true** when the condition is met — facts, scientific laws, instructions, habits. *If* here means *whenever*.

::: ex
- If you **heat** water to 100°C, it **boils**.
- If you **mix** red and yellow, you **get** orange.
- If it **rains**, the roads **get** flooded in Dhaka.
- If you **press** this button, the machine **stops**.
- Plants **die** if they **don't get** water.
:::

## 25.4 First Conditional (Real / Possible)

**Structure:** *If* + present simple, *will / shall / can / may / must* + V1 (or imperative).

Used when the condition is **likely** or **possible** in the future.

::: ex
- If you **work** hard, you **will succeed**.
- If he **comes**, I **shall go** with him.
- If it **doesn't rain**, we **can play** cricket.
- If you **see** him, **give** him my regards. (imperative result)
- If you **need** money, you **may borrow** mine.
- If the train **is** late, we **will miss** the meeting.
:::

::: rule
**Do not use *will / shall* in the *if*-clause** (the present tense already refers to the future):

✘ *If it* **will rain** *tomorrow, we shall stay home.* ✔ *If it* **rains** *tomorrow, we shall stay home.*

Exception: *will* in the *if*-clause meaning **willingness** (polite request): *If you* **will** *wait a moment, I'll call the manager.* (= if you are willing to wait)
:::

## 25.5 Second Conditional (Unreal Present / Unlikely Future)

**Structure:** *If* + past simple, *would / could / might* + V1.

Used for situations that are **imaginary, contrary to present fact, or unlikely**. Although the verb is in the past form, the meaning is **present or future**.

::: ex
- If I **were** a bird, I **would fly** to you. (I am not a bird.)
- If I **had** a car, I **would drive** to Sylhet. (I don't have a car.)
- If he **knew** the answer, he **would tell** us. (He doesn't know.)
- If I **won** the lottery, I **would build** a school. (unlikely)
- What **would** you **do** if you **saw** a tiger?
:::

::: rule
**Use *were* for all persons with *be* in the second conditional (formal and exam style):**

✘ *If I* **was** *you, I would accept it.* ✔ *If I* **were** *you, I would accept it.*

✔ *If he* **were** *here, he would help us.* / ✔ *If it* **were** *not for your help, I would fail.*
:::

### 25.5.1 First vs Second Conditional

The difference is in how **likely** the speaker thinks the condition is:

- *If I* **get** *the job, I* **will move** *to Dhaka.* (I think it is possible.)
- *If I* **got** *the job, I* **would move** *to Dhaka.* (I think it is unlikely.)

## 25.6 Third Conditional (Unreal Past)

**Structure:** *If* + past perfect, *would / could / might* + *have* + V3.

Used to imagine a **different past**: the condition did **not** happen, so the result did not happen either. It often expresses **regret** or **criticism**.

::: ex
- If he **had worked** hard, he **would have passed**. (He did not work hard; he failed.)
- If you **had come** earlier, you **would have met** him.
- If I **had known** your address, I **would have written** to you.
- If it **had not rained**, the match **would have been** held.
- She **might have survived** if the ambulance **had arrived** on time.
:::

::: rule
**Never use *would have* in the *if*-clause.**

✘ *If I* **would have known***, I would have told you.* ✔ *If I* **had known***, I would have told you.*

✘ *If he* **would have studied***, he would have passed.* ✔ *If he* **had studied***…*
:::

## 25.7 Mixed Conditionals

When the time of the condition and the time of the result are **different**, we mix the second and third types.

**(a) Past condition → present result:** *If* + past perfect, *would* + V1.

::: ex
- If I **had taken** the medicine yesterday, I **would be** well now.
- If he **had saved** money, he **would not be** poor today.
:::

**(b) Present condition (permanent) → past result:** *If* + past simple, *would have* + V3.

::: ex
- If I **were** braver, I **would have spoken** up at the meeting.
- If she **knew** English well, she **would have got** that job.
:::

## 25.8 Inverted Conditionals (Without *If*)

In formal English, *if* may be omitted and the auxiliary (**were, had, should**) placed before the subject (Chapter 16):

| With *if* | Inverted |
|---|---|
| If I **were** you, … | **Were I** you, … |
| If he **had** come, … | **Had he** come, … |
| If you **should** see him, … | **Should you** see him, … |
| If it **were not** for … | **Were it not** for … |
| If it **had not been** for … | **Had it not been** for … |

::: ex
- **Had I** known the truth, I would have acted differently.
- **Should** you **need** any help, please contact us.
- **Were** it **not for** the sun, there would be no life on earth.
- **Had it not been for** your help, I would have failed.
:::

## 25.9 Other Words Used for Conditions

| Word / phrase | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **unless** | if … not | **Unless** you work hard, you will fail. (= If you don't work hard…) ✘ Unless you don't work… |
| **provided (that) / providing (that)** | only if | You can go out **provided that** you finish your homework. |
| **as long as / so long as** | on condition that | You may stay **as long as** you keep quiet. |
| **on condition that** | only if (formal) | He was released **on condition that** he left the country. |
| **in case** | as a precaution | Take an umbrella **in case** it rains. |
| **suppose / supposing** | imagine that | **Suppose** you won the lottery, what would you do? |
| **even if** | whether or not | I will go **even if** it rains. |
| **only if** | (with inversion in main clause when first) | **Only if** you apologise **will** he forgive you. |
| **but for** | if it were not for / had it not been for | **But for** your help, I would have drowned. |
| **otherwise / or (else)** | if not | Work hard; **otherwise**, you will fail. |
| **without** | if there were no | **Without** water, we could not live. |
| **imagine / what if** | — | **What if** he doesn't come? |

::: ex
- **But for** the doctor, he would have died. = If it had not been for the doctor, he would have died.
- **Without** your advice, I would have made a mistake. = If you had not advised me, …
- Hurry up, **or** you will miss the bus. = If you don't hurry up, you will miss the bus.
:::

## 25.10 Conditionals with *Wish*, *If only*, *As if* (Related Structures)

These follow the same "back-shift" logic as unreal conditionals:

| Time of the unreal situation | *Wish / If only* | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present | + past simple / *were* | I wish I **were** taller. / If only I **knew** the answer! |
| Past | + past perfect | I wish I **had listened** to you. / If only he **had come**! |
| Future / annoyance | + *would* + V1 | I wish it **would stop** raining. / I wish you **wouldn't** shout. |
| Ability | + *could* + V1 | I wish I **could** fly. |

## 25.11 Conditionals in Indirect Speech

- First conditional: backshift normally: *He said, "If it rains, I* **will** *stay home."* → *He said that if it* **rained** *he* **would** *stay home.*
- Second and third conditionals **do not change**: *He said, "If I* **were** *rich, I* **would** *travel."* → *He said that if he* **were** *rich he* **would** *travel.* / *"If I* **had known**, *I* **would have come**." → *He said that if he* **had known** *he* **would have come**.

## 25.12 Common Errors in Conditionals

::: errs
1. ✘ If it **will rain**, we will stay home. ✔ If it **rains**, we will stay home.
2. ✘ If I **was** a king, I would help the poor. ✔ If I **were** a king, …
3. ✘ If he **would have come**, I would have met him. ✔ If he **had come**, …
4. ✘ If I had money, I **will** buy a car. ✔ If I had money, I **would** buy a car.
5. ✘ If he had worked hard, he **would pass**. ✔ … he **would have passed**.
6. ✘ **Unless** you **don't** study, you will fail. ✔ **Unless** you study, you will fail.
7. ✘ If you will heat ice, it melts. ✔ If you **heat** ice, it melts.
8. ✘ **Had he not** came, … ✔ **Had he not come**, …
9. ✘ **If had I** known, … ✔ **Had I** known, … / **If I had** known, …
10. ✘ **Hadn't** I seen it, I would not have believed it. ✔ **Had I not** seen it, …
11. ✘ If I **would be** you, I would go. ✔ If I **were** you, I would go.
12. ✘ **But for** you helped me, I would have failed. ✔ **But for your help**, I would have failed.
13. ✘ Take your coat **if** it gets cold (precaution). ✔ Take your coat **in case** it gets cold.
14. ✘ If he **comes**, I **would** go. ✔ If he **comes**, I **will** go. / If he **came**, I **would** go.
15. ✘ I wish I **can** speak French. ✔ I wish I **could** speak French.
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Conditional Logic:** In Sentence Correction, the two iron rules are: (1) **no *would* in the *if*-clause** and (2) **match the result-clause modal to the condition type** (present → *will*, past → *would*, past perfect → *would have*). In GMAT Critical Reasoning and GRE Reading, *if* and *only if* have exact logical meanings: "A **if** B" means B is **sufficient** for A; "A **only if** B" means B is **necessary** for A. *"You will pass only if you study"* does **not** guarantee that studying makes you pass — it only says you cannot pass without studying. *"Unless"* = "if not": *"Unless it rains, we'll play"* = *"If it doesn't rain, we'll play."*
:::
