## 13.1 What Are Modal Auxiliaries?

**Modal auxiliaries** (modals) are helping verbs that express the speaker's **attitude** towards an action — whether it is possible, permitted, necessary, obligatory, advisable, certain, or desired. They "colour" the meaning of the main verb.

**The modals:** *can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must, ought (to)*. **Semi-modals:** *need, dare, used to, had better, would rather, have to, be able to, be to.*

::: ex
- He **can** swim. (ability)
- **May** I come in? (permission)
- It **might** rain today. (possibility)
- You **should** respect your teachers. (advice/duty)
- We **must** obey the law. (obligation)
- You **ought to** help the poor. (moral duty)
:::

## 13.2 Grammatical Features of Modals

::: rule
1. **No -s in the third person singular:** *He can* (✘ cans), *She must* (✘ musts).
2. **Followed by the bare infinitive** (base form without *to*): *He can* **swim** (✘ can to swim, ✘ can swims, ✘ can swimming). Exceptions: *ought* **to**, *used* **to**, *have* **to**, *be able* **to**.
3. **No infinitive, no participle, no gerund forms:** ✘ *to can*, ✘ *canning*, ✘ *musted*. Use substitutes: *to be able to, to have to.*
4. **Two modals cannot stand together:** ✘ *He will can come.* ✔ *He will be able to come.* ✘ *You must can do it.* ✔ *You must be able to do it.*
5. **Negatives and questions without *do*:** *He cannot go. Can he go?* (✘ Does he can go?)
6. **Past reference with *have + V3*:** *He* **must have gone**. *You* **should have told** *me.*
:::

## 13.3 *Can* and *Could*

### 13.3.1 *Can*

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Present ability | She **can** speak three languages. / I **can't** swim. |
| Permission (informal) | You **can** go now. / **Can** I use your phone? |
| Possibility (theoretical) | Anyone **can** make a mistake. / The road **can** be dangerous in the rain. |
| Request (informal) | **Can** you help me? |
| Impossibility (certain negative deduction) | It **can't** be true. / He **can't** be at home; I saw him at the market. |

### 13.3.2 *Could*

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Past ability (general) | He **could** run very fast when he was young. |
| Polite request | **Could** you please open the window? |
| Polite permission | **Could** I borrow your pen? |
| Possibility (present/future, weaker) | It **could** rain later. / This **could** be the answer. |
| Conditional (unreal) | If I had money, I **could** buy a car. |
| Past possibility (with *have + V3*) | He **could have** won, but he didn't try. |
| Indirect speech (past of *can*) | He said that he **could** swim. |

::: note
**Could vs was able to.** For a **specific achievement** on one occasion in the past, use *was able to / managed to*, not *could*: ✘ *The fire spread fast, but everyone* **could** *escape.* ✔ *…everyone* **was able to** *escape / managed to escape.* In the **negative**, *couldn't* is fine: *I* **couldn't** *find the key.*
:::

## 13.4 *May* and *Might*

### 13.4.1 *May*

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Permission (formal) | **May** I come in, sir? / You **may** go now. |
| Possibility (present/future) | He **may** come today. / It **may** rain tonight. |
| Wish / prayer | **May** Allah bless you! / **May** you live long! / **May** our country prosper! |
| Purpose (after *so that / that*, present-future) | He works hard **so that** he **may** succeed. |
| Concession | Whatever you **may** say, I won't change my mind. / Try as he **may**, he cannot win. |

### 13.4.2 *Might*

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Past of *may* (reported speech) | He said that he **might** come. |
| Weaker possibility | It **might** rain, but I doubt it. |
| Purpose (past) | He worked hard **so that** he **might** succeed. |
| Polite suggestion / mild reproach | You **might** try calling him. / You **might** have told me earlier! |
| Past possibility | He **might have** missed the bus. |

::: tip
**Exam Tip — *so that* + may/might.** Use **may** after a present/future main verb and **might** after a past main verb: *He* **eats** *so that he* **may** *live.* / *He* **ate** *so that he* **might** *live.* ✘ *He worked hard so that he can pass* (informal) — the exam key prefers *might*.
:::

## 13.5 *Shall* and *Should*

### 13.5.1 *Shall*

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Simple future (1st person, traditional) | I **shall** be eighteen next month. |
| Offer / suggestion (questions) | **Shall** I carry your bag? / **Shall** we start? |
| Command, promise, threat (2nd/3rd person) | You **shall** get your reward. / He **shall** be punished. |
| Legal / formal obligation | The tenant **shall** pay the rent by the 5th of each month. |

### 13.5.2 *Should*

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Duty / advice / moral obligation | We **should** obey our parents. / You **should** see a doctor. |
| Probability / expectation | He **should** be home by now. / The parcel **should** arrive tomorrow. |
| After *lest* | Walk fast **lest** you **should** miss the train. |
| Mandative subjunctive (BrE) | It is necessary that he **should** go. / I suggest that he **should** apply. |
| Conditional (formal, uncertain) | If it **should** rain, the match will be cancelled. / **Should** you need help, call me. (inverted) |
| Past regret / criticism | You **should have** studied harder. (but you didn't) |
| Surprise / emotion (after *that*) | It is strange that he **should** say so. |
| In reported speech (past of *shall*) | I said that I **should** go. |

::: rule
**Lest … should.** *Lest* means "so that … not", so it never takes *not*: ✘ *Run fast lest you* **should not** *miss the bus.* ✔ *Run fast lest you* **should** *miss the bus.*
:::

## 13.6 *Will* and *Would*

### 13.6.1 *Will*

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Future (all persons in modern English) | She **will** come tomorrow. |
| Willingness / promise / determination | I **will** help you. / I **will** pass this time. |
| Request (informal) | **Will** you shut the door? |
| Habit / characteristic behaviour | Boys **will** be boys. / He **will** sit for hours without speaking. |
| Assumption | That **will** be the postman at the door. |
| Refusal (negative) | The car **won't** start. / He **won't** listen to me. |

### 13.6.2 *Would*

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Past of *will* (reported speech) | He said that he **would** come. |
| Polite request | **Would** you please help me? |
| Polite offer / invitation | **Would** you like some tea? |
| Past habit (repeated action) | When we were children, we **would** play in the field every afternoon. |
| Conditional (unreal) | If I were rich, I **would** help the poor. |
| Wish / preference | I **would** like to go. / I **would rather** stay home. |
| Past refusal | He **wouldn't** listen to my advice. |
| Unreal past | I **would have** helped you if you had asked. |

::: note
**Would vs used to.** Both express **past habits** (actions): *We* **would / used to** *swim in the river.* But only **used to** expresses **past states**: *There* **used to** *be a pond here* (✘ would be). *He* **used to** *live in Khulna* (✘ would live).
:::

## 13.7 *Must*

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Strong obligation / necessity (from the speaker) | I **must** finish this today. / You **must** wear a helmet. |
| Prohibition (*must not*) | You **must not** smoke here. / Students **mustn't** use mobile phones in the exam hall. |
| Logical certainty (positive deduction) | He has worked all day; he **must** be tired. / She **must** be the new teacher. |
| Past deduction | The ground is wet; it **must have** rained last night. |
| Strong recommendation | You **must** visit Sylhet; it's beautiful. |
| Insistence | If you **must** go, go quietly. |

### 13.7.1 *Must* vs *Have to*

- **Must** — obligation from the **speaker** (internal): *I* **must** *stop smoking.* (I feel it is necessary.)
- **Have to** — obligation from **outside** (rules, circumstances): *I* **have to** *wear a uniform at work.* (It is the rule.)
- *Must* has no past form; use **had to**: *Yesterday I* **had to** *go to the bank.* (✘ musted)
- Future: **will have to**: *You* **will have to** *wait.*

### 13.7.2 *Must not* vs *Need not / Don't have to*

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **must not** | prohibition — it is not allowed | You **must not** park here. |
| **need not / needn't** | no necessity — it is not necessary | You **needn't** come tomorrow; it's a holiday. |
| **don't have to** | no obligation | You **don't have to** pay; it's free. |

### 13.7.3 Opposite of *must* (deduction)

The negative of *must* (logical certainty) is **can't**, not *mustn't*: *He* **must** *be at home* (I'm sure he is). → *He* **can't** *be at home* (I'm sure he isn't).

## 13.8 *Ought to*

*Ought to* expresses **moral duty, obligation or strong advisability** — similar to *should* but a little stronger and more formal. It is the only modal followed by **to**.

::: ex
- We **ought to** love our country.
- You **ought to** respect your elders.
- He **ought not to** waste time. (✘ ought not waste)
- You **ought to have** helped him. (past — you didn't)
- **Ought** we **to** go? (formal question)
:::

::: errs
1. ✘ We **ought** obey our parents. ✔ We **ought to** obey our parents.
2. ✘ You **ought to not** do that. ✔ You **ought not to** do that.
3. ✘ He **oughts to** go. ✔ He **ought to** go.
:::

## 13.9 *Need* and *Dare* — The Double Life

*Need* and *dare* can be used as **modals** (in negatives and questions; no *-s*, bare infinitive, no *do*) or as **ordinary verbs** (with *-s*, *to*-infinitive, *do*-support).

| | As modal auxiliary | As main verb |
|---|---|---|
| **need** | You **need not** go. / **Need** I go? / He **needn't** worry. | You **don't need to** go. / Does he **need to** go? / He **needs to** work harder. / I **need** a pen. |
| **dare** | He **dare not** go there. / **Dare** he say so? / How **dare** you insult me! | He **doesn't dare to** go there. / He **dares to** challenge the champion. / I **dare** you **to** jump. (= challenge) |

::: rule
**As a modal, *need/dare* is used only in negative and interrogative sentences.** ✘ *He need go.* ✔ *He needs to go.* ✘ *He dares not go* — ✔ *He* **dare not** *go* (modal) or *He* **does not dare to** *go* (main verb).
:::

::: ex
- ✘ He **needs not** come. ✔ He **need not** come. / He **doesn't need to** come.
- ✘ How **dares** he speak like that? ✔ How **dare** he speak like that?
- **Needn't have** + V3 = it was done, but it was not necessary: *You* **needn't have bought** *bread; we had plenty.*
- **Didn't need to** = it was not necessary (and probably not done): *I* **didn't need to** *buy bread, so I didn't.*
:::

## 13.10 *Used to*

### 13.10.1 *Used to* + base form (past habit or state, no longer true)

::: ex
- I **used to** smoke, but I've stopped.
- There **used to** be a cinema hall here.
- **Did** you **use to** live in Rajshahi? (question)
- He **didn't use to** like rice. (negative) — also *He used not to like rice* (formal BrE).
- ✘ I **use to** go there every day (present). ✔ I **usually** go there every day.
:::

### 13.10.2 *Be / Get used to* + gerund (be accustomed to)

Here *to* is a **preposition**, so it is followed by a **noun or gerund**.

::: ex
- I **am used to getting** up early. (= accustomed to) ✘ am used to get up
- She **is used to** hard work.
- He will soon **get used to living** in the city.
- ✘ I am used to **walk** long distances. ✔ I am used to **walking** long distances.
:::

::: tip
**Exam Tip — Three "used" structures:** (1) *used to* + V1 = past habit (*I used to swim*); (2) *be used to* + V-ing = accustomed (*I am used to swimming*); (3) *be used to* + V1 = passive of *use* (*A knife* **is used to cut** *things* — it is employed for cutting). The other phrases that take *to* + V-ing: *look forward to, object to, with a view to, be accustomed to, be addicted to, be devoted to, take to, confess to, in addition to, when it comes to, adjust to.*
:::

## 13.11 *Had better* and *Would rather*

| Expression | Meaning | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **had better** | advice/warning (stronger than *should*) | had better + V1 | You **had better** consult a doctor. / You **had better not** be late. (✘ had better to; ✘ had not better) |
| **would rather** | preference | would rather + V1 + than + V1 | I **would rather** walk **than** take a rickshaw. |
| **would rather** + another subject | preference about someone else's action | would rather + subject + past tense | I **would rather** you **stayed** here. / I'd rather you **didn't** smoke. |
| **would sooner** | same as *would rather* | — | I **would sooner** die **than** beg. |

## 13.12 *Be to*, *Have to*, *Be able to*

- **Be to** + V1 = arrangement, plan, official order or destiny: *The Prime Minister* **is to** *visit Japan next week. You* **are to** *report at 9 a.m. He* **was** *never* **to** *see his homeland again.*
- **Have to / has to / had to** = necessity (external): *I* **have to** *catch the 8 o'clock train.*
- **Be able to** = ability (used where *can* has no form): *I* **will be able to** *finish it. I* **have been able to** *save some money.*

## 13.13 Modal Perfects — Talking About the Past

Modal + **have** + **past participle** refers to the past, usually to something that did **not** happen or to a deduction about the past.

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **must have** + V3 | certain deduction about the past | The streets are wet; it **must have rained**. |
| **can't / couldn't have** + V3 | impossible in the past | He **can't have seen** me; I wasn't there. |
| **may / might have** + V3 | possible in the past | She **may have missed** the bus. |
| **could have** + V3 | possible but didn't happen / unused ability | You **could have** won if you had tried. |
| **should / ought to have** + V3 | it was right but wasn't done (criticism, regret) | You **should have** informed the police. |
| **shouldn't have** + V3 | it was wrong but was done | You **shouldn't have** shouted at him. |
| **needn't have** + V3 | done but unnecessary | You **needn't have** brought an umbrella. |
| **would have** + V3 | unreal past result (3rd conditional) | I **would have** helped you if I had known. |

::: errs
1. ✘ He **must had** gone. ✔ He **must have** gone.
2. ✘ You **should of** told me. ✔ You **should have** told me. (*of* is a mishearing of *'ve*)
3. ✘ She **might has** forgotten. ✔ She **might have** forgotten.
:::

## 13.14 Modals Expressing Degrees of Certainty

From most certain to least certain (present):

| Certainty | Positive | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| 100% (fact) | He **is** at home. | He **isn't** at home. |
| ~95% (deduction) | He **must** be at home. | He **can't / couldn't** be at home. |
| ~70% (expectation) | He **should / ought to** be at home. | He **shouldn't** be at home. |
| ~50% (possibility) | He **may** be at home. | He **may not** be at home. |
| <50% (weak possibility) | He **might / could** be at home. | He **might not** be at home. |

## 13.15 Modals in Reported Speech

| Direct | Indirect (past reporting verb) |
|---|---|
| can | could |
| may | might |
| shall | should (or would) |
| will | would |
| must (obligation) | must / had to |
| could, might, should, would, ought to, used to | unchanged |

(See Chapter 27.)

## 13.16 Common Errors with Modals — Consolidated

::: errs
1. ✘ He **can speaks** English. ✔ He **can speak** English.
2. ✘ I **must to** go now. ✔ I **must** go now.
3. ✘ You **will can** do it. ✔ You **will be able to** do it.
4. ✘ **Do** you **can** swim? ✔ **Can** you swim?
5. ✘ He **musted** go yesterday. ✔ He **had to** go yesterday.
6. ✘ You **need not to** worry. ✔ You **need not** worry. / You **don't need to** worry.
7. ✘ He **dares not to** speak. ✔ He **dare not** speak.
8. ✘ You **had better to** go. ✔ You **had better** go.
9. ✘ I **would rather to** die. ✔ I **would rather** die.
10. ✘ I **used to getting** up early (past habit). ✔ I **used to get** up early.
11. ✘ I am used to **get** up early. ✔ I am used to **getting** up early.
12. ✘ Walk carefully **lest** you **may not** fall. ✔ … **lest** you **should** fall.
13. ✘ He worked hard so that he **may** pass. ✔ … so that he **might** pass.
14. ✘ **May** you **are** happy! ✔ **May** you **be** happy!
15. ✘ He **ought** respect his parents. ✔ He **ought to** respect his parents.
16. ✘ You **mustn't** come tomorrow; it's a holiday (= not necessary). ✔ You **needn't** come tomorrow.
17. ✘ She **must be** at home; her car isn't here (= deduction negative). ✔ She **can't be** at home.
18. ✘ I wish I **can** fly. ✔ I wish I **could** fly.
19. ✘ He said that he **will** come. ✔ He said that he **would** come.
20. ✘ It **might rained** yesterday. ✔ It **might have rained** yesterday.
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Modal Logic in Reading:** In GRE/GMAT Critical Reasoning, modals signal the **strength** of a claim. *"The policy* **must** *reduce costs"* is a strong claim easily weakened; *"The policy* **may** *reduce costs"* is a weak claim that is hard to attack. Assumption and inference questions often turn on the difference between **can** (possibility) and **will** (prediction), or between **should** (recommendation) and **must** (necessity). In Sentence Correction, ✘ *"would have" in an if-clause* is always wrong: ✘ *If he* **would have** *known* → ✔ *If he* **had** *known*.
:::
