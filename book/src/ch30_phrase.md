# Identification of Phrase {#ch30}

[Part A · Grammar]{.chpart}

## 30.1 What Is a Phrase?

A **phrase** is a group of words that makes sense but **not complete sense**, and that **does not contain a finite verb with its own subject**. A phrase works as a single part of speech — a noun, an adjective, an adverb, a verb, a preposition, a conjunction or an interjection.

::: ex
- **in the morning**
- **a very tall man**
- **to learn English**
- **having finished the work**
- **in spite of**
:::

### 30.1.1 Phrase vs Clause vs Sentence

| Group of words | Subject + finite verb? | Complete sense? | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phrase** | No | No | *on the table, to go home, the old man* |
| **Clause** | Yes | Sometimes (main) / No (subordinate) | *when he came, the book that I bought, he came* |
| **Sentence** | Yes (at least one main clause) | Yes | *He came when I was reading.* |

::: tip
**Exam Tip — The finite-verb test:** If the group of words contains a subject and a finite verb (*is, was, goes, went, has gone, will come*), it is a **clause**. If it has no finite verb (or only an infinitive, gerund or participle), it is a **phrase**. *Running fast* (phrase) / *As he was running fast* (clause).
:::

## 30.2 Kinds of Phrases (by Function)

### 30.2.1 Noun Phrase

A **noun phrase** does the work of a **noun**: it can be the subject, object or complement.

::: ex
- **To tell lies** is a sin. (subject)
- **Reading books** is my hobby. (subject — gerund phrase)
- I like **to play cricket**. (object of *like*)
- He wants **a new mobile phone**. (object)
- **The old man in the blue shirt** is my grandfather. (subject)
- Her ambition is **to become a doctor**. (complement)
- I don't know **what to do**. (object — *wh* + infinitive)
- He is fond of **playing chess**. (object of preposition)
:::

**Identification:** Ask *what?* or *who?* before the verb (subject) or after it (object). A noun phrase can be replaced by a single noun or by *it/this*: *I like* **to play cricket** → *I like* **it**.

### 30.2.2 Adjective Phrase

An **adjective phrase** does the work of an **adjective**: it qualifies a noun or pronoun. It usually comes **after** the noun.

::: ex
- He is a man **of great wealth**. (= a wealthy man)
- She wore a dress **of blue colour**. (= a blue dress)
- A man **in a black coat** came here.
- The house **on the hill** is ours.
- He is a man **of his word**. (= a reliable man)
- A girl **with blue eyes** came. (= a blue-eyed girl)
- The boy **sitting in the corner** is my brother. (participial phrase used as adjective)
- I have no money **to spend**. (infinitive phrase used as adjective)
- He is **very proud of his son**. (adjective phrase as complement)
:::

**Identification:** Ask *which? what kind of?* about a noun. An adjective phrase can often be replaced by a single adjective:

| Adjective phrase | Single adjective |
|---|---|
| a man **of honesty** | an **honest** man |
| a girl **of great beauty** | a **beautiful** girl |
| a man **of learning** | a **learned** man |
| a crown **of gold** | a **golden** crown |
| a statement **of no importance** | an **unimportant** statement |
| a person **of courage** | a **courageous** person |
| a ring **made of gold** | a **gold** ring |
| a matter **of great importance** | an **important** matter |
| a man **of sense** | a **sensible** man |
| a man **of experience** | an **experienced** man |

### 30.2.3 Adverb Phrase (Adverbial Phrase)

An **adverb phrase** does the work of an **adverb**: it modifies a verb, an adjective or an adverb, and tells *how, when, where, why, how much*.

::: ex
- He spoke **in a loud voice**. (= loudly — manner)
- She came **in the morning**. (time)
- The cat is sleeping **under the table**. (place)
- He works **with great care**. (= carefully — manner)
- He went to Dhaka **to see his father**. (purpose — infinitive phrase)
- **Without a doubt**, he is the best. (sentence adverbial)
- He did it **in a hurry**. (= hurriedly)
- He comes **every day**. (frequency)
- **Once upon a time**, there lived a king. (time)
:::

**Identification:** Ask *how? when? where? why? how often?* after the verb. Many adverb phrases can be replaced by a single adverb:

| Adverb phrase | Single adverb |
|---|---|
| **in a loud voice** | loudly |
| **with care** | carefully |
| **in a hurry** | hurriedly |
| **at that time** | then |
| **in this place** | here |
| **with great kindness** | kindly |
| **in a polite manner** | politely |
| **in a short time** | soon |
| **in no time** | quickly / immediately |
| **at once** | immediately |
| **by chance** | accidentally |
| **in the end** | finally |

### 30.2.4 Prepositional Phrase

**(a) A prepositional phrase** in the broad sense = preposition + its object (*on the table, in the morning, of great wealth*). It always works as an **adjective** or an **adverb**.

::: ex
- The book **on the table** is mine. (adjective — which book?)
- He put the book **on the table**. (adverb — where?)
:::

**(b) A phrasal (complex) preposition** = a group of words that works **as a preposition**:

*according to, along with, as for, as regards, as to, because of, by dint of, by means of, by virtue of, by way of, due to, for the sake of, in accordance with, in addition to, in case of, in comparison with, in consequence of, in favour of, in front of, in lieu of, in order to, in place of, in spite of, instead of, in the event of, on account of, on behalf of, owing to, with a view to, with reference to, with regard to, in search of, in the face of.*

::: ex
- He succeeded **by dint of** hard work.
- **In spite of** his illness, he attended the meeting.
- I am speaking **on behalf of** my friends.
- **In the event of** fire, use the stairs.
:::

### 30.2.5 Conjunctional Phrase

A group of words that works as a **conjunction**: *as soon as, as long as, as well as, as if, as though, so that, in order that, provided that, even if, even though, in case, now that, no sooner … than, so long as, by the time, the moment.*

::: ex
- He left **as soon as** the bell rang.
- Work hard **so that** you may succeed.
- He talks **as if** he were a king.
- Rahim **as well as** his friends was present.
:::

### 30.2.6 Interjectional Phrase

A group of words that works as an **interjection**: *Good heavens! What a pity! Alas for him! Well done! Dear me! Oh dear! My goodness! For shame! Thank God! Woe to you!*

::: ex
- **Good heavens!** What have you done?
- **What a pity!** He missed the train.
:::

### 30.2.7 Verb Phrase

A **verb phrase** is the main verb together with its auxiliaries (and sometimes particles): *has been working, will have finished, must have seen, give up, look after, put up with, take care of.*

::: ex
- He **has been working** since morning.
- You **should have told** me.
- He **gave up** smoking.
- I cannot **put up with** this noise.
:::

## 30.3 Phrases Classified by Form (Head Word)

Modern grammar also names phrases by their **head word** (the most important word):

| Form | Head | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun phrase | noun | **the tall old man** |
| Verb phrase | verb | **has been sleeping** |
| Adjective phrase | adjective | **very proud of his son** |
| Adverb phrase | adverb | **very quickly** |
| Prepositional phrase | preposition | **under the table** |
| Infinitive phrase | infinitive | **to learn English** |
| Gerund phrase | gerund | **swimming in the river** |
| Participial phrase | participle | **sitting in the corner** |
| Absolute phrase | noun + participle | **The rain having stopped**, we went out. |
| Appositive phrase | noun renaming another | Mr. Rahman, **our new teacher**, is kind. |

::: note
**Form and function are different questions.** *To learn English* is an **infinitive phrase** (form); in *I want to learn English* it works as a **noun phrase** (function), and in *He came to learn English* it works as an **adverb phrase** (function — purpose). Exams in Bangladesh ask for the **function**.
:::

## 30.4 The Same Phrase in Different Functions

| Sentence | Phrase | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **In the morning** is the best time for study. | in the morning | Noun (subject) |
| The walk **in the morning** refreshed me. | in the morning | Adjective (which walk?) |
| I walk **in the morning**. | in the morning | Adverb (when?) |
| **To err** is human. | to err | Noun |
| I have a lot of work **to do**. | to do | Adjective |
| He came here **to see me**. | to see me | Adverb (purpose) |
| **Playing football** is good exercise. | playing football | Noun (gerund phrase) |
| The boys **playing football** are my students. | playing football | Adjective (participial phrase) |

## 30.5 Identification Practice (Worked Examples)

::: ex
- *The man* **with a long beard** *is a saint.* → **Adjective phrase** (qualifies *man*).
- *He fought* **with great courage**. → **Adverb phrase** (how did he fight? = courageously).
- *I enjoy* **reading detective stories**. → **Noun phrase** (object of *enjoy*).
- **In spite of** *the rain, they played.* → **Prepositional (phrasal preposition)**.
- *She left* **as soon as** *the clock struck ten.* → **Conjunctional phrase**.
- **Having finished his work**, *he went out.* → **Adverb phrase** (time) — participial phrase.
- *Nobody knows* **how to solve it**. → **Noun phrase** (object).
- *He is a man* **of few words**. → **Adjective phrase**.
- *The sun* **having set**, *we returned.* → **Absolute phrase** (adverbial — time).
- **What a beautiful sight!** → **Interjectional phrase** (exclamatory).
- *He lives* **at the foot of the hill**. → **Adverb phrase** (place).
- *Dhaka,* **the capital of Bangladesh**, *is overcrowded.* → **Appositive (noun) phrase**.
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Phrases as Modifiers:** Most SAT/GMAT "modifier" questions are really about phrases: participial phrases (*Founded in 1921, …*), appositive phrases (*a city of 20 million, Dhaka…*), absolute phrases (*its population growing, the city…*) and prepositional phrases. Two checks: (1) a **phrase cannot stand alone as a sentence** (✘ "*Having finished the project on time.*" is a fragment), and (2) a modifying phrase must sit **next to the word it modifies** (Chapter 29).
:::
