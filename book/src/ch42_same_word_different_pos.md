## 42.1 The Principle

As Chapter 01 explained, **a word's part of speech depends on the job it does in a sentence**. Many common English words can work as two, three or even five different parts of speech without any change in spelling. Exams ask: *"Use the word 'round' as a noun, a verb, an adjective, an adverb and a preposition"*, or *"Identify the part of speech of the underlined word."*

To answer, look at the **position** and **function**:

- Noun → after an article/determiner, as subject/object.
- Verb → after a subject/modal/*to*, shows action or state, changes with tense.
- Adjective → before a noun or after a linking verb, describes.
- Adverb → modifies a verb, adjective or adverb; no object.
- Preposition → followed by an object (noun/pronoun).
- Conjunction → followed by a clause (subject + verb).
- Pronoun → stands alone in place of a noun.
- Interjection → stands apart, expresses feeling.

## 42.3 Words That Are Both Noun and Verb (Stress Shift)

Many two-syllable words are stressed on the **first syllable as nouns/adjectives** and on the **second syllable as verbs**:

| Word | Noun / Adjective (stress 1st) | Verb (stress 2nd) |
|---|---|---|
| record | a **RE**cord | to re**CORD** |
| present | a **PRE**sent (gift), the **pre**sent time | to pre**SENT** |
| object | an **OB**ject | to ob**JECT** |
| project | a **PRO**ject | to pro**JECT** |
| permit | a **PER**mit | to per**MIT** |
| conduct | good **CON**duct | to con**DUCT** |
| contract | a **CON**tract | to con**TRACT** |
| desert | the **DE**sert (Sahara) | to de**SERT** (abandon) |
| export / import | an **EX**port / **IM**port | to ex**PORT** / im**PORT** |
| increase / decrease | an **IN**crease / **DE**crease | to in**CREASE** / de**CREASE** |
| progress | **PRO**gress | to pro**GRESS** |
| rebel | a **RE**bel | to re**BEL** |
| subject | a **SUB**ject | to sub**JECT** |
| suspect | a **SUS**pect | to sus**PECT** |
| transfer | a **TRANS**fer | to trans**FER** |
| produce | farm **PRO**duce | to pro**DUCE** |
| content | the **CON**tent (matter) | con**TENT** (satisfied — adj.) |
| perfect | **PER**fect (adj) | to per**FECT** |
| absent | **AB**sent (adj) | to ab**SENT** oneself |

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Secondary Meanings:** GRE and SAT vocabulary questions love common words used in **uncommon roles**: *"The scientist* **tabled** *the proposal"* (AmE: postponed; BrE: put forward for discussion), *"a* **flag** *in his energy"* (to weaken — verb), *"The novel's* **qualified** *success"* (limited, not unconditional), *"She* **appropriated** *the funds"* (took for her own use), *"a* **wanting** *argument"* (deficient), *"to* **temper** *criticism"* (moderate), *"to* **champion** *a cause"* (support). When a familiar word seems not to fit, ask whether it is being used as a **different part of speech** with a less common meaning.
:::
