## 10.1 What Are Latin Adjectives?

Most English comparatives are followed by **than**: *taller than, better than, more beautiful than.* But a small group of comparative adjectives, which English borrowed from **Latin**, are followed by **to**, not *than*. These are called **Latin comparatives** or **Latin adjectives**. In Latin they ended in **-ior** (the Latin comparative suffix), and in English they end in **-or**.

| Latin adjective | Meaning | Correct use |
|---|---|---|
| **senior** | older / higher in rank | He is **senior to** me by two years. |
| **junior** | younger / lower in rank | She is **junior to** me. |
| **superior** | better / higher | This cloth is **superior to** that. |
| **inferior** | worse / lower | His work is **inferior to** yours. |
| **prior** | earlier | He arrived **prior to** the meeting. |
| **posterior** | later / behind | This event was **posterior to** the war. |
| **anterior** | earlier / in front | The front legs are **anterior to** the hind legs. |
| **exterior** | outer | The **exterior** walls (rarely used comparatively) |
| **interior** | inner | The **interior** of the house (rarely used comparatively) |
| **major** | greater | (not used with *to* in modern English) |
| **minor** | lesser | (not used with *to* in modern English) |
| **ulterior** | hidden / further | an **ulterior** motive (no comparison) |

::: rule
**Rule:** *Senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior, posterior, anterior* take **to** instead of *than*.

✘ He is **senior than** me. ✔ He is **senior to** me.

✘ This pen is **superior than** that. ✔ This pen is **superior to** that.
:::

## 10.2 Five Golden Rules for Latin Adjectives

### Rule 1 — Use *to*, never *than*

::: ex
- ✘ Rahim is **junior than** Karim. ✔ Rahim is **junior to** Karim.
- ✘ Bangladeshi jute is **superior than** Indian jute. ✔ Bangladeshi jute is **superior to** Indian jute.
- ✘ Nothing is **inferior than** dishonesty. ✔ … **inferior to** …
:::

### Rule 2 — Never use *more* or *most* with them

They are **already comparative** in meaning, so adding *more* is a double comparative.

::: ex
- ✘ He is **more senior to** me. ✔ He is **senior to** me.
- ✘ This is **more superior to** that. ✔ This is **superior to** that.
- ✘ He is **most senior** officer. ✔ He is **the senior-most** officer (Indian/Bangladeshi usage) / **the most senior** officer (accepted in modern British English as a *superlative*, but avoid *more senior to*).
:::

::: note
In modern English, *senior* and *junior* are also used as ordinary adjectives meaning *high-ranking* / *low-ranking*: *a* **senior** *officer, a* **junior** *clerk.* In that use they can take *very/most*: *She is a* **very senior** *journalist.* But **in comparison** (*X is senior/junior to Y*) only *to* is correct.
:::

### Rule 3 — Use *much / far / by far / a little* (not *very*) to intensify

::: ex
- ✔ He is **much senior** to me. / He is **far superior** to his rivals.
- ✘ He is **very superior** to me. (in comparison) ✔ He is **much superior** to me.
- ✔ He is senior to me **by three years**. (✘ He is three years senior than me.)
:::

### Rule 4 — Pronoun after *to* must be in the **objective case**

Because *to* is a preposition, the pronoun after it must be objective.

::: ex
- ✘ He is senior **to I**. ✔ He is senior **to me**.
- ✘ She is junior **to he**. ✔ She is junior **to him**.
:::

### Rule 5 — Compare like with like

As with all comparisons, the two things compared must be of the same kind (Chapter 24, Chapter 39).

::: ex
- ✘ The climate of Sylhet is superior **to Rajshahi**. ✔ The climate of Sylhet is superior **to that of** Rajshahi.
- ✘ Your handwriting is inferior **to your brother**. ✔ Your handwriting is inferior **to your brother's** (that of your brother).
:::

## 10.3 Other Words That Take *to* Instead of *than*

Besides the Latin comparatives, several other words also take **to** and are tested along with them in BCS and Bank exams:

| Word | Correct use | Wrong use |
|---|---|---|
| **prefer** (verb) | I **prefer** tea **to** coffee. | ✘ prefer tea **than** coffee |
| **preferable** | Death is **preferable to** dishonour. | ✘ more preferable than |
| **preference** | He showed a **preference for** tea **over** coffee. | — |
| **elder / older** | *elder* takes no comparison word (my elder brother); *older* takes **than** | ✘ He is elder **than** me |
| **outnumber / outweigh** (no comparison word) | The advantages **outweigh** the disadvantages. | ✘ outweigh than |
| **similar** | His car is **similar to** mine. | ✘ similar than / with |
| **equal** | Five plus five is **equal to** ten. | ✘ equal with / than |
| **identical** | This copy is **identical to (with)** the original. | ✘ identical than |
| **compare to / with** | Life is **compared to** a journey (likeness). / **Compare** this book **with** that (examine differences). | ✘ compare than |
| **different** | This is **different from** that. (BrE also *different to*; AmE *different than*) | exam key: **from** |
| **opposite** | His views are **opposite to** mine. | ✘ opposite than |
| **contrary** | This is **contrary to** the rules. | — |
| **analogous** | Analogous **to** | — |
| **akin / averse / adjacent / close / due / indifferent / prone / subject / vulnerable / liable** | all take **to** | (Chapter 18) |

### 10.3.1 *Prefer* — Patterns to Master

*Prefer* is one of the most frequently tested verbs. Learn all four patterns:

::: rule
1. **prefer + noun + to + noun:** I prefer **tea to coffee**.
2. **prefer + gerund + to + gerund:** I prefer **walking to cycling**.
3. **prefer + to-infinitive + rather than + bare infinitive:** I prefer **to walk rather than cycle**. (✘ prefer to walk than to cycle)
4. **would rather + bare infinitive + than + bare infinitive:** I **would rather walk than cycle**.
:::

::: ex
- ✘ I prefer reading **than** writing. ✔ I prefer reading **to** writing.
- ✘ I prefer **to die than** beg. ✔ I prefer **to die rather than** beg. / I **would rather** die **than** beg.
- ✘ I **would prefer** to stay home **than** going out. ✔ I **would rather** stay home **than** go out.
- ✘ Death is **more preferable** to dishonour. ✔ Death is **preferable** to dishonour.
:::

### 10.3.2 *Preferable*

*Preferable* already means "more desirable", so: ✘ *more preferable* ✔ *preferable*; ✘ *preferable than* ✔ *preferable to*.

## 10.4 Latin Words with Absolute Meaning

A related rule: some adjectives (many from Latin) have an **absolute** meaning and cannot be compared at all: *unique, perfect, complete, ideal, supreme, extreme, universal, eternal, infinite, absolute, entire, total, chief, round, square, empty, full, excellent, matchless, impossible, unanimous.*

::: ex
- ✘ This is **the most unique** design. ✔ This is **a unique** design.
- ✘ It is **more perfect** than that. ✔ It is **more nearly perfect** than that. / It is **better** than that.
- ✘ It was **the most extreme** case. (common in speech) — careful exam style: **an extreme** case.
- ✘ The decision was **very unanimous**. ✔ The decision was **unanimous**.
:::

## 10.5 Latin Comparatives in Context — Worked Sentences

::: ex
- Mr. Hasan is **senior to** Mr. Rahman in service, though **junior to** him in age.
- A soldier must obey his **superior** officers.
- Quality is **superior to** quantity.
- Prevention is **superior to** (better than) cure.
- The **prior** permission of the authority is necessary. / He had left **prior to** my arrival.
- In status, the Deputy Commissioner is **superior to** the Upazila Nirbahi Officer.
- He felt **inferior to** his talented classmates — he had an **inferiority** complex.
- The **interior** of the Sundarbans is dense; the **exterior** villages face cyclones.
- The **posterior** part of the brain controls balance.
- A **minor** (person under 18) cannot vote. / Bangla is a **major** language of the world.
:::

## 10.6 Nouns Formed from Latin Adjectives

| Adjective | Noun |
|---|---|
| senior | seniority (*promotion by seniority*) |
| junior | juniority (rare) |
| superior | superiority (*a sense of superiority*) |
| inferior | inferiority (*an inferiority complex*) |
| prior | priority (*Education gets top priority*) |
| major | majority (*the majority of people*) |
| minor | minority (*ethnic minorities*) |
| exterior | exteriority (rare) |
| interior | interiority (rare) |
| posterior | posteriority (rare) / posterity (*future generations*) |

## 10.7 Common Errors — Consolidated

::: errs
1. ✘ He is **senior than** me by two years. ✔ He is **senior to** me by two years.
2. ✘ Your pen is **superior than** mine. ✔ Your pen is **superior to** mine.
3. ✘ He is **more junior** to me. ✔ He is **junior to** me.
4. ✘ I prefer milk **than** tea. ✔ I prefer milk **to** tea.
5. ✘ He prefers **to walk than to ride**. ✔ He prefers **to walk rather than ride**. / He prefers **walking to riding**.
6. ✘ Death is **more preferable** than dishonour. ✔ Death is **preferable to** dishonour.
7. ✘ He is **very senior** to me. ✔ He is **much senior** to me.
8. ✘ She is junior to **I**. ✔ She is junior to **me**.
9. ✘ This cloth is **inferior from** that. ✔ This cloth is **inferior to** that.
10. ✘ This is **most unique**. ✔ This is **unique**.
11. ✘ My brother is **elder than** me. ✔ My brother is **older than** me. / He is my **elder** brother.
12. ✘ He is three years **senior than** me. ✔ He is **senior to** me **by three years**. / He is three years **older than** me.
13. ✘ The climate of Rangpur is **inferior to** Sylhet. ✔ … **inferior to that of** Sylhet.
14. ✘ I would rather die **than to** beg. ✔ I would rather die **than** beg.
15. ✘ He reached there **prior than** me. ✔ He reached there **before** me / **prior to** my arrival.
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Idiomatic Comparison:** The GMAT's idiom list includes **"prefer X to Y"**, **"X is preferable to Y"**, **"superior to"**, **"different from"**, **"regard X as Y"** (✘ regard X to be Y), and **"so X as to Y"**. A typical item: ✘ *"Many investors consider bonds* **as being superior than** *stocks in a downturn."* ✔ *"Many investors consider bonds* **superior to** *stocks in a downturn."* (Note also that *consider* takes no *as*: ✔ *consider X Y*.)
:::
