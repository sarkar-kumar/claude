## 27.1 Direct and Indirect Speech

There are two ways of reporting what someone said:

1. **Direct speech (direct narration):** the **exact words** of the speaker are given inside **inverted commas** (quotation marks): *He said, "I am ill."*
2. **Indirect speech (indirect / reported narration):** the **substance** of the speaker's words is reported in our own words, without inverted commas, usually with *that*: *He said that he was ill.*

**Parts of a direct speech sentence:**

- *He said* — the **reporting verb** (and reporting clause).
- *"I am ill."* — the **reported speech**.

## 27.2 General Rules for Changing Direct into Indirect Speech

::: rule
1. **Remove the comma and inverted commas** after the reporting verb.
2. **Use a suitable linking word**: *that* (statements), *if/whether* (yes/no questions), the wh-word itself (wh-questions), *to* + V1 (commands/requests).
3. **Change the reporting verb** according to the type of sentence (*said → told / asked / ordered / requested / advised / exclaimed / prayed / wished*).
4. **Change the person of the pronouns** (the PCT rule below).
5. **Change the tense** of the reported speech if the reporting verb is in the past.
6. **Change words showing nearness** of time and place (*now → then, here → there, today → that day*…).
:::

## 27.3 Change of Person — The "SON" Rule

A simple formula helps: **S-O-N = 1-2-3**.

- **1st person** (*I, we, me, my, our…*) of the reported speech changes according to the **S**ubject of the reporting verb.
- **2nd person** (*you, your…*) changes according to the **O**bject of the reporting verb.
- **3rd person** (*he, she, it, they…*) **N**o change.

::: ex
- **He** said to **me**, "**I** will help **you**." → He told me that **he** would help **me**. (I → he: subject; you → me: object)
- **You** said to **him**, "**I** like **your** book." → You told him that **you** liked **his** book.
- **She** said, "**He** is my brother." → She said that **he** was **her** brother. (*he* unchanged)
- **They** said to **us**, "**We** shall meet **you** tomorrow." → They told us that **they** would meet **us** the next day.
:::

::: note
When the reported speech contains *you* and the reporting verb has **no object**, assume the object from context or use *him/her/them*: *He said, "You are right."* → *He told me (him) that I was (he was) right.*
:::

## 27.4 Change of Tense

### 27.4.1 When Tenses Do Not Change

The tense of the reported speech **does not change** if:

1. The reporting verb is in the **present or future** tense: *He* **says**, *"I am ill."* → *He* **says** *that he* **is** *ill.* / *He* **will say**, *"I am ill."* → *He will say that he* **is** *ill.*
2. The reported speech expresses a **universal truth, habitual fact, proverb or historical fact**: *The teacher said, "The earth moves round the sun."* → *The teacher said that the earth* **moves** *round the sun.* / *He said, "Honesty is the best policy."* → *He said that honesty* **is** *the best policy.*
3. The reported speech contains an **unreal past / subjunctive / second or third conditional**: *He said, "I wish I were rich."* → *He said that he wished he* **were** *rich.*
4. The reported speech contains *could, would, should, might, ought to, used to, had better*: *He said, "You should go."* → *He said that I* **should** *go.*

### 27.4.2 Backshift When the Reporting Verb Is Past

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| Present indefinite: *am/is → was, are → were, write → wrote* | Past indefinite |
| Present continuous: *is writing* | Past continuous: *was writing* |
| Present perfect: *has written* | Past perfect: *had written* |
| Present perfect continuous: *has been writing* | Past perfect continuous: *had been writing* |
| Past indefinite: *wrote* | Past perfect: *had written* |
| Past continuous: *was writing* | Past perfect continuous: *had been writing* |
| Past perfect | No change |
| *shall / will* | *should / would* |
| *can / may* | *could / might* |
| *must* (obligation) | *must / had to* |
| *must* (permanent rule / deduction) | *must* (no change) |

## 27.5 Change of Words Showing Nearness

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| now | then |
| here | there |
| this | that |
| these | those |
| today | that day |
| tonight | that night |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| yesterday | the previous day / the day before |
| last night | the previous night / the night before |
| next week | the following week / the next week |
| last week | the previous week / the week before |
| ago | before |
| thus | so / in that way |
| hence | thence |
| come | go (often) |
| hither | thither |

::: note
If the reported speech is reported in the **same place** or on the **same day**, *here, this, today* need not change. *He said, "I will come here today."* reported the same day in the same place → *He said that he would come here today.*
:::

## 27.6 Assertive Sentences (Statements)

**Reporting verb:** *said → said*; *said to → told* (*informed, replied, stated, assured, remarked* according to sense).
**Linker:** *that*.

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| He said, "I am busy." | He said that he was busy. |
| She said to me, "I have finished my work." | She told me that she had finished her work. |
| Rahim said, "I saw a tiger yesterday." | Rahim said that he had seen a tiger the previous day. |
| They said, "We shall go to Cox's Bazar tomorrow." | They said that they would go to Cox's Bazar the next day. |
| He said, "I can swim." | He said that he could swim. |
| The boy said, "I may go there." | The boy said that he might go there. |
| He said to me, "You are right." | He told me that I was right. |
| My father said, "Honesty is the best policy." | My father said that honesty is the best policy. |
| He says, "I am tired." | He says that he is tired. |
| She said, "I must work hard." | She said that she must (had to) work hard. |

## 27.7 Interrogative Sentences (Questions)

**Reporting verb:** *said / said to → asked* (*enquired of, wanted to know, demanded of*).
**Linker:** *if / whether* for yes-no questions; the **wh-word** for wh-questions. Do **not** use *that*.
**Word order:** change the question form into **statement form** (subject + verb). Remove the question mark.

### 27.7.1 Yes/No Questions

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| He said to me, "Are you ill?" | He asked me **if / whether I was** ill. |
| She said to him, "Do you know me?" | She asked him **if he knew** her. |
| I said to her, "Have you finished?" | I asked her **whether she had finished**. |
| The teacher said to the boy, "Did you do it?" | The teacher asked the boy **if he had done** it. |
| He said to me, "Will you help me?" | He asked me **if I would help** him. |
| She said, "Can you swim?" | She asked **if I could** swim. |

### 27.7.2 Wh-Questions

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| He said to me, "What is your name?" | He asked me **what my name was**. |
| She said to him, "Where do you live?" | She asked him **where he lived**. |
| I said to her, "Why are you crying?" | I asked her **why she was crying**. |
| He said to me, "Who taught you English?" | He asked me **who had taught** me English. |
| The man said, "How did it happen?" | The man asked **how it had happened**. |
| He said to me, "When will you come?" | He asked me **when I would come**. |

::: rule
✘ *He asked me what* **was my name**. ✔ *He asked me what* **my name was**.
✘ *He asked me that whether I was ill.* ✔ *He asked me whether I was ill.*
✘ *She asked him where* **did he live**. ✔ *She asked him where* **he lived**.
:::

## 27.8 Imperative Sentences (Commands, Requests, Advice)

**Reporting verb:** according to the sense — *ordered, commanded* (command); *requested, begged, entreated, implored* (request / *please*); *advised* (advice); *forbade* (negative command); *told* (neutral); *urged, warned, reminded, suggested, proposed*.
**Linker:** **to + V1** (affirmative) / **not to + V1** (negative). *Please / kindly* is removed (the reporting verb *requested* carries the meaning).

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| He said to me, "Go out." | He **ordered** me **to go** out. |
| The teacher said to the boys, "Don't make a noise." | The teacher **told / ordered** the boys **not to make** a noise. / The teacher **forbade** the boys **to make** a noise. |
| She said to me, "Please help me." | She **requested** me **to help** her. |
| My father said to me, "Work hard." | My father **advised** me **to work** hard. |
| He said to the servant, "Bring a glass of water." | He **ordered** the servant **to bring** a glass of water. |
| The doctor said to the patient, "Take this medicine regularly." | The doctor **advised** the patient **to take** that medicine regularly. |
| He said, "Be quiet." | He **ordered** them **to be** quiet. |

### 27.8.1 Imperatives with *Let*

**(a) *Let us / Let's* (proposal) → *proposed / suggested* + *that* + *they/we* + *should* + V1:**

::: ex
- He said to me, "**Let us** go for a walk." → He **proposed / suggested to me that we should go** for a walk.
- They said, "Let's play football." → They **proposed that they should play** football.
:::

**(b) *Let* (permission / wish) → *wished / desired that* + *might / should be allowed to*:**

::: ex
- He said, "**Let** me go." → He **wanted that he might be allowed to** go. / He **requested to be allowed to** go.
- He said, "Let him do whatever he likes." → He **said that he might do** whatever he liked.
:::

## 27.9 Optative Sentences (Wishes, Prayers, Curses)

**Reporting verb:** *wished, prayed, cursed, desired, blessed*. **Linker:** *that*. *May* → *might*.

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| He said to me, "May you be happy." | He **wished that I might be** happy. |
| She said, "May Allah bless you." | She **prayed that Allah might bless** me / him. |
| The old man said to me, "May you live long." | The old man **blessed me saying that I might** live long. / The old man **prayed that I might live** long. |
| They said, "Long live our Prime Minister." | They **prayed that** their Prime Minister **might live long**. |
| He said, "Would that I were young again!" | He **wished that he were** young again. |
| He said, "May you go to hell!" | He **cursed** me **that I might** go to hell. |
| He said, "Goodbye, my friends." | He **bade** his friends **goodbye**. |
| She said, "Good morning, sir." | She **wished** him **good morning**. |

## 27.10 Exclamatory Sentences

**Reporting verb:** *exclaimed with joy / sorrow / wonder / surprise / regret / admiration*, *cried out, applauded*. **Linker:** *that*. The interjection is dropped; *what / how* is replaced by **very / great / such**, and the sentence becomes a statement.

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| He said, "Alas! I am undone." | He **exclaimed with sorrow that he was** undone. |
| They said, "Hurrah! We have won the game." | They **exclaimed with joy that they had** won the game. |
| She said, "What a beautiful flower it is!" | She **exclaimed with wonder that it was a very** beautiful flower. |
| He said, "How clever the boy is!" | He **exclaimed that the boy was very** clever. |
| The spectators said, "Bravo! Well played." | The spectators **applauded** the player, **saying that** he had played well. |
| I said, "Oh, what a fool I have been!" | I **exclaimed with regret that I had been a great** fool. |
| He said, "Fie! You are a liar." | He **exclaimed with disgust that I was** a liar. |

## 27.11 Mixed Sentences (More Than One Type)

When the reported speech contains two or more types of sentences, each part is reported with **its own reporting verb**, joined by *and, and added that, and asked, and then said, but*.

::: ex
- He said to me, "I am going to the market. **Will** you go with me?" → He told me that he was going to the market **and asked** me **if** I would go with him.
- The teacher said, "You are late. **Go** to your seat." → The teacher **told** me that I was late **and ordered** me **to go** to my seat.
- She said, "**Alas!** I have lost my purse. **Can** you help me?" → She **exclaimed with sorrow that** she had lost her purse **and asked** me **if** I could help her.
- He said to me, "**Where** are you going? **Don't** go there." → He **asked** me where I was going **and forbade** me **to go** there.
- Mother said, "It is late. Go to bed now." → Mother **said that** it was late **and told** me **to go** to bed then.
:::

## 27.12 Special Cases

**(a) *Yes / No* answers:** Replace with a full clause.

::: ex
- He said to me, "Are you ready?" I said, "**Yes**." → He asked me if I was ready. I **replied in the affirmative** (that I was).
- She said to him, "Will you come?" He said, "**No**." → She asked him whether he would come. He **replied in the negative** (that he would not).
:::

**(b) Vocative (address):** The noun of address becomes the **object** of the reporting verb.

::: ex
- He said, "**Rahim**, come here." → He **told Rahim to** come there.
- She said, "**Sir**, may I come in?" → She **respectfully asked** the teacher **if** she might come in.
- He said, "**Friends**, listen to me." → He **addressed his friends and asked them to** listen to him.
:::

**(c) *Thank you / Sorry / Congratulations / Welcome*:**

::: ex
- He said to me, "**Thank you**." → He **thanked** me.
- She said, "**Sorry**, I am late." → She **apologised for** being late.
- He said to me, "**Congratulations**!" → He **congratulated** me.
- They said, "**Welcome**, sir." → They **welcomed** him **respectfully**.
:::

**(d) *Must*:**

- Obligation in the present/future: *must → had to* (or no change): *He said, "I must go now."* → *He said that he* **had to** *go then.*
- Permanent obligation / rule / deduction: *must* stays: *He said, "All men must die."* → *He said that all men* **must** *die.*

**(e) *Shall* in questions (seeking advice/offer):** → *should*.

::: ex
- He said to me, "**Shall** I open the window?" → He asked me **if he should** open the window.
- She said, "What **shall** I do?" → She asked **what she should** do.
:::

**(f) Questions used as polite requests:**

::: ex
- He said to me, "**Will you please lend** me your pen?" → He **requested me to lend** him my pen.
:::

## 27.13 Indirect into Direct Speech

Reverse the process: restore the exact words, inverted commas, the original tense, pronouns and nearness words; choose the right punctuation (? ! .).

::: ex
- He told me that he was busy then. → He said to me, "I am busy now."
- She asked me whether I had finished my work. → She said to me, "Have you finished your work?"
- The teacher ordered the boys not to make a noise. → The teacher said to the boys, "Don't make a noise."
- He exclaimed with joy that they had won the match. → He said, "Hurrah! We have won the match."
- My mother prayed that I might pass the exam. → My mother said, "May you pass the exam!"
- He proposed that they should go for a picnic. → He said, "Let us go for a picnic."
:::

## 27.14 Common Errors in Narration

::: errs
1. ✘ He said me that he was ill. ✔ He **told** me that he was ill. / He **said to** me that…
2. ✘ He told that he was ill. ✔ He **said** that he was ill. (*told* needs a personal object)
3. ✘ He asked me that where I lived. ✔ He asked me **where I lived**.
4. ✘ He asked me where **did I live**. ✔ He asked me where **I lived**.
5. ✘ She asked me that whether I was ready. ✔ She asked me **whether** I was ready.
6. ✘ He told me to **not** go there. ✔ He told me **not to** go there.
7. ✘ He requested me **please** to help him. ✔ He **requested** me to help him.
8. ✘ He said that he **will** come tomorrow. ✔ He said that he **would** come **the next day**.
9. ✘ The teacher said that the sun **rose** in the east. ✔ … the sun **rises** in the east.
10. ✘ He ordered me **that** go out. ✔ He ordered me **to go** out.
11. ✘ She exclaimed with joy **what a beautiful** scene it was. ✔ She exclaimed with joy that it was **a very beautiful** scene.
12. ✘ He said that **he has** seen the film. ✔ He said that **he had** seen the film.
13. ✘ He asked me **if I am** hungry. ✔ He asked me **if I was** hungry.
14. ✘ He proposed **to me to** go for a walk. ✔ He proposed **to me that we should** go for a walk.
15. ✘ He said that he **saw** a tiger **yesterday**. ✔ He said that he **had seen** a tiger **the previous day**.
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Reported Speech and Sequence of Tenses:** The SAT/GMAT version of narration is the **sequence of tenses** in reported clauses. ✘ "The minister announced that the project **will** be completed by 2025." (past context) ✔ "…that the project **would** be completed by 2025." Also watch **embedded questions**: ✘ "Researchers asked how **could the disease spread**." ✔ "Researchers asked how **the disease could spread**." — the same statement word order you use in indirect questions (Chapter 37).
:::
