## 41.1 Why Change the Part of Speech?

In SSC, HSC, university admission and job tests you are often asked to **"Change the part of speech"** of a word, or to fill a blank with the correct **form** of a given word: *He is a man of great ___ (wise).* → **wisdom**. In MCQ papers, the same knowledge is tested as *word-form* errors (*He gave me an advise* → *advice*). The SAT and GMAT test word forms inside sentences (*The committee acted* **quick** → **quickly**).

The key is to know the **noun, verb, adjective and adverb forms** of common words. The suffixes in Chapter 40 help, but many forms are irregular and must simply be learnt.

::: rule
**Tips for using the right form in a sentence:**

- After an **article / possessive / adjective** → **noun**: *the* **beauty**, *his* **success**, *great* **courage**.
- After a **modal / to / subject** → **verb**: *can* **succeed**, *to* **simplify**, *they* **enrich**.
- Before a **noun** or after a **linking verb** → **adjective**: *a* **successful** *man*, *He is* **careful**.
- Modifying a **verb / adjective / whole sentence** → **adverb**: *He worked* **successfully**, **extremely** *hot*.
:::

## 41.3 Words Whose Forms Are Commonly Confused

| Pair | Part of speech | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **advice / advise** | noun / verb | Take my **advice**. / I **advise** you to rest. |
| **practice / practise** (BrE) | noun / verb | Practice makes perfect. / Practise daily. |
| **licence / license** (BrE) | noun / verb | a driving **licence** / He is **licensed** to drive. |
| **prophecy / prophesy** | noun / verb | His **prophecy** came true. / He **prophesied** a flood. |
| **device / devise** | noun / verb | a useful **device** / **devise** a plan |
| **belief / believe** | noun / verb | I **believe** in God; it is my firm **belief**. |
| **proof / prove** | noun / verb | Can you **prove** it? Show me the **proof**. |
| **loss / lose** | noun / verb | Don't **lose** heart; the **loss** is small. |
| **breath / breathe** | noun / verb | Take a deep **breath**; **breathe** slowly. |
| **cloth / clothe** | noun / verb | a piece of **cloth** / to feed and **clothe** the poor |
| **bath / bathe** | noun / verb | have a **bath** / **bathe** in the river |
| **choice / choose** | noun / verb | **Choose** carefully; it is your **choice**. |
| **success / succeed / successful / successfully** | n / v / adj / adv | He **succeeded**; his **success** made him a **successful** man who lived **successfully**. |
| **economic / economical** | adj (economics) / adj (thrifty) | **economic** growth / an **economical** car |
| **historic / historical** | adj (important) / adj (of history) | a **historic** speech / a **historical** novel |
| **childish / childlike** | negative / positive | **childish** behaviour / **childlike** innocence |
| **respectful / respectable / respective** | showing respect / deserving respect / each | a **respectful** student / a **respectable** family / their **respective** seats |

## 41.4 Sentence Practice (Worked Examples)

::: ex
- He is a man of great ___ (wise). → **wisdom**
- We should ___ (strong) our economy. → **strengthen**
- The ___ (arrive) of the guest was delayed. → **arrival**
- She sings ___ (beauty). → **beautifully**
- He was ___ (success) in the examination. → **successful**
- Ignorance is the cause of ___ (poor). → **poverty**
- He spoke with ___ (proud). → **pride**
- The ___ (deep) of the river is ten feet. → **depth**
- We must ___ (simple) the procedure. → **simplify**
- He is a ___ (danger) criminal. → **dangerous**
- The farmers ___ (rich) the soil with fertiliser. → **enrich**
- His ___ (behave) is praiseworthy. → **behaviour**
- He ___ (glory) the nation by his achievement. → **glorified**
- Education should be made ___ (compel). → **compulsory**
- We were ___ (surprise) at his success. → **surprised**
- He has a ___ (strength) body. → **strong**
- She ___ (critic) the plan. → **criticised**
- The ___ (free) of the press is essential for democracy. → **freedom**
- The girl ___ (frightened) when she saw the snake. → **was frightened**
- Because of his illness, he was ___ (able) to finish the work. → **unable**
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Word Forms in Context:** SAT Writing often gives four forms of the same word (*effective / effect / effectively / effectiveness*) and asks which fits. Decide the needed part of speech from its **position** first. In the GRE, related forms with very different meanings are traps: *credible* (believable) vs *credulous* (gullible) vs *creditable* (praiseworthy); *ingenious* (clever) vs *ingenuous* (innocent); *luxuriant* (lush) vs *luxurious* (expensive); *sanguine* (optimistic) vs *sanguinary* (bloody).
:::
