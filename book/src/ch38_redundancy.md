## 38.1 What Is Redundancy?

**Redundancy** (also called *tautology* or *pleonasm* in some cases) means using **more words than necessary** — repeating an idea that has already been expressed. A redundant word adds nothing to the meaning; it only makes the sentence clumsy.

::: ex
- ✘ He **returned back** home. (*return* already means *come back*)
- ✘ The **reason** for his absence is **because** he was ill. (*reason* and *because* repeat the cause)
- ✘ Please **repeat** it **again**. (*repeat* = say again)
:::

Redundancy questions are common in BCS and bank exams ("Find the superfluous word" / error detection) and are a favourite of the **GMAT and SAT**, where the **concise** answer is usually correct when the meaning is the same.

::: rule
**Rule of concision:** If a word or phrase can be removed **without changing the meaning**, it is redundant — remove it.
:::

### 38.2.4 Double Negatives

A sentence with two negatives either cancels itself or is non-standard.

::: ex
- ✘ I **don't** have **no** money. ✔ I don't have **any** money. / I have **no** money.
- ✘ He **can't hardly** walk. ✔ He **can hardly** walk.
- ✘ I **didn't** see **nobody**. ✔ I didn't see **anybody**. / I saw **nobody**.
- ✘ **Unless** you **don't** work hard … ✔ **Unless** you work hard …
- ✘ **Lest** you **should not** fall … ✔ **Lest** you **should** fall …
- ✘ **Until** he **does not** come … ✔ **Until** he comes …
- ✘ He **seldom or ever** comes. ✔ He **seldom, if ever,** comes.
:::

### 38.2.5 Double Conjunctions

| Redundant | Correct |
|---|---|
| although … but | although … (or) … but |
| though … but | though … (yet is accepted) |
| because … so | because … / … so |
| since … therefore | since … |
| as … therefore | as … |
| the reason is because | the reason is that |
| the reason why … is because | the reason why … is that / … because |
| both … as well as | both … and |
| due to the fact that | because |
| in spite of the fact that | although |
| whether or not (when *not* is unnecessary) | whether |

::: ex
- ✘ **Although** he tried hard, **but** he failed. ✔ **Although** he tried hard, he failed.
- ✘ **Because** it was raining, **so** we stayed inside. ✔ **Because** it was raining, we stayed inside.
- ✘ **The reason** he failed **is because** he was lazy. ✔ **The reason** he failed **is that** he was lazy. / He failed **because** he was lazy.
:::

### 38.2.7 Repetition of Subject, Object or Pronoun

::: ex
- ✘ My father **he** is a doctor. ✔ My father is a doctor.
- ✘ The book **which** I bought **it** is interesting. ✔ The book which I bought is interesting.
- ✘ The man **who** you met **him** yesterday is my uncle. ✔ The man (whom) you met yesterday is my uncle.
- ✘ This is the house **where** I live **in**. ✔ This is the house where I live. / … which I live in.
- ✘ I **myself personally** think so. ✔ I personally think so. / I myself think so.
:::

## 38.3 Tautology in Literature vs Redundancy in Exams

Writers sometimes repeat for **emphasis or rhythm** (e.g. *"I have done it with my own eyes and my own hands"*). Such deliberate repetition is a figure of speech (*pleonasm*, *tautology* — Chapter 63). In exam questions, however, redundancy is treated as an **error** unless it is clearly used for effect.

## 38.4 Common Redundancy Errors — Consolidated

::: errs
1. ✘ He **again repeated** the question. ✔ He **repeated** the question.
2. ✘ We should **cooperate together**. ✔ We should **cooperate**.
3. ✘ It was a **free gift** from the company. ✔ It was a **gift** from the company.
4. ✘ The **end result** was disappointing. ✔ The **result** was disappointing.
5. ✘ **Each and every** student **were** present. ✔ **Every** student **was** present.
6. ✘ The meeting was **postponed until later**. ✔ The meeting was **postponed**.
7. ✘ She is my **cousin sister**. ✔ She is my **cousin**.
8. ✘ He **returned back** from Dhaka yesterday. ✔ He **returned** from Dhaka yesterday.
9. ✘ The **reason** of his success is **because** he worked hard. ✔ The reason **for** his success is **that** he worked hard.
10. ✘ The ball is **round in shape**. ✔ The ball is **round**.
11. ✘ I saw it **with my own eyes** (in formal writing). ✔ I saw it **myself**.
12. ✘ **In my opinion, I think** he is right. ✔ **I think** he is right. / **In my opinion,** he is right.
13. ✘ He **descended down** the stairs. ✔ He **descended** the stairs.
14. ✘ The two rivers **merge together** near Chandpur. ✔ The two rivers **merge** near Chandpur.
15. ✘ It is a **new innovation**. ✔ It is an **innovation**.
16. ✘ The **true facts** were hidden. ✔ The **facts** were hidden.
17. ✘ He came at **7 a.m. in the morning**. ✔ He came at **7 a.m.**
18. ✘ I enjoyed **very much** the party **very much**. ✔ I enjoyed the party **very much**.
19. ✘ The **past history** of Bangladesh is glorious. ✔ The **history** of Bangladesh is glorious.
20. ✘ Please **refer back** to page 10. ✔ Please **refer** to page 10.
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Concision Is King:** On the SAT, when two choices are grammatically correct and mean the same thing, **the shorter one is almost always right**. GMAT Sentence Correction also penalises redundancy: ✘ *"The two companies agreed to merge together"*, ✘ *"The rise in temperature was increasing"*, ✘ *"The cost of living has increased by twice as much as before"*. Watch especially for **double time markers** (*"Previously, the company had earlier announced…"*) and **double cause words** (*"because of … due to"*).
:::
