## 7.1 What Is Gender?

In grammar, **gender** is the classification of nouns and pronouns according to **sex** — male, female, either, or none. English gender is mostly **natural gender** (based on real-life sex), unlike languages such as French, German, Arabic or Hindi, where a table or a river can be "masculine" or "feminine". This makes English gender simpler, but exams still test the masculine–feminine pairs, common-gender nouns, personification and pronoun agreement.

## 7.2 The Four Genders

| Gender | Refers to | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Masculine** | male beings | man, boy, father, king, lion, cock, he |
| **Feminine** | female beings | woman, girl, mother, queen, lioness, hen, she |
| **Common** | either male or female | child, parent, student, teacher, friend, baby, cousin, doctor, person, servant, orphan, enemy, pupil, relative, citizen, leader |
| **Neuter** | lifeless things / things with no sex | book, table, river, house, tree, stone, it |

::: note
Very young children and animals whose sex is unknown or unimportant are often referred to as **it**: *The baby is crying; it is hungry. The cat has hurt its paw.* Insects and small creatures are usually neuter: *The ant carried its food.*
:::

## 7.4 Common Gender Nouns

These nouns can refer to **either sex**: *baby, child, cousin, enemy, friend, guardian, leader, monarch, neighbour, orphan, parent, person, pupil, relation, relative, servant, spouse, student, teacher, thief, citizen, doctor, lawyer, engineer, writer, artist, singer, cook, clerk, infant, owner, passenger, president, minister, member, candidate, voter, deer, sheep, cattle, bird, fish, animal, calf, foal, cub.*

To specify sex, add a word: *a* **woman** *doctor* (plural *women doctors*), *a* **male** *nurse*, *a* **female** *engineer*, *a* **lady** *teacher*.

::: note
**Gender-neutral modern usage.** International tests (IELTS, SAT, GRE, GMAT) and modern style guides prefer neutral words: *chairperson/chair* (for chairman), *police officer* (policeman), *firefighter* (fireman), *flight attendant* (steward/stewardess), *actor* for both sexes, *author* and *poet* for both (*authoress, poetess* are now old-fashioned), *humankind* (mankind), *spokesperson* (spokesman), *salesperson*. Bangladeshi exams still ask the traditional feminine forms, so learn both.
:::

## 7.5 Gender of Neuter Nouns by Personification

When lifeless things or abstract ideas are spoken of **as if they were living beings**, they are said to be **personified** and are given a gender.

**Masculine (things strong, powerful, violent, great):** *the Sun, Death, Time, War, Summer, Winter, Anger, Fear, Love (Cupid), Ocean, Thunder, Mountain, Autumn, Despair, Murder, Wind, Day.*

::: ex
- The **Sun** sheds **his** light on all.
- **Death** lays **his** icy hand on kings. (James Shirley)
- **Time** waits for no one; **he** flies on.
- **Winter** came with **his** cold breath.
:::

**Feminine (things gentle, beautiful, graceful, fruitful):** *the Moon, the Earth, Nature, Spring, Hope, Peace, Mercy, Justice, Liberty, Charity, Faith, Fortune, Virtue, Night, Religion, Science, Truth, Victory, Ship, Country/Nation, University, Church, Fame, Music, Art.*

::: ex
- The **Moon** has hidden **her** face behind the clouds.
- **Nature** is kind to those who love **her**.
- The **ship** lost **her** way in the storm. / **She** sails tomorrow.
- **Bangladesh** is proud of **her** freedom fighters.
- **Spring** has come with all **her** beauty.
- **Fortune** favours the brave; **she** is fickle.
:::

## 7.6 Gender and Pronoun Agreement

A pronoun must agree with its antecedent (the noun it refers to) in gender as well as number and person (Chapter 08).

::: ex
- The boy lost **his** pen. / The girl lost **her** pen. / The tree lost **its** leaves.
- Each of the girls brought **her** own lunch.
- Every student must bring **his or her** ID card. (traditional exam form: *his*)
- ✘ The baby is crying for **his** mother (sex unknown) → ✔ … for **its** mother. (Both are accepted when the sex is known.)
- The cow gave **her** calf milk. (Higher animals are often referred to as *he/she*; pets almost always.)
:::

::: ex
- **Common-gender noun + both sexes:** *Each of the students — boys and girls — must submit* **his or her** *assignment.*
- **Mixed group:** *Rahim and Salma did* **their** *work.*
:::

## 7.7 Some Special Notes

1. **Masculine-for-both rule (traditional):** When a statement refers to both sexes, traditional grammar uses the masculine: *Man is mortal. Everyone should love* **his** *country.* (Modern English prefers *people / humans* and *his or her / their*.)
2. **Feminine for the larger/more important in some pairs:** *duck* (the general name for both sexes; *drake* only for the male), *goose* (general; *gander* for the male), *bee* (general), *cow* (general for cattle in everyday speech).
3. **Masculine as general:** *dog* (general; *bitch* for the female), *horse* (general; *mare* for female), *fox* (general; *vixen* for female), *lion*, *tiger*.
4. **Same word, two genders:** *cousin, friend, parent, spouse, neighbour*.
5. **Gender of compound titles:** *Lord Mayor → Lady Mayoress, Prince Consort → Queen (consort = spouse of the reigning monarch), Sultan → Sultana.*
6. **Fiancé** (man) / **fiancée** (woman) — the doubled *e* marks the feminine (from French). Similarly *blond* (m) / *blonde* (f), *protégé / protégée*, *divorcé / divorcée*, *née* (born as — feminine: *Mrs. Rahman, née Chowdhury*).

## 7.8 Common Errors with Gender

::: errs
1. ✘ She is my **cousin sister**. ✔ She is my **cousin**.
2. ✘ They are **woman doctors**. ✔ They are **women doctors**.
3. ✘ The feminine of *bachelor* is **bacheloress**. ✔ **spinster / maid**.
4. ✘ The masculine of *widow* is **widow-man**. ✔ **widower**.
5. ✘ The masculine of *duck* is **duckling**. ✔ **drake** (*duckling* = a young duck).
6. ✘ The feminine of *fox* is **foxess**. ✔ **vixen**.
7. ✘ The feminine of *wizard* is **wizardess**. ✔ **witch**.
8. ✘ The moon sheds **his** light. ✔ The moon sheds **her** light. (traditional personification)
9. ✘ Bangladesh is proud of **his** freedom fighters. ✔ Bangladesh is proud of **her** freedom fighters.
10. ✘ The feminine of *hero* is **heroess**. ✔ **heroine**.
11. ✘ The feminine of *monk* is **monkess**. ✔ **nun**.
12. ✘ The masculine of *mare* is **mare-horse**. ✔ **horse / stallion**.
13. ✘ The feminine of *stag* is **stagess**. ✔ **hind**.
14. ✘ The feminine of *testator* is **testatoress**. ✔ **testatrix**.
15. ✘ The feminine of *emperor* is **emperoress**. ✔ **empress**.
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Pronoun Gender and Clarity:** International tests treat gender mainly through **pronoun reference**. A sentence like *"When Ayesha met Salma, she told her the news"* is **ambiguous** (who told whom?), and such ambiguity is always wrong on the GMAT/SAT. Rewrite with a noun: *"When Ayesha met Salma, Ayesha told her the news."* Also note that the SAT accepts singular *they* only in informal contexts; with a singular antecedent like *each student*, the safe choice remains *his or her*.
:::
