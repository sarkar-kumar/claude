# Adverb {#ch15}

[Part A · Grammar]{.chpart}

## 15.1 Definition

An **adverb** is a word that **modifies** (adds to the meaning of) a **verb, an adjective, another adverb**, a preposition, a conjunction, a phrase, or a whole sentence. The word means "added to a verb" (Latin *ad* = to + *verbum* = verb), because most adverbs modify verbs.

::: ex
- She sings **sweetly**. (modifies the verb *sings*)
- He is **very** clever. (modifies the adjective *clever*)
- He walks **quite** slowly. (modifies the adverb *slowly*)
- The cat jumped **right** over the wall. (modifies the preposition *over*)
- He left **just** before I arrived. (modifies the conjunction *before*)
- He was punished **only** for his rudeness. (modifies the phrase)
- **Unfortunately**, the train was late. (modifies the whole sentence)
:::

::: rule
**An adverb never modifies a noun or pronoun** — that is the job of an adjective. ✘ *He is a* **really** *man.* ✔ *He is a* **real** *man.* But adverbs like *only, even, also, almost, quite, just* can focus on a noun phrase: **Only** *Rahim passed.* **Even** *a child knows this.*
:::

## 15.2 Kinds of Adverbs (by Meaning)

| Kind | Question answered | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Manner** | How? | quickly, slowly, carefully, well, badly, fast, hard, loudly, bravely, happily, so, thus |
| **Time** | When? | now, then, today, yesterday, tomorrow, soon, late, early, already, yet, still, before, ago, lately, recently, since |
| **Place** | Where? | here, there, everywhere, nowhere, up, down, in, out, above, below, near, far, away, abroad, inside, outside, upstairs |
| **Frequency** | How often? | always, usually, often, frequently, generally, sometimes, occasionally, seldom, rarely, hardly ever, never, once, twice, daily, weekly |
| **Degree / Quantity** | How much? To what extent? | very, too, so, quite, rather, fairly, almost, nearly, enough, much, extremely, completely, hardly, scarcely, barely, entirely, partly |
| **Affirmation / Negation** | — | yes, certainly, surely, indeed, of course, no, not, never |
| **Reason / Consequence** | Why? | therefore, hence, consequently, so, thus |
| **Interrogative** | (asks questions) | when, where, why, how, how often, how long |
| **Relative** | (joins clauses) | when, where, why, how — *This is the house* **where** *I was born.* |
| **Sentence / Viewpoint** | (comments on the whole) | fortunately, luckily, frankly, honestly, obviously, evidently, surely, perhaps, probably |

## 15.3 Formation of Adverbs

### 15.3.1 Adjective + *-ly*

| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| Most adjectives: add *-ly* | quick → quickly, careful → carefully, brave → bravely, sad → sadly |
| Ending in *-y* (consonant + y): *y → ily* | happy → happily, easy → easily, busy → busily, heavy → heavily |
| Ending in *-le*: drop *e*, add *y* | simple → simply, gentle → gently, able → ably, possible → possibly |
| Ending in *-ic*: add *-ally* | basic → basically, tragic → tragically, scientific → scientifically (exception: public → publicly) |
| Ending in *-ll*: add *-y* | full → fully, dull → dully |
| Ending in *-ue*: drop *e* | true → truly, due → duly |
| Others | whole → wholly, shy → shyly, sly → slyly, dry → drily/dryly |

### 15.3.2 Adverbs with the Same Form as Adjectives (Flat Adverbs)

*fast, hard, late, early, high, low, near, far, long, straight, right, wrong, deep, daily, weekly, monthly, yearly, kindly, well, little, much, enough, loud, clean, direct, free, tight, wide.*

| Word | As adjective | As adverb |
|---|---|---|
| fast | a **fast** train | He runs **fast**. |
| hard | a **hard** task | He works **hard**. |
| late | the **late** train | He came **late**. |
| early | an **early** riser | She gets up **early**. |
| straight | a **straight** line | Go **straight** home. |
| daily | a **daily** newspaper | It is published **daily**. |
| well | I am **well**. (healthy) | She sings **well**. |

::: note
**Well** is the adverb of **good**: *He is a* **good** *player; he plays* **well**. (✘ plays good). But *well* is also an adjective meaning *healthy*: *I don't feel* **well**.
:::

### 15.3.3 Pairs with Different Meanings (Very Frequently Tested)

| Without *-ly* | With *-ly* |
|---|---|
| **hard** = with effort: *He works* **hard**. | **hardly** = almost not: *He* **hardly** *works.* |
| **late** = not early: *He came* **late**. | **lately** = recently: *I haven't seen him* **lately**. |
| **near** = close: *Come* **near**. | **nearly** = almost: *I* **nearly** *fell.* |
| **high** = at a height: *The bird flew* **high**. | **highly** = very much: *He is* **highly** *educated.* |
| **short** = suddenly: *He stopped* **short**. | **shortly** = soon: *He will arrive* **shortly**. |
| **deep** = far down: *He dug* **deep**. | **deeply** = greatly: *I am* **deeply** *sorry.* |
| **free** = without cost: *Children travel* **free**. | **freely** = without restriction: *Speak* **freely**. |
| **most** = superlative: *What I like* **most** *is…* | **mostly** = mainly: *The audience were* **mostly** *students.* |
| **wide** = fully: *Open the door* **wide**. | **widely** = in many places: *He has travelled* **widely**. |
| **close** = near: *Stay* **close**. | **closely** = carefully: *Watch* **closely**. |
| **just** = exactly/recently: *He has* **just** *left.* | **justly** = fairly: *He was* **justly** *punished.* |
| **pretty** = fairly: *It's* **pretty** *good.* | **prettily** = attractively: *She was* **prettily** *dressed.* |

::: ex
- ✘ He worked **hardly** and passed. ✔ He worked **hard** and passed.
- ✘ He is **high** educated. ✔ He is **highly** educated.
- ✘ I have not seen him **late**. ✔ I have not seen him **lately**.
:::

## 15.4 Position of Adverbs

### 15.4.1 Adverbs of Manner, Place and Time — the "MPT" Order

When several adverbs come at the end of a clause, the usual order is **Manner → Place → Time** (MPT).

::: ex
- He sang **beautifully** (M) **at the concert** (P) **last night** (T).
- She worked **hard** (M) **in the office** (P) **yesterday** (T).
- ✘ He went yesterday to Dhaka by bus. ✔ He went **to Dhaka** (P) **by bus** (M — with verbs of movement, place often comes first) **yesterday** (T).
:::

::: note
With verbs of movement (*go, come, arrive, leave*), **place** usually comes before manner: *He went* **home quickly**. *She came* **here by bus**.
:::

### 15.4.2 Adverbs of Frequency (and *already, almost, just, never, also, only, even, still*)

They go:

- **before the main verb**: *He* **always** *comes late. I* **often** *visit my village.*
- **after *am / is / are / was / were***: *He is* **always** *late. She was* **never** *rude.*
- **after the first auxiliary**: *I have* **never** *seen a tiger. He can* **hardly** *walk. You should* **always** *tell the truth.*

::: ex
- ✘ He **comes always** late. ✔ He **always comes** late.
- ✘ She **always is** punctual. ✔ She **is always** punctual.
- ✘ I **never have** seen it. ✔ I **have never** seen it.
- **Sometimes, usually, often, occasionally** can also start a sentence: **Sometimes** *I walk to work.* (But not *always, never, seldom, ever*: ✘ *Always I go.*)
:::

### 15.4.3 Adverbs Modifying Adjectives/Adverbs

They come **before** the word they modify: *very good, extremely hot, quite well, too fast.* Exception: **enough** comes **after**: *good* **enough**, *fast* **enough** (✘ enough good).

### 15.4.4 The Position of *Only* (Meaning Changes!)

*Only* should be placed **immediately before** the word it modifies. Moving it changes the meaning:

::: ex
- **Only** I saw him yesterday. (No one else saw him.)
- I **only** saw him yesterday. (I did not speak to him — only saw.)
- I saw **only** him yesterday. (I saw nobody else.)
- I saw him **only** yesterday. (It was as recent as yesterday.)
- ✘ He **only** has ten taka (meaning: he has no more). ✔ He has **only** ten taka.
:::

The same applies to *even, just, almost, nearly, merely, hardly, scarcely*: *He* **almost** *ate the whole cake* vs *He ate* **almost** *the whole cake.*

### 15.4.5 Adverbs at the Start of a Sentence — Inversion

When negative or restrictive adverbs (*never, seldom, rarely, hardly, scarcely, no sooner, not only, little, nowhere, only then*) begin a sentence, the subject and auxiliary are **inverted**: **Never have I** *seen such a sight.* **Seldom does he** *come here.* (Chapter 16.)

## 15.5 Comparison of Adverbs

| Type | Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|---|
| One-syllable (and *early*) | fast, hard, soon, late, early | faster, harder, sooner, later, earlier | fastest, hardest, soonest, latest, earliest |
| Adverbs in *-ly* | carefully, quickly, beautifully | more carefully, more quickly | most carefully, most quickly |
| Irregular | well | better | best |
| | badly | worse | worst |
| | much | more | most |
| | little | less | least |
| | far | farther / further | farthest / furthest |

::: ex
- ✘ He ran **more fast** than me. ✔ He ran **faster** than me.
- ✘ She sings **more good** than her sister. ✔ She sings **better** than her sister.
- ✘ He drives **carefullier**. ✔ He drives **more carefully**.
:::

## 15.6 Some Important Adverbs and Their Correct Use

### 15.6.1 *Very* and *Much*

| *Very* | *Much* |
|---|---|
| with **positive** adjectives/adverbs: *very good, very fast* | with **comparatives**: *much better, much faster*; and with *too*: *much too heavy* |
| with **present participles** used as adjectives: *very interesting, very charming* | with **past participles** (verbal sense): *much admired, much obliged, much pleased* (also *very pleased*) |
| — | before *superlatives*: *much the best* (= by far) |

::: ex
- ✘ He is **very** better now. ✔ He is **much** better now.
- ✘ This is **much** interesting. ✔ This is **very** interesting.
- ✘ He was **very** praised. ✔ He was **much** praised.
- ✔ I am **very tired / very pleased** (adjectival participles — *very* accepted).
:::

### 15.6.2 *Too* and *Very*

- **Very** = to a high degree (neutral): *It is* **very** *hot today.*
- **Too** = more than enough / excessively (negative result): *It is* **too** *hot to go out.*
- ✘ He is **too** kind (meaning *very* kind) → ✔ He is **very** kind. ✘ She is **too** beautiful (as praise) → ✔ She is **very** beautiful.
- *Too* also means *also* (at the end): *I like it* **too**.

### 15.6.3 *Too*, *Also*, *Either*, *As well*

- **Too / as well** — at the end of affirmative sentences: *He can swim* **too / as well**.
- **Also** — in mid-position: *He can* **also** *swim.*
- **Either** — at the end of **negative** sentences: *He can't swim* **either**. (✘ He can't swim too.)

### 15.6.4 *Enough*

Comes **after** adjectives and adverbs, **before** nouns: *He is old* **enough**. *She runs fast* **enough**. *We have* **enough** *money.*

### 15.6.5 *Ago*, *Before*, *Since*

- **Ago** — counts back from **now**; used with the **simple past**: *I met him two years* **ago**. (✘ I have met him two years ago.)
- **Before** — counts back from a **past point**; used with the **past perfect**: *He said he had met her two years* **before**. Or simply *earlier, previously*: *I have seen him* **before**.
- **Since** — from a point in time up to now (perfect tenses): *I have known him* **since** *2015.*

::: ex
- ✘ He came here **before** two days. ✔ He came here two days **ago**.
- ✘ He **has left** an hour ago. ✔ He **left** an hour ago.
:::

### 15.6.6 *Already*, *Yet*, *Still*

- **Already** — sooner than expected (affirmative): *He has* **already** *finished.*
- **Yet** — up to now (negatives and questions, end position): *He hasn't come* **yet**. *Has he come* **yet**?
- **Still** — continuing (mid-position): *He is* **still** *sleeping.* *He* **still** *hasn't paid.* (stronger negative than *yet*)

### 15.6.7 *Hardly*, *Scarcely*, *Barely*, *Seldom*, *Rarely* — Negative Adverbs

These already have a **negative meaning**, so they must **not** be used with *not / no / never* (double negative):

::: ex
- ✘ I **can't hardly** believe it. ✔ I **can hardly** believe it.
- ✘ He **seldom or ever** comes. ✔ He **seldom, if ever,** comes. / He **seldom or never** comes.
- ✘ **Hardly** had he arrived **than** it began to rain. ✔ **Hardly** had he arrived **when** it began to rain.
- ✘ **Scarcely no** people came. ✔ **Scarcely any** people came. (*scarcely any* = almost no)
- Tag question after a negative adverb is positive: *He* **hardly** *works,* **does he**?
:::

### 15.6.8 *So* and *Such*

- **So** + adjective/adverb: *He is* **so** *tired. She ran* **so** *fast.*
- **Such** + (a/an) + adjective + noun: *He is* **such** *a tired man. They are* **such** *good friends.*
- **So … that / such … that** for result: *He was* **so** *weak* **that** *he couldn't walk. It was* **such** *a hot day* **that** *we stayed in.*
- ✘ He is **so** a good man. ✔ He is **such** a good man. / He is **so** good **a** man (formal).

### 15.6.9 *Quite*, *Rather*, *Fairly*, *Pretty*

- **Fairly** — positive, moderate: *The film was* **fairly** *good.*
- **Rather** — often with unpleasant ideas or "more than expected": *It is* **rather** *cold. The exam was* **rather** *difficult.* With comparatives: **rather** *better*.
- **Quite** — (a) completely with absolute adjectives: **quite** *right,* **quite** *impossible*; (b) fairly with gradable ones: **quite** *good*.
- **Pretty** — informal, like *rather/fairly*: **pretty** *good.*

### 15.6.10 *Else* and *Than*

*Else* is followed by **but**, not *than*: ✘ *It is nothing else than pride.* ✔ *It is nothing else* **but** *pride.* (Also ✔ *nothing* **other than** *pride*.)

### 15.6.11 *Ever* and *Never*

- **Ever** = at any time (questions, negatives, comparisons): *Have you* **ever** *been to Sylhet? It is the best film I have* **ever** *seen.*
- **Never** = at no time: *I have* **never** *been there.*
- ✘ He is the best man I have **never** met. ✔ … I have **ever** met.

### 15.6.12 *Abroad*, *Home*, *Upstairs*, *Downstairs*, *Downtown* — no preposition

::: ex
- ✘ He went **to abroad**. ✔ He went **abroad**.
- ✘ He came **to home**. ✔ He came **home**.
- ✘ She went **to upstairs**. ✔ She went **upstairs**.
- ✔ He is **at home**. (static — *at* is used)
:::

## 15.7 Adverb Clauses and Phrases (Introduction)

An adverb can be a single word (*quickly*), a phrase (*with great care*, *in the morning*) or a clause (*when he came*, *because it rained*). Adverb clauses are discussed in **Chapter 31**; they are classified as time, place, reason, purpose, result, condition, concession, comparison and manner.

## 15.8 Conjunctive Adverbs (Sentence Connectors)

*However, therefore, moreover, furthermore, nevertheless, nonetheless, consequently, otherwise, besides, hence, thus, meanwhile, still, then, instead, accordingly, indeed* — these join ideas between **sentences** or independent clauses but are **not conjunctions**. Between two independent clauses they need a **semicolon** (or a full stop), followed by a comma.

::: ex
- ✘ He was ill, **however** he attended the meeting. (comma splice) ✔ He was ill; **however**, he attended the meeting.
- ✔ He was ill. **Nevertheless**, he attended the meeting.
- ✔ It was raining; **therefore**, the match was postponed.
:::

## 15.9 Common Errors with Adverbs

::: errs
1. ✘ He speaks English **good**. ✔ He speaks English **well**.
2. ✘ She sings **sweet**. ✔ She sings **sweetly**.
3. ✘ He is **very** stronger than me. ✔ He is **much** stronger than me.
4. ✘ He worked **hardly** and passed. ✔ He worked **hard** and passed.
5. ✘ He came here **before** three days. ✔ He came here three days **ago**.
6. ✘ He **always is** late. ✔ He **is always** late.
7. ✘ I **can't hardly** hear you. ✔ I **can hardly** hear you.
8. ✘ He is **enough** strong. ✔ He is strong **enough**.
9. ✘ He went **to abroad** last year. ✔ He went **abroad** last year.
10. ✘ It is **too** hot today (meaning *very*). ✔ It is **very** hot today.
11. ✘ I don't like it **too**. ✔ I don't like it **either**.
12. ✘ He has **lately** come. ✔ He has come **late** / He has **recently** come.
13. ✘ She is **too** much beautiful. ✔ She is **very** beautiful.
14. ✘ He **seldom or ever** visits us. ✔ He **seldom, if ever,** visits us.
15. ✘ He is **highly** educated **from** Oxford. ✔ He was educated **at** Oxford. / He is **highly** educated.
16. ✘ He drove **careful**. ✔ He drove **carefully**.
17. ✘ This is the best book I have **never** read. ✔ … I have **ever** read.
18. ✘ He **only** died yesterday (meaning: as recently as yesterday). ✔ He died **only** yesterday.
19. ✘ **At first** I will discuss the causes, then the effects. ✔ **First**, I will discuss the causes, then the effects. (*At first* = in the beginning, as opposed to later.)
20. ✘ He returned **back** from Dhaka. ✔ He returned from Dhaka. (redundant *back*)
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Adjective/Adverb Confusion and Placement:** SAT Writing questions frequently ask you to choose between an adjective and an adverb: ✘ "The new policy was implemented **quick**." ✔ "…implemented **quickly**." ✘ "a **real** impressive result" ✔ "a **really** impressive result". The GMAT tests the **placement** of *only*: ✘ "The company only hired three engineers last year." (ambiguous) ✔ "The company hired **only three** engineers last year." On the GRE, conjunctive adverbs (*however, moreover, consequently, nonetheless*) are the key "signal words" that reveal whether the next sentence continues or reverses the argument.
:::
