# Pronoun {#ch08}

[Part A · Grammar]{.chpart}
:::

## 8.1 Definition

A **pronoun** is a word used **in place of a noun** so that the noun need not be repeated. The noun that a pronoun refers to is called its **antecedent**.

::: ex
- **Rahim** is absent today because **he** is ill. (*he* → Rahim)
- **The students** said that **they** would come. (*they* → the students)
- I bought **a book** and gave **it** to my sister. (*it* → a book)
:::

Without pronouns English would be clumsy: *Rahim said that Rahim had lost Rahim's book.* → *Rahim said that* **he** *had lost* **his** *book.*

## 8.2 Kinds of Pronouns

| Kind | Members | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Personal | I, we, you, he, she, it, they (and their object/possessive forms) | **She** loves **him**. |
| Possessive | mine, ours, yours, his, hers, its, theirs | This pen is **mine**. |
| Reflexive | myself, ourselves, yourself, yourselves, himself, herself, itself, themselves, oneself | He hurt **himself**. |
| Emphatic (Intensive) | the same *-self* forms used for emphasis | I **myself** saw it. |
| Demonstrative | this, that, these, those, such, the same | **This** is my house. |
| Indefinite | one, none, some, any, all, many, few, each, either, neither, someone, anybody, everything, nothing, other, another, several, both | **Someone** has stolen my bag. |
| Distributive | each, either, neither | **Each** of them got a prize. |
| Reciprocal | each other, one another | They love **each other**. |
| Relative | who, whom, whose, which, that, what, as, but | The man **who** came is my uncle. |
| Interrogative | who, whom, whose, which, what | **Who** is there? |
| Exclamatory | what | **What!** You failed? |

## 8.3 Personal Pronouns

### 8.3.1 Person, Number and Case

| Person | Number | Subjective (Nominative) | Objective | Possessive determiner | Possessive pronoun | Reflexive |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | Singular | I | me | my | mine | myself |
| 1st | Plural | we | us | our | ours | ourselves |
| 2nd | Sing./Plural | you | you | your | yours | yourself / yourselves |
| 3rd | Sing. masc. | he | him | his | his | himself |
| 3rd | Sing. fem. | she | her | her | hers | herself |
| 3rd | Sing. neuter | it | it | its | its | itself |
| 3rd | Plural | they | them | their | theirs | themselves |

- **First person** = the speaker (*I, we*).
- **Second person** = the person spoken to (*you*).
- **Third person** = the person or thing spoken about (*he, she, it, they*, and all nouns).

### 8.3.2 The Case Rules — The Heart of Pronoun Errors

::: rule
**Rule 1 — Subjective case** for the **subject** of a finite verb and for the **complement** after *be*: **He** *and* **I** *went there. It is* **I**. (formal) / *It is* **he** *who did it.*

**Rule 2 — Objective case** for the **object of a verb**, **object of a preposition**, and the **subject of an infinitive**: *She called* **him**. *Between you and* **me**. *I want* **him** *to go.*

**Rule 3 — Possessive case** before a **gerund** (formal): *I dislike* **his** *coming late.* (✘ him coming late)
:::

::: ex
- ✘ **Me and Rahim** went to the market. ✔ **Rahim and I** went to the market.
- ✘ Let **we** go. ✔ Let **us** go.
- ✘ This is between **you and I**. ✔ This is between **you and me**.
- ✘ He is taller than **me**. ✔ He is taller than **I** (am). (formal; *than me* is common informally)
- ✘ He likes you more than **I**. (meaning "more than he likes me") ✔ He likes you more than **me**. — the case shows the meaning!
- ✘ **Whom** do you think is the best? ✔ **Who** do you think is the best? (*who* is the subject of *is*)
- ✔ It was **she** who helped me. (formal) / It was **her** … (informal)
- ✔ Everyone except **him** came. (*except* is a preposition)
- ✔ The teacher wanted **us** students to stay. (✘ *we students*)
- ✔ **We** students demand justice. (✘ *us students* — here it is the subject)
:::

::: tip
**Exam Tip — the "cover-up" test.** In a compound subject or object, cover the other noun and test the pronoun alone. *"The prize was given to Karim and (I/me)."* → *given to me* ✔. *"(He/Him) and his brother came."* → *He came* ✔. *"Let you and (I/me) try."* → *Let me try* ✔.
:::

### 8.3.3 Order of Personal Pronouns — "231" and "Courtesy" Rules

When pronouns of different persons stand together, follow these rules:

::: rule
**For good or neutral statements: 2-3-1** — second person first, third person next, first person last (out of courtesy).

*You, he and I* are good friends. / *You and I* must go. / *He and I* are classmates.

**When admitting a fault or guilt: 1-2-3** — first person first (we accept the blame ourselves).

*I, you and he* are to blame. / *I and he* were guilty of the crime.

**In the plural, 1-2-3 is common:** *We, you and they* must work together.
:::

::: ex
- ✘ **I and you** will go. ✔ **You and I** will go.
- ✘ **I and Rahim** are friends. ✔ **Rahim and I** are friends.
- ✔ **I and my brother** are responsible for the loss. (confession — 1st person first is acceptable)
:::

### 8.3.4 Agreement of Pronoun with Antecedent

A pronoun must agree with its antecedent in **number, gender and person**.

::: ex
- **Every** boy must bring **his** book. (singular)
- **The girls** did **their** homework. (plural)
- **One** must do **one's** duty. (✘ *his* in strict BrE; ✔ *his* in AmE)
- **Each** of the members cast **his/her** vote.
- **Neither** of the girls lost **her** bag.
- **Rahim and Karim** have lost **their** books. (compound with *and* → plural)
- **Either Rahim or Karim** has lost **his** book. (with *or/nor* → agree with the nearer noun)
- **The committee** has submitted **its** report. / **The committee** are divided in **their** opinions.
- **Everybody** should love **his** country. (traditional exam answer)
:::

::: note
**Singular *they*.** In modern English, *they/their* is widely used for a singular person whose sex is unknown (*Someone left* **their** *umbrella*). Bangladeshi exam keys, however, almost always expect **his** (or *his or her*) after *everyone, everybody, each, every, anyone, nobody, no one, someone, a person*. Use *his* (or *his or her*) in exams.
:::

### 8.3.5 Special Uses of *It*

1. **For lifeless things, animals and babies (sex unknown):** *The baby stretched* **its** *arms.*
2. **As an empty (dummy) subject for time, weather, distance:** **It** *is ten o'clock.* **It** *is raining.* **It** *is five kilometres from here.*
3. **As an introductory (anticipatory) subject** for an infinitive, gerund or clause placed later: **It** *is difficult* **to learn Chinese**. **It** *is no use* **crying over spilt milk**. **It** *is true* **that he is honest**.
4. **For emphasis (cleft sentence):** **It was Rahim who** *broke the glass.* **It is** *in Dhaka* **that** *he lives.*
5. **To identify a person:** *Who is at the door? —* **It** *is the postman.*

### 8.3.6 *One* as a Pronoun

- **General person:** **One** *should keep* **one's** *promise.* (Keep *one/one's/oneself* consistent — ✘ *One should keep his promise* in strict BrE.)
- **Substitute for a countable noun:** *I need a pen. Can you lend me* **one**? *This shirt is old; I want a new* **one**. *These shoes are small; show me bigger* **ones**.
- **Not with uncountables:** ✘ *I like red wine more than white one.* ✔ … *than white.*

## 8.4 Possessive Pronouns

*Mine, ours, yours, his, hers, its, theirs* stand alone (no noun after them). *My, our, your, his, her, its, their* are possessive determiners (followed by a noun).

::: ex
- This is **my** book. → This book is **mine**.
- That is **your** pen. → That pen is **yours**.
- ✘ This pen is **your's** / **yours'**. ✔ This pen is **yours**.
- ✘ **Its** a cat. **It's** tail is long. ✔ **It's** a cat. **Its** tail is long.
- ✔ A friend **of mine** (double possessive — ✘ *a friend of me*).
- ✔ **Yours** sincerely / **Yours** faithfully (letter closings — no apostrophe).
:::

::: tip
**Exam Tip — no apostrophe in possessive pronouns.** *hers, its, ours, yours, theirs, whose* never take an apostrophe. *It's* = it is / it has. *Who's* = who is / who has. *They're* = they are; *there* = place; *their* = possessive.
:::

## 8.5 Reflexive and Emphatic Pronouns

### 8.5.1 Reflexive Pronouns

A **reflexive pronoun** is used as the **object** when the subject and the object are the **same** person/thing. The action "reflects" back on the doer.

::: ex
- He hurt **himself**. / She looked at **herself** in the mirror.
- We enjoyed **ourselves** at the party.
- The cat licked **itself**.
- They blamed **themselves** for the failure.
:::

**Verbs often used reflexively:** *absent oneself, avail oneself of, pride oneself on, revenge oneself on, acquit oneself, apply oneself to, enjoy oneself, help oneself, introduce oneself, behave oneself, amuse oneself, devote oneself to, exert oneself, resign oneself to.*

::: ex
- ✘ He **absented** from the class. ✔ He **absented himself** from the class.
- ✘ I **availed** the opportunity. ✔ I **availed myself of** the opportunity.
- ✘ He **prides** on his wealth. ✔ He **prides himself on** his wealth.
- ✔ **Help yourself** to the sweets.
:::

**Verbs not normally reflexive in English** (though they are in Bangla or other languages): *keep, rest, hide, stop, bathe, qualify, spread, open, turn, move, feel, wash, shave, dress, relax, concentrate, meet.*

::: ex
- ✘ **Stop yourself!** ✔ **Stop!**
- ✘ He **bathed himself** in the river. ✔ He **bathed** in the river.
- ✘ We should **qualify ourselves** for the job. ✔ We should **qualify** for the job.
- ✘ I **feel myself** happy. ✔ I **feel** happy.
- ✘ He **rested himself** for a while. ✔ He **rested** for a while.
:::

### 8.5.2 Emphatic (Intensive) Pronouns

The same *-self* forms used **for emphasis**, placed after the noun/pronoun or at the end. They can be removed without making the sentence wrong.

::: ex
- I **myself** saw the accident. / I saw the accident **myself**.
- The Principal **himself** came to the classroom.
- You **yourselves** are to blame.
:::

### 8.5.3 Errors with *-self* Pronouns

::: errs
1. ✘ Rahim and **myself** went there. ✔ Rahim and **I** went there. (A reflexive cannot be a subject on its own.)
2. ✘ He gave it to **myself**. ✔ He gave it to **me**.
3. ✘ **Hisself** / **theirselves** ✔ **himself** / **themselves**.
4. ✘ They love **themselves** (meaning each loves the other). ✔ They love **each other**.
:::

## 8.6 Demonstrative Pronouns

*This, that, these, those, such, the same* used **alone**:

::: ex
- **This** is my book; **that** is yours.
- **These** are mangoes; **those** are lichis.
- The climate of Dhaka is hotter than **that** of Sylhet. (*that* avoids repeating *climate*)
- The students of this school are better than **those** of that school.
- **Such** was his fate.
- He is a rogue, and I treat him as **such**.
:::

## 8.7 Indefinite Pronouns

Indefinite pronouns refer to people or things **in general**, not specific ones.

### 8.7.1 Singular Indefinite Pronouns (singular verb)

*anybody, anyone, anything, everybody, everyone, everything, nobody, no one, nothing, somebody, someone, something, each, either, neither, one, another, much, little.*

::: ex
- **Everyone is** happy. (✘ are)
- **Nobody has** come yet.
- **Something is** wrong.
- **Each** of the students **has** a book.
- **Neither** of the answers **is** correct.
:::

### 8.7.2 Plural Indefinite Pronouns (plural verb)

*both, few, many, several, others.*

::: ex
- **Both are** good. / **Many were** absent. / **Few have** passed. / **Several** of them **are** here.
:::

### 8.7.3 Singular or Plural (depends on the following noun) — *SANAM*

*Some, Any, None, All, Most* (also *more, half, part, a lot, plenty, the rest*):

::: ex
- **Some** of the **milk is** sour. / **Some** of the **mangoes are** sour.
- **All** of the **money was** stolen. / **All** of the **students were** present.
- **Most** of the **work is** done. / **Most** of the **books are** new.
- **None** of the **food was** eaten. / **None** of the **students was/were** absent.
:::

### 8.7.4 *None, No one, Nobody*

- *None* refers to persons or things; often followed by *of*: *None of them came.*
- *No one / nobody* refer to persons only and are never followed by *of*: ✘ *No one of them* → ✔ *None of them* / *No one*.

## 8.8 Distributive and Reciprocal Pronouns

**Distributive pronouns** — *each, either, neither* — refer to persons/things one at a time and take a **singular verb** (see Chapter 04).

::: ex
- **Each** of the boys **was** given a prize.
- **Either** of you **is** fit for the post. (one of two)
- **Neither** of the two brothers **is** married.
:::

**Reciprocal pronouns** — *each other* (traditionally for two) and *one another* (for more than two):

::: ex
- The two sisters love **each other**.
- The members of the team helped **one another**.
- ✔ They looked at **each other's** faces.
:::

(Modern usage often uses the two interchangeably, but exam keys prefer the traditional distinction.)

## 8.9 Relative Pronouns

A **relative pronoun** does two jobs: it **stands for a noun** (its antecedent) and it **joins** two clauses (so it also acts like a conjunction).

::: ex
- I know the man. He came here yesterday. → I know the man **who** came here yesterday.
:::

### 8.9.1 Forms and Uses

| Relative pronoun | Used for | Case | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **who** | persons | subjective | The boy **who** won the prize is my brother. |
| **whom** | persons | objective | The man **whom** you met is my uncle. |
| **whose** | persons (and things) | possessive | The girl **whose** bag was stolen is crying. / A house **whose** roof is red. |
| **which** | things, animals | subj./obj. | The book **which** I bought is interesting. |
| **that** | persons, animals, things | subj./obj. | The man **that** came here is a doctor. / This is the pen **that** I lost. |
| **what** | things (= *that which*; no antecedent) | subj./obj. | I know **what** you want. / **What** he said is true. |
| **as** | after *such, the same, as* | — | Such books **as** you need are here. |
| **but** | after a negative (= *who not / which not*) | — | There is no one **but** loves his country. (= who does not love) |

### 8.9.2 Special Uses of *That* (where *that* is preferred over *who/which*)

1. After **superlatives**: *He is the best student* **that** *I have ever taught.*
2. After **all, any, none, nothing, everything, something, anything, much, little, only, same, very**: *All* **that** *glitters is not gold. This is the same pen* **that** *I lost. He is the only man* **that** *can help us.*
3. After **two antecedents — a person and an animal/thing**: *The man and his dog* **that** *came here were hungry.*
4. After **interrogative who/what**: *Who is it* **that** *knocks? What is it* **that** *troubles you?*
5. After **It is / It was** (cleft): *It is the teacher* **that** *(who) deserves praise.*

::: ex
- ✘ He is the best player **who** I have seen. ✔ He is the best player **that** I have seen.
- ✘ All **which** glitters is not gold. ✔ All **that** glitters is not gold.
:::

### 8.9.3 *That* Cannot Be Used

- **After a preposition**: ✘ *the house in that he lives* ✔ *the house in* **which** *he lives* / *the house* **that** *he lives in*.
- **In a non-defining clause (after a comma)**: ✘ *My father,* **that** *is a doctor, …* ✔ *My father,* **who** *is a doctor, …*

### 8.9.4 Defining and Non-defining Relative Clauses

- **Defining (restrictive):** identifies *which* person/thing — **no commas**; *that* may be used. *The students* **who** *worked hard passed.* (only those students)
- **Non-defining (non-restrictive):** adds extra information — **commas**; *that* not allowed. *The students,* **who** *worked hard, passed.* (all the students worked hard and passed)

### 8.9.5 *Who* or *Whom*? — The Substitution Test

Replace the relative pronoun with *he/him* inside its own clause. If *he* fits → **who**; if *him* fits → **whom**.

::: ex
- The man **(who/whom)** I thought was honest cheated me. → *I thought* **he** *was honest* → **who** ✔
- The man **(who/whom)** I trusted cheated me. → *I trusted* **him** → **whom** ✔
- Give it to **whoever** needs it. (*whoever* is the subject of *needs*; the whole clause is the object of *to*)
- Give it to **whomever** you like. (*you like* **him**)
:::

### 8.9.6 Agreement of the Relative Pronoun

The verb in the relative clause agrees with the **antecedent** of the relative pronoun.

::: ex
- I, **who am** your friend, will help you. (✘ who is)
- You, **who are** my friend, should help me.
- He is one of the best players **who have** ever played for Bangladesh. (antecedent = *players*, plural)
- He is **the only one** of the players **who has** scored a century. (antecedent = *the only one*, singular)
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — "One of those who":** This is a classic GMAT/SAT trap. In *"She is one of those students who* **___** *always on time,"* the antecedent of *who* is *students* (plural), so the verb is **are**. But in *"She is the only one of the students who* **___** *always on time,"* the antecedent is *the only one*, so the verb is **is**.
:::

### 8.9.7 Position of the Relative Pronoun

Place the relative pronoun **as near as possible** to its antecedent to avoid absurdity:

::: ex
- ✘ I have a dog for my brother **that** barks loudly. ✔ For my brother I have a dog **that** barks loudly.
- ✘ He bought a horse from a farmer **which** was lame. ✔ He bought from a farmer a horse **which** was lame.
:::

### 8.9.8 Omission of the Relative Pronoun

The relative pronoun can be left out when it is the **object** in a defining clause: *The book (that) I bought is good. The man (whom) you met is my uncle.* It cannot be omitted when it is the **subject**: ✘ *The man came here is my uncle.*

## 8.10 Interrogative Pronouns

*Who, whom, whose, which, what* used to ask questions:

::: ex
- **Who** is knocking? (subject — person)
- **Whom** did you see? / **Who** did you see? (informal)
- **Whose** is this bag?
- **Which** of these do you want? (choice from a definite set)
- **What** is your name? (open choice)
- To **whom** did you give the money? (✘ To who)
:::

## 8.11 Pronoun Reference — Clarity Problems

A pronoun must clearly point to one antecedent. Vague or ambiguous pronouns are always wrong in SAT/GMAT and are tested in Bangladeshi correction questions too.

::: errs
1. ✘ Rahim told Karim that **he** had passed. (Who passed?) ✔ Rahim told Karim, "I have passed." / Rahim told Karim that Karim had passed.
2. ✘ In the newspaper **it** says that prices will rise. ✔ The newspaper says that prices will rise.
3. ✘ **They** say that the government is planning a new tax. (vague *they*) ✔ **Reports** say …
4. ✘ The teacher spoke to the students about **his** behaviour. (ambiguous) ✔ … about **their** behaviour / about **the students'** behaviour.
5. ✘ He failed in the exam, **which** upset his father. (*which* refers vaguely to the whole clause — acceptable in speech) ✔ His **failure** in the exam upset his father.
:::

## 8.12 Common Pronoun Errors — Consolidated

::: errs
1. ✘ **Who** are you looking for? (formal) ✔ **Whom** are you looking for? / For **whom** are you looking?
2. ✘ He and **me** are friends. ✔ He and **I** are friends.
3. ✘ It is **me**. (formal exams) ✔ It is **I**.
4. ✘ Let **he** go. ✔ Let **him** go.
5. ✘ Every student must do **their** homework. ✔ Every student must do **his** homework.
6. ✘ **One** should do **his** duty. ✔ **One** should do **one's** duty.
7. ✘ **Each** of the girls **have** done **their** work. ✔ **Each** of the girls **has** done **her** work.
8. ✘ The book is **her's**. ✔ The book is **hers**.
9. ✘ I **myself and Rahim** went. ✔ Rahim and **I** went.
10. ✘ He **enjoyed** at the party. ✔ He **enjoyed himself** at the party.
11. ✘ **I and you** will go. ✔ **You and I** will go.
12. ✘ The man **which** came here is my uncle. ✔ The man **who** came here is my uncle.
13. ✘ This is the boy **whom** I think deserves the prize. ✔ … **who** I think deserves the prize.
14. ✘ He is one of the best boys who **has** passed. ✔ … who **have** passed.
15. ✘ **None of them** are present (exam key). ✔ **None of them is** present.
16. ✘ The two brothers quarrelled with **one another**. ✔ … with **each other**.
17. ✘ **Whom** do you think will win? ✔ **Who** do you think will win?
18. ✘ I don't like **him** coming late. ✔ I don't like **his** coming late.
19. ✘ He is taller than **her**. ✔ He is taller than **she** (is).
20. ✘ My friend, **that** lives in Khulna, is a doctor. ✔ My friend, **who** lives in Khulna, is a doctor.
21. ✘ All **what** he said is false. ✔ All **that** he said is false. / **What** he said is false.
22. ✘ The boy **whom** came first got a prize. ✔ The boy **who** came first got a prize.
23. ✘ Neither of the **three** boys is guilty. ✔ **None** of the three boys is guilty.
24. ✘ **Everybody** have done **their** duty. ✔ **Everybody** has done **his** duty.
25. ✘ He **availed** of the chance. ✔ He **availed himself** of the chance.
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Pronoun Shift and Antecedent:** The SAT punishes **pronoun shifts** in person or number within a passage: ✘ *"If* **one** *wants to succeed,* **you** *must work hard."* ✔ *"If* **one** *wants to succeed,* **one** *must work hard."* The GMAT requires every pronoun to have a clear **noun** antecedent: ✘ *"Although Professor Rahman's research is well known,* **he** *rarely gives interviews"* — *he* refers to *Professor Rahman's*, a possessive, which many GMAT editors reject. ✔ *"Although Professor Rahman is well known for his research,* **he** *rarely gives interviews."*
:::
