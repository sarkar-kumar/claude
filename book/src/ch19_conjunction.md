# Conjunction {#ch19}

[Part A · Grammar]{.chpart}

## 19.1 Definition

A **conjunction** is a word that **joins** words, phrases, clauses or sentences. The word comes from Latin *con* (together) + *jungere* (to join).

::: ex
- Rahim **and** Karim are brothers. (joins two words)
- He came by bus **or** by train. (joins two phrases)
- He is poor **but** he is honest. (joins two clauses)
- I stayed at home **because** it was raining. (joins a main and a subordinate clause)
:::

::: note
**Conjunctions vs prepositions vs relative pronouns.** A conjunction only *joins*; it is not part of either clause it joins. A preposition governs a noun/pronoun object. A relative pronoun both joins and stands for a noun inside its own clause.

- He came **after** dinner. (preposition)
- He came **after** we had eaten. (conjunction)
- This is the man **who** helped me. (relative pronoun — *who* is the subject of *helped*)
:::

## 19.2 Kinds of Conjunctions

There are three main kinds:

1. **Coordinating conjunctions** — join words or clauses of **equal rank**.
2. **Subordinating conjunctions** — join a **dependent (subordinate) clause** to a main clause.
3. **Correlative conjunctions** — used **in pairs**.

(Some grammars add **compound conjunctions** — phrases acting as conjunctions: *as soon as, as long as, in order that, so that, provided that, even if, as if, as though, in case.*)

## 19.3 Coordinating Conjunctions

### 19.3.1 The FANBOYS

The seven main coordinating conjunctions can be remembered as **FANBOYS**: **F**or, **A**nd, **N**or, **B**ut, **O**r, **Y**et, **S**o.

| Conjunction | Relation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **for** | reason (formal; always after a comma, joins clauses only) | He felt sad, **for** he had failed. |
| **and** | addition | He sang **and** she danced. |
| **nor** | negative addition | He does not smoke, **nor** does he drink. (inversion after *nor*) |
| **but** | contrast | He is rich **but** unhappy. |
| **or** | alternative / choice | Will you have tea **or** coffee? |
| **yet** | contrast (= but still) | He is poor, **yet** he is honest. |
| **so** | result | It was raining, **so** we stayed in. |

### 19.3.2 Four Kinds of Coordination (by Meaning)

| Kind | Meaning | Conjunctions |
|---|---|---|
| **Cumulative / Copulative** | adds one statement to another | and, both … and, as well as, not only … but also, also, too, moreover, besides, furthermore |
| **Adversative** | contrasts two statements | but, yet, still, however, nevertheless, whereas, while, only |
| **Alternative / Disjunctive** | offers a choice between two | or, either … or, neither … nor, otherwise, else, or else |
| **Illative** | expresses an inference or result | so, therefore, hence, thus, consequently, accordingly, for |

::: ex
- He is clever **as well as** hard-working. (cumulative)
- He worked hard; **still** he failed. (adversative)
- Hurry up, **or else** you will miss the bus. (alternative)
- He was ill; **therefore** he could not come. (illative)
:::

::: note
*However, therefore, moreover, nevertheless, hence, consequently, otherwise* are strictly **conjunctive adverbs**, not true conjunctions. Between two independent clauses they need a **semicolon** (or full stop): *It rained;* **however***, the match continued.* ✘ *It rained, however the match continued* (comma splice). See Chapter 15 and Chapter 44.
:::

### 19.3.3 Special Notes on Coordinators

- **And** with *both*: *Both he* **and** *his wife came.* (✘ both … as well as)
- **And** joining two negatives: use *or / nor*: ✘ *He does not smoke and drink.* (ambiguous) ✔ *He does not smoke* **or** *drink.* / *He neither smokes* **nor** *drinks.*
- **But** after *not only* needs *also*: *Not only … but also*.
- **Or** in negatives: *I don't like tea* **or** *coffee.*
- **So** vs **so that**: *so* = result (*It rained,* **so** *we stayed in*); *so that* = purpose (*He worked hard* **so that** *he might pass*).
- **For** cannot start a sentence as a conjunction and cannot be preceded by another conjunction. It gives a reason **after** the main statement, which is already known.

## 19.4 Subordinating Conjunctions

A **subordinating conjunction** introduces a **subordinate (dependent) clause** and connects it to a **main clause**. The subordinate clause cannot stand alone.

### 19.4.1 Classification by Meaning

| Meaning | Conjunctions | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | when, whenever, while, as, before, after, since, till, until, as soon as, as long as, once, by the time, the moment, no sooner … than, hardly … when | Wait **until** I return. / **As soon as** he saw me, he ran away. |
| **Place** | where, wherever | Sit **where** you like. / **Wherever** he goes, he makes friends. |
| **Cause / Reason** | because, since, as, now that, seeing that, inasmuch as, for | I can't go **because** I am ill. / **Since** you insist, I will go. |
| **Purpose** | that, so that, in order that, lest | We eat **so that** we may live. / Walk carefully **lest** you should fall. |
| **Result / Consequence** | that, so … that, such … that | He was **so** tired **that** he fell asleep. |
| **Condition** | if, unless, provided (that), providing, on condition that, in case, suppose, supposing, as long as, only if, whether … or | **If** it rains, we'll stay in. / You will fail **unless** you work hard. |
| **Concession / Contrast** | though, although, even though, even if, while, whereas, however much, no matter how, however | **Although** he is rich, he is not happy. |
| **Comparison** | than, as … as, so … as, the … the | He is taller **than** I am. / He is **as** tall **as** his father. |
| **Manner** | as, as if, as though, like (informal) | Do **as** I say. / He talks **as if** he knew everything. |
| **Noun clause introducers** | that, whether, if | I know **that** he is honest. / I wonder **whether** he will come. |

### 19.4.2 Important Subordinating Conjunctions in Detail

**(a) *Because, since, as, for***

- **Because** — strongest; gives a reason that is new and important: *He failed* **because** *he was lazy.* Answers *Why?*
- **Since / As** — reason already known to the listener; usually at the start: **Since** *it is late, let's go home.* **As** *he was not at home, I left a message.*
- **For** — coordinating; adds a reason as an afterthought: *He must be ill,* **for** *he is absent today.*

::: ex
- ✘ The reason why he failed is **because** he was lazy. ✔ The reason why he failed is **that** he was lazy. (avoid *reason … because* — redundancy, Chapter 38)
- ✘ **Because** he was ill, **so** he did not come. ✔ **Because** he was ill, he did not come. / He was ill, **so** he did not come. (never use *because … so* or *although … but* together)
:::

**(b) *Though / Although* — never with *but***

::: ex
- ✘ **Although** he is poor, **but** he is honest. ✔ **Although** he is poor, he is honest. / He is poor **but** honest.
- ✔ **Though** he is poor, **yet** he is honest. (*though … yet* is accepted in traditional grammar for emphasis)
- **Even though / even if**: **Even if** *it rains, I will go.* (hypothetical) / **Even though** *it was raining, I went.* (fact)
- *Though* can come at the end (adverb): *He is poor; he is honest,* **though**.
:::

**(c) *Unless* (= if … not) — never with *not***

::: ex
- ✘ **Unless** you do **not** work hard, you will fail. ✔ **Unless** you work hard, you will fail. (= If you do not work hard, you will fail.)
- ✘ Unless you **will** study, you will fail. ✔ Unless you **study**, you will fail. (no *will* in the conditional clause)
:::

**(d) *Lest* (= so that … not) — with *should*, never with *not***

::: ex
- ✘ Run fast **lest** you **should not** miss the train. ✔ Run fast **lest** you **should** miss the train.
- ✔ Work hard **lest** you **fail**. (subjunctive base form also accepted)
:::

**(e) *Till / Until* — no *not***

::: ex
- ✘ Wait here **until** I do **not** come. ✔ Wait here **until** I come.
- ✔ He did **not** go **until** the work was finished. (*not* in the main clause is correct)
:::

**(f) *Since* (time) — with perfect tense in main clause, past simple after *since***

::: ex
- It **has been** five years **since** he **left** the village. (✘ since he has left)
- I **have known** him **since** we **were** children.
:::

**(g) *As if / As though* — unreal comparison (subjunctive)**

::: ex
- He talks **as if** he **were** a millionaire. (✘ was — formal key)
- She looked **as though** she **had seen** a ghost.
:::

**(h) *Whether* vs *If***

- **Whether** is used (not *if*): after prepositions (*It depends* **on whether** *…*), before *to*-infinitive (*I can't decide* **whether to** *go*), at the start of a subject clause (**Whether** *he comes or not doesn't matter*), and with *or not* directly after it (*whether or not*).
- **If / whether** both fine after *ask, know, wonder, see*: *I wonder* **if / whether** *he will come.*

**(i) *That***

- Introduces noun clauses: *I know* **that** *he is honest.* / **That** *he is guilty is clear.*
- Purpose (with *so* or alone): *He works hard* **(so) that** *he may succeed.*
- Result: *He was so weak* **that** *he could not walk.*
- *That* is often omitted in informal speech: *I know (that) he is honest.*

**(j) *Than* — comparison; case after *than***

::: ex
- He is taller **than** I (am). / I like you more **than** (I like) him.
- ✘ He is senior **than** me. ✔ He is senior **to** me. (Latin adjectives — Chapter 10)
- ✘ He had **scarcely** arrived **than** … ✔ … **when** … (*than* only after comparatives and *no sooner, rather, other, else* forms)
:::

## 19.5 Correlative Conjunctions

**Correlatives** are pairs of conjunctions used together. The two elements they join must be **grammatically parallel** (same part of speech / same structure). This is one of the most tested points in BCS, Bank and SAT/GMAT exams.

| Pair | Example |
|---|---|
| **both … and** | **Both** Rahim **and** Karim passed. |
| **either … or** | **Either** you **or** he must go. |
| **neither … nor** | **Neither** he **nor** his brother came. |
| **not only … but also** | He is **not only** intelligent **but also** hard-working. |
| **whether … or** | I don't know **whether** he will come **or** not. |
| **though / although … yet** | **Though** he is poor, **yet** he is honest. |
| **so … that** | He is **so** weak **that** he cannot walk. |
| **such … that** | It was **such** a hot day **that** we stayed in. |
| **as … as** | He is **as** tall **as** his father. |
| **so … as** (negative) | He is **not so** tall **as** his father. |
| **no sooner … than** | **No sooner** had he come **than** it began to rain. |
| **hardly / scarcely … when / before** | **Hardly** had he arrived **when** the phone rang. |
| **the … the** | **The** more you read, **the** more you learn. |
| **rather … than** | I would **rather** die **than** beg. |
| **if … then** | **If** you agree, **then** we can start. |
| **what with … what with** (= partly … partly) | **What with** illness **and what with** poverty, he lost hope. |

### 19.5.1 Rules for Correlatives

::: rule
**Rule 1 — Use the correct partner.** *Neither* goes with **nor** (✘ neither … or); *either* with **or**; *both* with **and** (✘ both … as well as); *not only* with **but also**; *no sooner* with **than** (✘ no sooner … when); *hardly / scarcely* with **when / before** (✘ hardly … than); *such* with **that / as**; *so* with **that / as**.

**Rule 2 — Keep the two parts parallel (same grammatical form)** — place each correlative immediately before the elements it joins.

**Rule 3 — Verb agreement with *either … or / neither … nor / not only … but also*:** the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject (proximity rule). With **both … and** the verb is **plural**.
:::

::: ex
- ✘ He **neither** reads **or** writes. ✔ He **neither** reads **nor** writes.
- ✘ **Both** Rahim **as well as** Karim came. ✔ **Both** Rahim **and** Karim came.
- ✘ He **not only** speaks English **but also** French. ✔ He speaks **not only** English **but also** French. (parallel: English / French)
- ✘ **Either** you can come today **or** tomorrow. ✔ You can come **either** today **or** tomorrow.
- ✘ He **both** likes tea **and** coffee. ✔ He likes **both** tea **and** coffee.
- ✘ **No sooner** did he see me **when** he ran away. ✔ **No sooner** did he see me **than** he ran away.
- ✘ **Scarcely** had I reached **than** it rained. ✔ **Scarcely** had I reached **when** it rained.
- ✔ **Either** the teacher **or** the students **are** to blame. (verb agrees with *students*)
- ✔ **Neither** the students **nor** the teacher **is** to blame. (verb agrees with *teacher*)
- ✔ **Not only** the students **but also** the teacher **was** present.
- ✔ **Both** the teacher **and** the students **were** present.
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Parallelism with Correlatives:** This is a top-five GMAT Sentence Correction rule. The words after each half of the pair must match in form. ✘ "The proposal was criticised **not only by** the opposition **but also** the press." ✔ "The proposal was criticised **not only by** the opposition **but also by** the press." ✘ "She wanted **either to study** law **or** medicine." ✔ "She wanted to study **either law or medicine**." On the GMAT, "not only … but" without *also* is accepted only when *but* introduces a full clause; when in doubt, use *but also*.
:::

## 19.6 Compound Conjunctions (Conjunctional Phrases)

*as soon as, as long as, as well as, as if, as though, in order that, so that, provided that, on condition that, even if, even though, in case, now that, so long as, inasmuch as, seeing that, the moment, by the time, every time, in spite of the fact that, due to the fact that, considering that, given that, except that.*

::: ex
- **As soon as** the bell rang, the students left.
- You may go out **as long as** you come back by ten.
- Take an umbrella **in case** it rains. (precaution — not *if*)
- **Now that** you are here, let's begin.
- I will lend you the book **provided that** you return it soon.
- **The moment** he saw the police, he fled.
:::

::: note
**In case vs if.** *In case* = as a precaution against something that may happen: *Take a sweater* **in case** *it gets cold* (take it anyway). *If* = only on that condition: *Take a sweater* **if** *it gets cold* (take it only when it is cold).
:::

## 19.7 Some Confusing Conjunctions

| Pair | Difference | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **as / like** | *as* + clause (conjunction); *like* + noun (preposition) | Do **as** I do. / He runs **like** a deer. ✘ Do like I do (informal) |
| **while / whereas** | *while* = during / although; *whereas* = in contrast | **While** I was reading, he came. / Rahim is rich **whereas** Karim is poor. |
| **since / for** | *since* (conjunction: reason / time point); *for* (coordinating: reason as afterthought) | |
| **so / so that** | result / purpose | |
| **than / then** | comparison / time | He is older **than** me. / **Then** he left. |
| **if / whether** | condition / alternative (noun clause) | |
| **because / because of** | conjunction + clause / preposition + noun | **because** it rained / **because of** the rain |
| **although / despite** | conjunction + clause / preposition + noun | **Although** it rained… / **Despite** the rain… (✘ despite of; ✘ despite it rained) |
| **unless / until** | condition / time | |
| **as well as / and** | *as well as* adds emphasis to the first subject; the verb agrees with the first | **Rahim, as well as his friends, is** coming. |

## 19.8 Common Errors with Conjunctions

::: errs
1. ✘ **Although** he is rich, **but** he is unhappy. ✔ **Although** he is rich, he is unhappy.
2. ✘ **Because** he was ill, **so** he did not come. ✔ **Because** he was ill, he did not come.
3. ✘ **Unless** you do **not** work hard, you will fail. ✔ **Unless** you work hard, you will fail.
4. ✘ Wait here **till** I do **not** return. ✔ Wait here **till** I return.
5. ✘ Walk fast **lest** you **should not** be late. ✔ Walk fast **lest** you **should** be late.
6. ✘ **Neither** he **or** I am guilty. ✔ **Neither** he **nor** I am guilty.
7. ✘ **Either** he **nor** his brother has done it. ✔ **Either** he **or** his brother has done it.
8. ✘ **Both** Rahim **as well as** Karim are present. ✔ **Both** Rahim **and** Karim are present.
9. ✘ **No sooner** had he left **when** she came. ✔ **No sooner** had he left **than** she came.
10. ✘ **Hardly** had he left **than** she came. ✔ **Hardly** had he left **when** she came.
11. ✘ He is **not only** a poet **but** a novelist. ✔ He is **not only** a poet **but also** a novelist.
12. ✘ It is **such** a heavy box **so** I can't lift it. ✔ It is **such** a heavy box **that** I can't lift it.
13. ✘ He is **so** weak **as** he cannot walk. ✔ He is **so** weak **that** he cannot walk.
14. ✘ He talks **like** he knows everything. ✔ He talks **as if** he knew everything.
15. ✘ I don't know **if or not** he will come. ✔ I don't know **whether or not** he will come.
16. ✘ **Despite of** the rain, we went out. ✔ **Despite** the rain / **In spite of** the rain, we went out.
17. ✘ It is ten years **since** he **has died**. ✔ It is ten years **since** he **died**.
18. ✘ **Since** he is ill, **therefore** he is absent. ✔ **Since** he is ill, he is absent.
19. ✘ The reason of his failure is **because** he was lazy. ✔ The reason **for** his failure is **that** he was lazy.
20. ✘ **Scarcely** I had reached home **when** it rained. ✔ **Scarcely had I** reached home when it rained.
21. ✘ I doubt **that** he will come (strict BrE exam key) ✔ I doubt **whether** he will come. (*doubt that* is fine in negatives/questions: *I don't doubt that…*)
22. ✘ He as well as his friends **were** present. ✔ He as well as his friends **was** present.
23. ✘ **Not only** he came **but also** he helped me. ✔ **Not only did he** come **but** he **also** helped me.
24. ✘ Take an umbrella **if** it rains (as precaution). ✔ Take an umbrella **in case** it rains.
25. ✘ He works hard **so that** he **can** pass (past main verb: *worked*). ✔ He **worked** hard **so that** he **might** pass.
:::
