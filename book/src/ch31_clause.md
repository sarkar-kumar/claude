# Identification of Clause {#ch31}

[Part A · Grammar]{.chpart}

## 31.1 What Is a Clause?

A **clause** is a group of words that contains a **subject** and a **finite verb** and forms **part of a sentence**.

::: ex
- **I know** (clause) **that he is honest** (clause).
- **When he came**, **I was reading**.
- **The book which you gave me** is interesting. (*which you gave me* is a clause)
:::

## 31.2 Kinds of Clauses

| Kind | Also called | Can stand alone? | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Principal / Main / Independent clause** | — | Yes — it makes complete sense | **I know** that he is honest. |
| **Subordinate / Dependent clause** | — | No — it depends on the main clause | I know **that he is honest**. |
| **Coordinate clause** | — | Yes — of equal rank with another main clause | **He is poor**, but **he is honest**. |

Subordinate clauses are of three kinds, according to the work they do:

1. **Noun clause** — does the work of a **noun**.
2. **Adjective (relative) clause** — does the work of an **adjective**.
3. **Adverb (adverbial) clause** — does the work of an **adverb**.

## 31.3 The Noun Clause

A **noun clause** is a subordinate clause that does the work of a **noun**. It answers *what?* or *who?* and can usually be replaced by *it / this / something*.

### 31.3.1 Words That Introduce Noun Clauses

*that, whether, if, what, who, whom, whose, which, when, where, why, how, whatever, whoever, whichever, whenever (rare)*

### 31.3.2 Functions of the Noun Clause

| Function | Example |
|---|---|
| **Subject of a verb** | **What he said** is true. / **That he is honest** is known to all. / **Whether he will come** is uncertain. |
| **Object of a verb** | I know **that he is honest**. / Tell me **where you live**. / I wonder **if it will rain**. |
| **Object of a preposition** | Pay attention to **what the teacher says**. / It depends on **how hard you work**. |
| **Complement of a verb (subjective complement)** | This is **what I want**. / The problem is **that we have no money**. |
| **In apposition to a noun** | The news **that he had died** shocked us. / The fact **that she is honest** is known. |
| **Object of a participle / infinitive** | Hearing **that his son had passed**, he was happy. / I want to know **why you are late**. |
| **Complement of an adjective** | I am sure **that he will come**. / I am afraid **that he is ill**. |

::: tip
**Exam Tip — Noun clause in apposition vs adjective clause.** In *The news* **that he had died** *shocked us*, the clause **explains the content** of *news* (= he had died), so it is a **noun clause in apposition**. In *The news* **that he brought** *shocked us*, the clause **describes which news** (*that* = which, object of *brought*), so it is an **adjective clause**. Test: if you can put *which* instead of *that*, it is an adjective clause; if you can put *namely* / *:* before it, it is a noun clause.
:::

## 31.4 The Adjective Clause (Relative Clause)

An **adjective clause** is a subordinate clause that does the work of an **adjective**: it qualifies a **noun or pronoun** in the main clause (its **antecedent**). It usually comes **right after** the noun it qualifies.

### 31.4.1 Words That Introduce Adjective Clauses

- **Relative pronouns:** *who, whom, whose, which, that, as, but*
- **Relative adverbs:** *when* (time), *where* (place), *why* (reason), *how* (manner — usually *the way*)

::: ex
- The man **who came here** is my uncle. (qualifies *man*)
- This is the book **which I bought yesterday**. (qualifies *book*)
- I know the girl **whose father is a doctor**. (qualifies *girl*)
- He is the man **whom we elected**. (qualifies *man*)
- This is the house **where I was born**. (qualifies *house*)
- I remember the day **when we first met**. (qualifies *day*)
- Tell me the reason **why you are late**. (qualifies *reason*)
- Such men **as are honest** are respected. (qualifies *men*)
- There is no mother **but loves her child**. (= who does not love — qualifies *mother*)
- He is not the man **(that) I thought him to be**. (relative omitted)
:::

::: note
**Relative adverb clauses vs noun clauses.** *This is the house* **where I was born** → adjective clause (qualifies *house*). *I know* **where he was born** → noun clause (object of *know*; no noun before it to qualify). *Tell me the time* **when he will come** → adjective clause; *Tell me* **when he will come** → noun clause.
:::

### 31.4.2 Defining and Non-defining Adjective Clauses

- **Defining (restrictive):** necessary to identify the noun; **no commas**: *The students* **who passed** *were rewarded.* (only those who passed)
- **Non-defining (non-restrictive):** extra information; **commas**; *that* not used: *My father,* **who is a doctor**, *lives in Sylhet.*

## 31.5 The Adverb Clause

An **adverb clause** is a subordinate clause that does the work of an **adverb**: it modifies a **verb, an adjective or an adverb** in the main clause, and tells **when, where, why, how, with what result, on what condition, in spite of what, to what extent**.

### 31.5.1 Kinds of Adverb Clauses

| Kind | Conjunctions | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | when, whenever, while, as, before, after, since, till, until, as soon as, as long as, once, by the time, no sooner … than | I will wait **till you come**. / **As soon as he saw me**, he ran away. |
| **Place** | where, wherever, whence, whither | Sit **where you like**. / **Wherever you go**, I shall follow you. |
| **Reason / Cause** | because, since, as, that, now that, inasmuch as | He was absent **because he was ill**. / **Since you insist**, I'll go. |
| **Purpose** | that, so that, in order that, lest | We eat **that we may live**. / Walk carefully **lest you should fall**. |
| **Result / Effect** | that (after *so / such*), so that | He was **so** weak **that he could not walk**. |
| **Condition** | if, unless, provided (that), in case, on condition that, supposing, whether … or | **If you work hard**, you will succeed. / **Unless you hurry**, you'll miss the bus. |
| **Concession / Contrast** | though, although, even if, even though, however, whatever, no matter what, while, whereas | **Though he is poor**, he is honest. / **However hard he tries**, he fails. |
| **Comparison (degree)** | than, as … as, so … as, the … the | He is taller **than I am**. / She is **as** clever **as her sister is**. |
| **Manner** | as, as if, as though | Do **as I tell you**. / He behaves **as if he were a king**. |
| **Extent / Proportion** | as, the … the | **The more you learn**, the more you earn. / **As you sow**, so shall you reap. |

### 31.5.2 How to Identify an Adverb Clause

1. It begins with a **subordinating conjunction** of time, place, reason, etc.
2. It modifies the **verb of the main clause** (answers *when? where? why? how?*).
3. It can often be **moved** to the beginning or end of the sentence: *I'll call you* **when I reach home** / **When I reach home**, *I'll call you.*

## 31.6 The Same Word Introducing Different Clauses

| Sentence | Clause | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| I don't know **when he will come**. | when he will come | Noun (object of *know*) |
| Tell me the time **when he will come**. | when he will come | Adjective (qualifies *time*) |
| I'll leave **when he comes**. | when he comes | Adverb (time) |
| I know **where he lives**. | where he lives | Noun |
| This is the village **where he lives**. | where he lives | Adjective |
| Stay **where you are**. | where you are | Adverb (place) |
| I know **that he is honest**. | that he is honest | Noun |
| This is the pen **that I lost**. | that I lost | Adjective |
| He worked hard **so that he might pass**. | so that he might pass | Adverb (purpose) |
| He was so tired **that he fell asleep**. | that he fell asleep | Adverb (result) |
| **What he says** is true. | what he says | Noun |
| **If you come**, I'll go. | if you come | Adverb (condition) |
| I wonder **if he will come**. | if he will come | Noun (object) |
| **As he was ill**, he stayed home. | as he was ill | Adverb (reason) |
| Do **as I say**. | as I say | Adverb (manner) |
| Such boys **as are lazy** fail. | as are lazy | Adjective (qualifies *boys*) |

## 31.7 Clauses and Sentence Structure

- A sentence with **one main clause** only → **simple sentence**.
- A sentence with **one main clause + one or more subordinate clauses** → **complex sentence**.
- A sentence with **two or more main (coordinate) clauses** → **compound sentence**.
- A sentence with **two or more main clauses + at least one subordinate clause** → **compound-complex (mixed) sentence**.

(See Chapter 33.)

## 31.8 Reducing Clauses to Phrases (and Expanding Phrases to Clauses)

This is the basis of many transformation questions (Chapter 34).

| Clause | Phrase |
|---|---|
| **When he saw the police**, he ran away. | **Seeing the police**, he ran away. / **On seeing the police**, he ran away. |
| **As he was ill**, he could not come. | **Being ill**, he could not come. / **Because of illness**, … |
| **Though he is rich**, he is not happy. | **In spite of being rich** / **Despite his wealth**, he is not happy. |
| The man **who is standing there** is my uncle. | The man **standing there** is my uncle. |
| He worked hard **so that he might pass**. | He worked hard **to pass**. / **in order to pass**. |
| **If you work hard**, you will succeed. | **By working hard**, you will succeed. |
| I know **that he is honest**. | I know **him to be honest**. / I know **his honesty**. |
| He is so weak **that he cannot walk**. | He is **too weak to walk**. |
| **After he had finished the work**, he left. | **Having finished the work**, he left. / **After finishing** … |

## 31.9 Identification Practice (Worked Examples)

::: ex
- *Do you know* **who he is**? → **Noun clause** (object of *know*).
- *He died in the village* **where he was born**. → **Adjective clause** (qualifies *village*).
- **Whatever you do**, *do it well.* → **Adverb clause** (concession).
- *I shall go* **if it does not rain**. → **Adverb clause** (condition).
- *The fact* **that he lied** *is clear.* → **Noun clause** (apposition to *fact*).
- *He spoke so softly* **that nobody heard him**. → **Adverb clause** (result).
- *This is the reason* **why he left**. → **Adjective clause** (qualifies *reason*).
- *I asked him* **why he left**. → **Noun clause** (object of *asked*).
- **That he will succeed** *is certain.* → **Noun clause** (subject).
- *He talks* **as if he were mad**. → **Adverb clause** (manner).
- *Men* **who are honest** *are respected.* → **Adjective clause**.
- *My belief is* **that he is innocent**. → **Noun clause** (complement).
- *Strike the iron* **while it is hot**. → **Adverb clause** (time).
- *I am glad* **that you have come**. → **Noun clause** (complement of the adjective *glad*) — some grammars call it an adverb clause of cause; exam keys in Bangladesh usually accept **noun clause**.
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Clauses and Sentence Boundaries:** SAT "Boundaries" questions test whether two clauses are correctly joined. Two **independent** clauses need a full stop, a semicolon, or a comma + coordinating conjunction (FANBOYS); a comma alone creates a **comma splice** (✘ *"The rain stopped, we went out."*). A **dependent** clause alone is a **fragment** (✘ *"Because the rain stopped."*). The GMAT also tests **which vs that** (non-defining vs defining) and the "touch rule" — *which* must follow the noun it describes.
:::
