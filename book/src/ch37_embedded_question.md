# Embedded Question {#ch37}

[Part A · Grammar]{.chpart}
:::

## 37.1 What Is an Embedded Question?

An **embedded question** (also called an *indirect question* or *included question*) is a question that is placed **inside** another sentence or question. It is not asked directly; it becomes a **noun clause**, usually the object of a verb like *know, tell, ask, wonder, remember, understand, explain, find out, be sure, decide, doubt*.

::: ex
- Direct question: *Where does he live?*
- Embedded: *I don't know* **where he lives**.
- Embedded in a question: *Do you know* **where he lives**?
:::

::: rule
**The key rule:** In an embedded question, use **statement word order** (subject + verb), **not question order**. Remove the auxiliary *do / does / did*, and do not invert the subject and auxiliary.
:::

## 37.2 Two Types of Embedded Questions

### 37.2.1 Embedded Wh-Questions

Keep the **wh-word** as the linker; then use **subject + verb**.

| Direct question | Embedded question |
|---|---|
| Where **does he live**? | I don't know where **he lives**. |
| What **is her name**? | Can you tell me what **her name is**? |
| Why **are you** late? | I wonder why **you are** late. |
| When **will the train** leave? | Do you know when **the train will** leave? |
| How **did he solve** the problem? | Please explain how **he solved** the problem. |
| Who **is** that man? | I'd like to know who **that man is**. |
| How much **does it cost**? | Could you tell me how much **it costs**? |
| Which bus **goes** to Mirpur? | Do you know which bus **goes** to Mirpur? (wh-word is the subject — order unchanged) |
| Who **broke** the glass? | I don't know who **broke** the glass. |

::: note
When the wh-word is itself the **subject** (*who, what, which + noun*), the word order is already subject + verb and does not change: *Who called?* → *I wonder* **who called**.
:::

### 37.2.2 Embedded Yes/No Questions

Use **if** or **whether** as the linker; then subject + verb.

| Direct question | Embedded question |
|---|---|
| **Is he** at home? | I don't know **if / whether he is** at home. |
| **Does she** like tea? | Can you tell me **if she likes** tea? |
| **Did they** finish the work? | I wonder **whether they finished** the work. |
| **Will it** rain tomorrow? | Nobody knows **whether it will** rain tomorrow. |
| **Can you** swim? | He asked me **if I could** swim. |
| **Have you** seen this film? | I'd like to know **whether you have** seen this film. |

## 37.3 Punctuation of Embedded Questions

- If the **whole sentence is a statement**, end with a **full stop**: *I wonder where he lives***.**
- If the **whole sentence is a question**, end with a **question mark**: *Do you know where he lives***?** / *Could you tell me what time it is***?**

## 37.4 Polite Questions — Using Embedded Forms

In formal and polite English (offices, banks, interviews), we often turn direct questions into embedded ones with introductory phrases:

*Could you tell me …? / Do you know …? / Would you mind telling me …? / I'd like to know … / I was wondering … / May I ask …? / Can you explain …? / Have you any idea …? / I'm not sure …*

::: ex
- *Where is the bank?* → **Could you tell me where the bank is?**
- *What time does the office open?* → **Do you know what time the office opens?**
- *How much is this?* → **Would you mind telling me how much this is?**
- *Is the manager in?* → **May I ask whether the manager is in?**
:::

## 37.5 Embedded Questions in Reported Speech

When the reporting verb is past, backshift the tense as in Chapter 27:

::: ex
- He said, "Where do you live?" → He asked me **where I lived**.
- She said, "Have you eaten?" → She asked me **if I had eaten**.
:::

## 37.6 *Whether* or *If*?

- **Both** are possible after verbs like *ask, know, wonder, see, find out*.
- Use **whether** (not *if*):
  - after a **preposition**: *It depends* **on whether** *he agrees.*
  - before an **infinitive**: *I can't decide* **whether to** *go.*
  - at the **beginning** of a sentence (subject clause): **Whether** *he comes or not is unimportant.*
  - directly before **or not**: *I don't know* **whether or not** *he will come.* (✘ if or not)

## 37.7 Embedded Questions with Infinitives

A wh-word + to-infinitive can replace an embedded question when the subjects are the same or general:

::: ex
- I don't know **what I should do**. → I don't know **what to do**.
- Tell me **how I can reach** the station. → Tell me **how to reach** the station.
- She can't decide **whether she should accept** the offer. → She can't decide **whether to accept** the offer.
- (✘ *why to go* — *why* is not used with an infinitive in this way.)
:::

## 37.8 Common Errors

::: errs
1. ✘ I don't know where **does he live**. ✔ I don't know where **he lives**.
2. ✘ Can you tell me what **is the time**? ✔ Can you tell me what **the time is**?
3. ✘ I wonder why **is she** crying. ✔ I wonder why **she is** crying.
4. ✘ Do you know when **will he** come? ✔ Do you know when **he will** come?
5. ✘ He asked me **that** whether I was ready. ✔ He asked me **whether** I was ready.
6. ✘ I don't know **if or not** he will come. ✔ I don't know **whether or not** he will come.
7. ✘ Please tell me how much **does this book cost**. ✔ Please tell me how much **this book costs**.
8. ✘ I asked her **what was her name**. ✔ I asked her **what her name was**.
9. ✘ I'm not sure **did he** pass. ✔ I'm not sure **whether he passed**.
10. ✘ Could you tell me where is the post office**.** ✔ Could you tell me where **the post office is?**
11. ✘ It depends on **if** he agrees. ✔ It depends on **whether** he agrees.
12. ✘ Nobody knows what **did happen**. ✔ Nobody knows what **happened**.
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner:** The GMAT tests embedded questions as **"whether vs if"** (in formal writing, *whether* is preferred when there are two alternatives: ✔ *"The study examined whether the drug was effective"*) and as **word order inside noun clauses** (✘ *"Scientists are unsure how old is the fossil"* → ✔ *"…how old the fossil is"*). The SAT tests the end punctuation: a statement that contains an embedded question ends with a **period**, not a question mark.
:::
