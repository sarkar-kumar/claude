## 12.1 What Is Conjugation?

**Conjugation** is the systematic arrangement of all the forms of a verb — according to **tense, person, number, voice and mood**. When you conjugate a verb, you show how it changes with *I, we, you, he, she, it, they* in every tense, in the active and in the passive voice.

Learning conjugation is not only a classroom exercise: it trains you to produce the correct **auxiliary + verb form** automatically, which is exactly what *Right Form of Verbs*, *Tense*, *Voice* and *Correction* questions test.

## 12.2 The Building Blocks

Every English verb has five basic forms:

| Form | *write* | *go* | *play* |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base form (V1) | write | go | play |
| -s form (V1 + s/es) | writes | goes | plays |
| Past (V2) | wrote | went | played |
| Past participle (V3) | written | gone | played |
| Present participle / gerund (V + ing) | writing | going | playing |

The **auxiliaries** used to build tenses are: *am / is / are / was / were / be / being / been; have / has / had; do / does / did; shall / will / should / would.*

::: rule
**Formulae of the twelve active tenses (structure):**

- Present Indefinite: Subject + V1 (+ s/es with third-person singular)
- Present Continuous: Subject + am/is/are + V-ing
- Present Perfect: Subject + have/has + V3
- Present Perfect Continuous: Subject + have/has + been + V-ing
- Past Indefinite: Subject + V2
- Past Continuous: Subject + was/were + V-ing
- Past Perfect: Subject + had + V3
- Past Perfect Continuous: Subject + had + been + V-ing
- Future Indefinite: Subject + shall/will + V1
- Future Continuous: Subject + shall/will + be + V-ing
- Future Perfect: Subject + shall/will + have + V3
- Future Perfect Continuous: Subject + shall/will + have + been + V-ing
:::

## 12.6 *Shall* and *Will* — Traditional Rule

Traditional British grammar (still followed in many Bangladeshi exam keys) uses:

| Meaning | 1st person (I, we) | 2nd & 3rd person (you, he, she, it, they) |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple future** (plain prediction) | **shall** | **will** |
| **Determination, promise, threat, command** | **will** | **shall** |

::: ex
- I **shall** be twenty next year. (simple future)
- I **will** help you, I promise. (determination/promise)
- You **will** pass if you work hard. (simple future)
- You **shall** be punished if you are late again. (threat)
- He **shall** leave the room at once. (command)
- Thou **shalt** not kill. (command — Biblical)
:::

In modern English, **will** is used for all persons in simple future, and *shall* is mainly used in questions offering help or suggestions: **Shall** *I open the window?* **Shall** *we go?*

## 12.7 Full Conjugation of *Write* — Active Voice (Affirmative)

## 12.8 Full Conjugation of *Write* — Passive Voice

Passive = **be** (in the right tense) + **past participle (V3)**. Only eight tenses are commonly used in the passive; the perfect continuous tenses and the future continuous are avoided.

| Tense | I | You / We / They | He / She / It |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present Indefinite | am written | are written | is written |
| Present Continuous | am being written | are being written | is being written |
| Present Perfect | have been written | have been written | has been written |
| Past Indefinite | was written | were written | was written |
| Past Continuous | was being written | were being written | was being written |
| Past Perfect | had been written | had been written | had been written |
| Future Indefinite | shall be written | will be written | will be written |
| Future Perfect | shall have been written | will have been written | will have been written |

::: note
*Present perfect continuous, past perfect continuous, future continuous and future perfect continuous* have no normal passive forms. Sentences in these tenses are changed into the passive only with awkward forms (*has been being written*), which are avoided in exams. See Chapter 26.
:::

## 12.9 Conjugation in the Negative and Interrogative

### 12.9.1 Rules

1. **With an auxiliary** (*am, is, are, was, were, have, has, had, shall, will, can, must*…): put **not** after the first auxiliary for negatives; move the first auxiliary **before the subject** for questions.
2. **Without an auxiliary** (present and past indefinite of ordinary verbs): use **do / does / did** + base form.

| Tense | Affirmative | Negative | Interrogative | Negative-Interrogative |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Present Indefinite | He writes. | He **does not write**. | **Does** he **write**? | **Doesn't** he write? / **Does** he **not** write? |
| Present Continuous | He is writing. | He **is not** writing. | **Is** he writing? | **Isn't** he writing? |
| Present Perfect | He has written. | He **has not** written. | **Has** he written? | **Hasn't** he written? |
| Present Perfect Continuous | He has been writing. | He **has not** been writing. | **Has** he been writing? | **Hasn't** he been writing? |
| Past Indefinite | He wrote. | He **did not write**. | **Did** he **write**? | **Didn't** he write? |
| Past Continuous | He was writing. | He **was not** writing. | **Was** he writing? | **Wasn't** he writing? |
| Past Perfect | He had written. | He **had not** written. | **Had** he written? | **Hadn't** he written? |
| Past Perfect Continuous | He had been writing. | He **had not** been writing. | **Had** he been writing? | **Hadn't** he been writing? |
| Future Indefinite | He will write. | He **will not** write. | **Will** he write? | **Won't** he write? |
| Future Continuous | He will be writing. | He **will not** be writing. | **Will** he be writing? | **Won't** he be writing? |
| Future Perfect | He will have written. | He **will not** have written. | **Will** he have written? | **Won't** he have written? |
| Future Perfect Continuous | He will have been writing. | He **will not** have been writing. | **Will** he have been writing? | **Won't** he have been writing? |

::: rule
**After *do / does / did*, always use the base form (V1):** ✘ *Did he went?* ✔ *Did he go?* ✘ *He doesn't likes it.* ✔ *He doesn't like it.* ✘ *Does she sings?* ✔ *Does she sing?*
:::

### 12.9.2 Contractions

| Full | Contracted | Full | Contracted |
|---|---|---|---|
| I am | I'm | is not | isn't |
| you are | you're | are not | aren't |
| he is / he has | he's | was not | wasn't |
| it is / it has | it's | were not | weren't |
| we have | we've | have not | haven't |
| they had / they would | they'd | has not | hasn't |
| I will / I shall | I'll | had not | hadn't |
| let us | let's | do not | don't |
| — | — | does not | doesn't |
| — | — | did not | didn't |
| — | — | will not | **won't** |
| — | — | shall not | **shan't** |
| — | — | cannot | **can't** |
| — | — | must not | mustn't |
| — | — | ought not | oughtn't |
| — | — | am not (question) | **aren't I?** (✘ amn't I) |

::: tip
**Exam Tip:** *Won't* (will not) and *shan't* (shall not) are irregular contractions. In tag questions, *I am* takes **aren't I?** (*I am right, aren't I?*). Contractions are informal; in formal essays and applications write the full forms.
:::

## 12.10 Conjugation of Imperative, Infinitive and Participle Forms

| Form | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Imperative | Write. / Don't write. / Let him write. | Let it be written. / Let it not be written. |
| Present infinitive | to write | to be written |
| Continuous infinitive | to be writing | — |
| Perfect infinitive | to have written | to have been written |
| Perfect continuous infinitive | to have been writing | — |
| Present participle | writing | being written |
| Past participle | — | written |
| Perfect participle | having written | having been written |
| Gerund | writing | being written |
| Perfect gerund | having written | having been written |

::: ex
- He seems **to be writing** a novel. (continuous infinitive)
- He is said **to have written** fifty books. (perfect infinitive)
- **Having written** the letter, he posted it. (perfect participle)
- The letter **being written**, he posted it. (absolute construction — passive participle)
- I hate **being disturbed**. (passive gerund)
:::

## 12.11 Conjugation of Modal Verbs (Pattern)

Modals have no *-s* form, no infinitive, no participles. They are followed by the **bare infinitive** (or *have + V3* for past reference):

| Modal + | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| present/future reference | He **can write**. / He **may write**. / He **must write**. | It **can be written**. / It **must be written**. |
| continuous | He **may be writing**. | — |
| past reference (perfect) | He **should have written**. / He **might have written**. / He **must have written**. | It **should have been written**. |

## 12.12 A Second Model: Conjugation of *Go* (Intransitive — No Passive)

| Tense | I | He / She / It | We / You / They |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present Indefinite | go | goes | go |
| Present Continuous | am going | is going | are going |
| Present Perfect | have gone | has gone | have gone |
| Present Perfect Continuous | have been going | has been going | have been going |
| Past Indefinite | went | went | went |
| Past Continuous | was going | was going | were going |
| Past Perfect | had gone | had gone | had gone |
| Past Perfect Continuous | had been going | had been going | had been going |
| Future Indefinite | shall go | will go | shall go (we) / will go (you, they) |
| Future Continuous | shall be going | will be going | shall/will be going |
| Future Perfect | shall have gone | will have gone | shall/will have gone |
| Future Perfect Continuous | shall have been going | will have been going | shall/will have been going |

::: note
**Has gone / has been.** *He* **has gone** *to Dhaka* = he went and is still there (not here now). *He* **has been** *to Dhaka* = he visited Dhaka and has come back. *Have you ever* **been** *to Cox's Bazar?* (✘ gone to)
:::

## 12.13 Conjugation Errors — Consolidated

::: errs
1. ✘ He **do not** know me. ✔ He **does not** know me.
2. ✘ **Do** she like mangoes? ✔ **Does** she like mangoes?
3. ✘ I **didn't saw** him. ✔ I **didn't see** him.
4. ✘ They **was** playing. ✔ They **were** playing.
5. ✘ He **have** finished. ✔ He **has** finished.
6. ✘ She **is go** to school. ✔ She **goes** / **is going** to school.
7. ✘ We **has been** waiting for an hour. ✔ We **have been** waiting for an hour.
8. ✘ He **will has** finished by then. ✔ He **will have** finished by then.
9. ✘ The letter **was wrote** by him. ✔ The letter **was written** by him.
10. ✘ The house **is building** now (meaning passive). ✔ The house **is being built** now.
11. ✘ I **amn't** late, **am I**? ✔ I'm not late, **am I**? / I'm late, **aren't I**?
12. ✘ **Didn't** you **went** there? ✔ **Didn't** you **go** there?
13. ✘ He **can to** swim. ✔ He **can** swim.
14. ✘ She **musts** go. ✔ She **must** go.
15. ✘ He **has went** home. ✔ He **has gone** home.
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Verb Phrase Integrity:** Many SAT/GMAT errors come from broken verb phrases: a modal followed by the wrong form (✘ *could of gone* ✔ *could have gone*; ✘ *should had known* ✔ *should have known*), a perfect tense with a simple past form (✘ *had ran* ✔ *had run*; ✘ *has began* ✔ *has begun*), or a continuous form missing *be* (✘ *The prices rising rapidly* ✔ *The prices are rising rapidly*). Conjugation tables are the cure: read them aloud until the correct pairs feel natural.
:::
