## 48.1 What Is an Idiom?

An **idiom** is a fixed group of words whose meaning **cannot be worked out from the meanings of the individual words**. *To kick the bucket* has nothing to do with kicking or buckets; it means *to die*. A **phrase** in the exam sense is a fixed expression (often idiomatic) such as *at a loss*, *by dint of*, *in the long run*.

Idioms and phrases are tested in almost every BCS Preliminary paper ("*The idiom 'a red-letter day' means…*"), in bank and admission tests, and in BCS Written translation. They also appear in GRE/SAT reading passages and in the IELTS speaking test.

::: tip
**How to learn idioms:**

1. Learn each idiom **with its meaning and an example** — never in isolation.
2. Group idioms by **key word** (*blood, heart, hand, head, eye, cat, dog, water, fire…*) or by **theme** (anger, success, failure, money).
3. Notice the **origin** where it helps: *Achilles' heel* (Greek myth), *Herculean task*, *Pyrrhic victory*, *cross the Rubicon*, *Hobson's choice* — the stories make them unforgettable.
4. Do not change the words of an idiom: ✘ *a stitch in time saves ten*; ✘ *by hook and crook* → ✔ *by hook or by crook*.
:::

**In black and white** [লিখিতভাবে]{.bn} — in writing. *Give me the agreement in black and white.*

**In cold blood** [ঠান্ডা মাথায়]{.bn} — deliberately and cruelly. *The prisoners were shot in cold blood.*

**In full swing** [পুরোদমে]{.bn} — at the height of activity. *The election campaign is in full swing.*

**In hot water** [বিপদে]{.bn} — in trouble. *He is in hot water with his boss.*

**In the long run** [শেষ পর্যন্ত]{.bn} — eventually. *Honesty pays in the long run.*

**In the nick of time** [ঠিক সময়ে]{.bn} — just in time. *The doctor arrived in the nick of time.*

**In the teeth of** [প্রবল বিরোধিতা সত্ত্বেও]{.bn} — in spite of strong opposition. *The bill was passed in the teeth of opposition.*

**In vain** [বৃথা]{.bn} — uselessly. *All his efforts were in vain.*

**Ins and outs** [খুঁটিনাটি]{.bn} — all the details. *He knows the ins and outs of banking.*

**Itching palm** [ঘুষ খাওয়ার প্রবণতা]{.bn} — greed for money. *The officer has an itching palm.*

**Jack of all trades** [সব কাজের কাজি]{.bn} — one who can do many things (but is master of none). *He is a jack of all trades.*

**Keep one's head** [মাথা ঠান্ডা রাখা]{.bn} — stay calm. *Keep your head in an emergency.*

**Keep the wolf from the door** [অভাব দূরে রাখা]{.bn} — avoid hunger and poverty. *He works two jobs to keep the wolf from the door.*

**Kick the bucket** [মারা যাওয়া]{.bn} — die (informal). *The old miser finally kicked the bucket.*

**Kill two birds with one stone** [এক ঢিলে দুই পাখি মারা]{.bn} — achieve two aims with one action. *By cycling to work I kill two birds with one stone — save money and keep fit.*

**Kith and kin** [আত্মীয়স্বজন]{.bn} — relatives. *He invited all his kith and kin.*
:::
