# Identifying the Missing Word {#ch36}

[Part A · Grammar]{.chpart}
:::

## 36.1 What Is This Item?

In **"Identifying the Missing Word"** (also called *Fill in the Blank*, *Sentence Completion* or *Cloze*) questions, a word has been removed from a sentence and you must supply the word that is **grammatically correct and makes sense**. The missing word may be:

- a **function word** — article, preposition, conjunction, pronoun, auxiliary, determiner; or
- a **content word** — usually a vocabulary item (noun, verb, adjective, adverb) chosen for meaning and collocation.

In BCS, bank and admission tests, both types appear; in the SAT and GRE (Text Completion, Sentence Equivalence) the focus is on **vocabulary in context and logic**.

## 36.2 A Step-by-Step Method

1. **Read the whole sentence** and grasp its general meaning.
2. **Decide what part of speech** is missing by looking at the words around the blank:
   - between a determiner and a noun → **adjective** (*a ___ boy*);
   - after a subject and before an object → **verb** (*He ___ the letter*);
   - before a noun after a verb/adjective → **preposition** or **article** (*fond ___ music*);
   - between two clauses → **conjunction** or **relative pronoun**.
3. **Look for clue words**: time markers (*since, ago*), contrast words (*but, although, despite*), cause/result words (*because, so, therefore*), correlatives (*either … ___*), fixed phrases (*look forward ___*).
4. **Predict the word** before reading the options (if any).
5. **Check grammar and collocation**: agreement, tense, countable/uncountable, idiom.
6. **Re-read the completed sentence** to make sure it sounds natural and logical.

## 36.3 Missing Articles

::: ex
- He is ___ honest man. → **an**
- ___ sun rises in the east. → **The**
- She is ___ best student in the class. → **the**
- He is ___ European. → **a**
- I waited for ___ hour. → **an**
- ___ Meghna is a big river. → **The**
- He plays ___ violin. → **the**
- He goes to ___ bed at ten. → (no article)
- What ___ beautiful day it is! → **a**
- ___ rich are not always happy. → **The**
:::

## 36.5 Missing Conjunctions and Correlatives

::: ex
- He is poor ___ honest. → **but**
- ___ he is rich, he is not happy. → **Although / Though**
- Neither Rahim ___ Karim came. → **nor**
- Either you ___ he must go. → **or**
- No sooner had he come ___ she left. → **than**
- Hardly had I reached ___ it began to rain. → **when**
- He is not only intelligent ___ also hard-working. → **but**
- Work hard ___ you will fail. → **or**
- Work hard ___ you will succeed. → **and**
- He was so tired ___ he fell asleep. → **that**
- Walk carefully ___ you should fall. → **lest**
- ___ you work hard, you will fail. → **Unless**
- I don't know ___ he will come or not. → **whether**
- He talks ___ if he were a king. → **as**
- Wait here ___ I return. → **till / until**
- He has been ill ___ he came back from Sylhet. → **since**
- ___ it was raining, we stayed indoors. → **As / Since / Because**
- He works hard ___ that he may succeed. → **so**
:::

## 36.9 Missing Content Words (Vocabulary in Context)

For meaning-based blanks, use **clue words** to identify the logic of the sentence.

### 36.9.1 Contrast Clues — *but, although, despite, however, yet, while, unlike, instead*

The blank means the **opposite** of an idea elsewhere in the sentence.

::: ex
- Although he is very rich, he lives a ___ life. → **simple / frugal / austere** (contrast with *rich*)
- Unlike his talkative brother, Rahim is quite ___. → **reticent / taciturn / quiet**
- The critic, usually harsh, was surprisingly ___ in his review. → **lenient / generous / laudatory**
:::

### 36.9.2 Support / Similarity Clues — *and, also, moreover, similarly, likewise, ; (semicolon), : (colon)*

The blank **continues** or **restates** an idea.

::: ex
- He is honest and ___. → **sincere / trustworthy**
- The speech was long; in fact, it was ___. → **interminable / verbose / tedious**
- She is generous: she ___ helps the needy. → **always / readily**
:::

### 36.9.3 Cause and Effect Clues — *because, since, so, therefore, thus, as a result, consequently*

::: ex
- Because he was ___, he could not finish the race. → **exhausted / injured**
- The evidence was ___, so the court acquitted him. → **insufficient / inconclusive / flimsy**
- He was ___ about the result; therefore, he did not sleep all night. → **anxious / worried**
:::

### 36.9.4 Definition / Example Clues

::: ex
- A person who cannot read or write is called ___. → **illiterate**
- He is a ___; he believes that everything happens for the best. → **optimist**
- A ___ disease spreads by contact. → **contagious**
:::

### 36.9.5 Collocation Clues

Some words naturally go together (*heavy rain, strong tea, make a mistake, do homework, take a decision* (BrE) / *make a decision*).

::: ex
- There was ___ rain last night. → **heavy** (✘ strong)
- He ___ a mistake. → **made** (✘ did)
- Please ___ your homework. → **do** (✘ make)
- She ___ a promise. → **made / kept / broke**
- He ___ attention to the lecture. → **paid**
- The doctor ___ him some medicine. → **prescribed**
- We must ___ an end to corruption. → **put**
- They ___ a great loss in business. → **suffered / incurred**
:::

## 36.10 Paired Blanks (Double Blanks)

When there are two blanks, the two words must fit **each other** as well as the sentence. Solve the easier blank first and eliminate options.

::: ex
- The professor's lecture was so ___ that many students ___. → **boring … fell asleep**
- Though the task seemed ___ at first, it proved quite ___. → **difficult … easy**
- His ___ nature made him ___ among his colleagues. → **amiable … popular**
- The policy was intended to ___ poverty, but it actually ___ the gap between rich and poor. → **reduce … widened**
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Text Completion Logic:** GRE Text Completion and the SAT "Words in Context" items are missing-word questions built on **logic signals**. Train yourself to (1) circle the **signal word** (*although, because, and, not … but, indeed*), (2) find the **clue phrase** that defines the blank, (3) **predict** a simple word of your own, then (4) match it to the options. In two- and three-blank GRE items, never choose a word just because it is difficult; choose it because the **whole passage** supports it. A classic GRE pattern: *"Far from being ___, the author's prose is remarkably lucid."* → the blank must mean the opposite of *lucid* — **opaque / abstruse / convoluted**.
:::
