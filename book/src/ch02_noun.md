## 2.1 Definition

A **noun** is a naming word. It is the name of a **person, place, animal, thing, substance, quality, action, state, feeling or idea**. The word *noun* comes from the Latin *nomen*, meaning "name".

::: ex
- **Person:** Kazi Nazrul Islam, mother, engineer, child
- **Place:** Chattogram, market, hospital, Bangladesh
- **Animal:** tiger, cow, hilsa, eagle
- **Thing:** chair, mobile, newspaper, bridge
- **Substance / material:** gold, water, rice, cotton
- **Quality:** kindness, courage, beauty, wisdom
- **Action / state:** laughter, journey, sleep, poverty
- **Feeling / idea:** love, fear, freedom, democracy
:::

## 2.2 Kinds of Nouns

Traditional grammar divides nouns into **five kinds**: Proper, Common, Collective, Material and Abstract. Modern grammars also classify them as **concrete/abstract** and **countable/uncountable**. You must know both systems.

### 2.2.1 Proper Noun

A **proper noun** is the name of a **particular** person, place or thing. It always begins with a **capital letter**.

::: ex
- **Persons:** Rabindranath, Sheikh Hasina, Shakespeare, Newton
- **Places:** Dhaka, Cox's Bazar, the Padma, the Himalayas, Asia
- **Days, months, festivals:** Friday, December, Eid-ul-Fitr, Pahela Baishakh
- **Books, newspapers, ships, organisations:** the Gitanjali, The Daily Star, the Titanic, the United Nations
- **Languages and nationalities:** Bangla, English, Japanese
:::

**Proper nouns used as common nouns.** When a proper noun stands for a *type* or *quality*, it behaves like a common noun and may take an article:

::: ex
- He is **the Newton** of our college. (= a great scientist)
- Kalidasa is called **the Shakespeare** of India. (= a great dramatist)
- Is there **a Daniel** among you? (= a wise judge)
- Shakespeare is the **Kalidasa** of England. (the comparison works both ways)
- There are two **Rahims** in our class. (two persons named Rahim)
:::

### 2.2.2 Common Noun

A **common noun** is a name given in common to **every person or thing of the same class or kind**.

::: ex
- **boy** (any boy), **city** (any city), **river**, **book**, **teacher**, **country**, **flower**
:::

*Dhaka* is a proper noun, but *city* is a common noun. *The Padma* is a proper noun, but *river* is a common noun.

**Common nouns used as abstract nouns.** Sometimes a common noun stands for a *quality*:

::: ex
- **The mother** in her rose up when she saw the child. (= motherly feelings)
- **The judge** in him prevailed over **the father**. (= sense of justice / fatherly love)
- **The pen** is mightier than **the sword**. (= writing / military force)
- **The cradle** and **the grave**. (= birth and death)
:::

### 2.2.3 Collective Noun

A **collective noun** is the name of a **group or collection** of persons, animals or things taken together and regarded as one whole.

| Collective noun | Group of | Collective noun | Group of |
|---|---|---|---|
| a **class** | students | a **herd** | cattle, elephants |
| a **team** | players | a **flock** | sheep, birds, goats |
| a **jury** | jurors | a **swarm** | bees, insects |
| a **committee** | members | a **pack** | wolves, dogs, cards |
| an **army** | soldiers | a **school / shoal** | fish |
| a **crowd** | people | a **pride** | lions |
| an **audience** | listeners | a **bevy** | ladies, quails |
| a **mob** | rioters | a **galaxy** | stars, beauties |
| a **crew** | sailors, airmen | a **bunch** | grapes, keys, flowers |
| a **fleet** | ships, vehicles | a **cluster** | stars, grapes |
| a **gang** | thieves, labourers | a **bouquet** | flowers |
| a **band** | musicians, robbers | a **library** | books |
| a **troupe** | actors, dancers | a **litter** | puppies, kittens |
| a **staff** | employees | a **brood** | chickens |
| a **regiment** | soldiers | a **caravan** | travellers, camels |
| a **congregation** | worshippers | a **chain** | mountains, shops |
| a **panel** | experts, judges | a **range** | mountains, products |
| a **board** | directors | a **heap / pile** | stones, books |
| a **choir** | singers | a **posse** | police, armed men |
| a **gaggle** | geese (on ground) | a **colony** | ants, bats |
| a **murder** | crows | a **parliament** | owls |
| a **troop** | monkeys, scouts | a **sheaf** | corn, papers |
| a **hive** | bees | a **suite** | rooms, furniture |
| a **pod** | whales, dolphins | an **anthology** | poems |
| a **flight** | stairs, birds | a **constellation** | stars |
| a **group** | islands (archipelago) | a **fleet** | ships |

Collective nouns are a favourite exam item, both for vocabulary ("A group of lions is called a ___") and for **subject-verb agreement**. A collective noun takes a **singular verb** when the group acts **as one unit**, and a **plural verb** when the members act **individually**:

::: ex
- The **jury has** given **its** verdict. (as one unit)
- The **jury were** divided in **their** opinions. (as individuals)
- The **committee has** submitted its report.
- The **committee are** quarrelling among themselves.
:::

See Chapter 23 for complete agreement rules.

### 2.2.4 Material Noun

A **material noun** is the name of a **substance or material** of which things are made. It is usually **uncountable** and does not normally take *a/an* or a plural form.

::: ex
- **gold, silver, iron, copper, wood, cotton, wool, silk, glass, stone**
- **water, milk, oil, rice, wheat, sugar, salt, tea, coffee, bread, butter, sand, air**
:::

::: ex
- **Gold** is a precious metal. (✘ *The gold is…* when speaking in general)
- **Rice** is the staple food of Bangladesh.
- **The gold** in this ring is pure. (*the* is used when the material is particular/specified)
- She bought **two kilos of rice** / **a bar of soap** / **a loaf of bread** / **a piece of chalk** / **a cup of tea**.
:::

**Material nouns used as common nouns** (when they mean an object made of the material):

::: ex
- She wears **glasses**. (spectacles)
- He drank two **coffees**. (cups of coffee — informal)
- He was given **irons**. (chains / fetters)
- Bring me a **paper**. (a newspaper) / He has submitted a **paper**. (an article)
- He threw a **stone**. (a piece of stone)
:::

### 2.2.5 Abstract Noun

An **abstract noun** is the name of a **quality, action, state, feeling or idea** — something we can think of or feel but cannot see or touch.

::: ex
- **Quality:** honesty, goodness, kindness, cruelty, bravery, wisdom, height, length
- **Action:** laughter, theft, movement, judgement, flight, hatred
- **State:** childhood, boyhood, slavery, sleep, sickness, death, poverty, youth
- **Arts and sciences:** grammar, music, chemistry, mathematics, literature
:::

## 2.3 Countable and Uncountable Nouns

This modern division is extremely important for the use of articles, determiners and verbs (see Chapters 03, 04 and 05).

- **Countable nouns** name things that can be counted: *one book, two books; a chair, three chairs.* They have singular and plural forms and take *a/an* in the singular.
- **Uncountable (mass) nouns** name things that cannot be counted as separate units: *water, rice, information, advice, furniture.* They normally have no plural form, do not take *a/an*, and take a **singular verb**.

| Use with | Countable | Uncountable |
|---|---|---|
| a / an | a book ✔ | ✘ an advice |
| plural form | books ✔ | ✘ informations |
| many / few / a few / several / a number of | many books ✔ | ✘ many furnitures |
| much / little / a little / a great deal of / an amount of | ✘ much books | much water ✔ |
| some / any / a lot of / lots of / plenty of / no | ✔ | ✔ |

::: tip
**Exam Tip:** The words *information, advice, furniture, luggage, baggage, equipment, scenery, machinery, poetry, news, knowledge, work* (= job), *homework, bread, money, traffic, weather, evidence, research, progress, damage, mischief, jewellery, stationery* are **uncountable** in English (although some are countable in Bangla). Chapter 03 deals with them in detail.
:::

## 2.4 Concrete and Abstract Nouns

- **Concrete nouns** name things that can be perceived by the five senses — seen, touched, heard, smelt or tasted: *table, music, perfume, mango, Rahim.* Proper, common, collective and material nouns are all concrete.
- **Abstract nouns** name ideas or qualities: *honesty, joy, freedom.*

## 2.5 Compound Nouns

A **compound noun** is made of two or more words that together act as a single noun.

| Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|
| noun + noun | bookshelf, toothbrush, football, bus stop, sister-in-law |
| adjective + noun | blackboard, greenhouse, software, full moon |
| verb + noun | pickpocket, breakfast, washing machine |
| noun + verb | haircut, sunrise, rainfall, nosebleed |
| verb + adverb/particle | breakdown, take-off, drawback, setback, check-in |
| adverb/particle + noun | bystander, underworld, outbreak, input |
| noun + prepositional phrase | commander-in-chief, man-of-war, mother-in-law |

Compound nouns can be written **closed** (*notebook*), **hyphenated** (*passer-by*) or **open** (*post office*). Plural forms are treated in Chapter 06 (*sisters-in-law, passers-by, commanders-in-chief, spoonfuls*).

## 2.6 Functions (Uses) of a Noun in a Sentence

A noun (or noun phrase/clause) can do these jobs:

| Function | Example |
|---|---|
| Subject of a verb | **Karim** plays football. |
| Direct object of a verb | I met **Karim**. |
| Indirect object | I gave **Karim** a book. |
| Object of a preposition | He is fond of **music**. |
| Subjective complement | He is a **doctor**. / She became a **minister**. |
| Objective complement | They made him **captain**. / We elected Rahim **chairman**. |
| Appositive | Mr. Rahman, **our headmaster**, is ill. |
| Vocative (address) | **Rahim**, come here. |
| Adjective-like (noun adjunct) | a **cotton** shirt, a **village** school, a **gold** ring |
| Adverbial noun | He walked **ten miles**. / Wait **a moment**. / He came **home**. |

::: note
**Noun used as an adjective (noun adjunct).** When a noun modifies another noun, it is normally **singular**, even if the meaning is plural: *a* **ten-taka** *note* (✘ ten-takas), *a* **five-year** *plan*, *a* **three-mile** *walk*, *a* **two-storey** *building*, *a* **shoe** *shop*, *a* **toothbrush**. (Exceptions: *sports car, sales manager, arms race, customs officer, savings account, goods train.*)
:::

## 2.7 Case of Nouns

**Case** shows the relationship of a noun or pronoun to other words in the sentence. English nouns have three cases:

1. **Nominative (subjective) case** — when the noun is the subject or subjective complement: **Rahim** went home. That man is **Rahim**.
2. **Objective (accusative) case** — when the noun is the object of a verb or preposition: I saw **Rahim**. I went with **Rahim**.
3. **Possessive (genitive) case** — shows ownership or relation: **Rahim's** book; the **boys'** hostel.

(Some grammars add the **dative** case for the indirect object — *I gave* **Rahim** *a pen* — and the **vocative** case for a noun of address — **Rahim**, *come here*.)

For nouns, nominative and objective forms look the same; only the **possessive** has a different form. For pronouns all three forms differ (*he / him / his*), so pronoun case errors are much more common (Chapter 08).

## 2.8 The Possessive Case — Rules

### 2.8.1 How to Form the Possessive

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Singular noun → add **'s** | the boy**'s** bag, my sister**'s** room |
| Plural noun ending in *s* → add only **'** | the boy**s'** hostel, the girl**s'** school, my parent**s'** house |
| Plural noun **not** ending in *s* → add **'s** | the children**'s** park, men**'s** clothes, women**'s** rights, the people**'s** will |
| Singular noun ending in *s* → **'s** (or only ' in classical names) | James**'s** car (also James'), the boss**'s** office; Moses**'** laws, Jesus**'** teachings, Socrates**'** philosophy |
| For goodness' sake type phrases (hissing sound before *sake*) | for goodness**'** sake, for conscience**'** sake, for justice**'** sake |
| Compound nouns → add 's to the **last** word | my brother-in-law**'s** car, the commander-in-chief**'s** order, somebody else**'s** pen |
| Joint possession → 's on the **last** name only | Rahim and Karim**'s** shop (one shop owned together) |
| Separate possession → 's on **each** name | Rahim**'s** and Karim**'s** shops (two shops) |
| Apposition → 's on the **second** noun | This is my friend Kamal**'s** book. / I bought it at Hossain the bookseller**'s**. |

### 2.8.2 When to Use 's and When to Use *of*

In general, the **'s** form is used for **living beings** (persons and animals), and the **of** form for **lifeless things**:

::: ex
- ✔ the **man's** hand, the **dog's** tail, the **teacher's** advice
- ✘ the **table's** leg ✔ the **leg of the table**
- ✘ the **house's** roof ✔ the **roof of the house**
- ✘ the **book's** cover ✔ the **cover of the book**
:::

**Exceptions — 's is also used with:**

- **Personified things:** *Nature's laws, Fortune's favourite, Death's door, the Sun's rays, the Moon's light, the Earth's surface.*
- **Time, distance, weight, value:** *a* **day's** *journey, a* **week's** *holiday,* **today's** *paper, a* **stone's** *throw, a* **mile's** *distance, a* **pound's** *weight, ten* **takas'** *worth, two* **hours'** *drive.*
- **Dignified or collective names of places/organisations:** *the* **nation's** *pride,* **Bangladesh's** *economy, the* **government's** *decision, the* **company's** *profit, the* **world's** *population.*
- **Some fixed phrases:** *at his* **wits'** *end, at* **arm's** *length, for* **mercy's** *sake, out of* **harm's** *way, to his* **heart's** *content, by a* **hair's** *breadth, in my* **mind's** *eye, at* **death's** *door, the* **needle's** *eye.*

### 2.8.3 The Double Possessive

A noun with **of** + **'s** (or of + possessive pronoun) is called the **double possessive**. It shows that the item is **one of several**:

::: ex
- He is a friend **of my father's**. (= one of my father's friends)
- This is a book **of mine**. (= one of my books) ✘ *a book of me*
- That is a novel **of Tagore's**.
:::

Compare: *a picture of the King* (= a picture showing the King) vs *a picture of the King's* (= a picture owned by the King).

### 2.8.4 Omission of the Noun after the Possessive

The noun after a possessive is often left out when it is understood, especially *house, shop, church, office*:

::: ex
- I stayed at my **uncle's** (house).
- He went to the **chemist's** (shop) / the **barber's** / the **doctor's**.
- She attended **St. Paul's** (Cathedral).
- This book is **Rahim's**, not **Karim's**.
:::

### 2.8.5 Common Possessive Errors

::: errs
1. ✘ The **its** colour is red. / ✘ **It's** colour is red. ✔ **Its** colour is red. (*its* = possessive; *it's* = it is.)
2. ✘ This pen is **your's**. ✔ This pen is **yours**. (No apostrophe in *yours, hers, ours, theirs, its.*)
3. ✘ The **girl's** hostel is far (meaning hostel for many girls). ✔ The **girls'** hostel is far.
4. ✘ I went to **the barber**. (the person) — acceptable; but *I went to the* **barber's** is the idiomatic form for the shop.
5. ✘ **Childrens'** books ✔ **Children's** books.
6. ✘ He is a friend **of me**. ✔ He is a friend **of mine**.
7. ✘ The **chair's leg** is broken. ✔ The **leg of the chair** is broken.
8. ✘ **Rahim's and Karim's** father is a doctor. (if they are brothers) ✔ **Rahim and Karim's** father is a doctor.
9. ✘ He walked a **mile** distance. ✔ He walked a **mile's** distance / a distance of a mile.
10. ✘ The **mother-in-law's** of the bride. ✔ The bride's **mother-in-law**. / The **mother-in-law's** house.
:::

## 2.9 Nouns and Their Gender and Number

Gender (*masculine, feminine, common, neuter*) and number (*singular, plural*) are treated fully in Chapters 06 and 07. For now remember:

- *Teacher, student, parent, friend, child, cousin, doctor* are **common gender** (either sex).
- *Table, book, river* are **neuter**.
- *Ship, moon, nation* are often personified as **feminine**; *sun, death, time, war* as **masculine**.

## 2.10 Noun Phrases and Noun Clauses

A **noun phrase** is a group of words with a noun as its head: *a very tall old man in a black coat*. A **noun clause** is a clause doing the job of a noun: *I know* **that he is honest**. (See Chapters 30–31.)

::: ex
- **The tall boy in the last bench** is my cousin. (noun phrase as subject)
- **To err** is human; **to forgive**, divine. (infinitive as noun)
- **Smoking** is injurious to health. (gerund as noun)
- **Whatever he says** is true. (noun clause)
:::

## 2.11 Common Errors in the Use of Nouns

::: errs
1. ✘ He gave me **an advice**. ✔ He gave me **some advice** / **a piece of advice**.
2. ✘ The **sceneries** of Sylhet are charming. ✔ The **scenery** of Sylhet is charming.
3. ✘ I have bought new **furnitures**. ✔ I have bought new **furniture** / some pieces of furniture.
4. ✘ He has many **informations**. ✔ He has a lot of **information**.
5. ✘ The **poets** Rabindranath and Nazrul **was** great. ✔ The poets … **were** great.
6. ✘ He is a **ten years** old boy. ✔ He is a **ten-year-old** boy. / The boy is **ten years old**.
7. ✘ I have two **dozens** of eggs. ✔ I have two **dozen** eggs. (But: *dozens of eggs* = an indefinite number.)
8. ✘ He bought a **five hundreds** taka note. ✔ He bought a **five-hundred-taka** note.
9. ✘ The **cattles** are grazing. ✔ The **cattle** are grazing. (*cattle* is plural in form and meaning.)
10. ✘ **Mathematics are** my favourite subject. ✔ **Mathematics is** my favourite subject.
11. ✘ The **news are** good. ✔ The **news is** good.
12. ✘ The **police has** arrested the thief. ✔ The **police have** arrested the thief.
13. ✘ He is my **cousin brother**. ✔ He is my **cousin**. (A Bangla-influenced error: *cousin* is common gender.)
14. ✘ My **family members is** large. ✔ My **family is** large. / My family members **are** many.
15. ✘ She is senior **than** me. ✔ She is senior **to** me. (See Chapter 10 on Latin adjectives.)
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Noun Precision:** GMAT Sentence Correction punishes **countable/uncountable mismatches** (*fewer* with countables, *less* with uncountables; *number* with countables, *amount* with uncountables): ✘ *The amount of people…* ✔ *The number of people…*; ✘ *less errors* ✔ *fewer errors*. It also tests **noun–noun parallelism** in comparisons: ✘ *The population of Dhaka is larger than Chattogram.* ✔ *The population of Dhaka is larger than* **that of** *Chattogram.* (We compare population with population, not population with a city.)
:::

| -ance, -ence, -ancy, -ency | state / act | importance, silence, vacancy, efficiency |
| -age | collection / act / place | baggage, marriage, orphanage |

Learning these suffixes will help you instantly recognise a noun in an unfamiliar sentence and answer "change the part of speech" questions (Chapter 41) with confidence.
