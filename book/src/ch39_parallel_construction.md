# Parallel Construction {#ch39}

[Part A · Grammar]{.chpart}

## 39.1 What Is Parallel Construction?

**Parallel construction** (parallelism or parallel structure) means using the **same grammatical form** for two or more ideas that are **equal in importance** and are joined together in a sentence. Words should be parallel with words of the same part of speech, phrases with phrases of the same type, and clauses with clauses.

::: ex
- ✘ He likes **swimming**, **reading** and **to play** chess. (gerund, gerund, infinitive)
- ✔ He likes **swimming**, **reading** and **playing** chess. (gerund, gerund, gerund)
- ✔ He likes **to swim**, **to read** and **to play** chess. (infinitive × 3)
:::

Parallelism makes writing **clear, balanced and elegant**. It is one of the top-tested rules in the **GMAT, SAT and GRE**, and appears regularly in BCS and bank error-detection questions.

::: rule
**When items are joined by *and, but, or, nor, yet*, by correlatives (*both … and, either … or, neither … nor, not only … but also, whether … or*), or by comparison words (*than, as … as, rather than, more … than*), they must be in the same grammatical form.**
:::

## 39.2 Parallelism in Lists and Series

### 39.2.1 Nouns with Nouns

::: ex
- ✘ He is known for his **honesty**, **sincerity** and **being punctual**.
- ✔ He is known for his **honesty**, **sincerity** and **punctuality**.
:::

### 39.2.2 Adjectives with Adjectives

::: ex
- ✘ She is **beautiful**, **intelligent** and **has a kind heart**.
- ✔ She is **beautiful**, **intelligent** and **kind-hearted**.
:::

### 39.2.3 Verbs with Verbs (Same Tense and Form)

::: ex
- ✘ He **came** in, **sat** down and **begins** to read.
- ✔ He **came** in, **sat** down and **began** to read.
- ✘ The job requires you to **type** letters, **answering** calls and **to meet** visitors.
- ✔ The job requires you to **type** letters, **answer** calls and **meet** visitors.
:::

### 39.2.4 Adverbs with Adverbs

::: ex
- ✘ He spoke **clearly**, **slowly** and **with confidence**.
- ✔ He spoke **clearly**, **slowly** and **confidently**.
:::

### 39.2.5 Phrases with Phrases

::: ex
- ✘ We looked **in the cupboard**, **under the bed** and **the kitchen**.
- ✔ We looked **in the cupboard**, **under the bed** and **in the kitchen**.
- ✘ The children were told **to wash** their hands and **that they should** sit quietly.
- ✔ The children were told **to wash** their hands and **to sit** quietly.
:::

### 39.2.6 Clauses with Clauses

::: ex
- ✘ I know **that he is honest** and **his hard work**.
- ✔ I know **that he is honest** and **that he works hard**.
- ✘ The teacher asked **who had finished** and **the reason for the delay**.
- ✔ The teacher asked **who had finished** and **why there had been a delay**.
:::

### 39.2.7 Repeating Articles, Prepositions, *to* and *that*

In a series, either use the function word **once** (before the first item, applying to all) or **repeat it before every item** — not before some.

::: ex
- ✘ He bought **a** pen, pencil and **an** eraser. ✔ He bought **a** pen, **a** pencil and **an** eraser.
- ✘ She has lived **in** Dhaka, Sylhet and **in** Khulna. ✔ She has lived **in** Dhaka, Sylhet and Khulna. / … **in** Dhaka, **in** Sylhet and **in** Khulna.
- ✘ I want **to** eat, drink and **to** sleep. ✔ I want **to** eat, drink and sleep. / … **to** eat, **to** drink and **to** sleep.
:::

## 39.3 Parallelism with Correlative Conjunctions

The element after each half of a correlative pair must have the **same form**, and each correlative should be placed **immediately before** the parallel element.

| Correlative | ✘ Faulty | ✔ Parallel |
|---|---|---|
| both … and | He is **both** a poet **and** writes novels. | He is **both** a poet **and** a novelist. |
| either … or | **Either** you can pay in cash **or** by cheque. | You can pay **either** in cash **or** by cheque. |
| neither … nor | He **neither** likes tea **nor** coffee. | He likes **neither** tea **nor** coffee. |
| not only … but also | She **not only** sings **but also** a dancer. | She **not only** sings **but also** dances. / She is **not only** a singer **but also** a dancer. |
| whether … or | I don't know **whether** to go **or** staying. | I don't know **whether** to go **or** (to) stay. |
| not … but | He is **not** lazy **but** he has no ability. | He is **not** lazy **but** incapable. |

## 39.4 Parallelism in Comparisons

When comparing, the two things compared must be **parallel in form and logical in meaning** (see also Chapter 24).

::: ex
- ✘ I prefer **reading** books **to watch** television. ✔ I prefer **reading** books **to watching** television.
- ✘ **To work** hard is better than **being** lazy. ✔ **Working** hard is better than **being** lazy. / **To work** hard is better than **to be** lazy.
- ✘ It is easier **to talk** than **doing** it. ✔ It is easier **to talk** than **to do** it.
- ✘ The salary of a doctor is higher than **a teacher**. ✔ The salary of a doctor is higher than **that of a teacher**.
- ✘ He would rather **die** than **begging**. ✔ He would rather **die** than **beg**.
:::

## 39.5 Parallelism with *Than*, *As*, *Rather than*, *Instead of*

::: ex
- ✘ He likes **to walk** rather than **riding**. ✔ He likes **walking** rather than **riding**. / He likes **to walk** rather than **(to) ride**.
- ✘ She chose **to study** medicine instead of **engineering studies**. ✔ She chose **to study** medicine instead of engineering. (noun–noun) / She chose **studying** medicine **over** **studying** engineering.
:::

## 39.6 Parallelism in Headings, Outlines and Bulleted Lists

All items in a list or set of headings should start with the same type of word:

::: ex
**✘ Faulty**

- Causes of traffic jam
- How to reduce it
- Government's role

**✔ Parallel**

- Causes of traffic jams
- Ways to reduce them
- The government's role
:::

## 39.7 Parallelism in Famous Quotations

Parallelism is a powerful rhetorical device (in rhetoric it is called *isocolon* when the parts are of equal length; *antithesis* when the parts contrast). Many famous sayings are built on it:

::: quote
"**Veni, vidi, vici.**" (I came, I saw, I conquered.) — Julius Caesar

"Government **of the people**, **by the people**, **for the people**, shall not perish from the earth." — Abraham Lincoln

"Ask not **what your country can do for you** — ask **what you can do for your country**." — John F. Kennedy

"**To err** is human, **to forgive** divine." — Alexander Pope

"Some books are **to be tasted**, others **to be swallowed**, and some few **to be chewed and digested**." — Francis Bacon

"Reading maketh **a full man**; conference **a ready man**; and writing **an exact man**." — Francis Bacon

"We shall fight **on the beaches**, we shall fight **on the landing grounds**, we shall fight **in the fields and in the streets**…" — Winston Churchill

"The struggle this time is **the struggle for our emancipation**; the struggle this time is **the struggle for our independence**." — Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujibur Rahman, 7 March 1971
:::

## 39.8 Common Parallelism Errors — Consolidated

::: errs
1. ✘ He was tall, handsome and **had intelligence**. ✔ He was tall, handsome and **intelligent**.
2. ✘ I like **to read** and **writing**. ✔ I like **reading** and **writing**.
3. ✘ The film was **long**, **boring** and **it was badly acted**. ✔ The film was **long**, **boring** and **badly acted**.
4. ✘ She **neither** wants to study **nor** a job. ✔ She wants **neither** to study **nor** to work.
5. ✘ He is **not only** famous in Bangladesh **but also** in India. ✔ He is famous **not only** in Bangladesh **but also** in India.
6. ✘ The manager asked us **to be punctual** and **that we should dress formally**. ✔ … **to be punctual** and **to dress formally**.
7. ✘ He is good at **painting**, **singing** and **to dance**. ✔ … **painting**, **singing** and **dancing**.
8. ✘ My duties are **cleaning** the room and **to cook** meals. ✔ … **cleaning** the room and **cooking** meals.
9. ✘ She **either** goes by bus **or** walking. ✔ She **either** goes by bus **or** walks.
10. ✘ The new policy is **efficient** and **saves money**. ✔ The new policy is **efficient** and **economical**.
11. ✘ He promised **to come** early and **that he would help** us. ✔ He promised **to come** early and **to help** us.
12. ✘ **Walking** is better exercise than **to run**. ✔ **Walking** is better exercise than **running**.
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — The Parallelism Toolkit:** GMAT Sentence Correction lists parallelism as one of its core concepts. Three markers should immediately trigger a parallelism check: (1) **lists** (A, B, and C), (2) **correlative pairs** (*not only … but also, both … and, either … or, neither … nor, whether … or, not … but*), (3) **comparisons** (*than, as, like, unlike, rather than, compared with*). Also be alert to **logical parallelism**: ✘ *"The report criticised the hiring of new staff and the employees who left"* compares an action (*hiring*) with people (*employees*) — ✔ *"…the hiring of new staff and the departure of senior employees."*
:::
