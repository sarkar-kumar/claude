## 21.1 What Is Tense?

**Tense** is the form of a verb that shows the **time** of an action or state — and also whether the action is **complete** or **continuing**. The word comes from the Latin *tempus* (time).

There are **three main tenses**:

1. **Present tense** — action in the present: *I write.*
2. **Past tense** — action in the past: *I wrote.*
3. **Future tense** — action in the future: *I shall write.*

Each tense has **four forms (aspects)**:

| Aspect | Shows | Present | Past | Future |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Indefinite (Simple)** | a simple action/fact/habit | I write | I wrote | I shall write |
| **Continuous (Progressive)** | an action in progress | I am writing | I was writing | I shall be writing |
| **Perfect** | an action completed before a point | I have written | I had written | I shall have written |
| **Perfect Continuous** | an action continuing for a period up to a point | I have been writing | I had been writing | I shall have been writing |

So there are **twelve tenses** in all. The structures were given in Chapter 12; this chapter explains **when to use each tense**, with special attention to the uses most often tested.

## 21.2 Present Indefinite (Simple Present) Tense

**Structure:** Subject + V1 (+ *s/es* for third-person singular). Negative/question with *do/does*.

### 21.2.1 Uses

1. **Habitual or repeated actions** — often with *always, usually, often, sometimes, seldom, never, every day, once a week*:
   *He* **gets up** *at six every day. She* **goes** *to school by bus.*
2. **Universal / general truths and facts**:
   *The sun* **rises** *in the east. Water* **boils** *at 100°C. Two and two* **make** *four. The earth* **moves** *round the sun.*
3. **Permanent situations**:
   *My father* **works** *in a bank. Rajshahi* **stands** *on the Padma.*
4. **Proverbs and maxims**:
   *Honesty* **is** *the best policy. Barking dogs seldom* **bite**.
5. **Fixed future events (timetables, programmes)**:
   *The train* **leaves** *at 8 p.m. The exam* **starts** *next Monday.*
6. **In time and conditional clauses referring to the future** (after *when, before, after, as soon as, until, till, if, unless, in case, provided*):
   *I will call you* **when** *he* **comes**. (✘ when he will come) *If it* **rains**, *we shall stay in.*
7. **Historical present** — to make past events vivid in narration:
   *Napoleon* **marches** *into Russia…*
8. **Quotations / what books say**:
   *Shakespeare* **says**, "Frailty, thy name is woman." *The Quran* **says**…
9. **Exclamatory sentences beginning with *here/there***:
   *Here* **comes** *the bus! There* **goes** *the bell!*
10. **Commentaries and demonstrations**:
    *Shakib* **bowls**, *and the batsman* **misses**.
11. **With stative verbs** (instead of continuous): *I* **know** *him. She* **loves** *music.*

::: rule
**Third-person singular -s/-es:** *he/she/it/singular noun* + verb + *s/es*: *He* **plays**, *she* **watches**, *it* **flies**, *the boy* **goes**. ✘ *He play.* ✘ *She don't know.* ✔ *She doesn't know.*
:::

## 21.3 Present Continuous (Progressive) Tense

**Structure:** Subject + *am/is/are* + V-ing.

### 21.3.1 Uses

1. **An action happening at the moment of speaking** (*now, at present, at the moment, look!, listen!*):
   *He* **is reading** *now. Look! The baby* **is crying**.
2. **A temporary action around the present** (not necessarily at this moment):
   *I* **am reading** *a novel by Humayun Ahmed these days. He* **is staying** *with his uncle this week.*
3. **A planned future arrangement** (with a future time word):
   *I* **am leaving** *for Chattogram tomorrow. We* **are meeting** *the Principal on Sunday.*
4. **Changing / developing situations**:
   *Prices* **are rising**. *The climate* **is getting** *warmer.*
5. **Annoying repeated habits (with *always / constantly / forever*)**:
   *He* **is always finding** *fault with others. She* **is constantly complaining**.

::: note
**Stative verbs are not normally used in continuous tenses** (Chapter 11): ✘ *I am knowing him.* ✔ *I know him.* ✘ *I am having a car.* ✔ *I have a car.* But: *I am having lunch* ✔ (*have* = eat).
:::

## 21.4 Present Perfect Tense

**Structure:** Subject + *have/has* + V3.

The present perfect connects the **past with the present**: an action happened at an **unspecified** time in the past and its **result or relevance** continues now.

### 21.4.1 Uses

1. **Recently completed actions** (with *just, already, yet, recently, lately, so far, up to now, till now, by now*):
   *He* **has just left**. *I* **have already finished** *my work. Have you* **eaten** *yet?*
2. **Past action with present result** (time not mentioned):
   *I* **have lost** *my pen* (so I don't have it now). *He* **has broken** *his leg.*
3. **Experience (ever / never / once / twice / before)**:
   *Have you* **ever been** *to Cox's Bazar? I* **have never seen** *snow. I* **have read** *this book twice.*
4. **Action/state that began in the past and continues up to now (with *for/since*)** — especially with stative verbs:
   *I* **have known** *him* **for** *ten years. She* **has lived** *here* **since** *2010.*
5. **With *this morning / today / this week / this year*** (period not yet finished):
   *I* **have drunk** *three cups of tea today.*
6. **After *It is the first / second / best… time***:
   *This is the first time I* **have visited** *Sylhet.*

::: rule
**Never use the present perfect with a definite past time** (*yesterday, last night, last year, ago, in 2020, when…, at 5 o'clock*). Use the **simple past** instead.

✘ *I have seen him yesterday.* ✔ *I saw him yesterday.*

✘ *He has come two days ago.* ✔ *He came two days ago.*

✘ *When have you come?* ✔ *When did you come?*

✘ *He has died in 2010.* ✔ *He died in 2010.*
:::

::: tip
**Exam Tip — *since* clauses.** In "It is/has been … since …" sentences, use the **simple past** after *since*: *It is (has been) five years since he* **died** (✘ has died). *Ten years have passed since we* **met**.
:::

## 21.5 Present Perfect Continuous Tense

**Structure:** Subject + *have/has been* + V-ing.

### 21.5.1 Uses

1. **An action that began in the past and is still continuing** (with *for / since / how long*):
   *He* **has been reading** *for two hours. It* **has been raining** *since morning. How long* **have** *you* **been waiting**?
2. **A recently finished action whose effect is visible**:
   *Your eyes are red. Have you* **been crying**? *I'm tired because I* **have been working** *all day.*

::: note
**Present perfect vs present perfect continuous.** *I* **have written** *three letters* (completed, number of results). *I* **have been writing** *letters all morning* (activity, duration). With stative verbs use the simple perfect: *I* **have known** *him for years* (✘ have been knowing).
:::

## 21.6 Past Indefinite (Simple Past) Tense

**Structure:** Subject + V2. Negative/question with *did* + V1.

### 21.6.1 Uses

1. **Completed action at a definite past time** (*yesterday, last week, ago, in 1971, then, when*):
   *He* **went** *to Dhaka yesterday. Bangladesh* **became** *independent in 1971.*
2. **Past habits**:
   *He* **walked** *five miles every day when he was young.* (also *used to walk / would walk*)
3. **A series of completed actions in a story**:
   *He* **came** *in,* **sat** *down and* **began** *to read.*
4. **Unreal present (subjunctive) after *if, wish, as if, it is time, would rather***:
   *If I* **had** *wings, I would fly. I wish I* **knew** *the answer. It is time we* **left**.
5. **Polite requests (distancing)**:
   *I* **wondered** *if you could help me.*

## 21.7 Past Continuous (Progressive) Tense

**Structure:** Subject + *was/were* + V-ing.

### 21.7.1 Uses

1. **An action in progress at a particular time in the past**:
   *At 8 p.m. yesterday I* **was watching** *TV.*
2. **An action in progress interrupted by another (shorter) action** — continuous for the long action, simple past for the short one:
   *I* **was reading** *when he* **came** *in. While she* **was cooking**, *the phone* **rang**.
3. **Two parallel actions in progress** (*while*):
   *While I* **was reading**, *my sister* **was playing**.
4. **Background description in a story**:
   *The sun* **was shining** *and the birds* **were singing**.
5. **Past annoying habit** (with *always*):
   *He* **was always borrowing** *money from me.*

## 21.8 Past Perfect Tense

**Structure:** Subject + *had* + V3.

The past perfect shows **the earlier of two past actions** ("past of the past").

### 21.8.1 Uses

1. **An action completed before another past action or time** — the earlier action takes the past perfect, the later one the simple past:
   *The train* **had left** *before I* **reached** *the station. When I arrived, the meeting* **had already started**. *By 2010, he* **had written** *ten novels.*
2. **After *after*** (action finished first):
   *After he* **had finished** *his homework, he went out.*
3. **Before *before / when / till***: the action in the main clause that happened first:
   *The patient* **had died** *before the doctor came.*
4. **In *no sooner … than / hardly … when / scarcely … when***:
   *No sooner* **had** *he* **arrived** *than it began to rain.*
5. **Unfulfilled past wishes and third conditionals**:
   *I wish I* **had studied** *harder. If he* **had worked** *hard, he would have passed.*
6. **Reported speech** (backshift of past simple / present perfect):
   *He said that he* **had seen** *the film.*

::: rule
**Past perfect + past simple, never two past perfects for a sequence and never past perfect alone without a past reference.**

✘ *I had gone to Dhaka last year.* (no second past action) ✔ *I went to Dhaka last year.*

✘ *The patient died before the doctor had come.* ✔ *The patient* **had died** *before the doctor* **came**.
:::

## 21.9 Past Perfect Continuous Tense

**Structure:** Subject + *had been* + V-ing.

### 21.9.1 Uses

1. **An action that continued for a period up to a past point**:
   *He* **had been waiting** *for an hour when the bus came. They* **had been living** *in Khulna for ten years before they moved to Dhaka.*
2. **Cause of a past situation**:
   *His eyes were red because he* **had been crying**.

## 21.10 Future Indefinite (Simple Future) Tense

**Structure:** Subject + *shall/will* + V1.

### 21.10.1 Uses

1. **Prediction / future fact**: *He* **will be** *twenty next year. It* **will rain** *tomorrow.*
2. **Spontaneous decision / offer / promise**: *I* **will help** *you. I* **will carry** *your bag.*
3. **Threat / command (shall in 2nd/3rd person — traditional)**: *You* **shall be** *punished.*
4. **Main clause of first conditional**: *If you work hard, you* **will pass**.

### 21.10.2 Other Ways of Expressing the Future

| Form | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **be going to** + V1 | intention / plan already made; prediction from present evidence | I **am going to** buy a car. / Look at the clouds — it **is going to** rain. |
| **Present continuous** | personal fixed arrangements | I **am meeting** him tomorrow. |
| **Present simple** | timetables, schedules | The plane **takes off** at 9. |
| **be about to** + V1 | immediate future | The train **is about to** leave. |
| **be to** + V1 | official plans, orders | The PM **is to** visit China. |
| **be on the point of** + V-ing | very near future | He **is on the point of leaving**. |

## 21.11 Future Continuous Tense

**Structure:** Subject + *shall/will be* + V-ing.

1. **An action in progress at a future time**: *At this time tomorrow, I* **shall be flying** *to London.*
2. **A future action as a matter of course / routine**: *I* **will be seeing** *him at the office tomorrow anyway.*
3. **Polite enquiries about plans**: **Will** *you* **be using** *the car tonight?*

## 21.12 Future Perfect Tense

**Structure:** Subject + *shall/will have* + V3.

**An action that will be completed before a future time/action** (often with *by, by the time, before*):

*I* **shall have finished** *the work* **by** *Friday. By the time you arrive, the train* **will have left**. *By 2030, he* **will have retired**.

## 21.13 Future Perfect Continuous Tense

**Structure:** Subject + *shall/will have been* + V-ing.

**An action that will have continued for a period up to a future point**:

*By next June, I* **shall have been teaching** *here for ten years. By 6 p.m. he* **will have been working** *for twelve hours.*

## 21.14 Sequence of Tenses

**Sequence of tenses** means that the tense of the verb in a **subordinate clause** usually follows (depends on) the tense of the verb in the **main clause**.

::: rule
**Rule 1 — Present or future in the main clause:** the subordinate clause can be in **any tense** required by the sense.

*He says that he* **is** / **was** / **will be** *ill.* / *He will say that he* **has finished**.

**Rule 2 — Past in the main clause:** the subordinate clause is normally in a **past tense**.

*He said that he* **was** *ill.* (✘ is) / *I knew that he* **would** *come.* (✘ will) / *She told me that she* **had finished** *the work.*

**Exception A — Universal truths, habitual facts and proverbs** stay in the present:

*The teacher said that the earth* **moves** *round the sun.* / *He said that honesty* **is** *the best policy.*

**Exception B — Comparison with *than / as*:** any tense as sense requires:

*He loved me more than he* **loves** *you.* / *She was better than she* **is** *now.*

**Exception C — Purpose clauses:** present → *may*, past → *might*:

*He works hard so that he* **may** *succeed.* / *He worked hard so that he* **might** *succeed.*
:::

::: ex
- ✘ He told me that he **will** come. ✔ He told me that he **would** come.
- ✘ I found that he **is** absent. ✔ I found that he **was** absent.
- ✔ Galileo proved that the earth **moves** round the sun.
- ✘ He said that he **has** seen it. ✔ He said that he **had** seen it.
:::

## 21.15 Tenses with Special Conjunctions and Phrases — Exam Patterns

| Pattern | Correct tense combination | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **since** + point of time | main: present perfect / perfect continuous; *since*-clause: simple past | I **have known** him since he **was** a child. |
| **It is / It has been … since** | simple past after *since* | It **is** two years since he **left**. |
| **No sooner … than** | past perfect (inverted) … simple past | No sooner **had** he **come** than she **left**. |
| **Hardly / Scarcely … when / before** | past perfect … simple past | Hardly **had** I **gone** when he **arrived**. |
| **before** (past) | earlier action: past perfect; later: simple past | The train **had left** before we **reached**. |
| **after** (past) | after + past perfect, main: simple past | After he **had eaten**, he **slept**. |
| **when / as soon as / till / until / before / after / if / unless** (future meaning) | present simple (not *will*) | I'll wait until he **comes**. |
| **It is (high / about) time** | simple past (subjunctive) | It is high time we **started**. |
| **wish** (present / past) | past / past perfect | I wish I **were** rich. / I wish I **had seen** it. |
| **as if / as though** | past / past perfect | He talks as if he **knew** everything. |
| **would rather** + another subject | simple past | I'd rather you **went** now. |
| **for + period** with continuing action | perfect continuous | He **has been sleeping** for two hours. |
| **ago** | simple past | He **died** two years ago. |
| **by + future time / by the time** | future perfect | By next month, I **will have finished**. |
| **while** (two past actions) | past continuous + past continuous / simple past | While I **was reading**, he **slept**. |
| **lest** | *should* + V1 | Run lest you **should miss** the bus. |
| **this is the first time** | present perfect | This is the first time I **have flown**. |
| **if** (1st / 2nd / 3rd conditional) | present → will; past → would; past perfect → would have | Chapter 25 |

## 21.16 Common Errors in Tense

::: errs
1. ✘ I **have seen** him yesterday. ✔ I **saw** him yesterday.
2. ✘ He **is knowing** the answer. ✔ He **knows** the answer.
3. ✘ I **am living** here since 2015. ✔ I **have been living** here since 2015.
4. ✘ He **has died** two years ago. ✔ He **died** two years ago.
5. ✘ I will wait until he **will come**. ✔ I will wait until he **comes**.
6. ✘ If it **will rain**, we shall stay in. ✔ If it **rains**, we shall stay in.
7. ✘ The train **left** before we **had reached** the station. ✔ The train **had left** before we **reached** the station.
8. ✘ He said that he **will** come. ✔ He said that he **would** come.
9. ✘ It is ten years since he **has gone**. ✔ It is ten years since he **went**.
10. ✘ He **was** ill **since** Monday. ✔ He **has been** ill since Monday.
11. ✘ I **didn't saw** him. ✔ I **didn't see** him.
12. ✘ He **is working** here for five years. ✔ He **has been working** here for five years.
13. ✘ When **have** you **come**? ✔ When **did** you **come**?
14. ✘ The sun **rose** in the east (general truth). ✔ The sun **rises** in the east.
15. ✘ It is high time we **go** home. ✔ It is high time we **went** home.
16. ✘ He **had gone** to Sylhet last week. ✔ He **went** to Sylhet last week.
17. ✘ No sooner **he reached** the station than the train left. ✔ No sooner **had he reached** …
18. ✘ I wish I **was** a bird. (exam key) ✔ I wish I **were** a bird.
19. ✘ By the time you come, I **shall finish** the work. ✔ … I **shall have finished** the work.
20. ✘ Look! The baby **cries**. ✔ Look! The baby **is crying**.
21. ✘ I **am seeing** a tiger in the zoo now. ✔ I **can see** a tiger in the zoo now.
22. ✘ The teacher said that the earth **moved** round the sun. ✔ … **moves** round the sun.
23. ✘ This is the first time I **visit** Sylhet. ✔ … I **have visited** Sylhet.
24. ✘ I **have been knowing** him for years. ✔ I **have known** him for years.
25. ✘ While I **walked** in the park, it **was beginning** to rain. ✔ While I **was walking** in the park, it **began** to rain.
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Tense Logic:** The GMAT and SAT test **logical sequence**: use the past perfect only when two past actions need ordering (an unnecessary past perfect is also wrong), use *would* (not *will*) for future-in-the-past, and keep tenses **consistent** unless the time actually changes. ✘ "By the time the rescue team arrived, the climbers **were trapped** for three days." ✔ "By the time the rescue team arrived, the climbers **had been trapped** for three days." ✘ "Since 2010, the company **expanded** into five countries." ✔ "Since 2010, the company **has expanded** into five countries." (*since* + present perfect)
:::
