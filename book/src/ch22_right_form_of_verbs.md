## 22.1 What Is "Right Form of Verbs"?

In a *Right Form of Verbs* question, a verb is given in its base form inside brackets and you must write the correct form according to the **tense, subject, voice, mood** and **structure** of the sentence: *He (go) to school yesterday.* → *He* **went** *to school yesterday.*

This item appears in SSC, HSC, university admission, BCS written, bank written and many job tests, and in disguised forms (error detection, fill in the blanks) in MCQ papers. The secret is to recognise the **clue word or structure** that decides the form. Below, the rules are grouped by such clues. Each example shows the verb in brackets and the correct answer in bold.

::: tip
**Five-step method:** (1) Find the subject — singular or plural? (2) Look for time clues (*yesterday, now, since, for, ago, tomorrow, already*). (3) Look for structure clues (*if, wish, as if, it is time, would rather, no sooner, lest, let, make, used to, look forward to*). (4) Decide active or passive (is the subject the doer?). (5) Check whether the verb follows a preposition, a modal or *to*.
:::

## 22.2 Rules Based on Time Clues

**Rule 1 — Present indefinite** with habitual adverbs (*always, usually, often, sometimes, regularly, every day, generally, daily, occasionally, seldom, never*) and universal truths:

::: ex
- He always (speak) the truth. → **speaks**
- The sun (rise) in the east. → **rises**
- She often (visit) her grandmother. → **visits**
- Water (boil) at 100°C. → **boils**
- Every day she (get) up at six. → **gets**
:::

**Rule 2 — Present continuous** with *now, at present, at this moment, look, listen, still*:

::: ex
- Look! The boy (run) after the bus. → **is running**
- Listen! Someone (sing). → **is singing**
- At present he (live) in Khulna. → **is living**
- He (read) now. → **is reading**
:::

**Rule 3 — Present perfect** with *just, already, yet, ever, never, recently, lately, so far, till now, up to now, since, for* (with continuing period), *this week / today*:

::: ex
- He just (finish) the work. → **has just finished**
- I already (see) the film. → **have already seen**
- Have you ever (be) to Sylhet? → **been**
- I never (see) such a beautiful scene. → **have never seen**
- She not (come) yet. → **has not come**
:::

**Rule 4 — Present perfect continuous** with *for / since* + continuing action:

::: ex
- He (wait) for you for an hour. → **has been waiting**
- It (rain) since morning. → **has been raining**
- They (live) here since 2000. → **have been living** / **have lived**
:::

**Rule 5 — Past indefinite** with *yesterday, ago, last (night/week/year), the other day, long ago, once upon a time, in 1971, then*:

::: ex
- He (go) to Dhaka yesterday. → **went**
- I (see) him two days ago. → **saw**
- Bangladesh (become) independent in 1971. → **became**
- Once upon a time there (live) a king. → **lived**
- The other day I (meet) an old friend. → **met**
:::

**Rule 6 — Future indefinite** with *tomorrow, next (day/week/year), soon, in future, coming*:

::: ex
- He (come) tomorrow. → **will come**
- We (go) to Cox's Bazar next week. → **shall go** / **will go**
:::

**Rule 7 — Future perfect** with *by + future time / by the time + present*:

::: ex
- By next month, I (finish) the book. → **shall have finished**
- By the time you arrive, the train (leave). → **will have left**
:::

## 22.3 Rules Based on Two Past Actions

**Rule 8 — *before*:** the action that happened first → past perfect; *before*-clause → past simple.

::: ex
- The patient (die) before the doctor came. → **had died**
- He had left before I (reach). → **reached**
- Before the police came, the thieves (flee). → **had fled**
:::

**Rule 9 — *after*:** *after*-clause → past perfect; main clause → past simple.

::: ex
- After he (finish) the work, he went home. → **had finished**
- After the sun had set, we (return). → **returned**
:::

**Rule 10 — *when / as soon as* with two past actions:** the earlier → past perfect (if sequence matters); interrupted action → past continuous.

::: ex
- When I reached the station, the train (leave) already. → **had already left**
- When he came, I (read) a book. → **was reading**
- I was taking a bath when the phone (ring). → **rang**
- While she (cook), the guests arrived. → **was cooking**
:::

**Rule 11 — *No sooner … than / Hardly … when / Scarcely … when*:** first part → past perfect (with inversion); second → past simple.

::: ex
- No sooner had he reached the station than the train (leave). → **left**
- No sooner (he / arrive) than it began to rain. → **had he arrived**
- Hardly had we started when it (begin) to rain. → **began**
- Scarcely had I gone out when he (come). → **came**
:::

## 22.4 Rules with *Since* and *It is … since*

**Rule 12 — Main clause present perfect; *since*-clause past simple.**

::: ex
- Many years have passed since he (die). → **died**
- It is two years since I (see) him. → **saw**
- I have known him since he (be) a child. → **was**
- Ten years (pass) since we left the village. → **have passed**
:::

## 22.5 Conditional Sentences (*If*)

**Rule 13 — First conditional (real future):** *If* + present simple → *will/shall/can/may* + V1.

::: ex
- If you work hard, you (prosper). → **will prosper**
- If it (rain), we shall not go. → **rains**
- If he comes, I (go). → **shall go**
:::

**Rule 14 — Second conditional (unreal present):** *If* + past simple (*were*) → *would/could/might* + V1.

::: ex
- If I (be) a bird, I would fly. → **were**
- If I were you, I (not do) it. → **would not do**
- If he worked hard, he (succeed). → **would succeed**
- If I (have) wings, I would fly. → **had**
:::

**Rule 15 — Third conditional (unreal past):** *If* + past perfect → *would/could/might have* + V3.

::: ex
- If he had worked hard, he (pass). → **would have passed**
- If you (come) earlier, you would have met him. → **had come**
- Had I known, I (help) you. → **would have helped**
:::

(See Chapter 25 for all conditionals.)

**Rule 16 — *Unless / in case / provided / until / till / as soon as / when / before / after* with future meaning take the present simple, not *will*.**

::: ex
- Wait here until I (return). → **return**
- Unless you (read) attentively, you will fail. → **read**
- I will call you as soon as I (reach). → **reach**
- Take an umbrella in case it (rain). → **rains**
:::

## 22.6 The Subjunctive and Unreal Structures

**Rule 17 — *Wish*:** present unreal → past simple (*were* for *be*); past unreal → past perfect; future wish → *would/could*.

::: ex
- I wish I (be) a king. → **were**
- I wish I (know) his address. → **knew**
- He wishes he (attend) the party yesterday. → **had attended**
- I wish you (stop) smoking. → **would stop**
:::

**Rule 18 — *As if / As though*:** present unreal → past simple / *were*; past unreal → past perfect.

::: ex
- He talks as if he (be) a king. → **were**
- He behaved as if he (know) everything. → **knew**
- She looked as though she (see) a ghost. → **had seen**
:::

**Rule 19 — *It is time / It is high time / It is about time* + subject → past simple; *It is time* + *to* + V1.**

::: ex
- It is high time you (change) your habit. → **changed**
- It is time we (leave). → **left**
- It is time (go) to bed. → **to go**
:::

**Rule 20 — *Would rather / had rather / would sooner* + another subject → past simple.**

::: ex
- I would rather you (go) now. → **went**
- She would rather we (not tell) him. → **didn't tell**
:::

**Rule 21 — Mandative subjunctive** after *suggest, propose, recommend, insist, demand, order, request, urge, require, desire*; and *it is necessary / essential / important / imperative / vital that* → base form (or *should* + V1).

::: ex
- The doctor suggested that he (take) rest. → **take** / **should take**
- It is necessary that every student (be) present. → **be**
- He insisted that she (attend) the meeting. → **attend**
- The committee recommended that the plan (be) accepted. → **be**
:::

**Rule 22 — Optative (prayer/wish) with *May*:**

::: ex
- May Allah (bless) you. → **bless**
- Long (live) Bangladesh! → **live**
- May you (be) happy. → **be**
:::

**Rule 23 — *Lest* → *should* + V1.**

::: ex
- Walk carefully lest you (fall). → **should fall**
- He ran fast lest he (miss) the train. → **should miss**
:::

## 22.7 Rules Based on Modals and Special Verbs

**Rule 24 — After modals (*can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must*), *do/does/did*, *need not, dare not, had better, would rather, let* → bare infinitive (V1).**

::: ex
- He can (swim). → **swim**
- You had better (consult) a doctor. → **consult**
- I would rather (die) than beg. → **die**
- Let him (go). → **go**
- He did not (come) yesterday. → **come**
- You need not (worry). → **worry**
:::

**Rule 25 — *Make, let, have* (causative, active) → object + V1; in passive *made to* + V1.**

::: ex
- He made me (laugh). → **laugh**
- The teacher made the boy (stand) up. → **stand**
- He was made (work) hard. → **to work**
- I had my hair (cut). → **cut** (past participle)
- I got the letter (type). → **typed**
- I got him (sign) the paper. → **to sign**
:::

**Rule 26 — Verbs of perception (*see, hear, watch, notice, feel*) + object → V1 (whole action) or V-ing (action in progress).**

::: ex
- I saw him (cross) the road. → **cross** / **crossing**
- We heard her (sing) a song. → **sing** / **singing**
:::

**Rule 27 — *Used to* + V1 (past habit) vs *be/get used to* + V-ing (accustomed).**

::: ex
- I used to (go) for a walk every morning. → **go**
- He is used to (work) hard. → **working**
- She will soon get used to (live) in the city. → **living**
:::

**Rule 28 — *Ought* → *to* + V1.**

::: ex
- You ought (help) the poor. → **to help**
:::

## 22.8 Rules Based on Infinitive and Gerund

**Rule 29 — Preposition + V-ing (gerund).** Watch especially: *look forward to, with a view to, be used to, be accustomed to, object to, be devoted to, take to, in addition to, confess to.*

::: ex
- He is fond of (swim). → **swimming**
- I am looking forward to (see) you. → **seeing**
- He went to Dhaka with a view to (get) a job. → **getting**
- She is accustomed to (rise) early. → **rising**
- He left without (say) goodbye. → **saying**
- Instead of (study), he went to the cinema. → **studying**
:::

**Rule 30 — Verbs + gerund: *avoid, enjoy, mind, finish, stop, deny, admit, suggest, consider, practise, keep, miss, risk, postpone, can't help, it's no use, it's worth, can't stand*.**

::: ex
- He avoided (meet) me. → **meeting**
- Would you mind (open) the door? → **opening**
- I can't help (laugh). → **laughing**
- It is no use (cry) over spilt milk. → **crying**
- The book is worth (read). → **reading**
- He has given up (smoke). → **smoking**
:::

**Rule 31 — Verbs + to-infinitive: *want, decide, hope, agree, refuse, promise, plan, expect, wish, afford, fail, manage, pretend, seem, learn*.**

::: ex
- He wants (go) abroad. → **to go**
- They decided (start) a business. → **to start**
- He refused (pay) the bill. → **to pay**
:::

**Rule 32 — Purpose → to-infinitive.**

::: ex
- He went to the market (buy) fish. → **to buy**
- We eat (live). → **to live**
:::

**Rule 33 — *Too … to* / *enough … to* → to-infinitive.**

::: ex
- He is too weak (walk). → **to walk**
- She is old enough (vote). → **to vote**
:::

## 22.9 Participle Rules

**Rule 34 — Perfect participle for an action completed before the main action.**

::: ex
- (Finish) his work, he went out. → **Having finished**
- (Lose) all his money, he returned home. → **Having lost**
:::

**Rule 35 — Absolute construction.**

::: ex
- The sun (set), we returned home. → **having set**
- The weather (be) fine, we went out. → **being**
- God (will), we shall meet again. → **willing**
:::

**Rule 36 — Present participle (active) vs past participle (passive) as adjectives.**

::: ex
- A (rolling) stone gathers no moss. → **rolling**
- I saw a (break) chair. → **broken**
- The news was (shock). → **shocking**
- We were (shock) at the news. → **shocked**
- The (wound) soldier was taken to hospital. → **wounded**
:::

## 22.10 Passive Voice Rules

**Rule 37 — When the subject receives the action → *be* + V3 (passive).**

::: ex
- The letter (write) by him yesterday. → **was written**
- English (speak) all over the world. → **is spoken**
- The bridge (build) now. → **is being built**
- The work (finish) already. → **has already been finished**
- The thief (catch) yet? → **Has the thief been caught yet?**
- A new road (construct) next year. → **will be constructed**
- He was seen (go) out. → **to go**
:::

**Rule 38 — *Let* passive: *Let* + object + *be* + V3.**

::: ex
- Let the door (shut). → **be shut**
- Let it (do) at once. → **be done**
:::

**Rule 39 — *Need* + V-ing = passive sense.** *The house needs (paint).* → **painting** (= to be painted)

## 22.11 Subject-Verb Agreement Rules (Summary of Chapter 23)

**Rule 40 — Singular subject → singular verb; plural → plural.** Watch for words between subject and verb, *each / every / everyone / neither / either*, *one of*, *the number of / a number of*, collective nouns, *as well as / with / along with*, *either…or / neither…nor*, uncountable nouns, *many a*, *the + adjective*.

::: ex
- Each of the boys (have) a pen. → **has**
- Every man and woman (be) responsible. → **is**
- One of my friends (live) in London. → **lives**
- The number of students (be) increasing. → **is**
- A number of students (be) absent. → **were**
- Rahim as well as his friends (be) present. → **was**
- Neither he nor I (be) guilty. → **am**
- Either you or he (have) done it. → **has**
- Many a man (have) died in the war. → **has**
- The rich (be) not always happy. → **are**
- The news (be) good. → **is**
- Bread and butter (be) my favourite breakfast. → **is**
- Slow and steady (win) the race. → **wins**
- Ten kilometres (be) a long distance. → **is**
- Physics (be) my favourite subject. → **is**
- The police (have) arrested the thief. → **have**
- The jury (be) divided in their opinion. → **were**
- Each student and each teacher (be) present. → **was**
- Nobody (know) the answer. → **knows**
- Who (do) not know Rabindranath? → **does**
:::

## 22.12 Other Important Rules

**Rule 41 — Two verbs joined by *and / or / but* take the same form.**

::: ex
- He came and (sit) beside me. → **sat**
- She sings and (dance) well. → **dances**
:::

**Rule 42 — *Sequence of tenses*: past main verb → past in subordinate clause (except universal truths).**

::: ex
- He said that he (will) come. → **would**
- I found that he (be) absent. → **was**
- The teacher said that the earth (be) round. → **is**
:::

**Rule 43 — Relative pronoun agrees with its antecedent.**

::: ex
- I who (be) your friend will help you. → **am**
- He is one of the best players who (have) played for the country. → **have**
- He is the only one of the boys who (have) passed. → **has**
:::

**Rule 44 — *Would that / O that / If only* (wish) → past / past perfect.**

::: ex
- Would that I (be) young again! → **were**
- If only I (know) the truth earlier! → **had known**
:::

**Rule 45 — *Be about to / be on the point of*.**

::: ex
- The train is about (leave). → **to leave**
- He was on the point of (die). → **dying**
:::

**Rule 46 — *Mind* / *Would you mind* + V-ing; *Would you like* + to-infinitive.**

::: ex
- Would you mind (shut) the window? → **shutting**
- Would you like (have) some tea? → **to have**
:::

**Rule 47 — Narration in the past: imperative → *to* + V1.**

::: ex
- He told me (go) at once. → **to go**
- The teacher ordered the students (not make) a noise. → **not to make**
:::

**Rule 48 — *Bear*: *born* (birth, passive) / *borne* (carry, active perfect).**

::: ex
- He was (bear) in 1971. → **born**
- She has (bear) three children. → **borne**
- The expenses will be (bear) by the company. → **borne**
:::

**Rule 49 — *Hang*: *hanged* (execution) / *hung* (things).**

::: ex
- The murderer was (hang) yesterday. → **hanged**
- The picture was (hang) on the wall. → **hung**
:::

**Rule 50 — *Lie / lay*:**

::: ex
- He (lie) on the bed all day yesterday. → **lay**
- The hen has (lay) an egg. → **laid**
- He (lie) to me yesterday (told a lie). → **lied**
:::

## 22.13 Mixed Practice with Explanations

::: ex
- He (be) ill for a week. → **has been** (*for* + continuing state)
- I (know) him since 2010. → **have known** (*since* + stative verb)
- She (cook) when I reached home. → **was cooking** (interrupted past action)
- If I (be) rich, I would help the poor. → **were** (2nd conditional)
- He looks as if he (be) sick. → **were** (*as if* unreal)
- The children (play) in the field since morning. → **have been playing**
- I wish I (can) fly. → **could**
- No sooner (we / reach) the stadium than the match started. → **had we reached**
- It is ten years since my father (die). → **died**
- The Padma Bridge (open) in June 2022. → **was opened**
- Two hours (be) a long time to wait. → **is** (a period of time treated as one unit)
- Let us (go) home. → **go**
- He has been (read) since morning. → **reading**
- The poor (need) help. → **need**
- She avoided (answer) my question. → **answering**
- He was made (sign) the paper. → **to sign**
- Unless he (come), we shall start. → **comes**
- The boy (sit) under the tree is my brother. → **sitting**
- (Eat) too much is harmful. → **Eating**
- Neither the teacher nor the students (be) present. → **were**
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Choosing the Verb Form:** The SAT and GMAT never print a verb in brackets, but most of their verb questions are "right form of verb" questions in disguise. Each answer choice tests one clue: a time marker (*since, by the time, ago*), a structure (*if, wish, require that, so … that*), agreement with a distant subject, or a non-finite form that cannot be a main verb. Apply the five-step method above to every underlined verb and most wrong choices fall away at once.
:::
