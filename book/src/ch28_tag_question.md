## 28.1 What Is a Tag Question?

A **tag question** (question tag) is a short question added at the **end of a statement** to ask for **confirmation** or **agreement**. It is very common in spoken English.

::: ex
- You are a student, **aren't you?**
- He doesn't smoke, **does he?**
- They have finished, **haven't they?**
:::

The statement is called the **main clause** and the short question is the **tag**.

## 28.2 The Basic Rules

::: rule
1. **Positive statement → negative tag; negative statement → positive tag.**
   *He is rich,* **isn't he?** / *He isn't rich,* **is he?**
2. **The tag uses the same auxiliary** (or modal) as the statement. If there is no auxiliary, use **do / does / did**.
   *She can swim,* **can't she?** / *He works hard,* **doesn't he?** / *They went home,* **didn't they?**
3. **The subject of the tag is always a pronoun** referring to the subject of the statement.
   *Rahim is ill,* **isn't he?** (✘ isn't Rahim)
4. **Negative tags are contracted** (*isn't, aren't, doesn't, can't, won't*). Uncontracted (*is he not?*) is formal and rare.
5. **A comma** separates the statement from the tag, and the sentence ends with a **question mark**.
:::

## 28.4 Special Cases

### 28.4.1 *I am* → *aren't I?*

::: ex
- I am right, **aren't I?** (✘ amn't I? ✘ am not I?)
- I am not late, **am I?**
:::

### 28.4.2 Imperatives

| Imperative | Tag |
|---|---|
| Open the door, | **will you?** / **won't you?** / **would you?** / **can you?** / **could you?** |
| Don't make a noise, | **will you?** (negative imperative → *will you?* only) |
| Please help me, | **will you?** |
| Sit down, | **won't you?** (invitation) |
| Be quiet, | **will you?** |

### 28.4.3 *Let's* and *Let me / him*

::: ex
- **Let's** go for a walk, **shall we?** (suggestion including the speaker)
- **Let us** go (= allow us to go), **will you?** (request to the listener)
- **Let me** help you, **will you?** / **shall I?**
- **Let him** do it, **will you?**
:::

### 28.4.4 Negative and Restrictive Words in the Statement

Words like *never, no, nothing, nobody, none, nowhere, hardly, scarcely, barely, seldom, rarely, few, little* make the statement **negative**, so the tag is **positive**.

::: ex
- He **never** tells a lie, **does he?**
- She **hardly** eats anything, **does she?**
- **Nobody** came, **did they?**
- **Nothing** happened, **did it?**
- There is **no** water, **is there?**
- **Few** people know him, **do they?**
- He **seldom** comes here, **does he?**
- **Little** is known about him, **is it?**
:::

::: note
*A few* and *a little* are positive: *A few people came,* **didn't they?** / *There is a little milk,* **isn't there?** Words with negative prefixes (*unhappy, impossible, dislike, careless*) do **not** make the sentence negative: *He is unhappy,* **isn't he?** / *She dislikes tea,* **doesn't she?**
:::

### 28.4.5 Indefinite Pronouns as Subjects

| Subject | Tag pronoun |
|---|---|
| everybody, everyone, somebody, someone, anybody, nobody, no one | **they** |
| everything, something, anything, nothing | **it** |
| this, that | **it** |
| these, those | **they** |
| one | **one** (formal) / **you** |

::: ex
- **Everyone** is present, **aren't they?**
- **Somebody** called me, **didn't they?**
- **No one** knows the answer, **do they?**
- **Everything** is ready, **isn't it?**
- **Nothing** is impossible, **is it?**
- **This** is your book, **isn't it?**
- **Those** are your shoes, **aren't they?**
- **One** should be honest, **shouldn't one?**
:::

### 28.4.6 *There is / There are*

The tag uses **there**: *There is a problem,* **isn't there?** / *There are many books,* **aren't there?** / *There was no one,* **was there?**

### 28.4.7 Complex Sentences

The tag agrees with the **main clause**, not with the subordinate clause:

::: ex
- I know **that he is honest**, **don't I?** (main clause: *I know*)
- He said that he would come, **didn't he?**
- If you study, you will pass, **won't you?**
:::

But with *I think / I believe / I suppose / I'm sure* + clause, the tag usually agrees with the **subordinate clause**:

::: ex
- **I think** he is right, **isn't he?**
- **I don't think** she will come, **will she?** (the negation belongs to the idea)
- **I suppose** you are tired, **aren't you?**
:::

### 28.4.8 Compound Sentences

The tag follows the **last clause**: *He is rich but he is not happy,* **is he?**

### 28.4.9 *Must* (Deduction)

When *must* expresses **deduction**, the tag reflects the meaning, not *must*:

::: ex
- He **must** be tired, **isn't he?** (= I'm sure he is tired)
- You **must** have seen him, **haven't you?**
- You **must** come, **mustn't you?** (obligation)
:::

### 28.4.10 Same-Polarity Tags (Positive + Positive)

Used to show **surprise, interest, or sarcasm** (not to ask for confirmation):

::: ex
- So you are getting married, **are you?** (surprise)
- Oh, you've finished already, **have you?** (interest)
:::

### 28.4.11 Exclamations

*What a beautiful day it is,* **isn't it?** / *How clever she is,* **isn't she?**

## 28.5 Intonation and Meaning

- **Falling intonation** on the tag: the speaker is sure and expects agreement — *It's cold today, ↘isn't it?*
- **Rising intonation** on the tag: the speaker is not sure and is really asking — *You've met him before, ↗haven't you?*

## 28.6 Answering Tag Questions

Answer according to the **facts**, not according to the form of the question:

::: ex
- He isn't coming, is he? — **No, he isn't.** (he is not coming) / **Yes, he is.** (he is coming)
- You don't smoke, do you? — **No, I don't.** / **Yes, I do.**
:::

## 28.7 Common Errors in Tag Questions

::: errs
1. ✘ He is a doctor, **is not he?** ✔ He is a doctor, **isn't he?**
2. ✘ Rahim is ill, **isn't Rahim?** ✔ Rahim is ill, **isn't he?**
3. ✘ I am right, **amn't I?** ✔ I am right, **aren't I?**
4. ✘ He plays football, **isn't he?** ✔ He plays football, **doesn't he?**
5. ✘ She never tells a lie, **doesn't she?** ✔ She never tells a lie, **does she?**
6. ✘ Let's go, **will we?** ✔ Let's go, **shall we?**
7. ✘ Don't do it, **won't you?** ✔ Don't do it, **will you?**
8. ✘ Everybody came, **didn't he?** ✔ Everybody came, **didn't they?**
9. ✘ Nothing happened, **didn't it?** ✔ Nothing happened, **did it?**
10. ✘ There is a mosque near here, **isn't it?** ✔ … **isn't there?**
11. ✘ He hardly works, **doesn't he?** ✔ He hardly works, **does he?**
12. ✘ He used to smoke, **usedn't he?** (archaic) ✔ He used to smoke, **didn't he?**
13. ✘ She has finished, **doesn't she?** ✔ She has finished, **hasn't she?**
14. ✘ You had better go, **didn't you?** ✔ You had better go, **hadn't you?**
15. ✘ This is your pen, **isn't this?** ✔ This is your pen, **isn't it?**
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner:** Tag questions do not appear in the SAT, GRE or GMAT, which test formal written English. But the skill behind them — identifying the **true main clause and its auxiliary** — is exactly what you need for subject-verb agreement and verb-form questions in long, complex sentences.
:::
