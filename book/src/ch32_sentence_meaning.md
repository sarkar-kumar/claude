# Sentence & Its Classification (By Meaning) {#ch32}

[Part A · Grammar]{.chpart}
:::

## 32.1 What Is a Sentence?

A **sentence** is a group of words that expresses a **complete sense** (thought). It begins with a **capital letter** and ends with a **full stop (.)**, a **question mark (?)** or an **exclamation mark (!)**.

::: ex
- Rahim reads in class ten. (complete sense — sentence)
- in class ten (no complete sense — not a sentence; a phrase)
- When Rahim reads (no complete sense — a clause / fragment)
:::

### 32.1.1 Essentials of a Sentence

1. **Completeness of sense** — it expresses a complete idea.
2. **Word order** — the words are arranged in a correct grammatical order (*He goes to school*, not *School to goes he*).
3. **A subject and a finite predicate** — at least one subject and one finite verb (the subject may be understood, as in imperatives: *(You) Sit down.*).
4. **Expectancy and appropriateness** — the words must fit each other in meaning (✘ *The stone ate rice* — grammatical but meaningless).

### 32.1.2 Subject and Predicate

Every sentence has two parts:

- **Subject** — the person or thing about which something is said.
- **Predicate** — what is said about the subject (the verb + everything that goes with it).

| Subject | Predicate |
|---|---|
| The sun | rises in the east. |
| My elder brother | works in a bank in Dhaka. |
| To err | is human. |
| What he said | is true. |
| (You) | Shut the door. |

The **simple subject** is the main noun/pronoun (*brother*); the **complete subject** includes its modifiers (*My elder brother*). The **simple predicate** is the finite verb (*works*); the **complete predicate** includes objects, complements and modifiers.

## 32.2 Classification by Meaning (Function)

According to meaning or purpose, sentences are of **five** kinds:

| Kind | Purpose | End mark | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Assertive (Declarative)** | makes a statement — affirmative or negative | . | The earth is round. / He does not smoke. |
| **Interrogative** | asks a question | ? | Where do you live? |
| **Imperative** | gives a command, request, advice, suggestion, prohibition | . or ! | Shut the door. / Please help me. |
| **Optative** | expresses a wish, prayer or curse | ! | May you live long! |
| **Exclamatory** | expresses strong or sudden feeling | ! | What a beautiful flower it is! |

## 32.3 Assertive Sentences

An **assertive sentence** makes a statement of fact, opinion or belief. It may be **affirmative** (positive) or **negative**.

::: ex
- Dhaka is the capital of Bangladesh. (affirmative)
- I like mangoes. (affirmative)
- He does not tell lies. (negative)
- Nobody came to the party. (negative in meaning)
- He never smokes. (negative)
:::

**Structure:** Subject + Verb + (Object / Complement / Adverbial). Ends with a full stop.

## 32.4 Interrogative Sentences

An **interrogative sentence** asks a question. There are three main types:

1. **Yes/No questions** — begin with an auxiliary verb; the answer is *yes* or *no*:
   **Are** *you a student?* **Do** *you like tea?* **Can** *he swim?* **Have** *you finished?*
2. **Wh-questions** — begin with a question word (*who, whom, whose, what, which, when, where, why, how, how much, how many, how long*):
   **Where** *do you live?* **Why** *are you late?* **Who** *broke the glass?*
3. **Alternative questions** — offer a choice: *Will you have tea* **or** *coffee?*

Also: **tag questions** (*You are ready, aren't you?* — Chapter 28) and **rhetorical questions** (questions that do not expect an answer, used for emphasis: *Who does not love his country?* = Everybody loves his country.)

::: rule
**Word order in questions:** auxiliary/modal **before** the subject: *Is he…? Does she…? Can they…?* If there is no auxiliary in the statement, use *do/does/did*: *He works hard* → *Does he work hard?* No inversion when the question word is the subject: *Who came?* (✘ Who did come?)
:::

## 32.5 Imperative Sentences

An **imperative sentence** expresses a **command, request, advice, suggestion, instruction, invitation or prohibition**. The subject *you* is usually **understood**.

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Command / order | Stand up. / Get out of the room. |
| Request | Please lend me your pen. / Kindly help me. |
| Advice | Work hard. / Never tell a lie. |
| Prohibition (negative command) | Don't smoke here. / Do not touch the wire. |
| Suggestion / proposal | Let's go for a walk. / Let us sing. |
| Invitation | Come and have dinner with us. |
| Instruction | Turn left at the crossing. / Mix the flour with water. |
| Emphatic | **Do** come tomorrow. / **Do** be quiet. |
| With subject (for emphasis) | **You** sit here, and **you** sit there. |

**Structure:** V1 + (object/complement) — *Shut the door.* Negative: *Do not / Don't / Never* + V1. Suggestion: *Let us / Let's* + V1.

## 32.6 Optative Sentences

An **optative sentence** expresses a **wish, prayer, blessing or curse**. Most begin with **May**; some with *Long live, God bless, Would that, O that, If only*.

::: ex
- **May** Allah bless you!
- **May** you live long!
- **May** our country prosper!
- **Long live** Bangladesh!
- **God save** the King!
- **Would that** I were a bird!
- **May** you go to hell! (curse)
- **Wish** you a happy birthday! (informal)
:::

**Structure:** *May* + subject + V1 (base form) + … ! — ✘ *May you are happy!* ✔ *May you* **be** *happy!*

## 32.7 Exclamatory Sentences

An **exclamatory sentence** expresses **sudden or strong feeling** — joy, sorrow, surprise, wonder, anger, admiration. It usually begins with **What** or **How**, or with an **interjection**.

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **What + a/an + adjective + noun + subject + verb!** | **What a** beautiful bird it is! |
| **What + adjective + plural/uncountable noun + subject + verb!** | **What** lovely flowers these are! / **What** fine weather it is! |
| **How + adjective/adverb + subject + verb!** | **How** beautiful the bird is! / **How** sweetly she sings! |
| **Interjection + sentence** | **Alas!** He is dead. / **Hurrah!** We have won. |
| **Exclamatory wish** | **Had I** the wings of a dove! / **Oh, that** I were young again! |

::: rule
**Word order in exclamations is subject + verb (not question order):** ✘ *What a beautiful bird is it!* ✔ *What a beautiful bird* **it is**! ✘ *How beautiful is the bird!* (literary) — standard: *How beautiful* **the bird is**!
:::

## 32.8 How to Identify the Kind of Sentence

| If the sentence… | It is… |
|---|---|
| states something and ends with a full stop | Assertive |
| begins with an auxiliary / question word and ends with ? | Interrogative |
| begins with a base-form verb, *Please, Don't, Let, Never* | Imperative |
| begins with *May / Long live / God bless / Would that* (wish/prayer) | Optative |
| begins with *What / How* + adjective + subject + verb, or with an interjection, and ends with ! | Exclamatory |

::: ex
- *Never tell a lie.* → Imperative (advice).
- *How fast he runs!* → Exclamatory.
- *How fast does he run?* → Interrogative.
- *May you be successful!* → Optative.
- *Nobody likes a liar.* → Assertive (negative in sense).
- *Who can bear such an insult?* → Interrogative (rhetorical).
- *Let him go.* → Imperative.
- *What a fool he is!* → Exclamatory.
- *What is your name?* → Interrogative.
:::

## 32.9 Sentence Fragments and Run-on Sentences

### 32.9.1 Fragments

A **fragment** is a group of words punctuated as a sentence but lacking a subject, a finite verb or complete sense.

::: ex
- ✘ **Because he was ill.** (subordinate clause alone) ✔ He stayed home because he was ill.
- ✘ **Running to catch the bus.** (no finite verb) ✔ He was running to catch the bus.
- ✘ **The man who lives next door.** (noun + relative clause, no main verb) ✔ The man who lives next door is a doctor.
:::

### 32.9.2 Run-ons and Comma Splices

A **run-on (fused) sentence** joins two independent clauses with no punctuation; a **comma splice** joins them with only a comma.

::: ex
- ✘ It was raining we stayed inside. (run-on)
- ✘ It was raining, we stayed inside. (comma splice)
- ✔ It was raining**;** we stayed inside. / It was raining**,** **so** we stayed inside. / **As** it was raining, we stayed inside. / It was raining**.** We stayed inside.
:::

## 32.10 Changing One Kind into Another (Introduction)

Sentences can be transformed from one kind into another without changing the meaning — assertive ↔ interrogative, assertive ↔ exclamatory, imperative ↔ assertive, optative ↔ assertive. These transformations are treated fully in **Chapter 34**. A few examples:

::: ex
- Everybody loves his country. (assertive) → Who does not love his country? (interrogative)
- It is a very beautiful bird. (assertive) → What a beautiful bird it is! (exclamatory)
- Shut the door. (imperative) → You are ordered to shut the door. (assertive)
- May you be happy! (optative) → I wish that you may be happy. (assertive)
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Complete Sentences:** The SAT Writing section regularly tests **fragments** and **comma splices**. A correct sentence must contain at least one **independent clause** (subject + finite verb + complete sense). Watch for long sentences whose "verb" is actually a participle: ✘ *"The ancient city of Mahasthangarh, located in Bogura and dating back to the third century BCE."* — no main verb. ✔ *"The ancient city of Mahasthangarh, located in Bogura,* **dates** *back to the third century BCE."*
:::
