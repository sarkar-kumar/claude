## 14.1 Non-finite Verbs — An Overview

**Non-finite verbs** (also called *verbals*) are verb forms that are **not limited by the subject or tense**. They cannot be the main verb of a sentence by themselves, but they keep some verb qualities (they can take objects and be modified by adverbs) while doing the work of a **noun, adjective or adverb**.

| Non-finite | Form | Works as | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Infinitive** | (to) + V1 | noun, adjective, adverb | I want **to learn**. |
| **Gerund** | V + ing | noun | **Swimming** is good exercise. |
| **Present participle** | V + ing | adjective (and in continuous tenses) | a **running** train |
| **Past participle** | V3 | adjective (and in perfect tenses/passive) | a **broken** glass |
| **Perfect participle** | having + V3 | adverbial (time/reason) | **Having finished** the work, he left. |

Because the gerund and the present participle look the same (*-ing*), the main skill tested is **telling them apart by function**.

## 14.2 The Infinitive

### 14.2.1 Forms

| | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Simple | to write | to be written |
| Continuous | to be writing | — |
| Perfect | to have written | to have been written |
| Perfect continuous | to have been writing | — |

### 14.2.2 Uses of the *to*-Infinitive

**(a) As a noun (noun infinitive):**

::: ex
- **To err** is human, **to forgive** divine. (subject)
- It is difficult **to please everyone**. (*it* = introductory subject; the infinitive is the real subject)
- He wants **to go** abroad. (object)
- Her aim is **to become** a doctor. (complement)
- He has no choice but **to resign**. (object of a preposition — rare)
- I don't know **what to do / how to begin / where to go**. (*wh-word* + infinitive)
:::

**(b) As an adjective (qualifying a noun — gerundial/qualifying infinitive):**

::: ex
- I have no money **to spend**. (= money that I can spend)
- Give me a chair **to sit on**.
- He was the first **to arrive** / the last **to leave**.
- This is a house **to let**. (to be let — for rent)
- She has a lot of work **to do**.
:::

**(c) As an adverb (purpose, cause, result, degree):**

::: ex
- He went to the market **to buy** vegetables. (purpose)
- I am glad **to see** you. (cause — qualifying an adjective)
- He is too weak **to walk**. (degree/result)
- She is old enough **to vote**.
- He worked hard **only to fail**. (unexpected result)
- **To tell the truth**, I forgot it. (sentence adverbial — independent infinitive)
:::

### 14.2.3 The Bare Infinitive (without *to*)

The infinitive is used **without *to*** after:

1. **Modal auxiliaries**: *can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must* — *He can* **swim**.
2. **need, dare** (as modals): *You need not* **go**. *He dare not* **speak**.
3. **Verbs of perception** (active): *see, hear, watch, feel, notice, observe, behold, perceive* — *I saw him* **go** *out.* (In the passive *to* returns: *He was seen* **to go** *out.*)
4. **make, let, have, bid** (active): *He made me* **cry**. *Let him* **go**. *I had him* **repair** *it.* (Passive: *I was made* **to cry**.)
5. **had better, would rather, would sooner, rather than, sooner than**: *You had better* **go**. *I would rather* **die** *than* **beg**.
6. **but / except** after *do + anything/nothing/everything*: *He did nothing but* **cry**. *She could do nothing except* **wait**.
7. **why / why not** (questions/suggestions): *Why* **wait**? *Why not* **try** *again?*
8. **help** (optional *to*): *Help me* **(to) carry** *this.*
9. **than** in comparisons: *It is better to die* **than** **(to) live** *in disgrace.*

::: ex
- ✘ He made me **to laugh**. ✔ He made me **laugh**.
- ✘ Let him **to** go. ✔ Let him **go**.
- ✘ You had better **to** consult a doctor. ✔ You had better **consult** a doctor.
- ✘ I heard her **to sing**. ✔ I heard her **sing**.
- ✔ She was heard **to sing**. (passive — *to* is used)
:::

### 14.2.4 Split Infinitive

A **split infinitive** places an adverb between *to* and the verb: *to* **boldly** *go.* Traditional grammar (and many exam keys) considers this wrong; modern usage allows it when it sounds natural.

::: ex
- ✘ (traditional) I want **to clearly understand** the problem. ✔ I want **to understand** the problem **clearly**.
- ✘ He decided **to immediately leave**. ✔ He decided **to leave immediately**.
:::

### 14.2.5 Infinitive with *too … to* and *so … that*

::: ex
- He is **too** weak **to walk**. = He is **so** weak **that** he **cannot** walk.
- The box is **too** heavy **for me to lift**. = The box is **so** heavy **that** I cannot lift **it**.
- ✘ The box is too heavy for me to lift **it**. ✔ The box is too heavy for me to lift. (no object pronoun in the *too … to* structure)
:::

## 14.3 The Gerund

A **gerund** is the *-ing* form of a verb used as a **noun**. It names an action: *swimming, reading, smoking.* Because it is a verb-noun, it can take an object and be modified by an adverb, yet it does every job a noun does.

### 14.3.1 Functions of the Gerund

| Function | Example |
|---|---|
| Subject of a verb | **Walking** is good exercise. / **Smoking** is injurious to health. |
| Object of a verb | I like **reading** novels. / He stopped **smoking**. |
| Object of a preposition | He is fond of **singing**. / She is good at **painting**. / Thank you for **coming**. |
| Complement | His hobby is **collecting** stamps. / Seeing is **believing**. |
| In apposition | His favourite pastime, **gardening**, keeps him busy. |
| In compound nouns (gerund + noun = purpose) | a **swimming** pool (a pool for swimming), a **walking** stick, a **reading** room, a **frying** pan, a **dining** table, **drinking** water, a **sewing** machine, a **driving** licence, a **sleeping** bag |

### 14.3.2 Gerund after Prepositions

A verb that comes immediately after a preposition must be in the **gerund** form.

::: ex
- He is fond **of swimming**. (✘ fond of swim / fond to swim)
- She is interested **in learning** French.
- I am tired **of waiting**.
- He left **without saying** goodbye.
- **Instead of going** home, he went to the cinema.
- **Before going** to bed, brush your teeth.
- He insisted **on paying** the bill.
- She succeeded **in passing** the exam.
- I am looking forward **to meeting** you. (*to* = preposition)
- He objected **to being** called a liar.
:::

### 14.3.3 Possessive before the Gerund

In formal English, a noun or pronoun before a gerund takes the **possessive** form (because the gerund is a noun).

::: ex
- I don't like **his** coming late. (✘ him coming late — informal)
- Do you mind **my** smoking here?
- I was surprised at **Rahim's** failing the exam.
- We insisted on **their** staying for dinner.
:::

### 14.3.4 Verbs Followed Only by the Gerund

*admit, anticipate, appreciate, avoid, consider, defer, delay, deny, detest, dislike, enjoy, escape, excuse, fancy, finish, forgive, give up, imagine, involve, keep (on), mention, mind, miss, pardon, postpone, practise, prevent, quit, recall, recollect, recommend, report, resent, resist, risk, stop (= cease), suggest, tolerate, understand*

**Phrases followed by the gerund:** *can't help, can't stand, can't bear (also inf.), it's no use, it's no good, it's worth, there's no point in, be busy, feel like, look forward to, be used to, get used to, object to, with a view to, be accustomed to, be averse to, be addicted to, confess to, be committed to, take to, devote oneself to, in addition to.*

::: ex
- ✘ I enjoy **to read**. ✔ I enjoy **reading**.
- ✘ He avoided **to meet** me. ✔ He avoided **meeting** me.
- ✘ Would you mind **to open** the window? ✔ Would you mind **opening** the window?
- ✘ I can't help **to laugh**. ✔ I can't help **laughing**.
- ✘ It's no use **to cry** over spilt milk. ✔ It's no use **crying** over spilt milk.
- ✘ This book is worth **to read**. ✔ This book is worth **reading**.
- ✘ He suggested **to go** for a walk. ✔ He suggested **going** for a walk.
- ✘ I look forward to **hear** from you. ✔ I look forward to **hearing** from you.
- ✘ He came here with a view to **see** me. ✔ He came here with a view to **seeing** me.
- ✘ He is addicted to **drink**. ✔ He is addicted to **drinking** (or to *drink* the noun = alcohol ✔).
:::

### 14.3.5 Verbs Followed Only by the Infinitive

*afford, agree, aim, appear, arrange, ask, attempt, beg, care, choose, claim, consent, decide, demand, deserve, determine, endeavour, expect, fail, hesitate, hope, intend, learn, long, manage, neglect, offer, plan, prepare, pretend, proceed, promise, prove, refuse, resolve, seek, seem, strive, swear, tend, threaten, undertake, venture, volunteer, vow, want, wish, would like.*

::: ex
- He **decided to** resign. / They **agreed to** help. / She **refused to** sign. / I **hope to** see you soon. / He **pretended to** be ill. / We **can't afford to** buy a car.
:::

### 14.3.6 Verbs Followed by Either (Change in Meaning)

| Verb | + Gerund | + Infinitive |
|---|---|---|
| **remember** | memory of a past action: *I remember* **posting** *the letter.* (I posted it and I recall it.) | duty, not to forget (future): *Remember* **to post** *the letter.* |
| **forget** | forget a past experience: *I'll never forget* **visiting** *the Taj Mahal.* | fail to do: *I forgot* **to lock** *the door.* |
| **stop** | quit an activity: *He stopped* **smoking**. | stop in order to do something else: *He stopped* **to smoke**. |
| **try** | experiment, test: *Try* **adding** *some salt.* | make an effort: *I tried* **to lift** *the box but couldn't.* |
| **regret** | be sorry about the past: *I regret* **telling** *him the secret.* | be sorry to announce: *I regret* **to inform** *you that you have failed.* |
| **mean** | involve: *Missing the bus means* **walking** *home.* | intend: *I meant* **to call** *you.* |
| **go on** | continue the same activity: *He went on* **talking**. | move on to a new activity: *After the speech, he went on* **to answer** *questions.* |
| **need** | passive meaning: *The car needs* **washing**. (= needs to be washed) | active: *I need* **to wash** *the car.* |
| **like** | enjoy (habit): *I like* **swimming**. | choose/think it right: *I like* **to see** *the dentist twice a year.* |
| **be afraid of / be afraid to** | fear something may happen: *I'm afraid of* **falling**. | be unwilling to do because of fear: *I'm afraid* **to jump**. |
| **used to / be used to** | *be used to* **working** (accustomed) | *used to* **work** (past habit) |

**Little or no change in meaning:** *begin, start, continue, love, hate, prefer, intend, propose, can't bear, attempt* — *It began* **raining / to rain**.

## 14.4 The Participle

A **participle** is a verb form that works partly as a **verb** and partly as an **adjective**. It is a "verbal adjective".

### 14.4.1 Kinds of Participles

| Kind | Form | Active/Passive | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Present participle** | V + ing | active meaning, ongoing | a **sleeping** child, a **rolling** stone |
| **Past participle** | V3 | passive or completed meaning | a **broken** chair, **fallen** leaves, a **lost** child |
| **Perfect participle (active)** | having + V3 | action completed before another | **Having finished** his work, he went out. |
| **Perfect participle (passive)** | having been + V3 | completed passive action | **Having been warned** twice, he was dismissed. |

### 14.4.2 Uses of Participles

**(a) As an adjective before a noun (attributive):** *a* **burning** *house, a* **running** *train, a* **smiling** *face, the* **rising** *sun, a* **written** *test, a* **broken** *promise, a* **wounded** *soldier, a* **retired** *officer.*

**(b) As a predicative adjective (after a linking verb):** *The news is* **shocking**. *He seemed* **worried**. *The baby kept* **crying**.

**(c) As part of a participial phrase qualifying a noun (reduced relative clause):**

::: ex
- The man **standing at the gate** is my uncle. (= who is standing)
- I saw a boy **crying for help**.
- The book **written by Tagore** is famous. (= which was written)
- Goods **made in Bangladesh** are sold abroad.
:::

**(d) In continuous tenses and the passive (with *be/have*):** *He is* **reading**. *The letter was* **written**. *He has* **gone**.

**(e) Adverbial participle phrases (time, reason, condition, manner, concession):**

::: ex
- **Walking along the road**, I met an old friend. (time: when I was walking)
- **Being ill**, he could not attend the class. (reason: as he was ill)
- **Having lost** all his money, he returned home. (time/reason: after he had lost)
- **Turning to the left**, you will find the post office. (condition: if you turn)
- He came **running**. (manner)
- **Admitting** what you say, I still cannot agree. (concession)
:::

::: rule
**The subject of the participle must be the subject of the main clause.** Otherwise the participle "dangles" (has nothing to attach to) and makes the sentence wrong — often absurd.

✘ **Walking along the road**, *a snake bit him.* (The snake was not walking!)
✔ **Walking along the road**, *he was bitten by a snake.* / *While he was walking along the road, a snake bit him.*

See Chapter 29 (Dangling Modifier).
:::

### 14.4.3 The Absolute Construction (Nominative Absolute)

When a participle has **its own subject** (different from the subject of the main clause), the phrase is called an **absolute phrase** (or *nominative absolute*). It is grammatically independent and usually shows time, reason or condition.

::: ex
- **The sun having set**, we returned home. (= When the sun had set)
- **The weather being fine**, we went for a picnic. (= As the weather was fine)
- **God willing**, we shall meet again. (= If God wills)
- **The teacher entering the room**, the students stood up.
- **All things considered**, the plan is good.
- **Weather permitting**, the match will be held tomorrow.
:::

::: note
In the absolute construction the noun/pronoun is in the **nominative** case: ✘ *Him being absent, the meeting was postponed.* ✔ **He being absent**, *the meeting was postponed.*
:::

### 14.4.4 Present Participle vs Past Participle (Active vs Passive Meaning)

| Present participle (active: doing) | Past participle (passive: done to) |
|---|---|
| a **boring** lecture (it bores us) | **bored** students (they are bored) |
| an **interesting** book | an **interested** reader |
| a **frightening** dream | a **frightened** child |
| a **loving** mother | a **loved** mother |
| the **governing** body | the **governed** people |
| **falling** leaves (in the act of falling) | **fallen** leaves (already on the ground) |

## 14.5 Gerund vs Present Participle — How to Tell Them Apart

Both end in *-ing*. Ask: **Is it working as a noun or as an adjective/verb?**

| Test | Gerund (noun) | Present participle (adjective/verb) |
|---|---|---|
| Can it be replaced by a noun / *it*? | **Swimming** is fun. → *It* is fun. ✔ | — |
| Does it show the **purpose** of the noun after it? | a **walking** stick = a stick **for** walking | a **walking** man = a man **who is** walking |
| Is it after a preposition? | fond **of reading** | — |
| Is it part of a continuous tense? | — | He **is reading**. |
| Does it describe a noun (= *which/who is …ing*)? | — | a **barking** dog = a dog that barks |

::: ex
- **Reading** is my hobby. → gerund (subject)
- I saw a boy **reading** a book. → participle (describes *boy*)
- a **dancing** girl (a girl who is dancing) → participle / a **dancing** hall (a hall for dancing) → gerund
- a **swimming** fish (participle) / a **swimming** pool (gerund)
- a **sleeping** child (participle) / a **sleeping** pill (gerund)
- a **drinking** man (participle) / **drinking** water (gerund — water for drinking)
- a **running** horse (participle) / **running** shoes (gerund)
:::

## 14.6 Verbal Nouns (vs Gerunds)

A **verbal noun** also ends in *-ing*, but it behaves **fully as a noun**: it takes an article (*the*) and an *of*-phrase, and cannot take a direct object.

::: ex
- **The reading of** novels is a pastime. (verbal noun)
- **Reading** novels is a pastime. (gerund — takes object *novels*)
- **The killing of** birds is prohibited. / **Killing** birds is prohibited.
- ✘ **The reading** novels / ✘ **Reading of** novels (mixed forms) — choose one pattern.
:::

## 14.7 Common Errors with Infinitives, Gerunds and Participles

::: errs
1. ✘ He is fond **to swim**. ✔ He is fond **of swimming**.
2. ✘ I am looking forward **to see** you. ✔ … **to seeing** you.
3. ✘ She avoided **to answer**. ✔ She avoided **answering**.
4. ✘ He made me **to wait**. ✔ He made me **wait**.
5. ✘ I am used to **work** at night. ✔ I am used to **working** at night.
6. ✘ It is no use **to try**. ✔ It is no use **trying**.
7. ✘ **Being** a rainy day, we stayed at home. ✔ **It being** a rainy day, we stayed at home.
8. ✘ **Reaching** the station, the train had left. ✔ **Reaching** the station, I found that the train had left.
9. ✘ I don't mind **him** smoking (formal). ✔ I don't mind **his** smoking.
10. ✘ He insisted **to pay**. ✔ He insisted **on paying**.
11. ✘ He prevented me **to go**. ✔ He prevented me **from going**.
12. ✘ She is capable **to do** it. ✔ She is capable **of doing** it.
13. ✘ The box is too heavy for me to lift **it**. ✔ The box is too heavy for me to lift.
14. ✘ I saw him **to go**. ✔ I saw him **go** / **going**.
15. ✘ He refused **paying** the bill. ✔ He refused **to pay** the bill.
16. ✘ We decided **going** to Cox's Bazar. ✔ We decided **to go** to Cox's Bazar.
17. ✘ He stopped **to smoke** (meaning he quit). ✔ He stopped **smoking**.
18. ✘ I prefer **to walk than ride**. ✔ I prefer **walking to riding**. / I prefer **to walk rather than ride**.
19. ✘ He is busy **to write** letters. ✔ He is busy **writing** letters.
20. ✘ He came here with a view **to see** me. ✔ He came here with a view **to seeing** me.
21. ✘ The **interested** novel kept me awake. ✔ The **interesting** novel kept me awake.
22. ✘ After **finished** the work, he left. ✔ After **finishing** the work, he left. / After **he had finished** …
23. ✘ **Him** being ill, we postponed the trip. ✔ **He** being ill, we postponed the trip.
24. ✘ He was seen **steal** the money. ✔ He was seen **to steal** the money.
25. ✘ It is time **for going** to bed. ✔ It is time **to go** to bed.
:::

::: gre
**SAT / GRE / GMAT Corner — Idiomatic Infinitive vs Gerund:** The GMAT idiom list is full of these: **"ability to"** (✘ ability of doing), **"capable of"** + V-ing, **"forbid X to do"** (✘ forbid from), **"prohibit X from doing"**, **"prevent X from doing"**, **"require X to do"**, **"in an attempt to"**, **"the attempt to"**, **"consider X Y"** (no *as* / *to be* in strict style), **"a tendency to"**, **"responsible for"** + V-ing, **"credit X with doing"**. And remember the GMAT's dislike of the *-ing* form where a finite verb or a noun is clearer: ✘ *"The rising of prices…"* ✔ *"The rise in prices…"*.
:::
